**This chapter contains two major scenes and a transitional note.** The wedding at Qanah (vv.1–12) introduces the first "sign" (*sēmeion*) — a term John uses deliberately instead of "miracle" (*dynamis*) or "wonder" (*teras*). The temple clearing (vv.13–22) introduces the distinction between ναός (*naos*, the inner sanctuary) and ἱερόν (*hieron*, the temple complex). The chapter closes with a narrator's comment on Yeshua's knowledge of human nature (vv.23–25). Proper names follow TT transliteration: Qanah (not Cana), Yeshua (not Jesus), Kfar Nachum (not Capernaum), Yerushalayim (not Jerusalem).

## CHAPTER OVERVIEW

**What happens:** On the third day, Yeshua attends a wedding at Qanah in the Galil. When the wine runs out, his mother tells him; he responds with a distancing phrase ("What to me and to you, woman?") and states that his hour has not yet come. His mother tells the servants to do whatever he says. Six stone jars used for Jewish purification rites are filled with water; when the servants draw out and bring it to the chief steward, it has become wine. The steward, not knowing the source, remarks that the good wine has been kept until now. The narrator identifies this as the beginning of the signs and says his followers trusted in him. After a brief stay in Kfar Nachum, Yeshua goes up to Yerushalayim for the Passover. He finds those selling cattle, sheep, and doves in the temple complex, and money-changers seated there. Making a whip of cords, he drives them all out and overturns the tables. When the Yehudim demand a sign for his authority, he answers: "Destroy this sanctuary, and in three days I will raise it up." They object that the sanctuary took forty-six years to build. The narrator explains retrospectively that he was speaking about the sanctuary of his body. The chapter closes with many in Yerushalayim trusting in his name because of the signs, but Yeshua not entrusting himself to them — because he knew what was in a human being.

**Key themes:** Sign (*sēmeion*) as revelatory act, not mere display of power; the "hour" (*hōra*) that has not yet come — a motif that runs through the Gospel until 12:23 and 13:1; the mother of Yeshua (unnamed in John's Gospel); the temple as contested space; the body as sanctuary; Yeshua's knowledge of human nature; trust (*pistis*) that is superficial versus trust that is genuine.

**Watch for:** The distancing idiom τί ἐμοὶ καὶ σοί (*ti emoi kai soi*, "what to me and to you") — a Semitic expression of disengagement, not rudeness; the address γύναι (*gynai*, "woman") — formal, not disrespectful, but unusual from son to mother; the narrator's retrospective explanation at vv.21–22, which introduces a hermeneutical key (the disciples "remembered" after the resurrection); the wordplay on "raising up" (ἐγείρω, *egeirō*) — which covers both rebuilding a structure and raising a body from death; the connection between the six stone purification jars and the Jewish purification system; the deliberate use of *naos* (inner sanctuary) rather than *hieron* (temple complex) in Yeshua's statement.

**Connection:** The transformation of water to wine at a wedding connects to the creation narrative in Genesis: the God who made water (Gen 1:6–10) and who planted a garden with fruit (Gen 2:8–9) now transforms water into wine through the *logos*-made-flesh. The temple-clearing scene and the "destroy this sanctuary" statement anticipate the passion narrative and connect backward to the creation-destruction-rebuilding motif in Genesis (the flood narrative, Gen 6–9). The six stone jars for purification connect to the Jewish purity system rooted in Levitical law.

## VERSE-BY-VERSE STUDY

### **Verse 1**

And on the third day a wedding took place in Qanah of the Galil, and the mother of Yeshua was there.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔵 **"ON THE THIRD DAY" — τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῇ τρίτῃ**
> - The phrase τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῇ τρίτῃ (*tē hēmera tē tritē*) = "on the third day." Counted from the last event narrated in ch.1 (the call of Philippos and Nathanael). The accumulated day-markers in 1:29, 1:35, 1:43, and now 2:1 create a sequence spanning roughly one week — echoing the seven-day creation structure of Gen 1. Whether this echo is deliberate or coincidental is debated. The phrase "on the third day" will recur with different significance at v.19 (the resurrection prediction).
>
> 🟢 **QANAH — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Κανά** (*Kana*) = Greek form of Hebrew/Aramaic קָנָה (*Qanah*). TT renders the Semitic form. The site is traditionally identified with Kafr Kanna (near Natseret) or Khirbet Qana (9 km north of Natseret). Archaeological evidence at Khirbet Qana includes a large village with evidence of Jewish habitation in the first century CE and later Christian pilgrimage to a cave venerated as the site of the miracle.
>
> 🟡 **"THE MOTHER OF YESHUA" — UNNAMED**
> - The Fourth Gospel never names Yeshua's mother. She appears here and at the cross (19:25–27) but is never called "Miriam" or "Mary." The text identifies her relationally — "the mother of Yeshua" — not personally. Whether this omission is theologically motivated, compositionally incidental, or reflects a tradition in which her personal name was not central to the narrative is unresolvable from the text alone.

---

### **Verse 2**

And Yeshua also was invited, and his followers, to the wedding.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **"FOLLOWERS" — μαθηταί (*mathētai*)**
> - **μαθηταί** (*mathētai*) = learners, students, followers. The term describes those who follow a teacher to learn. By this point in the narrative (following 1:35–51), Yeshua has gathered at least five followers: Andreas, the unnamed disciple, Shimon Kefa, Philippos, and Nathanael.

---

### **Verse 3**

And *when* wine ran short, the mother of Yeshua says to him, "They have no wine."

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔵 **"WINE RAN SHORT" — ὑστερήσαντος τοῦ οἴνου**
> - **ὑστερήσαντος τοῦ οἴνου** = genitive absolute, "the wine having run short" — temporal: "when the wine ran short." The verb **ὑστερέω** (*hystereō*) = to lack, fall short, run out. Running out of wine at a wedding was a serious social failure for the host family — it implied inadequate provision and brought shame.
>
> 🟡 **THE MOTHER'S STATEMENT — IMPLICIT REQUEST?**
> - "They have no wine" is a statement of fact, not an explicit request. Whether the mother expects Yeshua to act, and in what way, is not stated. The text does not explain what she anticipated. Reading her words as a veiled request imports an assumption the text does not make explicit.

---

### **Verse 4**

And Yeshua says to her, "What to me and to you, woman? My hour has not yet come."

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — τί ἐμοὶ καὶ σοί ("WHAT TO ME AND TO YOU")**
> - **τί ἐμοὶ καὶ σοί, γύναι** (*ti emoi kai soi, gynai*) = literally "what to me and to you, woman?" This is a Semitic idiom (Hebrew: מַה־לִּי וָלָךְ, *mah-li valakh*) found in the HB at Judges 11:12, 2 Sam 16:10, 1 Kings 17:18, 2 Kings 3:13, 2 Chron 35:21. In each HB occurrence, the expression creates distance — "what business do we have with each other?" or "what is there between us?" The idiom ranges from mild disengagement to sharp rebuff depending on context. The TT translates literally per Rule 1. Any softening ("What concern is that to you and to me?" or "How does that concern us?") obscures the Semitic idiom.
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — γύναι ("WOMAN") AS ADDRESS**
> - **γύναι** (*gynai*) = vocative of γυνή (*gynē*, "woman"). In Greek usage, this address was formal and respectful — comparable to "madam" — not dismissive. Yeshua uses the same address at the cross (19:26). However, it is unusual as a son's address to his mother — no parallel exists in Greek or Jewish literature of a son addressing his mother as "woman" in a domestic context. The TT renders "woman" literally. The cultural dissonance is real and should not be smoothed over.
>
> 🟡 **"MY HOUR HAS NOT YET COME" — οὔπω ἥκει ἡ ὥρα μου**
> - **ἡ ὥρα μου** (*hē hōra mou*) = "my hour." The "hour" (*hōra*) becomes a major Johannine motif, recurring at 7:30, 8:20 (the hour has not yet come), 12:23, 12:27, 13:1, 17:1 (the hour has come). The "hour" ultimately refers to Yeshua's death, glorification, and return to the Father. Here at the beginning, the hour "has not yet come" — but Yeshua acts anyway. The relationship between the "not yet" and the subsequent action is left unexplained by the text.

---

### **Verse 5**

His mother says to the servants, "Whatever he says to you, do *it*.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔵 **ECHO OF GEN 41:55 — "DO WHATEVER HE SAYS"**
> - The mother's instruction to the servants — ὅ τι ἂν λέγῃ ὑμῖν ποιήσατε (*ho ti an legē hymin poiēsate*) — echoes Pharaoh's instruction about Yosef in Gen 41:55 LXX: πορεύεσθε πρὸς Ιωσηφ καὶ ὃ ἐὰν εἴπῃ ὑμῖν ποιήσατε ("go to Yosef, and whatever he says to you, do"). Whether the echo is deliberate is debated; the verbal overlap is close enough to note. In both cases, an authoritative figure directs subordinates to obey the instructions of one who will provide.
>
> 🟡 **THE MOTHER'S ROLE — MEDIATOR OR CATALYST?**
> - Despite Yeshua's distancing response, the mother acts as though the request will be fulfilled. The text does not explain her confidence. She neither repeats her request nor argues — she redirects the servants to Yeshua's authority. Her narrative role in John is catalytic (here) and receptive (at the cross, 19:26–27), never doctrinal.

---

### **Verse 6**

Now there were six stone water jars standing there, according to the purification *rites* of the Yehudim, each holding two or three measures.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **"PURIFICATION" — καθαρισμός (*katharismos*)**
> - **κατὰ τὸν καθαρισμὸν τῶν Ἰουδαίων** = "according to the purification of the Yehudim." The stone jars were for ritual hand-washing before meals and other purification rites prescribed by Jewish tradition (cf. Mark 7:3–4). Stone vessels were preferred because, under halakhic ruling, stone does not contract ritual impurity (*tum'ah*) — unlike clay, which must be broken if contaminated.
>
> 🔵 **"TWO OR THREE MEASURES" — μετρητάς**
> - **μετρητής** (*metrētēs*) = a liquid measure of approximately 39 liters (about 10 gallons). Two to three *metrētai* per jar = 78–117 liters (20–30 gallons) each. Six jars at this capacity = approximately 470–700 liters (120–180 gallons) total. The quantity is enormous — far exceeding any single wedding's needs. The scale is part of the sign.
>
> 🟡 **SIX JARS — SYMBOLIC NUMBER?**
> - Six in Jewish numerology falls one short of seven (completion/fullness). Whether the six jars symbolize the incompleteness of the purification system — now to be superseded by what Yeshua provides — is a common interpretive reading. The text states the number without commentary. The TT records the number without supplying the symbolism.

---

### **Verse 7**

Yeshua says to them, "Fill the jars with water." And they filled them to the top.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔵 **"TO THE TOP" — ἕως ἄνω**
> - **ἕως ἄνω** (*heōs anō*) = "up to the top/brim." The servants' obedience is total — no half-measures. The detail eliminates the possibility of admixture: the jars were completely full of water.

---

### **Verse 8**

And he says to them, "Draw out now and bring *it* to the chief steward." And they brought *it*.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **"CHIEF STEWARD" — ἀρχιτρίκλινος (*architriklinos*)**
> - **ἀρχιτρίκλινος** (*architriklinos*) = the chief steward or master of the feast — the person responsible for managing the banquet, tasting the wine, and directing the serving. A known social role in Greco-Roman and Jewish banquet culture. The chief steward functions as an unwitting witness: he tastes the wine without knowing its origin.

---

### **Verse 9**

And when the chief steward tasted the water having become wine — and did not know from where it was, but the servants who had drawn the water knew — the chief steward calls the bridegroom

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔵 **"THE WATER HAVING BECOME WINE" — PARTICIPIAL DESCRIPTION**
> - **τὸ ὕδωρ οἶνον γεγενημένον** (*to hydōr oinon gegenēmenon*) = "the water having become wine." The perfect participle **γεγενημένον** (*gegenēmenon*) from γίνομαι (*ginomai*) = "having become" — the transformation is presented as completed. The narrator describes the result without narrating the process. There is no moment of transformation described — the water was drawn and the water had become wine. The mechanism is absent from the text.
>
> 🟡 **"DID NOT KNOW FROM WHERE" — JOHANNINE IRONY**
> - **οὐκ ᾔδει πόθεν ἐστίν** = "did not know from where it is." The theme of not knowing "from where" (*pothen*) recurs in John (3:8, 7:27–28, 8:14, 9:29–30, 19:9). The question of origin — where Yeshua comes from, where his authority comes from — runs through the Gospel. The chief steward does not know the origin of the wine; the servants (who obeyed) do know. Knowledge of origin is connected to proximity to the action.

---

### **Verse 10**

and says to him, "Every man sets out the good wine first, and when *they* have become drunk, the lesser. You have kept the good wine until now."

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **"BECOME DRUNK" — μεθυσθῶσιν (*methysthōsin*)**
> - **μεθυσθῶσιν** (*methysthōsin*) = aorist passive subjunctive of μεθύσκω (*methyskō*) = "to become drunk/intoxicated." The chief steward's observation is practical, not theological: banquet custom was to serve better wine first, before guests' palates dulled. The text does not moralize about drunkenness — it records the steward's observation about standard practice.
>
> 🟡 **"KEPT THE GOOD WINE UNTIL NOW" — NARRATIVE IRONY**
> - The steward assumes the bridegroom made a deliberate choice to reverse the normal order. The reader knows the bridegroom did nothing — the good wine has an entirely different source. The irony operates at two levels: within the story (the steward misidentifies the agent) and for the reader (the "good wine" comes from the *logos*-made-flesh, not from the wedding host).

---

### **Verse 11**

This beginning of the signs Yeshua did in Qanah of the Galil and revealed his glory, and his followers trusted in him.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "SIGN" (*SĒMEION*), NOT "MIRACLE"**
> - **σημεῖον** (*sēmeion*) = sign — something that points to a reality beyond itself. John never uses **δύναμις** (*dynamis*, "power/mighty work") or **τέρας** (*teras*, "wonder/portent") to describe Yeshua's acts. The Synoptics use *dynamis* frequently. John's term *sēmeion* frames the act as revelatory — it signifies something, it does not merely display power. The water-to-wine is not called a "miracle" — it is called a "sign." The distinction is deliberate and load-bearing for John's theology.
>
> 🟢 **"BEGINNING OF THE SIGNS" — ἀρχὴν τῶν σημείων**
> - **ταύτην ἐποίησεν ἀρχὴν τῶν σημείων** = "this he did *as* the beginning of the signs." The word **ἀρχή** (*archē*) = beginning — the same word that opens the Gospel ("In the beginning"). This is the first sign; it inaugurates a sequence. John numbers the first two signs explicitly (2:11, 4:54); subsequent signs are not numbered.
>
> 🟢 **"REVEALED HIS GLORY" — ἐφανέρωσεν τὴν δόξαν αὐτοῦ**
> - **δόξα** (*doxa*) = glory. In John 1:14, the *logos*-made-flesh's glory was seen — "glory as of an only one from a father." The sign at Qanah is the first public manifestation of that glory. The verb **φανερόω** (*phaneroō*) = to make visible, to reveal what was hidden. The glory was already present (1:14); the sign makes it visible.
>
> 🟢 **"TRUSTED" — ἐπίστευσαν**
> - **ἐπίστευσαν εἰς αὐτόν** (*episteusan eis auton*) = "trusted in him" — aorist. This trust, prompted by a sign, will be contrasted at vv.23–25 with trust that Yeshua does not reciprocate. The quality of sign-based trust is a recurring question in John (cf. 4:48, 6:26, 20:29).

---

### **Verse 12**

After this he went down to Kfar Nachum — he and his mother and his brothers and his followers — and they stayed there not many days.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **KFAR NACHUM — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Καφαρναούμ** (*Kapharnaoum*) = Greek form of Hebrew/Aramaic כְּפַר נַחוּם (*Kfar Nachum*, "village of Nachum"). TT renders the Semitic form. Kfar Nachum was a fishing village on the northwest shore of the Kinneret (Sea of Galilee). The Synoptics present it as the base of Yeshua's Galilean ministry.
>
> 🟡 **"HIS BROTHERS" — οἱ ἀδελφοὶ αὐτοῦ**
> - **ἀδελφοί** (*adelphoi*) = brothers. The term can mean full siblings, half-siblings, step-siblings, or kinsmen depending on context and tradition. The text does not specify the relationship further. Later theological traditions (perpetual virginity of Mary) read *adelphoi* as "cousins" or "step-brothers" (from a prior marriage of Yosef). The text itself uses the standard word for "brothers" without qualification.
>
> 🔵 **"WENT DOWN" — κατέβη**
> - **κατέβη** (*katebē*) = "went down." Geographically accurate: Qanah is in the Galilean highlands; Kfar Nachum is on the shore of the Kinneret, approximately 200 meters below sea level. John's geographical references are consistently accurate to the terrain.

---

### **Verse 13**

And the Passover of the Yehudim was near, and Yeshua went up to Yerushalayim.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **"PASSOVER" — πάσχα (*pascha*)**
> - **τὸ πάσχα** (*to pascha*) = Passover — from Hebrew פֶּסַח (*pesach*). John mentions three Passovers (2:13, 6:4, 11:55), implying a ministry of at least two to three years. The Synoptics narrate only one Passover. The placement of the temple clearing at the first Passover (rather than the last, as in the Synoptics) is a significant structural difference — see note at v.14.
>
> 🔵 **"WENT UP" — ἀνέβη**
> - **ἀνέβη** (*anebē*) = "went up." Yerushalayim sits at approximately 750 meters elevation. One always "goes up" to Yerushalayim — both geographically and theologically (pilgrimage ascent).

---

### **Verse 14**

And he found in the temple *complex* those selling cattle and sheep and doves, and the money-changers seated.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — ἱερόν (*HIERON*) vs. ναός (*NAOS*)**
> - **ἐν τῷ ἱερῷ** (*en tō hierō*) = "in the *hieron*" — the temple complex as a whole, including the courts, porticoes, and commercial areas. This is distinct from **ναός** (*naos*), the inner sanctuary (the holy place and holy of holies), which Yeshua will reference at v.19. The commerce took place in the Court of the Gentiles — the outermost court of the temple complex. The TT renders *hieron* as "temple complex" (in italics to mark the distinction) and *naos* as "sanctuary."
>
> 🟢 **"MONEY-CHANGERS" — κερματιστάς (*kermatistas*)**
> - **κερματιστής** (*kermatistēs*) = one who exchanges coins (*kerma* = small coin). Pilgrims arriving for Passover needed to exchange foreign currency (Roman, Greek, Syrian coins bearing images) for Tyrian shekels — the only currency accepted for the half-shekel temple tax (Exod 30:13). The money-changing operation was a necessary infrastructure of temple worship, not inherently corrupt — though the potential for exploitative exchange rates is attested in rabbinic sources.
>
> 🟡 **TEMPLE CLEARING — JOHANNINE PLACEMENT**
> - The Synoptics place the temple clearing in the final week of Yeshua's ministry (Matt 21:12–13, Mark 11:15–17, Luke 19:45–46). John places it at the beginning. Three options: (1) John relocated the event for theological reasons (the programmatic statement at the start of the ministry); (2) the Synoptics relocated it (the climactic act precipitating arrest); (3) there were two separate incidents. The TT records the placement without resolving the question.

---

### **Verse 15**

And having made a whip out of cords, he drove *them* all out of the temple *complex*, both the sheep and the cattle, and he poured out the coins of the money-changers and overturned the tables.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔵 **"HAVING MADE A WHIP" — φραγέλλιον ποιήσας**
> - **φραγέλλιον** (*phragellion*) = a whip, from Latin *flagellum*. The word is a loanword — one of several Latin terms in John (cf. πραιτώριον, *praitōrion*, 18:28; λιθόστρωτον, *lithostrōton*, 19:13). **ἐκ σχοινίων** (*ek schoiniōn*) = "out of cords/rushes." The whip is improvised from materials at hand. Only John mentions the whip.
>
> 🔵 **"ALL" — πάντας**
> - **πάντας** (*pantas*) = masculine accusative plural, "all" — grammatically, this could refer to the people, the animals, or both. The following clause specifies "both the sheep and the cattle" (*ta te probata kai tous boas*), which some read as epexegetical (explaining *pantas* as referring to the animals only). Others read *pantas* as including the sellers. The grammatical ambiguity is genuine.

---

### **Verse 16**

And to those selling the doves he said, "Take these *things* from here; do not make the house of my Father a house of commerce."

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🟡 **"HOUSE OF MY FATHER" — τὸν οἶκον τοῦ πατρός μου**
> - Yeshua refers to the temple as "the house of my Father" — a claim of filial relationship with God that the narrator does not qualify. The Synoptic parallels quote Isa 56:7 ("my house shall be called a house of prayer") and Jer 7:11 ("den of robbers"). John does not quote either — the charge is different: not robbery (*lēstērion*) but commerce (*emporion*). The issue is not exploitation but the presence of commerce in the Father's house at all.
>
> 🟢 **"COMMERCE" — ἐμπόριον (*emporion*)**
> - **ἐμπόριον** (*emporion*) = a place of trade, market. From ἔμπορος (*emporos*) = merchant, trader. The English word "emporium" derives from this. The charge is commercialization, not theft.

---

### **Verse 17**

His followers remembered that it is written, "The zeal of your house will consume me."

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — PSALM 69:9 (LXX 68:10) QUOTATION**
> - **ὁ ζῆλος τοῦ οἴκου σου καταφάγεταί με** = "the zeal of your house will consume me." Quoted from Psalm 69:9 (LXX 68:10). The Hebrew original reads: כִּי־קִנְאַת בֵּיתְךָ אֲכָלָתְנִי (*ki-qin'at beytekha akhalatni*) = "for the zeal of your house has consumed me" — past tense. The LXX renders the verb as future: καταφάγεταί (*kataphagetai*) = "will consume." John follows the LXX future tense, which shifts the psalm from a past lament to a prophetic anticipation. The "zeal" (*zēlos*) that will "consume" Yeshua points forward to the passion — his zeal for God's house will lead to his death.
>
> 🟢 **"ZEAL" — ζῆλος (*zēlos*)**
> - **ζῆλος** (*zēlos*) = zeal, intense devotion, jealous passion. The word carries both positive (righteous devotion) and negative (jealousy, rivalry) connotations depending on context. Here it describes consuming devotion to God's house.

---

### **Verse 18**

Then the Yehudim answered and said to him, "What sign do you show us, *seeing* that you do these things?"

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🟡 **DEMAND FOR A SIGN — AUTHORITY QUESTION**
> - The Yehudim demand a **σημεῖον** (*sēmeion*) — a sign authenticating Yeshua's authority to disrupt the temple operations. The demand assumes that prophetic or messianic actions require validating credentials. The irony: Yeshua has just performed the first sign (v.11), but this audience was not present at Qanah. They demand what has already been given elsewhere.
>
> 🟢 **"THE YEHUDIM" — οἱ Ἰουδαῖοι**
> - In this context, "the Yehudim" (*hoi Ioudaioi*) = the Jerusalem-based authorities who control the temple operations — institutional, not ethnic. Same semantic range as noted in John 1:19.

---

### **Verse 19**

Yeshua answered and said to them, "Destroy this sanctuary, and in three days I will raise it up."

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — ναός (*NAOS*), NOT ἱερόν (*HIERON*)**
> - **λύσατε τὸν ναὸν τοῦτον** = "destroy this *naos*." Yeshua switches from *hieron* (the temple complex, v.14) to *naos* (the inner sanctuary). The *naos* is the sacred core — the dwelling place of God's presence, the holy of holies. The narrator will explain (v.21) that Yeshua was speaking about his body as the *naos*. The shift from *hieron* to *naos* is deliberate — the body is not the "temple complex" (the institutional structure) but the "sanctuary" (the dwelling place of divine presence).
>
> 🔵 **"DESTROY" — λύσατε (AORIST IMPERATIVE)**
> - **λύσατε** (*lysate*) = aorist imperative of λύω (*lyō*) = to loose, unbind, destroy. The imperative form could be conditional ("if you destroy...") or permissive ("go ahead, destroy...") or even prophetic ("you will destroy..."). The text leaves the force ambiguous. The Synoptic trial accounts (Matt 26:61, Mark 14:58) will echo this saying as an accusation.
>
> 🔵 **"I WILL RAISE IT UP" — ἐγερῶ αὐτόν**
> - **ἐγερῶ** (*egerō*) = future active of ἐγείρω (*egeirō*) = to raise up, awaken. The verb covers both reconstruction (raising a building) and resurrection (raising from death). The double meaning is the hinge of the entire exchange — the Yehudim hear "rebuild"; the narrator intends "resurrect." This wordplay is accessible only in retrospect (v.22).

---

### **Verse 20**

Then the Yehudim said, "This sanctuary was built *over* forty-six years, and you will raise it up in three days?"

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **"FORTY-SIX YEARS" — τεσσεράκοντα καὶ ἓξ ἔτεσιν**
> - Herod the Great began the temple renovation/expansion in approximately 20/19 BCE (Josephus, *Antiquities* 15.380). Forty-six years from that date yields approximately 27/28 CE — a dating marker for the chronology of Yeshua's ministry. The construction was technically still ongoing (it was not completed until approximately 63 CE, only seven years before its destruction in 70 CE). The verb **οἰκοδομήθη** (*oikodomēthē*) = aorist passive, "was built" — summarizing the construction as a completed whole, even though work continued.

---

### **Verse 21**

But he was speaking about the sanctuary of his body.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — NARRATOR'S RETROSPECTIVE EXPLANATION**
> - **ἐκεῖνος δὲ ἔλεγεν περὶ τοῦ ναοῦ τοῦ σώματος αὐτοῦ** = "but that one was speaking about the *naos* of his body." This is the narrator's interpretive comment, inserted to guide the reader. The imperfect **ἔλεγεν** (*elegen*) = "he was speaking" — the narrator explains what Yeshua was saying all along. The identification body = *naos* (sanctuary, dwelling place of God) is explicit. This hermeneutical key is given by the narrator, not by Yeshua — within the story, the Yehudim do not receive this explanation.
>
> 🟡 **BODY AS SANCTUARY — THEOLOGICAL CLAIM**
> - The claim that Yeshua's body is the *naos* (the dwelling place of divine presence) connects to 1:14 ("the word became flesh and dwelt/tabernacled among us"). If the *logos* dwells in flesh, then the flesh is the new sanctuary. The destruction and raising of this sanctuary = death and resurrection. The claim replaces the stone temple with a bodily temple — a shift with profound implications for the entire sacrificial-temple system.

---

### **Verse 22**

When therefore he was raised from *the* dead, his followers remembered that he said this, and they trusted the scripture and the word that Yeshua had spoken.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔵 **"WAS RAISED" — ἠγέρθη (AORIST PASSIVE)**
> - **ἠγέρθη** (*ēgerthē*) = aorist passive of ἐγείρω (*egeirō*) = "was raised." The passive voice implies an agent: he was raised (by God). This contrasts with v.19 where Yeshua says "I will raise it up" (active) — suggesting both divine agency and self-agency in the resurrection. The tension between passive and active voice is preserved, not resolved.
>
> 🟡 **"REMEMBERED" — ἐμνήσθησαν**
> - **ἐμνήσθησαν** (*emnēsthēsan*) = "they remembered." This is the second occurrence of post-event remembering in this chapter (cf. v.17). In John, remembering (*mimnēskomai*) is a hermeneutical act — the followers understand Yeshua's words only after the event they predicted. The text explicitly presents understanding as retrospective: they did not understand at the time. The narrator does not disguise this.
>
> 🟢 **"THE SCRIPTURE" — τῇ γραφῇ**
> - Which scripture? The text does not specify. Candidates include Psalm 16:10 ("you will not abandon my soul to Sheol, nor let your faithful one see the pit"), Hosea 6:2 ("on the third day he will raise us up"), or the broader scriptural pattern of divine vindication. The TT does not supply a referent the text leaves unidentified.

---

### **Verse 23**

And when he was in Yerushalayim at the Passover, at the feast, many trusted in his name, seeing the signs that he was doing.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🟡 **TRUST BASED ON SIGNS — QUALIFIED**
> - **πολλοὶ ἐπίστευσαν εἰς τὸ ὄνομα αὐτοῦ** = "many trusted in his name." The trust described here is prompted by signs — the same basis as the followers' trust at v.11. But the following verses (24–25) will qualify this trust as insufficient. John distinguishes between sign-prompted trust (which Yeshua does not reciprocate) and a deeper trust. The Greek is identical in form to v.11 — the difference is in Yeshua's response to it.
>
> 🟢 **"THE SIGNS" — τὰ σημεῖα**
> - **σημεῖα** (*sēmeia*) = plural of *sēmeion*. The narrator refers to signs (plural) beyond the one at Qanah. These Jerusalem signs are not narrated — the reader is told they happened without being shown what they were.

---

### **Verse 24**

But Yeshua himself was not entrusting himself to them, because he knew all *people*,

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — WORDPLAY ON *PISTEUŌ* (TRUST/ENTRUST)**
> - **αὐτὸς δὲ Ἰησοῦς οὐκ ἐπίστευεν αὑτὸν αὐτοῖς** = "but Yeshua himself was not entrusting himself to them." The verb **πιστεύω** (*pisteuō*) is used in v.23 for the crowd's trust in Yeshua and in v.24 for Yeshua's non-trust toward the crowd. The wordplay is untranslatable without preserving the same verb root: they *trusted* in him, but he did not *entrust* himself to them. The imperfect **ἐπίστευεν** (*episteuen*) = ongoing non-trust — habitual, not a one-time decision.
>
> 🟡 **"HE KNEW ALL" — διὰ τὸ αὐτὸν γινώσκειν πάντας**
> - Yeshua's non-entrustment is grounded in knowledge: he knew all people. This claim of comprehensive human knowledge anticipates v.25 and runs through the Gospel (cf. 6:64, 13:11, 18:4). The narrator presents Yeshua's knowledge as penetrating — not based on external testimony but on direct perception.

---

### **Verse 25**

and because he had no need that anyone should testify concerning the human being — for he himself knew what was in the human being.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔵 **"THE HUMAN BEING" — τοῦ ἀνθρώπου / τῷ ἀνθρώπῳ**
> - **τί ἦν ἐν τῷ ἀνθρώπῳ** = "what was in the human being." The article **τῷ** (*tō*) is generic — "the human being" as a category, not a specific person. Yeshua's knowledge is not limited to certain individuals; it extends to human nature itself. The statement is anthropological: he knows what humans are. The chapter that began with a wedding ends with a statement about the unreliability of human nature.
>
> 🟢 **"TESTIFY" — μαρτυρήσῃ**
> - **μαρτυρέω** (*martyreō*) = to testify — the same word family introduced in 1:7 for Yochanan's role. Yeshua does not need external witnesses to tell him about people — he knows directly. The legal/testimony language continues.

## CROSS-CHAPTER TRACKING (Genesis → John)

**Gen 1 creation week → John 1–2 day sequence:**

| Day | Genesis 1 | John 1–2 |
|-----|-----------|----------|
| Day 1 | Light created (1:3–5) | Yochanan's testimony (1:19–28) |
| Day 2 | Sky/waters separated (1:6–8) | "The next day" — lamb of God (1:29–34) |
| Day 3 | Land, vegetation (1:9–13) | "The next day" — first followers (1:35–42) |
| Day 4 | Luminaries (1:14–19) | "The next day" — Philippos, Nathanael (1:43–51) |
| Day 7 | "The third day" = day 3 from day 4 | Wedding at Qanah — first sign (2:1–11) |

The day-sequence across John 1–2 may echo the creation week, culminating in the first sign on what corresponds to the seventh day — the day of completion and rest. If deliberate, the wedding at Qanah is presented as a new-creation event. The parallel is suggestive but not confirmed by the text itself.

**Gen 1:2 (water) → John 2:1–11 (water → wine):**

| Element | Gen 1:2, 6–10 | John 2:6–9 |
|---------|----------------|------------|
| Water | Primordial waters, separated and ordered | Water in stone purification jars |
| Transformation | Water → structured creation (seas, land) | Water → wine |
| Agent | God's word/command | Yeshua's word/command |
| Result | Ordered cosmos | Revealed glory |

In Genesis, water is the raw material from which God orders creation. In John 2, water is the raw material from which the sign produces wine. Both transformations occur by verbal command. The *logos* who was the agent of creation (1:3) now transforms creation's elements.

**Gen 2:8–9 (garden / fruit) → John 2:1–10 (wedding / wine):**

The garden of Eden contained "every tree pleasant to look at and good for food" (Gen 2:9). Wine — the product of the vine — is associated in the HB with joy, abundance, and the messianic age (Amos 9:13, Joel 3:18, Isa 25:6). The abundance of wine at Qanah (470–700 liters) echoes the extravagance of Eden's provision and the prophetic expectation of overflowing wine in the age to come.

**Gen 28:22 (Beyt-El = house of God) → John 2:16 (house of my Father):**

| Element | Gen 28:22 | John 2:16 |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Phrase | "This will be the house of God" (*beyt elohim*) | "The house of my Father" (*oikos tou patros mou*) |
| Location | A stone pillar at Beyt-El | The temple in Yerushalayim |
| Claim | Yaakov identifies a place as God's dwelling | Yeshua identifies the temple as his Father's house |
| Authority | Yaakov names the site | Yeshua claims filial authority over the site |

Yaakov designated a stone as God's house after the ladder vision. Yeshua claims the temple as his Father's house — and then redefines the true sanctuary as his body (v.21). The trajectory moves from stone pillar to stone temple to bodily temple.
