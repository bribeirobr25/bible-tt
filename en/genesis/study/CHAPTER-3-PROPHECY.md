## The seed of the woman (Gen 3:15)

**Verse:** Genesis 3:15
**Text says:** "And I will place enmity between you and between the woman, and between your seed and between her seed; he shall strike you *on* the head, and you shall strike him *on* the heel."
**Context:** YHWH God speaks to the serpent after the humans have eaten from the tree. This is part of the consequences pronounced on the serpent (3:14-15).
**Subject:** The serpent's seed and the woman's seed — the text does not identify who "her seed" refers to beyond "offspring of the woman."
**Fulfillment status:** DEBATED
**Fulfillment notes:**
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: The verse describes ongoing enmity between humanity and snakes. Some rabbinic traditions read "seed of the woman" as referring to a future redeemer, but this is not the dominant reading. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: This is the *protoevangelium* — the first announcement of the Gospel. "Her seed" (singular) is read as Christ; the serpent is Satan. The head-striking is Christ's victory over evil; the heel-striking is the crucifixion. This reading is attested from Irenaeus (2nd c. CE) onward. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: The Quran does not directly reference this verse. Islamic tradition acknowledges enmity between Iblis (Satan) and humanity but does not derive it from Genesis 3:15 specifically. [DOCUMENTED]

**Scholarly note:** The Hebrew *hu* ("he") is masculine singular, referring grammatically to "seed" (*zera*, masc.). Whether the singular implies an individual or the collective offspring is UNCERTAIN. The LXX uses *autos* (masc.) rather than *auto* (neut., which would match the neut. *sperma*), suggesting the translators may have read an individual referent. [POSSIBLE]

## The serpent's curse — permanent degradation (Gen 3:14)

**Verse:** Genesis 3:14
**Text says:** "Cursed *are* you from all the livestock and from all the living things of the field; on your belly you shall go, and dust you shall eat all the days of your life."
**Context:** YHWH God speaks directly to the serpent.
**Subject:** The serpent — whether a literal snake, a symbol, or a spiritual being is not resolved by the text.
**Fulfillment status:** CLAIMED
**Fulfillment notes:**
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: The serpent's curse explains why snakes crawl. Some midrashic traditions say the serpent originally had legs. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: The serpent represents Satan (Revelation 12:9, 20:2); the curse is eschatological degradation. [DOCUMENTED]
