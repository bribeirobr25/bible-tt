# TT Companion Enrichment Research: Genesis 1–12, John 1–3, Matthew 1–3, Mark 1–3

## TL;DR
- This research surfaces **24 well-sourced, genuinely novel candidate entries** that go beyond the already-covered material, each anchored in a primary text (inscription, ANE composition, Targum, Qur'an) or peer-reviewed scholarship and tagged with the project's claim-type, confidence, and provenance labels.
- The strongest, lowest-risk additions are five VERIFIED anchors: the **Tell Fekheriye bilingual statue** (tselem/demut, Gen 1:26), the **Etemenanki primary inscriptions** (Babel, Gen 11), the **Deir 'Alla "Balaam son of Beor" inscription** (Matt 2 reception), the **Galilee Boat** (Mark 1 fishing economy), and **Raymond Brown's analysis of the four women** in Matthew's genealogy.
- The single most reliability-sensitive issue across the set is the **comparative-parallel-vs-dependence boundary**: popular web sources repeatedly overstate dependence (Memphite Theology→Genesis, Legion→Legio X Fretensis, Sumerian King List→Genesis 5). Every such entry below carries an explicit "parallel ≠ dependence" flag for authoring.

## Key Findings
The richest novel veins are: **(B) ANE parallels** and **(C/I) archaeology / world-at-the-time** for Genesis; **(C) Judean–Galilean archaeology** and **(F) Islamic and Targumic reception** for the gospels; and **(D) philology** grounded in standard editions and lexica. Most candidates trace cleanly to a named credentialed scholar or a critical edition; a minority surfaced through aggregator pages but rest on solid primary/peer-reviewed sources, which are named below as the citation of record. Two items (John 5 Bethesda; Mark 5 Gerasene) fall **outside the authored chapter ranges** and are flagged for exclusion unless scope expands.

## Details by Book and Section

### GENESIS 1–12

**G-1. Tell Fekheriye bilingual inscription — tselem & demut (Gen 1:26–27).**
- Section: C (Historical/Archaeological) + D (Philology). Claim-type: HISTORICAL-ARCHAEOLOGICAL / COMPARATIVE PARALLEL. Confidence: VERIFIED (artifact), PROBABLE (relevance).
- Source: The Hadad-yithʿi statue from Tell Fekheriye (Syria), ca. 9th c. BCE, Akkadian–Aramaic bilingual, **KAI 309**; the Aramaic uses *ṣlm* and *dmwt* — the same word-pair as Gen 1:26. Peer-reviewed: **W. Randall Garr, "'Image' and 'Likeness' in the Inscription from Tell Fakhariyeh," *Israel Exploration Journal* 50.3/4 (2000): 227–234** [PEER-REVIEWED].
- Content: A near-life-size basalt royal statue (2 m) glosses itself with both "image" (*ṣlm*) and "likeness" (*dmwt*), applied to a physical statue representing a king before the god Hadad. This furnishes the closest extrabiblical attestation of the exact Genesis word-pair and supports reading the human as a royal/representational "image" of God.
- Not covered: existing content treats raqia/firmament cosmology but neither the tselem/demut terminology nor this inscription.

**G-2. Memphite Theology (Shabaka Stone) — creation by the spoken word (Gen 1:3ff).**
- Section: B (Egyptian parallels). Claim-type: COMPARATIVE PARALLEL (NOT dependence). Confidence: DOCUMENTED.
- Source: The "Memphite Theology" preserved on the Shabaka Stone (British Museum EA 498), promulgated under Pharaoh Shabaka ~710 BCE (composition possibly older). Cited in **Christopher B. Hays, *Hidden Riches: A Sourcebook for the Comparative Study of the Hebrew Bible and Ancient Near East* (Westminster John Knox, 2014), p. 66** [ACADEMIC POPULAR].
- Content: Ptah creates the gods and world by the thought of the "heart" and the command of the "tongue" — creation by divine word, paralleling Genesis 1's recurring "and God said." Hays notes the parallel but flags a key contrast: no Sabbath-rest motif appears in any ANE creation text.
- Flag: Present as a parallel *concept* of "creation by word," not as a source of Genesis. The Memphite/Logos link is real but routinely overstated in popular sources.

**G-3. Atrahasis — the purpose of humanity, contrasted (Gen 1:26–28; 2:7).**
- Section: B (Mesopotamian parallels). Claim-type: COMPARATIVE PARALLEL. Confidence: DOCUMENTED.
- Source: *Atrahasis Epic* (Old Babylonian, 18th c. BCE); standard critical edition **W. G. Lambert & A. R. Millard, *Atra-ḫasīs: The Babylonian Story of the Flood* (Oxford, 1969)** [PEER-REVIEWED/PRIMARY].
- Content: In Atrahasis, humanity is fashioned from clay mixed with the blood of a slain god expressly to relieve the lesser gods (the Igigi) of irrigation labor; Genesis inverts the motif — humans bear the divine image and receive a dominion mandate rather than serving as the gods' workforce. This is distinct from the already-covered Atrahasis *flood* parallel; the novel point here is the *anthropology* (the purpose of humankind).
- Flag: Explicitly distinguish the flood parallel (already covered) from this anthropological contrast (new).

**G-4. Sumerian King List — antediluvian longevity (Gen 5).**
- Section: B (Mesopotamian parallels). Claim-type: COMPARATIVE PARALLEL. Confidence: DOCUMENTED.
- Source: Sumerian King List, esp. the **Weld-Blundell Prism (WB 444, Ashmolean)** and the Larsa tablet (WB 62); peer-reviewed treatment in **Gerhard F. Hasel, "The Genealogies of Gen 5 and 11 and Their Alleged Babylonian Background," *Andrews University Seminary Studies*** (engaging W. G. Lambert) [PEER-REVIEWED]; primary text T. Jacobsen, *The Sumerian King List* (Oriental Institute, 1939).
- Content: The List records (in WB 444) eight pre-Flood kings with fantastically long reigns measured in *sars* (3,600) and a Flood that "swept over," after which reigns shorten — structurally analogous to Genesis 5's ten long-lived antediluvian patriarchs followed by post-Flood decline. Hasel stresses the *differences* (8 kings vs. 10 patriarchs; tens of thousands of years vs. centuries) against older "borrowing" claims.
- Flag: Avoid the Answers-in-Genesis "scribal sexagesimal-error" hypothesis as fringe; present the structural parallel plus scholarly caution about dependence.

**G-5. Etemenanki ziggurat — primary inscriptions (Gen 11:1–9).**
- Section: C (Archaeological) + B (Mesopotamian). Claim-type: HISTORICAL-ARCHAEOLOGICAL / COMPARATIVE PARALLEL. Confidence: VERIFIED (structure), PROBABLE (Babel association).
- Source: Nabopolassar foundation cylinders and Nebuchadnezzar II inscriptions on the restoration of Etemenanki ("House of the Foundation of Heaven and Earth"); the "Tower of Babel Stele" (Schøyen Collection MS 2063); excavations by Robert Koldewey (from 1913). **Andrew R. George, "The Tower of Babel: Archaeology, History and Cuneiform Texts," *Archiv für Orientforschung* 51 (2005/6): 75–95** [PEER-REVIEWED].
- Content: Nabopolassar describes restoring the ziggurat with "baked bricks" and "bitumen" — the very materials of Gen 11:3 ("brick for stone, bitumen for mortar"). Nebuchadnezzar boasts of raising its top "to heaven" and of mobilizing peoples from many lands. George dates the original construction to ca. 1500–1000 BCE.
- Not covered: existing content does not cite Etemenanki or these inscriptions.

**G-6. Book of the Watchers (1 Enoch 6–16) & related Qumran texts — Gen 6:1–4.**
- Section: B (Second Temple) + F (Later reception). Claim-type: COMPARATIVE PARALLEL / LATER RECEPTION. Confidence: VERIFIED (texts exist), DOCUMENTED (interpretation).
- Source: 1 Enoch 6–16 (Book of the Watchers, ca. 3rd–2nd c. BCE), preserved in Aramaic among the Dead Sea Scrolls; the *Book of Giants* (4QEnGiants); the *Genesis Apocryphon* (1QapGen/1Q20). Standard critical edition: **George W. E. Nickelsburg, *1 Enoch 1* (Hermeneia, Fortress, 2001)** [PEER-REVIEWED].
- Content: The Watchers tradition expands Gen 6:1–4's "sons of God," "daughters of men," and Nephilim into a full narrative of angelic descent, illicit union, and giant offspring — the interpretive framework presupposed by Jude 6 and 2 Peter 2:4. The Genesis Apocryphon dramatizes Lamech's anxiety over Noah's paternity.
- Flag: Present as Second Temple *reception/expansion* of Gen 6, not as the meaning of Genesis itself. Goes beyond already-covered Enuma Elish / flood parallels.

**G-7. Ebla tablets — the patriarchal-name debate as a self-correction case study (Gen 11–14).**
- Section: C (Archaeological) + I (World at the time). Claim-type: HISTORICAL-ARCHAEOLOGICAL (with strong CAUTION). Confidence: UNCERTAIN.
- Source: The Tell Mardikh / Ebla archive — **more than 17,000 cuneiform tablets and fragments** (the main Palace G archive of ~15,000 found in 1975, plus ~1,600 in 1976; dating ca. 2500–2250 BCE), excavated by Paolo Matthiae from 1964; epigraphy by Giovanni Pettinato. For the cautionary consensus: Alfonso Archi's critiques and the *Ebla–biblical controversy* literature; **Alan Millard, "Ebla and the Bible"** [ACADEMIC POPULAR].
- Content: Early sensational claims (that Ebla names Sodom/Gomorrah, an "Ur in Haran," or the biblical patriarchs) are now largely rejected; the consensus is that Ebla has "no bearing on the historical accuracy of the biblical Patriarchs." The genuinely enriching point is the *methodological cautionary tale* of how name-similarities were over-read and then corrected.
- Flag: Author this as a "how scholarship self-corrects" entry; do NOT repeat the retracted Sodom/Gomorrah claims as fact.

### JOHN 1–3

**J-1. Targumic Memra — the Jewish-Aramaic background to the Logos (John 1:1).**
- Section: B (Second Temple/rabbinic) + F (reception). Claim-type: COMPARATIVE PARALLEL. Confidence: PROBABLE (debated).
- Source: Targum Neofiti, Targum Onqelos, Targum Pseudo-Jonathan substituting *Memra* ("Word") for God as agent of creation/revelation (e.g., Neofiti Gen 1:1 "the Memra of the Lord created…"; Pseudo-Jonathan Gen 3:8). Scholarship: **Martin McNamara, *Targum and Testament Revisited* (Eerdmans, 2010)**; **Daniel Boyarin, "The Gospel of the Memra: Jewish Binitarianism and the Prologue to John," *Harvard Theological Review* 94.3 (2001): 243–284** [PEER-REVIEWED]. Counter-view: **C. K. Barrett, *The Gospel According to St John*** (who calls Memra "a blind alley").
- Content: The Targums offer a Jewish-Aramaic precedent for speaking of God's "Word" as a quasi-personal mediator — a candidate background distinct from the already-covered Greek/philosophical Logos. Boyarin argues Memra is a genuine divine mediator concept; Barrett argues it is only a reverential circumlocution for the divine Name.
- Flag: Present both Boyarin (strong reading) and Barrett (skeptical) — genuinely contested.

**J-2. eskēnōsen — "tabernacled / pitched his tent" (John 1:14).**
- Section: D (Philology). Claim-type: TEXTUAL / STRONG INFERENCE. Confidence: VERIFIED (lexical), PROBABLE (allusion).
- Source: The verb σκηνόω (*eskēnōsen*, "dwelt") shares its root with σκηνή (*skēnē*), the LXX word for the tabernacle (Hebrew *miškān*, Exod 25:8–9), with an aural resonance to *Shekhinah*; cf. Wisdom "encamping" (Sir 24:8–10). Standard: **BDAG s.v. σκηνόω**; **Raymond E. Brown, *The Gospel According to John I–XII* (Anchor Bible 29, Doubleday, 1966)**, ad loc. [PEER-REVIEWED].
- Content: John says the Word "tented" among us — evoking the wilderness tabernacle where God's glory dwelt, so that the incarnate Word becomes the new locus of divine presence/glory (*doxa*). The verb is unique to John among the gospels.
- Not covered: existing content covers Logos, *anothen*, and Colwell's rule — not *eskēnōsen*.

**J-3. "Bethany beyond the Jordan" — al-Maghtas / Wadi al-Kharrar (John 1:28).**
- Section: C (Archaeological). Claim-type: HISTORICAL-ARCHAEOLOGICAL. Confidence: PROBABLE (identification debated).
- Source: Excavations at Wadi al-Kharrar / Tell al-Kharrar (Jordan) since 1996 under Mohammad Waheeb; UNESCO World Heritage inscription (2015) as "Baptism Site 'Bethany beyond the Jordan' (Al-Maghtas)." **Mohammad Waheeb, "Archaeological Excavations at the Baptism Site 'Bethany Beyond the Jordan,'" *Bible and Spade* 14.2 (2001)** [ACADEMIC POPULAR].
- Content: Roman/Byzantine churches, chapels, hermit caves, and spring-fed baptismal pools have been excavated on the east bank; the spring-fed wadi (not the muddy main river) offered a stable immersion site. Textual point worth pairing: Origen emended "Bethany" to "Bethabara" because he could not locate an eastern Bethany, but the oldest manuscripts read "Bethany."
- Flag: A west-bank tradition (Qasr al-Yahud) competes; present as probable, not certain.

**J-4. "Forty-six years" and the naos/hieron distinction (John 2:20).**
- Section: C (Historical) + D (Philology). Claim-type: HISTORICAL / TEXTUAL. Confidence: PROBABLE.
- Source: **Josephus, *Antiquities* 15.380, 15.421** (Herod began the temple in his 18th regnal year, ~20–19 BCE); **15.354** and ***War* 1.401**; **20.219** (the complex still unfinished under Albinus, 62–64 CE). The Greek distinguishes ναός (*naos*, sanctuary) in 2:20 from ἱερόν (*hieron*, the whole precinct) [PRIMARY].
- Content: The "forty-six years" places the episode ~27–28 CE, dovetailing with Luke 3:1. Scholars debate whether the dative ("built in/over 46 years") implies completed or ongoing construction (**C. K. Barrett** vs. **Andreas Köstenberger**).
- Flag: In scope (John 2 is within chapters 1–3). The Pool of Bethesda (John 5) is OUT of scope and excluded.

### MATTHEW 1–3

**M-1. The four women of the genealogy (Matt 1:3–6).**
- Section: A (Source-text feature) + F (reception). Claim-type: TEXTUAL + STRONG INFERENCE (interpretation). Confidence: VERIFIED (text); the theories PROBABLE/POSSIBLE.
- Source: **Raymond E. Brown, *The Birth of the Messiah* (Anchor Bible Reference Library, Doubleday, 1977; rev. 1993), pp. 71–74** [PEER-REVIEWED standard critical monograph]. Counterpoints: **Jane Schaberg, *The Illegitimacy of Jesus* (Harper & Row, 1987), pp. 20–22**; **Amy-Jill Levine, "Matthew," in *Women's Bible Commentary*, 3rd ed. (Westminster John Knox, 2012), pp. 465–477**.
- Content: An otherwise strictly patrilineal genealogy names four Hebrew Bible women (Tamar, Rahab, Ruth, "the wife of Uriah") while omitting the matriarchs (Sarah, Rebekah, Leah, Rachel). Brown surveys three theories: (a) "sinners" (Jerome); (b) "Gentiles/foreigners" (Luther); (c) his own preferred reading — each involves an "extraordinary or irregular union" plus female "initiative" that becomes the instrument of God's providence, foreshadowing Mary (the fifth woman, 1:16). Bathsheba is named obliquely as "the wife of Uriah [the Hittite]," keeping both the foreignness link and David's scandal in view; Schaberg and Levine read the women instead as vulnerable figures and question the Gentile-inclusion theory (Tamar and Bathsheba are not explicitly non-Israelite in the Hebrew Bible).
- Flag: Present the competing theories descriptively; do not adjudicate. Goes beyond the already-covered "genealogy structure."

**M-2. Balaam's star oracle (Num 24:17) at Qumran and Bar Kokhba — background to the magi (Matt 2:1–12).**
- Section: B (Second Temple) + F (reception). Claim-type: COMPARATIVE PARALLEL / LATER RECEPTION. Confidence: PROBABLE.
- Source: Num 24:17 ("a star shall come out of Jacob, a scepter out of Israel") read messianically at Qumran (**CD-A VII 18–20; 1QM XI; 4Q175 Testimonia**) and applied by Rabbi Akiba to Simon "Bar Kokhba" (son of the star), **y. Taʿanit 68d**. Scholarship: **George van Kooten & Peter Barthel (eds.), *The Star of Bethlehem and the Magi* (Brill, 2015)**, incl. the essay on Balaam's star oracle in the DSS (pp. 399–429) [PEER-REVIEWED].
- Content: The messianic-star interpretation of Numbers 24:17 was current in Second Temple Judaism; Matthew's star/magi narrative plausibly engages this tradition. Philo (*De praemiis* 95) and the LXX's messianic rendering corroborate its currency.
- Not covered: existing content treats "the star" generally but not the Num 24:17 / Qumran / Bar Kokhba reception chain.

**M-3. Deir 'Alla plaster inscription — "Balaam son of Beor" (extrabiblical attestation; Matt 2 reception).**
- Section: C (Archaeological) + B (ANE). Claim-type: HISTORICAL-ARCHAEOLOGICAL (the Matthew link is COMPARATIVE). Confidence: VERIFIED (artifact).
- Source: Tell Deir 'Alla plaster text (Jordan), ink-on-plaster, ca. 880–770 BCE (building destroyed ~800 BCE by earthquake), discovered 1967 by Henk J. Franken. *Editio princeps*: **J. Hoftijzer & G. van der Kooij, *Aramaic Texts from Deir 'Alla* (Brill, 1976)**; critical study **Jo Ann Hackett, *The Balaam Text from Deir 'Allā* (Harvard Semitic Monographs 31, Scholars Press, 1984)**; **Baruch A. Levine, "The Deir 'Alla Plaster Inscriptions," *Journal of the American Oriental Society* 101.2 (1981): 195–205**; **KAI 312** [all PEER-REVIEWED/PRIMARY].
- Content: The text opens naming "Balaam son of Beor, a seer of the gods" (*blʿm br bʿr*) — the only extrabiblical attestation of a named Hebrew Bible figure, confirming a Transjordanian Balaam-seer tradition. The dialect (Aramaic vs. Canaanite/"Gileadite") is debated; the divine world is polytheistic (the *šaddayin*/*ʾelohin*).
- Flag: CRITICAL — the inscription does NOT contain the "star" oracle; it attests the *existence of a Balaam-son-of-Beor seer tradition*, not Numbers 24:17. State this distinction explicitly. Anti-ethnogenesis: do not link to any modern group.

**M-4. Josephus on John the Baptist (Matt 3:1–6 context).**
- Section: C (Historical, non-biblical attestation) + I (World at the time). Claim-type: HISTORICAL. Confidence: VERIFIED.
- Source: **Josephus, *Antiquities* 18.116–119** [PRIMARY], with standard discussion in **John P. Meier, *A Marginal Jew*, vol. 2 (Doubleday, 1994)** [PEER-REVIEWED].
- Content: Josephus independently attests John as a historical mass-baptizing preacher of righteousness executed by Herod Antipas (whom Josephus locates at the fortress Machaerus) — an external, non-Christian witness to the figure of Matthew 3.
- Flag: Note that Josephus gives a *political* rationale for the execution (fear of unrest), differing from the gospels' account; present descriptively.

### MARK 1–3

**K-1. The Galilee Boat ("Kinneret Boat," 1986) — the fishing economy (Mark 1:16–20).**
- Section: C (Archaeological) + I (World at the time). Claim-type: HISTORICAL-ARCHAEOLOGICAL. Confidence: VERIFIED (boat); the "Jesus" link is explicitly NOT claimed.
- Source: The Ancient Galilee Boat, discovered 1986 near Kibbutz Ginosar. **Shelley Wachsmann, "The Galilee Boat—2,000-Year-Old Hull Recovered Intact," *Biblical Archaeology Review* 14.5 (1988): 18–33**; Wachsmann (ed.), *The Excavations of an Ancient Boat in the Sea of Galilee (Lake Kinneret)*, ʿAtiqot 19 (1990) [PEER-REVIEWED].
- Content: A vessel with preserved dimensions of **8.2 m long × 2.3 m wide × 1.25 m deep** (27 × 7.5 × 4.1 ft), built of cedar planks on oak frames with pegged mortise-and-tenon joints, capable of carrying ~15 people. Radiocarbon dated the hull to **"40 B.C., plus or minus 80 years"** (i.e., 120 BCE–40 CE), with pottery (a cooking pot and lamp) and nails yielding a 50 BCE–50 CE range. Constructed of **at least 12 wood types** with evidence of repeated repairs and reused timber — illustrating a modest, resource-constrained fishing economy of exactly the kind behind the Zebedee/Simon enterprises (Mark 1:19–20, "with the hired men").
- Flag: State plainly there is NO evidence linking the boat to Jesus; it is type-representative only.

**K-2. Qur'anic reception of Jesus and Mary — Surahs Maryam (19) and Āl ʿImrān (3).**
- Section: F (Islamic reception). Claim-type: LATER RECEPTION. Confidence: DOCUMENTED.
- Source: **Qur'an 19:16–34 (Maryam)** and **3:33–59 (Āl ʿImrān)**; 3:45 names Jesus "a Word from Him" (*kalima*). Scholarship: **Gabriel Said Reynolds, *The Qur'an and the Bible* (Yale, 2018)** [PEER-REVIEWED]; **Seyyed Hossein Nasr et al. (eds.), *The Study Quran* (HarperOne, 2015)**.
- Content: The Qur'an independently affirms the virginal conception (3:47; 19:20) and calls Jesus "a Word/*kalima* from God" — a striking point of contact with both Matthew's virginal conception and John's Logos, documented as 7th-century Islamic reception. Modern scholarship treats the account as engaging earlier Christian apocrypha (e.g., the Protoevangelium of James, Gospel of Pseudo-Matthew).
- Flag: Present as documented Islamic tradition/reception, never as endorsement; describe the Qur'an's explicit rejection of Jesus' divinity (5:73, 116) neutrally. (Maps most naturally to Matthew 1–2, secondarily to John's Logos; can be cross-listed.)

**K-3. Capernaum / Sepphoris urbanization — calibrating "the world at the time" (Mark 1:21 context).**
- Section: I (World at the time). Claim-type: HISTORICAL-ARCHAEOLOGICAL (debated). Confidence: PROBABLE.
- Source: **Nathan Schumer, "The Population of Sepphoris: Rethinking Urbanization in Early and Middle Roman Galilee," *Journal of Ancient Judaism* 8.1 (2017): 90ff** [PEER-REVIEWED]; cross-reference **Mark A. Chancey, *The Myth of a Gentile Galilee* (Cambridge, 2002)** [PEER-REVIEWED].
- Content: Schumer argues early Roman Sepphoris "ranged from 2,000–4,300 people (about half the size of previous estimates)" and that "urbanization in early Roman Galilee was fairly moderate and not fundamentally transformative for the region," reaching only ~8,000–8,500 after 70 CE. This corrects the popular "Jesus shaped by cosmopolitan Sepphoris" narrative: a smaller, more modestly Hellenized Galilee, Aramaic-dominant, with Greek mainly an administrative-elite language (Chancey) — a more accurate "world at the time" frame for Mark's Galilean opening.
- Flag: Anti-ethnogenesis compliant; present the urbanization question as actively debated.

**K-4. The Gerasene demoniac and Legio X Fretensis — Mark 5 (OUT OF SCOPE).**
- Section: B/C (Greco-Roman) + I. Verse: Mark 5:1–20. Claim-type: COMPARATIVE PARALLEL / SPECULATION. Confidence: POSSIBLE/UNCERTAIN.
- Source: **Christopher B. Zeichman, *The Roman Army and the New Testament* (Lexington/Fortress, 2018)**; popular anti-imperial reading in **Ched Myers, *Binding the Strong Man* (Orbis, 1988)**.
- Content: The demons named "Legion," drowned with the swine, have been read as anti-Roman satire keyed to Legio X Fretensis (boar emblem). Zeichman shows the legion was NOT stationed at Gerasa in Jesus' day (it arrived after 66 CE) and the Decapolis was largely pro-Roman — so the link is weaker than popular treatments claim.
- Flag: OUT OF SCOPE (Mark 5, not 1–3). Retained here only for future reference; if ever used, present as contested/speculative, not established.

## Recommendations
**Stage 1 — author the five VERIFIED anchors first** (Tell Fekheriye, Etemenanki, Deir 'Alla, Galilee Boat, Brown's four women): lowest reliability risk, highest enrichment value, each grounded in a primary edition or standard monograph.

**Stage 2 — author the high-value comparative/reception entries** (Memphite Theology, Atrahasis anthropology, 1 Enoch Watchers, Targumic Memra, eskēnōsen, Num 24:17 reception, Josephus on the Baptist, Qur'anic reception), each with an explicit claim-type label and a one-line "parallel ≠ dependence / reception ≠ endorsement" caveat in the rendered text.

**Stage 3 — author the calibration/cautionary entries** (Ebla self-correction, Sepphoris urbanization, "Bethany beyond the Jordan," forty-six-years naos/hieron) where the enrichment is partly *methodological* — showing how evidence is weighed.

**Verification before publication:** confirm the exact wording of Brown's three theories against print pp. 71–74; confirm the Garr *IEJ* (50.3/4: 227–234), Levine *JAOS* (101.2: 195–205), Boyarin *HTR* (94.3: 243–284), and Schumer *JAJ* (8.1: 90ff) page ranges against the journals; confirm Josephus section numbers against a critical edition (Loeb/Niese).

**Thresholds that change priorities:** (1) If a candidate cannot be traced to a named credentialed scholar or a primary critical edition, demote it to "needs verification" and do not author it. (2) If the authored chapter range expands to include John 5 or Mark 5, promote the Bethesda pool and Gerasene/Legion entries (the latter only with the Zeichman caveat). (3) If a comparative parallel is found to rest only on aggregator/apologetic sources after checking, drop the dependence framing entirely and retain only the verifiable artifact/text.

## Caveats
- **Source laundering risk:** several findings first surfaced via non-academic aggregator pages (Wikipedia, ministry/apologetics blogs, devotional sites). The named primary/peer-reviewed sources above are the citation of record; do not cite the aggregators.
- **Comparative-parallel discipline:** the Memphite Theology, Atrahasis, Sumerian King List, Memra, and Legion items must each be framed as parallels/analogies, never as demonstrated literary dependence. Popular sources consistently blur this line.
- **Reception is documented, not endorsed:** the Qur'anic, Targumic, and 1 Enoch items are post-biblical or parabiblical traditions to be presented descriptively; never treat them as evidence about the events themselves or adjudicate their theology.
- **Anti-ethnogenesis:** no candidate links an ancient people to a modern ethnic, national, or political entity; this is flagged explicitly for the Deir 'Alla, Ebla, and Galilee/Sepphoris entries.
- **Scope discipline:** John 5 (Bethesda) and Mark 5 (Gerasene/Legion) lie outside the currently authored ranges and are excluded from authoring; Matthew 3 / Mark 1 John-the-Baptist material should foreground the *specific* Josephus passage (Ant. 18.116–119) to remain novel against existing coverage.
- **Speculative tier:** the Legion/Legio X Fretensis reading and any "scientific" treatment of antediluvian longevity should be labeled SPECULATIVE/UNCERTAIN; no fringe-science framing of Genesis lifespans should be presented as mainstream.
