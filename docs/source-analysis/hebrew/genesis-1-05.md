# Genesis 1:5 — source-analysis notes

**Source:** וַיִּקְרָא אֱלֹהִים לָאוֹר יוֹם וְלַחֹשֶׁךְ קָרָא לָיְלָה וַיְהִי־עֶרֶב וַיְהִי־בֹקֶר יוֹם אֶחָד
**Method:** `../METHOD.md`. Internal working notes — see `../README.md`.

**Macro:** All converting-vav verbs so far (*vayomer, vayar, vayavdel, vayikra*) are **3ms singular** — the *yod* prefix + form encode "he," singular. English can't show this; it must add a pronoun. The verse closes the first day with a cardinal, not an ordinal (see *yom echad*).

### vayikra — וַיִּקְרָא
- **Morphology:** root *q-r-ʾ*; converting-vav → "and he called/named," 3ms past. (Same form = the Hebrew title of Leviticus.) Standard past *qara* also appears in this very verse — compare *vayikra* (with converter) vs. *qara* (plain).
- **Range:** "call / name" (here "named"); also "read," "summon" elsewhere.
- **TT status:** converting-vav; "and God called."

### la-or yom — לָאוֹר יוֹם
- **Morphology:** *la-* ("to-the," preposition+article fused) + *or* + *yom* "day."
- **Smoothed:** "called the light Day" (drop the "to," which English doesn't need).

### ve-la-choshech kara laylah — וְלַחֹשֶׁךְ קָרָא לָיְלָה
- *ve-* "and" + *la-* "to-the" + *choshech*; *kara* (plain past, no converter) "called"; *laylah* "night."
- **Smoothed:** "and the darkness he called Night."

### vayhi-erev vayhi-voker — וַיְהִי־עֶרֶב וַיְהִי־בֹקֶר
- **Morphology:** *vayhi* "and was" (converting-vav, 3**ms** — *erev* "evening" and *boker* "morning" are masculine).
- **Literal:** "and was evening and was morning." Recurs at each day's close.

### yom echad — יוֹם אֶחָד
- **Morphology:** *yom* "day" + *echad* "one" (**cardinal**, masculine to agree with *yom*). Adjective follows noun.
- **Range:** "one day / day one" — a **single unit**, NOT "first day." "First" is *rishon*, an unrelated word (cf. Exod 12). The remaining creation days use **ordinals** (second, third…); Day One is the deliberate outlier.
- **Traditional rendering:** "the first day" — imports an ordinal Hebrew does not use.
- **TT status:** Rule 18 (cardinal vs. ordinal); "one day."
