# Hebrew word studies — source-analysis notes

Standalone lexeme studies (not tied to one Genesis verse), distilled for future authoring across books. **Method:** `../METHOD.md`. Internal working notes — see `../README.md`.

### ki — כִּי
- **Morphology:** particle (*kaf* + *yod*).
- **Range (wide):** "because" (its **main** sense, e.g. Gen 2:3) · "that" (complementizer, e.g. Gen 1:4 *ki tov*) · "when" (e.g. Deut 17:14) · emphatic/asseverative (e.g. Gen 18:15, "indeed/no you did laugh" — also serves to separate clauses where unpointed text could otherwise be misread) · "although / even though" (e.g. Josh 17:18, where *ki* occurs 5× with several senses left to interpretation).
- **Note:** much like EN "set/that," sense is context-determined. Flag the active sense per occurrence.

### shalach — שָׁלַח (e.g. "let my people go," Exod)
- **Morphology:** verb, imperative in *shalach et ami*.
- **Range:** "send" is the **dominant** biblical sense (thousands of cases: send spies, Num 13:16; send gifts, Gen 45:23) — active dispatch. But it can also mean "let go / release" (Job 39:5 of the wild donkey; Gen 8:7–10 Noah *sending* the dove out). 
- **Two defensible renderings:** "let my people go" (passive non-obstruction) vs. "send my people away" (active dispatch — and Exod 12:33 shows the Egyptians actively doing exactly this). The text supports both; present, don't resolve.
- **TT relevance:** model case for ambiguity preservation (Rule 2) when reached.

### shechorah — שְׁחוֹרָה (Song of Songs 1:5, "I am black")
- **Morphology:** adjective, **feminine singular** (terminal *hey*). Hebrew adjectives are gendered, so the speaker is unambiguously **female** — information English "black" (genderless) cannot carry. The verse does not describe a male (e.g. Solomon).
- **vav as "and," not "but":** *shechorah ani ve-navah* — the *vav* prefix = "and." Most English versions render "black **but** comely"; the conjunction more naturally = "and" (the two qualities coexist, cf. contrastive vs. coordinating *vav*). "I am black **and** beautiful." (*navah* = "beautiful/fair," inferable from elsewhere in the Song.)
- **TT relevance:** gender via morphology (Rule 27); conjunction not over-translated (Rule 11/idiom); a translation-history caution (the "but" likely reflects translators' era, not the Hebrew).

### et — אֵת
- **Morphology:** definite-direct-object marker; first + last alphabet letters (alef–tav). ~6,000× — the most frequent word in the Tanakh.
- **Function:** grammatical only; precedes a definite direct object; **adds no meaning** and can be dropped (cf. EN indefinite article "a," which many languages lack). Sometimes also = "with" (Ps; rare), and forms object-pronouns when conjugated (e.g. *oto* "him"). Less frequent in elevated/poetic text.
- **Rule 3 caution:** the alef–tav = alpha-and-omega linkage (Revelation) is a **valid religious interpretation but not a plain-reading fact**; the TT does not import it. (Greek has extra letters, incl. omega, with no Hebrew counterpart.)
- **TT status:** untranslated; transliteration-decisions log.

### ve-ahavta le-re'acha kamocha — וְאָהַבְתָּ לְרֵעֲךָ כָּמוֹךָ (Lev 19:18)
- **ve-ahavta** — root *ʾ-h-b* "love" + suffix *-ta* (2ms past) + **converting-vav** → "and you shall love" (future). Note: *vav* = "and," **not** "but" (as some versions render); context agrees (the clause extends, not contradicts).
- **le-re'acha** — unusual *le-* ("to") prefix on the object (the expected object marker would be *et*, as in Deut 6:5); this *le-* + "love" combination occurs only here → literally "love **to** your fellow."
- **re'a** — "fellow / companion / friend" (Ps 122:8; 1 Chr 27:33 of David's friend; Job's friends), broader than modern EN "neighbor." Many (esp. Jewish) translations use "fellow."
- **kamocha** — "as yourself / as if he were you" (*kamo* + suffix).
- **Smoothed:** "and you shall love your fellow as if he were you."
- **TT relevance:** converting-vav; *re'a* lexical scope; *le-* idiom.

### Yitzchak — יִצְחָק (the name "Isaac")
- **Morphology:** a **verb** — *yi-* (future/3ms prefix) + root **tz-ch-q** → "he will laugh," future tense.
- **Root range:** *tz-ch-q* spans "laugh" (Gen 17:17 Abraham; 18:15 Sarah, with converting-vav forms *vayitzchak* / *titzchak*) but often carries **negative** color — "mock / ridicule" (Gen 21:9 *metzachek*); the variant root with *sin* (Yishak, e.g. Ps 2:4) clearly = "ridicule"; also "rejoice" (Ps 126:2); and intimacy (Gen 26:8).
- **Naming logic:** Gen 21:6 (Sarah: people who hear "will laugh/giggle" at her old-age birth) ties the future-tense name to ongoing reaction — "he will laugh / be laughed at." The future tense and the mockery undertone are both invisible in "Isaac."
- **TT relevance:** name-as-meaning (note at first occurrence); future tense + converting-vav; ambiguity (laugh/mock/rejoice).
