# Genesis 1:1 — source-analysis notes

**Source:** בְּרֵאשִׁית בָּרָא אֱלֹהִים אֵת הַשָּׁמַיִם וְאֵת הָאָרֶץ
**Method:** `../METHOD.md`. Internal working notes — see `../README.md`.

**Macro:** Seven words in Hebrew (most translations have more). Seven is a structuring motif across the chapter (seven days; "good" 7×). The clause is verb–subject–object (*bara Elohim*…) — inversion typical of biblical narrative, rare in modern Hebrew. This line is the chapter header and introduces the seven-count.

### bereshit — בְּרֵאשִׁית
- **Morphology:** prefix *be-* ("in / in-the") + *reshit*, from root **r-ʾ-sh** ("head"). One token = EN "in (the) beginning."
- **Lexical family:** *rosh* "head" → "prime/chief" (*rosh ha-memshalah* = head of government); *rishon* "first"; *reshit* "beginning."
- **Range:** "in beginning" with a figurative/elevated tone ("at the prime/outset"). The ordinary modern word for "in the beginning" is *bat'chilah* — different root; *bereshit* is formal/lyrical.
- **Traditional rendering:** "In the beginning" adds "the"; Hebrew has no article on *reshit* here.
- **Literal:** "In-beginning." **Smoothed:** "In beginning" (no article) per TT.
- **TT status:** encoded — Gen 1:1 no-article decision (editorial log).

### bara — בָּרָא
- **Morphology:** qatal, 3ms; precedes its subject (VSO).
- **Range:** "created," but specifically divine creation-from-nothing; distinct from *asah* "made/formed." Effectively reserved for that primal act; rare in everyday Hebrew.
- **Traditional rendering:** "created" — adequate, but loses the *bara*/*asah* distinction English collapses.
- **Confidence:** the from-nothing scope is **Possible**, not Verified — debated; label accordingly.
- **TT status:** glossary (*bara* = created, distinct from *asah*); editorial log.

### Elohim — אֱלֹהִים
- **Morphology:** grammatically plural (the *-im* suffix), but the governing verb *bara* is **3ms singular** → plural of majesty/respect, single referent. Do not read numeric plurality.
- **Traditional rendering:** "God." Note: the same lexeme renders "gods" elsewhere (e.g. the Decalogue's "other gods").
- **TT status:** glossary (*Elohim* = God); Rule 20 (no theological capitalization beyond proper-noun).

### et — אֵת (×2)
- **Morphology:** definite-direct-object marker; first + last letters of the alphabet (alef–tav).
- **Range:** no semantic content; no equivalent in English; droppable without changing meaning. Do **not** read alpha-and-omega symbolism into it (that is a later interpretive overlay — Rule 3; see `hebrew-word-studies.md` §et).
- **TT status:** untranslated; transliteration-decisions log (functionless marker).

### ha-shamayim — הַשָּׁמַיִם
- **Morphology:** *ha-* "the" + *shamayim*, a **plural-only** noun (cf. *mayim*).
- **Range:** "sky/skies" (tangible), not "heaven" as a spiritual realm — *shamayim* keeps the physical sense that "heaven" has largely lost.
- **TT status:** glossary (*shamayim* = sky/skies, not Heaven); Rule 3 overlay table.

### ve-et ha-aretz — וְאֵת הָאָרֶץ
- **Morphology:** *ve-* "and" + *et* + *ha-* "the" + *aretz*.
- **Range:** "the land / the ground," not "Earth" as a planet (cf. *kadur ha-aretz* = the globe, where *kadur* "ball" supplies "planet"). *aretz* also = "ground" (Lev 11) and "land/territory" (Gen 13:15). Soil specifically is *adamah* — a different word, important later in the chapter.
- **TT status:** glossary (*eretz* = land, not planet Earth; *adamah* = soil distinct).
