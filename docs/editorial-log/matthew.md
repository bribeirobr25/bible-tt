## Entry M-001: Fulfillment Formula Policy

- **Verse:** Matthew 1:22, 2:5-6, 2:15, 2:17-18, 2:23, 3:3 (book-wide policy)
- **Language(s) affected:** EN, PT, DE, ES
- **Rule(s) invoked:** CORE Rule 1 (Source-Text Fidelity), CORE Rule 14 (Cross-References), GS §Divine Name Policy
- **Decision:** Matthew's fulfillment/quotation formulas are classified into 6 types. Each type receives consistent treatment:

| Type | Formula Pattern | Example | Treatment |
|------|----------------|---------|-----------|
| **Direct** | ἵνα πληρωθῇ ("so that it might be fulfilled") | Matt 1:22 (Isa 7:14) | Translate formula; note OT source; track modifications |
| **Modified** | Same formula but Matthew modifies the quoted text | Matt 2:6 (Mic 5:1 reversed + 2 Sam 5:2 blended) | Translate; note modifications with confidence labels per layer |
| **Composite** | Multiple OT sources blended | Matt 2:6 (Mic 5:1 + 2 Sam 5:2) | Identify each source; label: certain (modified wording), probable (2 Sam echo), possible (interpretive adjustment) |
| **Typological** | Historical event reapplied | Matt 2:15 (Hos 11:1) | Translate; note Hosea refers to Israel's exodus; Matthew reapplies to Yeshua |
| **Temporal-resultive** | τότε ἐπληρώθη ("then was fulfilled") | Matt 2:17 (Jer 31:15) | Translate distinct formula; note structural difference from purpose-style formulas |
| **Unresolved** | No known single OT source | Matt 2:23 ("Natsri") | Translate; note each proposed source with confidence labels; do not resolve |

- **Alternatives considered:** Treating all formulas uniformly (rejected: the formulas themselves differ syntactically and hermeneutically); resolving the "Natsri" source (rejected: Prime Directive — the source is genuinely unidentified).
- **Justification:** Matthew's fulfillment formulas are a signature literary device. Uniform treatment would obscure the variety in Matthew's own hermeneutic. Each type requires its own editorial-log entry when new examples appear.
- **Status:** provisional
- **Reviewers:** unassigned

## Entry M-002: parthenos/almah — Matthew 1:23

- **Verse:** Matthew 1:23
- **Language(s) affected:** EN, PT, DE, ES
- **Rule(s) invoked:** CORE Rule 2 (Ambiguity Preservation), CORE Rule 22 (Text-Critical Restraint), GS §Locked Glossary, GS §Divine Name Policy (by analogy)
- **Decision:** Render Tier 1 with slash: EN "virgin/young woman" / PT "virgem/jovem mulher" / DE "Jungfrau/junge Frau" / ES "virgen/joven mujer." Tier 2 note explains the Greek-Hebrew layering.
- **This is a one-verse exception.** It does NOT create a general precedent that Hebrew source-layer alternatives may enter GS Tier 1 text. Every similar case requires separate editorial-log approval.

**Why this case crosses the threshold:**
Matthew 1:23 quotes Isaiah 7:14 through the LXX tradition. The Greek text has παρθένος (*parthenos*) unambiguously. The Hebrew source (Isaiah 7:14) has עַלְמָה (*almah*), which has a broader semantic range. Matthew's quotation is the single most reception-sensitive cross-source verse in the GS, and the Hebrew-Greek tension at this exact point is one of the oldest translation debates in biblical scholarship.

**Why Option A was not chosen:**
The existing Matthew introductions (all 4 locales) adopted Option A on principle: translate the Greek as-is ("virgin"), document the Hebrew layer in a Tier 2 note. This is the cleaner GS principle and was articulated in Section F4 of the German Matthew INTRODUCTION: "Die TT übersetzt *parthenos*, wie es im griechischen Text des Matthäus steht."

Option B (slash) is adopted instead because: (a) the project's Prime Directive prioritizes making the source-text complexity visible to the reader; (b) the Isaiah 7:14 almah/parthenos translation tradition is so widely known that concealing the Hebrew layer behind a note risks the appearance of choosing sides; (c) the slash makes the dual-source-layer visible at the point of reading, which is more transparent than requiring the reader to check the note.

**Test for future cases:** A GS Tier 1 slash incorporating a non-Greek source term requires ALL of: (i) a direct OT quotation where the Greek term translates a Hebrew term with materially different semantic range; (ii) the translation difference is widely known and reception-sensitive; (iii) the slash does not distort the Greek text's own meaning; (iv) a dedicated editorial-log entry with full rationale.

- **Alternatives considered:** Option A: Tier 1 "virgin" + Tier 2 note on almah (rejected per rationale above, though acknowledged as the cleaner GS principle).
- **Status:** provisional
- **Reviewers:** unassigned
- **Cross-references:** All 4 Matthew INTRODUCTION files must be updated to reflect Option B (Section F4 rewrite).

## Entry M-003: Kingdom of the Skies

- **Verse:** Matthew 3:2 (book-wide — ~33 occurrences in Matthew)
- **Language(s) affected:** EN, PT, DE, ES
- **Rule(s) invoked:** CORE Rule 1, GS §Locked Glossary (basileia)
- **Decision:** Render βασιλεία τῶν οὐρανῶν as EN "kingdom of the skies" / PT "reino dos céus" / DE "Königreich der Himmel" / ES "reino de los cielos." Tier 2 note at first occurrence explains: (a) Matthew's preference for "skies/heavens" over "God" (cf. Mark/Luke's "kingdom of God"); (b) the plural οὐρανῶν; (c) the relationship between "kingdom of the skies" and "kingdom of God."
- **Alternatives considered:** "kingdom of heaven" (rejected: imports later cosmological abstraction); "reign of the skies" (rejected: basileia's spatial sense is active alongside the dynamic sense — slash per glossary, but here the spatial metaphor is primary).
- **Justification:** The rendering preserves the spatial/physical resonance of οὐρανοί (skies, heavens) that Matthew's usage carries. The relationship to "kingdom of God" is documented, not resolved.
- **Status:** provisional
- **Reviewers:** unassigned

## Entry M-004: angelos kyriou — Formulaic Narrative

- **Verse:** Matthew 1:20, 1:24, 2:13, 2:19
- **Language(s) affected:** EN, PT, DE, ES
- **Rule(s) invoked:** GS §Divine Name Policy (Option C), CORE Rule 14
- **Decision:** ἄγγελος κυρίου in Matthew's narrative is classified as a formulaic narrative phrase echoing LXX/HB מַלְאַךְ יהוה, NOT a direct OT quotation. It does NOT trigger Option C's YHWH note requirement.
- **Alternatives considered:** Apply Option C (treat as OT quotation — rejected: the phrase is Matthew's own narrative voice using a formulaic expression, not a direct quotation of a specific OT verse); ignore the LXX/HB echo entirely (rejected: the echo is real and should be noted).
- **Justification:** GS Divine Name Policy (Option C) is explicitly framed around "when the Greek Scriptures quote or allude to Hebrew Bible passages containing the Tetragrammaton." A formulaic narrative phrase is neither a quotation nor a specific allusion. Tier 2 note acknowledges the LXX/HB echo without triggering the full YHWH apparatus.
- **Status:** provisional
- **Reviewers:** unassigned

## Entry M-005: hypo kyriou in Fulfillment Formula

- **Verse:** Matthew 1:22, 2:15
- **Language(s) affected:** EN, PT, DE, ES
- **Rule(s) invoked:** GS §Divine Name Policy (Option C)
- **Decision:** The narrator's κυρίου in "τὸ ῥηθὲν ὑπὸ κυρίου διὰ τοῦ προφήτου" ("what was spoken by the Lord through the prophet") DOES trigger Option C. Tier 2 note identifies the OT source and states that the underlying Hebrew has YHWH as speaker.
- **Alternatives considered:** Do not apply Option C (rejected: the narrator explicitly attributes the quoted words to the Lord — the same Lord who speaks in the OT passages being quoted, where YHWH is the speaker).
- **Justification:** The fulfillment formula identifies YHWH as the speaker of the quoted prophecy. This is functionally an attribution of the quoted text to YHWH, even though the formula itself is Matthew's narrative construction.
- **Status:** provisional
- **Reviewers:** unassigned
- **Cross-references:** Distinguishes from Entry M-004 (angelos kyriou does NOT trigger; hypo kyriou DOES trigger).

## Entry M-006: pneuma hagion — Anarthrous

- **Verse:** Matthew 1:18, 1:20
- **Language(s) affected:** EN, PT, DE, ES
- **Rule(s) invoked:** GS §Greek Article System, CORE Rule 11
- **Decision:** Greek πνεῦμα ἅγιον is anarthrous at Matt 1:18 and 1:20. Target-language translations that add an article must mark the addition per Rule 11.
- **Alternatives considered:** Suppress article marking (rejected: Greek anarthrous/articular distinction is theologically debated for pneuma; Rule 11 transparency requires marking).
- **Justification:** German "vom heiligen Wind/Geist" contains an article contraction ("vom" = "von dem") not present in the Greek. This is a grammatical necessity in German but must be marked. EN/PT/ES similarly add articles that the Greek lacks.
- **Status:** provisional
- **Reviewers:** unassigned
