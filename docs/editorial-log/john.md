## Entry J-001

- **Verse:** John 1:1 (book-wide policy)
- **Language(s) affected:** EN, PT, DE, ES
- **Rule(s) invoked:** CORE Rule 4 (Transliterate Strategic Terms), GS §Locked Glossary
- **Decision:** Render λόγος (*logos*) as lowercase "word" / "palavra" / "Wort" / "palabra" in main text. Do NOT transliterate; do NOT capitalize.
- **Alternatives considered:** Transliterate as *logos* (rejected: Rule 4 threshold not met — the Greek has clear semantic content); capitalize as "Word" (rejected: imports Johannine theological tradition into typography).
- **Justification:** Rule 4 requires transliteration only when semantic content is untranslatable. λόγος has clear meaning ("word/reason/account"). Capitalization would signal the Johannine referent before the reader encounters it — a Rule 21 violation (smuggling commentary into main text). Tier 2 note at John 1:1 explains full semantic range.
- **Status:** provisional
- **Reviewers:** unassigned
- **Cross-references:** GS §Locked Glossary (logos entry)

## Entry J-002

- **Verse:** John 1:1c
- **Language(s) affected:** EN, PT, DE, ES
- **Rule(s) invoked:** GS §Greek Article System, CORE Rule 1 (Source-Text Fidelity)
- **Decision:** Translate the predicate nominative structure faithfully: EN "and the word was God" / PT "e a palavra era Deus" / DE "und das Wort war Gott" / ES "y la palabra era Dios." Tier 2 note explains anarthrous predicate, Colwell's rule, qualitative reading.
- **Alternatives considered:** "and God was the word" (rejected in EN/PT/ES: inverts subject-predicate; acceptable in some word orders but misleading); "and the word was divine" (rejected: over-resolves the qualitative predicate into an adjective).
- **Justification:** The article on λόγος identifies it as subject; anarthrous θεός is predicate. The construction asserts something about the nature of the logos. The translation must preserve this structure without resolving the theological question.
- **Status:** provisional
- **Reviewers:** unassigned
- **Cross-references:** Entry J-003 (DE-specific)

## Entry J-003

- **Verse:** John 1:1c (German-specific)
- **Language(s) affected:** DE
- **Rule(s) invoked:** CORE Rule 19 (Priority Order When Rules Conflict), CORE Rule 1 (Meaning), CORE Rule 5 (Preserve Grammar)
- **Decision:** Render as "und das Wort war Gott" (subject-first in German), NOT "und Gott war das Wort" (which preserves Greek word order but inverts the meaning in German).
- **Alternatives considered:** "und Gott war das Wort" (preserves Greek θεός-first order but in German reads as "God was the word" — subject-first parsing inverts the predicate-subject relationship).
- **Justification:** Rule 19 priority resolution: Rule 1 (meaning fidelity) takes priority over Rule 5 (preserve source grammar) when they conflict. German word order parses "und Gott war das Wort" as subject-predicate ("God was the word"), which reverses the Greek's predicate-subject structure. The chapter file's verse-1 note already contains the grammatical analysis (Colwell's rule, anarthrous theos) that supports this resolution.
- **Status:** provisional
- **Reviewers:** unassigned
- **Cross-references:** Entry J-002

## Entry J-007

- **Verse:** John 3:16-21 (also applicable to 3:31-36)
- **Language(s) affected:** EN, PT, DE, ES
- **Rule(s) invoked:** CORE §Punctuation Governance (speech-boundary policy), Prime Directive
- **Decision:** Quotation marks must NOT resolve whether John 3:16-21 is Yeshua's direct speech or the narrator's commentary. The Greek text does not mark this boundary. Typography must preserve the ambiguity.
- **Alternatives considered:** Close quotation marks at 3:15 (rejected: resolves ambiguity the source preserves); close at 3:21 (rejected: same problem in opposite direction); remove all quotation marks from the discourse section (possible but complicates readability).
- **Justification:** The Prime Directive requires preserving what the source text leaves ambiguous. The speaker-attribution question at John 3:16 is one of the oldest Johannine interpretive debates. Typographic resolution would violate the Prime Directive. Tier 2 note identifies the ambiguity.
- **Status:** provisional
- **Reviewers:** unassigned

## Entry J-009

- **Verse:** John 1:51, 3:12-13, 3:27, 3:31
- **Language(s) affected:** EN, PT, DE, ES
- **Rule(s) invoked:** CORE Rule 16 (Cross-Language Alignment)
- **Decision:** Render οὐρανός as EN "sky" / PT "céu" / DE "Himmel" / ES "cielo." Cross-language tension noted: EN "sky" is more spatial/literal; PT/DE/ES traditional forms carry inherited theological abstraction. EN also uses "heavenly things" adjectively (John 3:12) while using "sky" as noun — this tension is documented but accepted.

- **Justification:** Each language uses its most transparent natural equivalent. The cross-language tension is real but honest — it reflects the different semantic ranges of the target-language terms. Tier 2 note documents the decision.
- **Status:** provisional
- **Reviewers:** unassigned

## Entry J-010

- **Verse:** John 3:3, 3:7
- **Language(s) affected:** EN, PT, DE, ES
- **Rule(s) invoked:** CORE Rule 2 (Ambiguity Preservation), GS §Locked Glossary (anōthen)
- **Decision:** Render ἄνωθεν with slash: EN "from above/again" / PT "de cima/de novo" / DE "von oben/wieder" / ES "de arriba/de nuevo."

## Entry J-011

- **Verse:** John 1:3-4
- **Language(s) affected:** EN, PT, DE, ES
- **Rule(s) invoked:** CORE §Punctuation Governance, Prime Directive
- **Decision:** Preserve the punctuation ambiguity at the John 1:3-4 boundary. The Greek can be read as "without him nothing came into being. What has come into being — in him was life" OR "without him nothing came into being that has come into being. In him was life." Typography must not resolve this.
- **Alternatives considered:** Period after "nothing came into being" (resolves toward reading 1); period after "that has come into being" (resolves toward reading 2).
- **Justification:** Both readings have ancient manuscript support (early manuscripts lack punctuation). The Prime Directive requires preserving what the source leaves ambiguous. Em-dash used to mark the boundary without resolving it.
- **Status:** provisional
- **Reviewers:** unassigned

## Entry J-012

- **Verse:** John 2:13, 2:23 (book-wide policy)
- **Language(s) affected:** EN, PT, DE, ES
- **Rule(s) invoked:** GS §Locked Glossary (pascha), CORE Rule 16, GS §Cross-Supplement Glossary Alignment
- **Decision:** Render πάσχα (*pascha*) as "Pesach" in all four target languages.
- **Alternatives considered:** EN "Passover" (rejected: English-specific rendering not available in other languages); PT "Páscoa" / ES "Pascua" (rejected: in modern PT/ES, these words can mean Easter — creates Rule 3 anachronism risk); DE "Pessach" (acceptable but "Pesach" without doubled-s is closer to the Aramaic/Hebrew form).
- **Justification:** This is a governed exception. The GS normally renders Greek terms via Greek semantics, not through Hebrew cognates. πάσχα qualifies for exception because: (a) HB→GS cross-supplement alignment (Rule 16) — πάσχα is the Greek form of Hebrew/Aramaic פֶּסַח, and the HB festival identity must be preserved; (b) the LXX/Hebrew cognate is stable and universally recognized; (c) PT "Páscoa" and ES "Pascua" carry Easter contamination in modern usage, creating a Rule 3 violation risk. This does NOT create a general rule that Greek terms are rendered through Hebrew — future similar cases require their own editorial-log entries with specific justification.
- **Status:** provisional
- **Reviewers:** unassigned

## Entry J-015

- **Verse:** PT-BR John 1:14, 1:18, 3:16, 3:18 (book-wide policy)
- **Language(s) affected:** PT-BR
- **Rule(s) invoked:** Rule 1 (Controlled Lexical Consistency), Rule 3 (no imported theology), GS Locked Glossary §monogenēs cross-supplement-alignment
- **Decision:** Render μονογενής (*monogenēs*) as `único-nascido` in PT-BR throughout the Gospel of John, replacing the previous `unigênito`. Aligns PT-BR with EN ("only-born"), DE ("einziggeborenen"), ES ("único-nacido"). Applied across `content/pt-br/john/CHAPTER-{1,2,3}.md` and `content/pt-br/john/study/CHAPTER-{1,3}-CONTEXT.md` — 22 occurrences in active TT renderings, glossary table entries, Tier 2 notes, and continuous-reading repetition.
- **Alternatives considered:**
  - **Retain `unigênito`** (rejected): Rule 3 violation — `unigênito` (from Latin *unigenitus*) carries the post-Nicene `gennaō → begetting` connotation that the GS glossary explicitly rejects. The CHANGELOG-v3.1 GS glossary entry on *monogenēs* warns against this importation.
  - **Use `unigerado`** (rejected): preserves the `mono-genēs` morphology but is awkward in modern PT-BR; less idiomatic than `único-nascido`.
  - **Calque `único-gerado`** (rejected): same `gennaō` connotation problem as `unigênito`.
- **Justification:** Rule 1 cross-supplement glossary alignment requires PT-BR to render *monogenēs* consistently with EN/DE/ES. The other three locales chose forms based on the μόνος + γένος etymology (only/single + kind/birth), avoiding γεννάω (*gennaō*, beget). PT-BR `único-nascido` (literally "only-born") matches that etymological choice and the project's Rule-3 anti-imported-theology stance.
- **Note on contrast preservation:** Four meta-commentary lines in Tier 2 notes / Tier 3 companions retain the word `unigênito` in explicit contrast: "A TT evita o tradicional 'unigênito' (Vulgata)..." These are intentional references to the rejected traditional rendering, not active TT translation choices. Each is allow-listed in `scripts/lint-allowlist.txt` rule §0.5 with rationale.
- **Lint protection:** §0.5 (PT-BR `unigênito` blocking rule) confirms zero active uses; allow-list captures the four meta-commentary references.
- **ES extension (Phase 4 audit):** the §0.5 lint scoped only PT-BR. Phase 4 audit pass surfaced parallel ES bugs in `content/es/john/CHAPTER-1.md` (active text at John 1:18 main + continuous reading: `el unigenito Dios` → `el único-nacido Dios`), 2 glossary-table hyphenation drifts (`único nacido` → `único-nacido`), and 4 references in CHAPTER-1-CONTEXT.md A8 entry (`"Dios unigenito"` / `"Hijo unigenito"` quoting TT renderings of the variant → `"Dios único-nacido"` / `"Hijo único-nacido"`). All applied. Remaining ES `unigenito` references (in CONTEXT meta-commentary discussing the rejected Vulgata-derived form) parallel the PT-BR allow-listed cases — they describe the avoided traditional rendering, not active TT translations. ES has no §0.5-equivalent lint; ES editor sign-off at Rule-28 review is the authoritative check.
