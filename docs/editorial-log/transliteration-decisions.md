# Transliteration Decision Log

**Purpose:** Record why each Hebrew term crossed or did not cross the Rule 4 transliteration threshold.
**Rule 4 criterion:** Transliterate when (1) no accurate target-language equivalent exists, (2) common translation carries misleading baggage, or (3) the term is structurally/cosmologically critical.
**Created:** 2026-04-17 (v2.4.2)

---

## Transliterated (crossed threshold)

### רָקִיעַ → *raqia*
- **Threshold crossed:** Criterion 2 (misleading baggage) + Criterion 3 (cosmologically critical).
- **Why not "firmament":** Medieval Latin *firmamentum* imports solid-dome cosmology not explicit in Hebrew.
- **Why not "expanse":** Neutral but loses the root sense (ר-ק-ע = "to hammer/beat") that the note system is built to explain.
- **Policy:** Transliterated throughout Genesis 1 in Transparent Edition (all languages). Reader Edition may substitute "expanse" but must preserve "raqia" in notes.
- **First occurrence:** Gen 1:6.

### אִישׁ / אִשָּׁה → *ish / ishah* (at Gen 2:23 only)
- **Threshold crossed:** Criterion 1 (no equivalent preserving wordplay).
- **Why transliterate:** The phonetic pair *ish/ishah* is the entire point of Gen 2:23. English "man/woman" does not sound related. Transliteration makes the Hebrew wordplay visible.
- **Policy:** Transliterated at Gen 2:23 with parenthetical gloss; all other occurrences render as "man/woman" normally.
- **First occurrence:** Gen 2:23.

### חַוָּה → *Chava*
- **Threshold crossed:** Criterion 2 (traditional "Eve" drifted far from Hebrew via Greek *Εὔα* → Latin *Eva*).
- **Why transliterate:** Preserves wordplay with חַי (*chay*, "living") visible in the name. "Eve" obscures this connection entirely.
- **Policy:** Transparent Edition: *Chava*. Reader Edition: may use "Eve" with note.
- **First occurrence:** Gen 3:20.

## Not transliterated (did not cross threshold)

### נָחָשׁ → "serpent" (not *nachash*)
- **Threshold not crossed:** "Serpent" is a clean, direct English rendering.
- **Why not transliterate:** Unlike *raqia* (where every English equivalent carries cosmological baggage), "serpent" is semantically accurate without importing false connotation. The theological overlay (serpent = Satan) is a later reader's import, not a translation problem — it is addressed in the note, not by changing the rendering.
- **Consideration:** If future chapters reveal that *nachash* carries untranslatable connotation (e.g., wordplay with *nachash* = "divination/enchantment" at other occurrences), the decision may be revisited.

### תְהוֹם → "the deep" (not *tehom*)
- **Threshold not crossed:** "The deep" is functionally adequate.
- **Why not transliterate:** The term is not subject to the same cosmological baggage as *raqia*. "The deep" preserves the watery-abyss sense without over-specifying (Rule 12). The possible connection to Akkadian *Tiamat* is noted in Tier 2/3, not in the rendering.

### הִנֵּה → "see" (not *hinneh*)
- **Threshold not crossed:** "See" is a functional English deictic attention-marker.
- **Why not transliterate:** The term is pragmatically equivalent. No false connotation imported. Transliteration would add a stumbling-block without transparency gain.
- **Policy:** Locked in glossary (v2.4.2) as EN "see", PT "eis", DE "siehe".

### תּוֹלְדוֹת → "generations" (not *toledot*)
- **Threshold not crossed in main text:** "Generations" captures the core sense.
- **Why noted but not transliterated:** The root (י-ל-ד = "to beget") and structural-marker function are explained in the note. A transliteration in main text would interrupt narrative flow without proportional gain. Transliteration appears in the note as *toledot* for reference.

### אֵד → "mist" (not *ed*)
- **Threshold not crossed in main text:** "Mist" is the dominant traditional rendering.
- **Why noted:** The word is genuinely UNCERTAIN (only 2 occurrences in Hebrew Bible). The uncertainty is flagged in the note with alternatives (stream, flow, vapor). Transliterating *ed* in the main text would not help the reader since the Hebrew itself is obscure.
