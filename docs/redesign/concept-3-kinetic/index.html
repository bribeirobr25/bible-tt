The difference

What translations
hide from you

Traditional
Now the serpent was more crafty than any of the wild animals the LORD God had made. He said to the woman, “Did God really say, ‘You must not eat from any tree in the garden’?”
Transparent
And the serpent was arum (crafty/shrewd) from all the living things of the field that YHWH God had made. And it said to the woman: “Indeed, did God say, ‘You shall not eat from any tree of the garden’?”

01 The name they replaced

The Hebrew says YHWH — four consonants, no vowels. Most Bibles swap it for ‘the LORD.’ We put back what was there.

02 A hidden pun

‘Crafty’ (arum) sounds almost identical to ‘naked’ (arom) from the verse before. The author did that on purpose. Most translations erase it.

03 Both sides lie

The serpent exaggerates the rule. The woman corrects it — but quietly changes the original command too. We keep both distortions visible.

04 You can see the seams

Every word in italics was added to make English work. It is not in the Hebrew. You always know what is original and what is scaffolding.

What this is

Every translation makes choices.
Most hide them.

This one shows you exactly where — in English, Portuguese, German, and Spanish. It is not a devotional. It is not a replacement for scholarly editions. It is a disciplined attempt to let you see what is actually there.

When we don’t know something, we say so. When scholars disagree, we show you both sides.
