John

At a glance

What
A theological gospel presenting Jesus as the eternal “word” (logos) made flesh.
When
Events around 28–30 CE; written near the end of the first century (~90–100 CE).
Who
Attributed to John, “the disciple whom Jesus loved”; authorship is debated.
To whom
A late-first-century community, addressing both believers and doubters of Jesus’s identity.
Why
“That you may trust that Jesus is the anointed one, the son of God — and have life in his name” (20:31).

First book of the Greek Scriptures in the TT

The opening — “In the beginning was the word” — directly echoes Genesis 1. Where Hebrew narrative turns on verb tense, Greek turns on aspect (how the speaker views an action): John 1 contrasts the imperfect ēn (“was,” ongoing) with the aorist egeneto (“came into being”). Names follow TT transliteration — Yochanan (John), Yeshua (Jesus), Kefa (Peter) — and hoi Ioudaioi is rendered Yehudim with its referential scope noted at each occurrence.
