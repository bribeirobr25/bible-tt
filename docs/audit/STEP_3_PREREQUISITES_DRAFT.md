## Prerequisite (c) — Descriptive (non-apologetic) framing draft for project-lead review

The Wesley Huff source frames the comparison apologetically (*"closer in proximity than the vast majority of the people who we would just kind of assume existed"*). The TT cannot reproduce that voice; the paragraph must describe the comparative-transmission data without arguing *for* the NT.

**Proposed EN draft (parallel paragraphs in John §E5 + Matthew §E4):**

> **§E5 (John) / §E4 (Matthew). Comparative transmission interval**
> **[HISTORICAL / ARCHAEOLOGICAL — VERIFIED]**
>
> The interval between the composition of the Gospels (c. 65–95 CE) and the earliest surviving manuscript witnesses (P52, c. 100–150 CE for John 18; P64+67, late 2nd c. for Matthew 3, 5, 26; P66 + P75, c. 200–225 CE for John; P45, mid-3rd c. for the four-Gospel collection) is, by the standards of ancient historiography, relatively short. For comparison: Arrian's *Anabasis of Alexander*, the most comprehensive surviving biography of Alexander the Great (d. 323 BCE), was composed c. 130–135 CE — a gap of approximately 450 years between subject and biography. Tacitus's *Annales*, covering events from 14 CE onward, was composed c. 116 CE — a gap of approximately 80–100 years between events and narrative. The NT's transmission interval sits at the shorter end of the range typical for ancient narrative literature about historical figures. **This does not bear on the historical accuracy of the Gospels' contents; it bears only on the textual-transmission gap between composition and first surviving witness.** The same data is sometimes deployed apologetically — that is not the TT's frame: the comparison is descriptive, not evidential.
>
> **Source:** Nongbri, "The Use and Abuse of P52," *HTR* 98 (2005): 23–48; Roberts, *An Unpublished Fragment of the Fourth Gospel in the John Rylands Library* (1935); Arrian, *Anabasis of Alexander*, Preface (cf. Bosworth, *Historical Commentary*, OUP, 1980/1995); Tacitus, *Annales* (Loeb Classical Library; cf. Goodyear, *The Annals of Tacitus*, Cambridge, 1972/1981).

**Key Rule-3 safeguards I built in:**
1. **"By the standards of ancient historiography, relatively short"** — a description, not a value claim.
2. **The bolded sentence**: "This does not bear on the historical accuracy of the Gospels' contents; it bears only on the textual-transmission gap between composition and first surviving witness." — explicitly disclaims the apologetic upshot.
3. **Final sentence**: "The same data is sometimes deployed apologetically — that is not the TT's frame: the comparison is descriptive, not evidential." — names the apologetic frame as a known external use, distancing the TT from it.
4. **P52 framing** mirrors the verified §C2 anchor (range, not midpoint).
5. **All datings sourced** to Nongbri/Roberts/Bosworth/Goodyear with no rhetorical embellishment.
