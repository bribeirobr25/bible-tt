## Yeshua (Jesus)
**See:** matthew/PEOPLE.md
**In Luke:** The central figure, announced by Gavriel (Gabriel) to Miryam (Mary) — "you will conceive… and will call his name Yeshua" (1:31), "Son of *the* Most High," heir to "the throne of David his father" (1:32-33), conceived by the holy wind/spirit (1:35). His coming is greeted by the leaping of the unborn Yochanan (John) and Elisheva (Elizabeth)'s blessing (1:41-45). Born in Beit-Lechem (Bethlehem), the city of David, laid in a feeding-trough (2:6-7); announced to shepherds as "a savior… Messiah, Lord" (2:11); circumcised and named on the eighth day (2:21); presented in the temple, where Shimon (Simeon) sings over him and Channah (Anna) gives thanks (2:25-38); at twelve found among the teachers, "in the *things* of my Father" (2:41-49). Immersed and praying, the sky opens, the wind/spirit descends, and a voice from heaven says "You are my son, the beloved" (3:21-22). The genealogy traces him, "as was supposed" the son of Yosef (Joseph), back through David and Avraham (Abraham) to Adam, "of God" (3:23-38). The canonical bio fields (birth, parents, profession, death by crucifixion) live in matthew/PEOPLE.md; this entry adds the Luke-specific narrative role and the ascending genealogy (see the table below).

## Yochanan the Immerser (John the Baptist)
**See:** matthew/PEOPLE.md
**In Luke:** Luke alone narrates his conception and birth. Announced by Gavriel (Gabriel) to his father, the priest Zecharyah (Zechariah), in the temple (1:11-20): he will be great before the Lord, filled with the holy wind/spirit from the womb, abstaining from wine and strong drink, going before the Lord "in *the* spirit and power of Eliyahu (Elijah)" (1:13-17). Conceived by the aged, formerly barren Elisheva (Elizabeth) (1:24); leaps in his mother's womb at Miryam (Mary)'s greeting (1:41, 44); born, circumcised, and named "Yochanan" against the family's expectation, loosing his father's tongue (1:57-66); grows and stays in the wilderness (1:80). In Luke 3 "the word of God came upon" him (3:2); he proclaims an immersion of repentance for the release of sins, preaches concrete ethics to crowds, tax-collectors, and soldiers (3:7-14), points beyond himself to a "stronger one" (3:15-17), and is shut in prison by Herodes (Herod) the tetrarch (3:19-20). The canonical bio fields (lineage, lifestyle, death under Antipas) live in matthew/PEOPLE.md; this entry adds the Luke-specific role.

## Miryam (Mary)
**See:** matthew/PEOPLE.md
**In Luke:** Far more prominent than in Matthew. A virgin of Natzeret (Nazareth) betrothed to Yosef (Joseph), addressed by Gavriel (Gabriel) as the "having-been-favored one" (1:28); she questions ("How will this be?") and consents — "may it happen to me according to your word" (1:34-38). She visits Elisheva (Elizabeth) and sings the Magnificat (1:46-55). She bears Yeshua (Jesus) in Beit-Lechem (Bethlehem) (2:7), "treasures" and "ponders" the shepherds' report (2:19), hears Shimon (Simeon)'s words that "a sword will pass through your own soul" (2:35), and at the temple again "kept all these matters in her heart" (2:51). The canonical bio fields live in matthew/PEOPLE.md; this entry adds the Luke-specific role — in Luke she speaks and acts as an agent, the narrative's center in chapters 1–2.

## Zecharyah (Zechariah)
**Meaning:** Hebrew זְכַרְיָה (*Zecharyah*), "YHWH remembers"; Greek Ζαχαρίας
**Origin:** Priest of the division of Aviyah, married to Elisheva (Elizabeth)
**Familiar name:** Zechariah
**Profession:** Priest (*hiereus*) of the eighth priestly division, Aviyah (1:5, 8-9)
**Hometown:** the hill country of Yehudah (Judah) (cf. 1:39, 65)
**First mention:** Luke 1:5
**Mentioned in:** Luke 1:5-25, 57-79; 3:2 (named as Yochanan/John's father)
**Key events in Luke 1–3:** Serving in the temple, chosen by lot to burn incense, he is visited by Gavriel (Gabriel) and told that Elisheva will bear a son to be named Yochanan (1:8-17); he doubts ("By what will I know this? For I am old") and is struck mute as a sign (1:18-22); after the birth he writes "John is his name," his speech returns, and he prophesies the Benedictus (1:62-79), blessing the Lord for a "horn of salvation" in David's house and naming his son the prophet of the Most High.
**Spouse(s):** Elisheva (Elizabeth)
**Children:** Yochanan (John) the Immerser (1:13, 57)
**Archaeological evidence:** No direct archaeological evidence for the individual.
**Extra-biblical mentions:** Not attested outside the NT; not to be confused with the prophet Zechariah (the book) or the Zechariah son of Berechiah named at Luke 11:51 / Matt 23:35.
**Historicity status:** UNCERTAIN
**Books appearing in:** Luke
**Character arc:** A righteous, blameless priest whose long-prayed-for son arrives in old age; his doubt earns nine months of silence, broken into prophecy at the naming — moving from disbelief to praise.

**Note:** His muteness (and possible deafness, implied by the signs made to him at 1:62) is both the requested "sign" and a discipline for unbelief; his question (1:18) is deliberately contrasted with Miryam (Mary)'s inquiry (1:34).

## Elisheva (Elizabeth)
**Meaning:** Hebrew אֱלִישֶׁבַע (*Elisheva*), "my God *is an* oath" / "God *is* fullness" — the name of Aharon (Aaron)'s wife (Ex 6:23); Greek Ἐλισάβετ
**Origin:** Of the daughters of Aharon (Aaron) — priestly descent; wife of Zecharyah (Zechariah)
**Familiar name:** Elizabeth
**Profession:** not stated (wife of a priest)
**Hometown:** the hill country of Yehudah (Judah) (1:39-40)
**First mention:** Luke 1:5
**Mentioned in:** Luke 1:5-7, 13, 24-25, 36, 40-45, 57-61
**Key events in Luke 1–3:** Righteous and blameless but barren and advanced in years (1:6-7); conceives in old age and stays hidden five months, declaring the Lord has "taken away my disgrace" (1:24-25); at Miryam (Mary)'s greeting her baby leaps and she is filled with the holy wind/spirit, blessing Mary as "the mother of my lord" (1:41-45); bears a son and insists, against the relatives, on the name Yochanan (John) (1:57-60).
**Spouse(s):** Zecharyah (Zechariah)
**Children:** Yochanan (John) the Immerser (1:57)
**Archaeological evidence:** No direct archaeological evidence.
**Extra-biblical mentions:** Not attested outside the NT. Luke 1:36 calls her Mary's "relative" (*syngenis*); the exact kinship is unstated ("cousin" is later tradition).
**Historicity status:** UNCERTAIN
**Books appearing in:** Luke
**Character arc:** A barren-then-blessed mother in the line of Sarah and Channah (Hannah); her joy and her wind/spirit-filled blessing of Mary make her the first human to recognize the unborn Yeshua (Jesus).

**Note:** The barren-wife reversal (1:7, 25) deliberately echoes Sarah (Gen 18), Rachel (Gen 30:23), and Channah (Hannah, 1 Sam 1).

## Gavriel (Gabriel)
**Meaning:** Hebrew גַּבְרִיאֵל (*Gavriel*), "God *is* my strength" / "man of God"; Greek Γαβριήλ
**Origin:** An angelic messenger (*angelos*), "the *one* standing before God" (1:19)
**Familiar name:** Gabriel
**Profession:** Messenger / interpreting angel (cf. Daniel 8:16; 9:21)
**First mention:** Luke 1:11 (as "a messenger of the Lord"); named at 1:19
**Mentioned in:** Luke 1:11-20, 26-38
**Key events in Luke 1–3:** Appears to Zecharyah (Zechariah) in the temple to announce Yochanan (John)'s birth and self-identifies — "I am Gabriel, the *one* standing before God" (1:19); announces to muteness Zechariah for his unbelief (1:20); six months later is sent to Miryam (Mary) in Natzeret (Nazareth) to announce the conception of Yeshua (Jesus) by the holy wind/spirit (1:26-38).
**Archaeological evidence:** Not applicable (angelic figure).
**Extra-biblical mentions:** Daniel 8:16; 9:21 (the interpreting messenger); extensive Second Temple angelology (1 Enoch, the Qumran texts) names Gabriel among the chief angels.
**Historicity status:** LITERARY
**Books appearing in:** Luke; Daniel
**Character arc:** The same named messenger frames both annunciations; he draws the John/Yeshua parallel and, by his rebuke of Zechariah and gentleness toward Mary, sharpens the doubt-versus-faith contrast.

**Note (Rule 30):** Gavriel (Gabriel) is an *angelos*, a messenger — not God. His speech is therefore rendered in ordinary quotation marks, NOT the `@@…@@` divine-speech markers. Only God's/the wind-spirit's direct speech is divine-marked (e.g. the voice from heaven, 3:22). See luke.md L-001.

## Yosef (Joseph)
**Meaning:** Hebrew יוֹסֵף (*Yosef*), "he adds" / "may he add" (Gen 30:24); Greek Ἰωσήφ
**Origin:** A man of Natzeret (Nazareth), "of the house and family of David" (1:27; 2:4)
**Familiar name:** Joseph
**Profession:** not stated in Luke (a craftsman per Matt 13:55, outside Luke's scope)
**Hometown:** Natzeret (Nazareth) of the Galilee (2:4); travels to Beit-Lechem (Bethlehem), his ancestral city of David
**First mention:** Luke 1:27
**Mentioned in:** Luke 1:27; 2:4-5, 16, 22-24, 33, 39, 41-51; 3:23
**Key events in Luke 1–3:** Betrothed to Miryam (Mary) (1:27; 2:5); travels from Nazareth to Beit-Lechem for the census because he is of David's line (2:4-5); present at the birth and the shepherds' visit (2:16); brings the child to the temple for the purification and presentation (2:22-24), marveling at Shimon (Simeon)'s words (2:33); searches three days for the twelve-year-old Yeshua (Jesus) in Jerusalem (2:43-48); named at the head of the genealogy — Yeshua "being a son — as was supposed — of Yosef (Joseph)" (3:23).
**Spouse(s):** Miryam (Mary) (betrothed, 1:27; 2:5)
**Children:** Yeshua (Jesus) (legally, 2:5-7, 33, 48); the genealogy is reckoned through him (3:23)
**Archaeological evidence:** No direct archaeological evidence.
**Extra-biblical mentions:** Not in Josephus; later apocryphal traditions (Protevangelium of James) elaborate his role.
**Historicity status:** UNCERTAIN
**Books appearing in:** Luke; Matthew; John 1:45; 6:42
**Character arc:** The Davidic-line husband through whom the journey to Bethlehem (and so the genealogy) runs; in Luke he is "father" in the parents' own speech (2:48), while the narrator hedges biological paternity with "as was supposed" (3:23).

**Note:** Luke's genealogy names Yosef (Joseph)'s father as **Eli** (3:23), where Matthew 1:16 reads **Ya'aqov (Jacob)** — a long-standing crux (see the genealogy note below).

## Shimon (Simeon)
**Meaning:** Hebrew שִׁמְעוֹן (*Shimon*), "he has heard"; Greek Συμεών
**Origin:** A man of Yerushalayim (Jerusalem), "righteous and devout" (2:25)
**Familiar name:** Simeon
**Profession:** not stated; a devout man awaiting "the consolation of Israel," with the holy wind/spirit upon him (2:25-27)
**Hometown:** Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) (2:25)
**First mention:** Luke 2:25
**Mentioned in:** Luke 2:25-35
**Key events in Luke 1–3:** Promised by the holy wind/spirit that he would not die before seeing "the Messiah of *the* Lord" (2:26); led into the temple, he takes the child Yeshua (Jesus) in his arms and sings the Nunc Dimittis — "my eyes saw your salvation… a light for revelation of *the* nations" (2:28-32); blesses the parents and tells Miryam (Mary) that the child is "set for *the* falling and rising of many in Israel" and that "a sword will pass through your own soul" (2:34-35).
**Archaeological evidence:** No direct archaeological evidence.
**Extra-biblical mentions:** Not attested outside the NT.
**Historicity status:** UNCERTAIN
**Books appearing in:** Luke
**Character arc:** The faithful remnant of Israel personified; having waited for consolation, he recognizes salvation in an infant, then voices the first warning of the cross and of Mary's grief.

**Note (Rule 30):** Shimon (Simeon)'s words — both the Nunc Dimittis (2:29-32) and the oracle to Mary (2:34-35) — are *human* speech, not divine-marked; the canticle is rendered as flowing prose (luke.md L-002). He addresses God as *despotēs* ("Master"), not *kyrios*.

## Channah (Anna)
**Meaning:** Hebrew חַנָּה (*Channah*), "favor" / "grace" — the name of Shemu'el (Samuel)'s mother (1 Sam 1); Greek Ἅννα
**Origin:** A prophetess, daughter of Penuel, of the tribe of Asher (2:36)
**Familiar name:** Anna
**Profession:** Prophetess (*prophētis*); a widow serving in the temple with fasting and prayer night and day (2:36-37)
**Hometown:** resident in the temple at Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) (2:37)
**First mention:** Luke 2:36
**Mentioned in:** Luke 2:36-38
**Key events in Luke 1–3:** Very old — widowed after seven years of marriage, then a widow "until eighty-four years" — she never leaves the temple (2:36-37); arriving at the very hour Shimon (Simeon) speaks, she gives thanks to God and speaks of the child "to all who were awaiting *the* redemption of Yerushalayim (Jerusalem)" (2:38).
**Spouse(s):** an unnamed husband, with whom she lived seven years (2:36)
**Children:** none stated
**Archaeological evidence:** No direct archaeological evidence.
**Extra-biblical mentions:** Not attested outside the NT.
**Historicity status:** UNCERTAIN
**Books appearing in:** Luke
**Character arc:** The female prophetic witness paired with Simeon; in the line of Miryam, Devorah, and Chuldah, she becomes the first to proclaim the child to the expectant remnant.

**Note:** Her father's name Penuel (*Phanouēl*) means "face of God" (Gen 32:31); her own name Channah ties the temple scene again to Channah (Hannah), mother of Shemu'el (Samuel). "Eighty-four" may be her age or the length of her widowhood — the Greek allows both.

## Caesar Augustus
**Meaning:** "Augustus" (Latin *Augustus*, "venerable, exalted") — an honorific title, not a birth name
**Origin:** Gaius Octavius (Octavian), grand-nephew and heir of Julius Caesar
**Familiar name:** Caesar Augustus
**Full designation:** Imperator Caesar Divi Filius Augustus, first Roman emperor (r. 27 BCE – 14 CE)
**Profession:** Roman emperor (*princeps*)
**First mention:** Luke 2:1
**Mentioned in:** Luke 2:1
**Key events in Luke 1–3:** Issues the census decree "to register all the inhabited *world*" (2:1) that brings Yosef (Joseph) and the pregnant Miryam (Mary) to Beit-Lechem (Bethlehem) — the imperial backdrop against which the true king is born unnoticed.
**Archaeological evidence:** EXTENSIVE — coins, inscriptions (the *Res Gestae Divi Augusti*), statuary, and architecture across the empire; one of the best-documented figures of antiquity.
**Extra-biblical mentions:** Suetonius, *Divus Augustus*; Cassius Dio; Tacitus; Augustus's own *Res Gestae*; Josephus.
**Historicity status:** VERIFIED
**Books appearing in:** Luke
**Character arc:** Appears only as the distant author of the census decree; his imperial honorifics (*sōtēr*, "savior"; "lord") stand in deliberate contrast to the savior announced to the shepherds (2:11).

## Quirinius
**Meaning:** Latin family name (Publius Sulpicius Quirinius)
**Origin:** Roman senator and military commander
**Familiar name:** Quirinius
**Profession:** Roman governor / legate of the province of Syria
**First mention:** Luke 2:2
**Mentioned in:** Luke 2:2
**Key events in Luke 1–3:** Named as governing Syria when "this registration *was the* first" took place (2:2).
**Archaeological evidence:** Attested in Roman inscriptions and by Josephus; his known census of Judea fell c. 6 CE.
**Extra-biblical mentions:** Josephus, *Antiquities* 17–18 (the census of 6 CE); Tacitus, *Annals* 3.48; the *Lapis Tiburtinus* (debated).
**Historicity status:** VERIFIED (individual); the dating in Luke 2:2 is a recognized crux
**Books appearing in:** Luke
**Character arc:** A purely chronological marker — but a contested one: his documented census (c. 6 CE) falls roughly a decade after Herod the Great's death (4 BCE), under whom Luke 1:5 sets John's conception.

**Note:** The Quirinius dating problem (2:2) is flagged for review (Rule 13); *prōtē* ("first") admits the comparative reading "before Quirinius governed." See Luke 2 §A1.

## Tiberius Caesar
**Meaning:** Latin praenomen (Tiberius Caesar Augustus; born Tiberius Claudius Nero)
**Origin:** Stepson and successor of Caesar Augustus
**Familiar name:** Tiberius
**Profession:** Roman emperor (r. 14 – 37 CE)
**First mention:** Luke 3:1
**Mentioned in:** Luke 3:1
**Key events in Luke 1–3:** His "fifteenth year" dates the start of Yochanan (John)'s ministry (3:1) — the anchor of Luke's sixfold synchronism.
**Archaeological evidence:** EXTENSIVE — coins, inscriptions, statuary.
**Extra-biblical mentions:** Tacitus, *Annals* 1–6; Suetonius, *Tiberius*; Cassius Dio; Josephus.
**Historicity status:** VERIFIED
**Books appearing in:** Luke (named); referenced indirectly elsewhere (the "Caesar" of the tribute question)
**Character arc:** A dating reference only; the "fifteenth year" admits more than one reckoning (3:1, flagged for review).

## Pontius Pilate
**Meaning:** Roman name (Pontius, family name; Pilatus, cognomen)
**Origin:** Roman of the equestrian order
**Familiar name:** Pontius Pilate
**Profession:** Roman prefect/governor of Judea (26 – 36 CE)
**First mention:** Luke 3:1
**Mentioned in:** Luke 3:1 (named in the synchronism); his governing role is developed later in Luke (ch. 23)
**Key events in Luke 1–3:** Named "governing Yehudah (Judea)" in the dating formula for John's ministry (3:1).
**Archaeological evidence:** VERIFIED — the "Pilate Stone" from Caesarea Maritima (1961) names him as prefect of Judea; coins of his prefecture are known.
**Extra-biblical mentions:** Josephus, *Antiquities* 18 and *War* 2; Philo, *Embassy to Gaius* 38; Tacitus, *Annals* 15.44 (Christ executed under Pilate).
**Historicity status:** VERIFIED
**Books appearing in:** Luke; Matthew; Mark; John; Acts; 1 Timothy 6:13
**Character arc:** In Luke 1–3 a chronological marker only; his role in the passion is outside this scope.

## Herodes (Herod) the tetrarch
**Meaning:** "heroic" (Greek Ἡρῴδης) — here Herod Antipas
**Origin:** Son of Herod the Great; tetrarch under Roman authority
**Familiar name:** Herod Antipas
**Profession:** Tetrarch of the Galilee and Peraea (r. 4 BCE – 39 CE)
**First mention:** Luke 3:1
**Mentioned in:** Luke 3:1, 19-20
**Key events in Luke 1–3:** Named in the synchronism as "tetrarch of the Galilee" (3:1); reproved by Yochanan (John) over Herodias, his brother's wife, and "all the evils that Herod had done," he adds to them by shutting John in prison (3:19-20).
**Archaeological evidence:** Attested — coins of Antipas; the city of Tiberias he founded.
**Extra-biblical mentions:** Josephus, *Antiquities* 18.116-119 (John's execution at Machaerus), 18.240-256 (Antipas's exile).
**Historicity status:** VERIFIED
**Books appearing in:** Luke; Matthew; Mark; Acts 4:27; 13:1
**Character arc:** The ruler who silences the prophet; Luke narrates the imprisonment proleptically, removing John before the baptism so the focus falls on Yeshua (Jesus).

**Note:** Distinct from his father, Herod the Great (Luke 1:5; full entry in matthew/PEOPLE.md), and from Herod Philip his brother (3:1). "Herodias" and the tetrarchs Philippos (Philip) and Lysanias are named only in passing in Luke 1–3.

**Note — divergence from Matthew (crux, flagged for Hellenist review per Rule 13):**
- **Joseph's father:** Luke reads "of **Eli**" (3:23); Matthew reads "Ya'aqov (Jacob) fathered Yosef (Joseph)" (Matt 1:16).
- **David's line:** Luke runs through David's son **Natan** (3:31); Matthew through **Shelomoh (Solomon)** (Matt 1:6) — the structural root of the post-Davidic divergence.
- **Direction and reach:** Luke **ascends** and continues *past* Avraham (Abraham) through Terach, the Shem line, the antediluvian patriarchs, to Adam and "of God" (3:34-38); Matthew **descends** and stops at Abraham.
- **LXX-only Qenan:** Luke's list includes a second **Qenan (Kenan)** between Arpakhshad and Shelach (3:36) — present in the LXX of Gen 11:12-13 but absent from the Hebrew Masoretic text.
- **Convergence:** From David back to Nachshon (3:31-32) the line matches Ruth 4:18-22 and Matthew 1:3-6; the Zerubbavel–She'altiel pair (3:27) also overlaps Matthew 1:12, though the surrounding names differ.
- Proposed harmonizations (Luke gives Miryam/Mary's line via Eli; levirate marriage; legal versus biological descent — all keyed to the "as was supposed" hedge of 3:23) are contested. The TT renders Luke's list as the Greek gives it and does not resolve the line's owner. See Luke 3 §A4 and docs/editorial-log/luke.md.
