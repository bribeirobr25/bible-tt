## Note on Luke's mode of allusion

Luke 3 contains the chapter's **one extended formula citation** — Isaiah 40:3–5, introduced "as it is written in the book of the words of Yesha'yahu (Isaiah) the prophet" — quoted **further than Matthew or Mark** (Luke alone reaches v.5, "all flesh will see the salvation of God"). The chapter also carries the **prophetic-call formula** (Jer 1 / Hos 1 pattern, 3:2), the **Bat-Qol** at the baptism (a fusion of Ps 2:7 and Isa 42:1, 3:22 — the chapter's one divine-marked saying, Rule 30), and the **ascending genealogy** (3:23–38) that runs the line back through David's son Natan, past Abraham, to Adam, "of God." John's preaching is **human** prophecy — not divine-marked.

## Isaiah 40:3–5 — the voice in the wilderness; all flesh will see salvation (Luke 3:4–6)

**Verse:** Luke 3:4–6
**Text says:** "as it is written in *the* book of *the* words of Yesha'yahu (Isaiah) the prophet: 'A voice of *one* crying in the wilderness: "Prepare the way of the Lord, make his paths straight. Every ravine will be filled and every mountain and hill will be made low, and the crooked *places* will become straight and the rough *ways* into smooth roads; and all flesh will see the salvation of God."'"
**Context:** The narrator frames Yochanan's (John's) whole wilderness ministry with this citation — the one extended formula quotation in the chapter, introduced with an explicit "as it is written." It follows the prophetic-call notice (3:2) and the summary of John's immersion-of-repentance (3:3).
**Subject:** The voice preparing YHWH's highway and the universal sight of salvation. Isaiah 40:3–5 (LXX) is the opening of the consolation prophecies — the herald's call to prepare a road through the wilderness for the returning YHWH leading the exiles home. Per Option C, the *kyrios* of the quotation renders YHWH (Isaiah's "way of YHWH"); the Gospel applies the preparation toward Yeshua, fusing the referents. Luke extends the citation to 40:5, "all flesh (*pasa sarx*) will see the salvation (*to sōtērion*) of God."
**Fulfillment status:** CLAIMED
**Fulfillment notes:** This **is an explicit citation** ("as it is written… Isaiah the prophet") — the chapter's one direct, formula-introduced quotation. Two features carry the TT's restraint: (1) the LXX/Hebrew word-order difference — the Hebrew accents read "a voice crying: 'In the wilderness prepare…'" while the LXX (which Luke follows) attaches "in the wilderness" to the voice; the TT follows the Greek Luke uses and notes the difference. (2) Luke's **redactional extension** to v.5 ("all flesh will see the salvation of God") goes beyond Matthew 3:3 / Mark 1:3 (which stop at 40:3) — the universal horizon is Luke's own emphasis, mirrored by the genealogy's ascent to Adam (3:38). The referent of "the Lord" is fused (YHWH in Isaiah; the preparation directed toward Yeshua in the Gospel); the TT preserves the fusion (Rule 13). **[textual — VERIFIED (citation); referent-fusion — POSSIBLE]** → See Cross-Chapter Tracking (Isa 40:3–5 → Luke 3:4–6) in `content/en/luke/CHAPTER-3.md`. For the Isa 40:3-portion as quoted by Matthew/Mark, see `content/en/matthew/study/CHAPTER-2-PROPHECY.md` context and the Mark companions.
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: Isaiah 40:3–5 is read as the herald's proclamation opening the book of consolation — preparing a way through the wilderness for YHWH to lead the exiles back from Babylon; "all flesh will see" the glory/salvation of God. It is not read as a prophecy of John the Baptist; the Hebrew accentuation ("in the wilderness prepare the way") is the standard Jewish reading. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: All four Gospels apply Isaiah 40:3 to John as the wilderness voice preparing the way for Yeshua; Luke alone extends to v.5's universal "all flesh will see the salvation of God," read as the gospel's reach to all nations. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: The Quran honors Yahya (John) as a prophet (Surah 3:39; 19:12–15) but does not connect him to Isaiah 40 or to a wilderness-herald prophecy. [DOCUMENTED]

## Jeremiah 1:1–2; Hosea 1:1 — the prophetic-call formula (Luke 3:2)

**Verse:** Luke 3:2
**Text says:** "in *the* high priesthood of Chanan (Annas) and Qayafa (Caiaphas), *the* word of God came upon Yochanan (John) the son of Zecharyah (Zechariah) in the wilderness."
**Context:** The close of the sixfold dating synchronism (vv.1–2) and the commissioning of John. The dating-by-rulers + "the word of God came upon" pattern is the HB prophetic-call idiom.
**Subject:** The prophet commissioned by the word-event. "The word of God came upon John" (*egeneto rhēma theou epi Iōannēn*) reproduces the Septuagintal prophetic-commission formula (Jer 1:1–2, "the word of YHWH that came to Jeremiah… in the days of… king of Judah"; Hos 1:1; Joel 1:1), including the dating-by-reigns frame and the priestly-son detail (Jeremiah son of a priest; John son of the priest Zechariah).
**Fulfillment status:** CLAIMED
**Fulfillment notes:** This is **allusion by idiom**, not a citation — Luke does not quote Jeremiah or Hosea, but the dating-formula + "the word of God came upon" exactly matches the prophetic-call pattern, casting John as a called prophet in their line. **Important (Rule 30):** this is *narrated* commissioning, not God's quoted direct speech — John's subsequent preaching (vv.7–17) is **human** prophecy and is NOT divine-marked. The CLAIMED label reflects that Luke deliberately invokes the prophetic-call mould; there is no predictive-fulfillment content — the formula identifies John's *office*, not the realization of a specific prophecy. **[comparative parallel — PROBABLE]** → See Cross-Chapter Tracking (Jer 1:1–2 / Hos 1:1 → Luke 3:2).
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: Jeremiah 1:1–2 and Hosea 1:1 are the call-notices of those prophets; the "word of YHWH came to…" formula marks prophetic commissioning throughout the HB. Luke's use is recognized as casting John in the prophetic mould; it does not entail John's specific role within the Jewish frame. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: John is read as the last and greatest of the prophets in the old line, commissioned by the word-event to prepare for Yeshua; the priestly-son detail strengthens the Jeremiah parallel. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: Yahya is named a prophet given "judgment / wisdom" as a child (Surah 19:12); the specific HB prophetic-call formula is not part of the Quranic presentation. [DOCUMENTED]

## Psalm 2:7 + Isaiah 42:1 — the voice from heaven at the baptism (Luke 3:22)

**Verse:** Luke 3:22
**Text says:** "and the holy {a:wind/spirit} descended in bodily form like a dove upon him, and a voice came from heaven: @@\"You are my son, the beloved; in you I am well pleased.\"@@"
**Context:** Yeshua, having been immersed and praying, sees the sky opened and the wind/spirit descend; the voice from heaven (the *Bat-Qol*, "daughter of a voice") speaks. **This is the chapter's one divine-marked saying** (Rule 30) — God's direct speech, in `@@…@@`.
**Subject:** The Son in whom God delights. "You are my son" reproduces Psalm 2:7 (the royal enthronement decree, "You are my son; today I have begotten you"); "the beloved… in you I am well pleased" draws on Isaiah 42:1, the opening of the first Servant Song ("my chosen, in whom my soul delights; I have put my spirit upon him"), to which the descent of the wind/spirit (3:22a) also corresponds. The voice fuses royal sonship (Ps 2) with the chosen Servant (Isa 42).
**Fulfillment status:** CLAIMED
**Fulfillment notes:** This is **allusive fusion**, not a citation formula — the voice is not introduced by "as it is written," and it weaves two texts (Ps 2:7 + Isa 42:1) into a single declaration rather than quoting either verbatim. The Ps 2:7 link is partial in NA28 ("You are my son" without "today I have begotten you"); a notable **Western variant** (Codex Bezae, some Old Latin/patristic witnesses) quotes Psalm 2:7 **in full** ("today I have begotten you"). The TT follows NA28 (matching Mark 1:11) and flags the variant for Hellenist review (Rule 13). The CLAIMED label reflects that the heavenly voice asserts the sonship/Servant identity; the wording is allusive and the text-critical question is live. **[textual — PROBABLE; the Western Ps 2:7-full variant — POSSIBLE]** → See Cross-Chapter Tracking (Ps 2:7 / Isa 42:1 → Luke 3:22).
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: Psalm 2:7 is read as the enthronement decree to the Davidic king ("my son" = the anointed king by adoption/coronation), in its royal-coronation setting; Isaiah 42:1 of the chosen Servant on whom God's spirit rests (the Servant's identity debated). Neither is read as predicting a heavenly voice at Yeshua's baptism. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: The baptismal voice is read as the Father's declaration of Yeshua's divine sonship and Servant-vocation, fusing the royal Psalm 2 with the Servant of Isaiah 42 — sonship and mission together. The Western "today I have begotten you" variant featured in some early adoptionist debates. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: Islam affirms Isa as a prophet and the Messiah but firmly rejects divine sonship language (Surah 19:35; 112:1–4). The baptismal voice and its "my son" declaration are therefore read against the Quran's insistence that God neither begets nor is begotten. [DOCUMENTED]

## 2 Samuel 5:14 (cf. Zechariah 12:12); Genesis 5 / 11 / Ruth 4; Genesis 1:27 — the ascending genealogy to Adam, "of God" (Luke 3:23–38)

**Verse:** Luke 3:23–38
**Text says:** "And Yeshua (Jesus) himself was beginning *to be* about thirty years *old*, being a son — as was supposed — of Yosef (Joseph), of Eli… of Natan, of David… of Avraham (Abraham), of Terach, of Nachor… of Shem, of Noach (Noah)… of Enosh, of Shet, of Adam, of God."
**Context:** The chapter closes with the genealogy, placed after the baptism (not at the book's opening as in Matthew). It ascends — "son of… of… of…" — from Yeshua back to Adam and to God (~76 names), via the hedge "as was supposed" (*hōs enomizeto*) over Joseph's paternity.
**Subject:** The lineage of Yeshua traced past Israel to the first human and to God. The line runs through David's son **Natan** (2 Sam 5:14; cf. Zech 12:12), not Shelomoh (Solomon) as in Matthew 1:6 — the structural root of the two genealogies' divergence; converges with Ruth 4:18–22 / Matthew between David and Nachshon; continues the Shem line of Gen 11:10–26 (including the **LXX-only "Qenan"** of Gen 11:12–13, absent from the Hebrew Masoretic text); and the antediluvian line of Gen 5, ending "of Adam, of God" — Adam as son of God by creation (Gen 1:27; 5:1).
**Fulfillment status:** DEBATED
**Fulfillment notes:** A genealogy is **not a prophecy** — it makes no predictive claim and carries no citation formula. It is included here as **fulfillment-adjacent**: it underwrites the Davidic-descent claim of the annunciation (1:32–33; 2:4) and stages the universal horizon of 3:6 ("all flesh") by running the line **past Abraham to Adam and to God** — the decisive theological difference from Matthew, who descends from Abraham (Israel's covenant root) through Solomon. The DEBATED label reflects the unresolved cruxes the TT flags rather than harmonizes (Rule 13): (a) the Natan-vs.-Solomon split and the broad post-Davidic divergence from Matthew (proposed harmonizations — Mary's line via Eli; levirate; legal vs. biological descent — are all contested); (b) the "as was supposed" hedge over Joseph's paternity and whose line the list traces; (c) the LXX-only Qenan. The full table and cross-book see-stubs (to `genesis` for Adam → the patriarchs, to `matthew` for the David line) live in `luke/PEOPLE.md`. **[historical — POSSIBLE; the Adam-sonship climax — theological — PROBABLE]** → See Cross-Chapter Tracking (Gen 5 / Gen 11 / Ruth 4 → Luke 3:31–38).
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: Genealogies trace covenant lineage (Gen 5; 11; Ruth 4; 1 Chr); David's sons include both Natan and Solomon (2 Sam 5:14). The LXX "Qenan" reflects the Greek text-tradition, not the Hebrew. Adam as created by God (Gen 1:27; 5:1) is foundational, but "son of God" is not a lineage title in Jewish reading. The genealogy's christological framing is not accepted. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: Luke's ascending line to Adam and God is read as universalizing — Yeshua as the new Adam, savior of all humanity, not only of Israel — answering the "all flesh" of 3:6; the Natan-line and the divergence from Matthew are explained by various harmonizations (Mary's line; legal descent). [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: The Quran traces the prophets' lineage (Adam, Nuh, Ibrahim, etc.) and honors Isa within it, but does not endorse the "son of God" climax; Adam is God's creation and vicegerent, not "son" (Surah 3:59, "the likeness of Isa with God is as the likeness of Adam: He created him from dust"). [DOCUMENTED]
