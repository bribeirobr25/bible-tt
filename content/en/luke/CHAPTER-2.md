## READING GUIDE

**Main Text:** Primary translation — readable but faithful to Greek structure. **Notes:** Essential Greek features immediately below each verse.

**How the text is marked:**
- *Words in italics* — added for English grammar (not in the Greek text)
- {a:wind/spirit} — a word the Greek leaves open to two meanings, both kept
- @@"…"@@ — direct speech of God or the holy wind/spirit

Notes are tagged by type — **Critical · Lexical · Grammatical · Theological** — each shown with its colour (see the legend at the top of the Notes view).

**Luke 2 is the nativity chapter.** A census decree of Caesar Augustus, under Quirinius, brings Yosef and the pregnant Miryam from Natzeret to Beit-Lechem, the city of David (vv.1-5); there Yeshua is born and laid in a feeding-trough, "because there was no place for them in the *kataluma*" (vv.6-7). Messengers announce the birth to shepherds, and the heavenly host sings the Gloria (vv.8-20). On the eighth day the child is circumcised and named; he is presented in the temple at Yerushalayim, where Shimon receives him and sings the Nunc Dimittis, then speaks to Miryam (vv.21-35), and the prophetess Channah gives thanks (vv.36-38). The family returns to Natzeret and the child grows (vv.39-40). At twelve, the boy Yeshua remains in the temple and is found among the teachers; his words to his parents are the only saying recorded from his youth (vv.41-52). **Note on speech-marking (Rule 30):** the messenger and the heavenly host (the Gloria, vv.13-14) are *angelos*-class — their speech is NOT marked as divine. Shimon's and Channah's words are *human* — not marked. Only the boy Yeshua's saying (v.49) is divine-marked. The two canticles (Gloria, Nunc Dimittis) are *human/angelic* speech rendered as flowing prose. Proper names follow TT transliteration: Yeshua (not Jesus), Miryam (not Mary), Yosef (not Joseph), Beit-Lechem (not Bethlehem), Natzeret (not Nazareth), Yerushalayim (not Jerusalem), Shimon (not Simeon), Channah (not Anna).

## CHAPTER OVERVIEW

**What happens:** A decree of Caesar Augustus orders a census of the whole inhabited world, the first while Quirinius governed Syria. Everyone goes to be registered, each to his own town; so Joseph travels up from Nazareth in Galilee to Bethlehem in Judea, the city of David, because he is of David's house, together with Mary, who is pregnant. While they are there, she gives birth to her firstborn son, wraps him in strips of cloth, and lays him in a feeding-trough, because there is no place for them in the guest-room/inn. Shepherds are keeping watch over their flock by night when a messenger of the Lord stands before them in the glory of the Lord; he announces good news of a savior born in David's city, and gives them a sign — a baby wrapped in cloth, lying in a feeding-trough. Suddenly a multitude of the heavenly host praises God, singing the Gloria. The shepherds go to Bethlehem, find the child, report what was said, and return glorifying God; Mary keeps all these matters, pondering them in her heart. On the eighth day the child is circumcised and named Jesus. When the days of purification are complete, the parents bring him to the temple in Jerusalem to present him to the Lord and to offer the poor person's sacrifice. There the righteous Simeon, awaiting the consolation of Israel, takes the child and sings the Nunc Dimittis, then blesses the parents and tells Mary that her child is set for the falling and rising of many and that a sword will pass through her own soul. The prophetess Anna, very old, gives thanks and speaks of the child to all awaiting redemption. The family returns to Nazareth; the child grows and is filled with wisdom. Each year his parents go to Jerusalem for Passover; when he is twelve, he stays behind in the temple, and after three days of searching they find him among the teachers, hearing and questioning them. To his mother's reproach he answers that he must be in the things of his Father; they do not understand. He goes down with them and is submissive, and Mary keeps all these matters in her heart. The boy advances in wisdom and stature and favor.

**Key themes:** The collision of imperial power (Caesar Augustus, the census) with the hidden birth of the true king in David's lowly city; the firstborn laid in a feeding-trough with "no place in the guest-room" — the reversal motif made physical; the announcement of "good news… a savior… Messiah, Lord" to *shepherds*, the lowliest witnesses, recalling David the shepherd; the Gloria as the angelic praise that frames the birth (an *angelos*-class song, not divine speech); Mary "treasuring" and "pondering" (vv.19, 51); obedient observance of Torah (circumcision, purification, redemption of the firstborn, the poor person's offering, the Passover pilgrimage); Simeon and Anna as the faithful remnant of Israel awaiting "consolation" and "redemption"; the Nunc Dimittis as a "light for revelation to the nations" — salvation reaching beyond Israel; the prophecy of the sword and of the child "set for falling and rising"; the boy in the temple "in the things of his Father" — the first hint of a sonship that supersedes the family.

**Watch for:** *Katalyma* (v.7) — "guest-room" or "lodging/inn"; Luke uses a different word for a commercial inn (*pandocheion*, 10:34), and *katalyma* elsewhere means "guest-room" (22:11), so the rendering is genuinely open. The messenger and the host (vv.9-14) are *angelos*-class — NOT divine-marked. The Gloria (v.14) turns on the genitive *eudokias* ("of good will/favor") — "peace among people of *his* favor/good will," not the older "good will toward men" of a variant reading (*eudokia*, nominative); the case is a known crux. The Nunc Dimittis (vv.29-32) and Simeon's words to Mary (vv.34-35) are *human* — not marked. *Prōtotokos* (v.7), "firstborn," carries no necessary implication about later children but is theologically loaded. The boy Yeshua's words (v.49), *en tois tou patros mou*, are elliptical — "in my Father's house" or "about my Father's business/affairs" — and are the one divine-marked saying in the chapter (Rule 30). The two canticles are rendered as prose.

**Connection:** The chapter is steeped in HB precedent. Beit-Lechem as David's city and the birthplace of the ruler echoes Micah 5:1 (Eng. 5:2) and 1 Samuel 16. The shepherds recall David, shepherd-king (1 Sam 16:11). Simeon's "consolation of Israel" and "light to the nations" draw on Isaiah 40:1, 42:6, 49:6, 52:9-10. The presentation and redemption of the firstborn fulfill Exodus 13:2, 12-15 and Leviticus 12; the two-bird offering is the provision for the poor (Lev 12:8). Anna in the temple echoes the faithful widows and the prophetic women of Israel. The growth-formula of the child (vv.40, 52) follows Samuel (1 Sam 2:26) and Samson (Judg 13:24). The temple scene at twelve and "in the things of my Father" anticipates the sonship theme that runs through the Gospel.

## VERSE-BY-VERSE STUDY

### **Verse 1**

And it happened in those days *that* a decree went out from Caesar Augustus to register all the inhabited *world*.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE CENSUS AND IMPERIAL FRAMING**
> - **δόγμα παρὰ Καίσαρος Αὐγούστου ἀπογράφεσθαι πᾶσαν τὴν οἰκουμένην** (*dogma para Kaisaros Augoustou apographesthai pasan tēn oikoumenēn*) = "a decree from Caesar Augustus to register all the inhabited world." Luke locates the birth on the stage of world history; *oikoumenē* ("the inhabited *world*") is the rhetorical reach of Roman power. The contrast between Caesar's decree and the hidden birth of the true king is structural. → For the census, its dating problems, and Augustus, see Companion §A1.
>
> 🟢 **CAESAR AUGUSTUS — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Καῖσαρ Αὔγουστος** (*Kaisar Augoustos*) = Caesar Augustus (Gaius Octavius / Imperator Caesar Divi Filius Augustus, r. 27 BCE–14 CE). The TT keeps the familiar Latin-derived form; no Hebrew underlies it.
>
> 🔵 **"AND IT HAPPENED" — *EGENETO* (SEPTUAGINTALISM)**
> - **Ἐγένετο δέ** (*Egeneto de*) = "And it happened." The Septuagintal narrative formula continues from chapter 1. The TT preserves the formula and adds *that* in italics before the dependent clause.

---

### **Verse 2**

This registration *was the* first, *while* Quirinius was governing Syria.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — QUIRINIUS — THE DATING CRUX**
> - **αὕτη ἀπογραφὴ πρώτη ἐγένετο ἡγεμονεύοντος τῆς Συρίας Κυρηνίου** (*hautē apographē prōtē egeneto hēgemoneuontos tēs Syrias Kyrēniou*) = "this registration was first, while Quirinius was governing Syria." Quirinius's known census fell c. 6 CE — roughly a decade after the death of Herod the Great (4 BCE), under whom Luke 1:5 sets John's conception. **πρώτη** (*prōtē*, "first") is debated: "the first registration" vs. "before Quirinius governed" (a comparative sense). The TT renders the surface ("the first, while Quirinius governing") and flags the historical difficulty for review. → For the Quirinius problem and proposed solutions, see Companion §A1. **[historical — UNCERTAIN]**
>
> 🟢 **QUIRINIUS / SYRIA — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Κυρήνιος** (*Kyrēnios*) = Quirinius (Publius Sulpicius Quirinius). **Συρία** (*Syria*) = the Roman province of Syria. The TT keeps the familiar Latin-derived forms.

### **Verse 3**

And all were going to be registered, each to his own town.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"EACH TO HIS OWN TOWN" — *EIS TĒN HEAUTOU POLIN***
> - **ἐπορεύοντο πάντες ἀπογράφεσθαι, ἕκαστος εἰς τὴν ἑαυτοῦ πόλιν** (*eporeuonto pantes apographesthai, hekastos eis tēn heautou polin*) = "all were going to be registered, each to his own town." Whether a Roman census required return to an ancestral town is historically disputed; the TT renders the text and leaves the practice to the companion. The imperfect *eporeuonto* conveys the general movement.

### **Verse 4**

And Yosef (Joseph) also went up from the Galilee, from *the* town of Natzeret (Nazareth), into Yehudah (Judea), to *the* city of David which is called Beit-Lechem (Bethlehem), because he was of *the* house and family of David,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — BEIT-LECHEM, THE CITY OF DAVID (MIC 5:1)**
> - **εἰς πόλιν Δαυὶδ ἥτις καλεῖται Βηθλέεμ** (*eis polin Dauid hētis kaleitai Bēthleem*) = "to *the* city of David which is called Bethlehem." Beit-Lechem is David's home town (1 Sam 16:1, 17:12) and, in Micah 5:1 (Eng. 5:2), the birthplace of the coming ruler. Luke's "city of David" here is Bethlehem (contrast the usual "city of David" = the citadel of Jerusalem) — the Davidic descent of Joseph (and so of Yeshua) is the engine of the journey. → For the Bethlehem/Micah background, see the PROPHECY companion. **[theological — PROBABLE]**
>
> 🟢 **TT TRANSLITERATIONS — FIRST OCCURRENCES**
> - **Ἰωσήφ** (*Iōsēph*) = Yosef (Hebrew: **Yosef**, "he adds"). Familiar: Joseph.
> - **Ναζαρέθ** (*Nazareth*) = Natzeret. Familiar: Nazareth.
> - **Βηθλέεμ** (*Bēthleem*) = Beit-Lechem (Hebrew: **Beit-Lechem**, "house of bread"). Familiar: Bethlehem.
> - **Ἰουδαία** (*Ioudaia*) = Yehudah. Familiar: Judea. The TT renders the region's name consistently with chapter 1.

### **Verse 7**

and she bore her firstborn son, and wrapped him in strips of cloth and laid him in a {a:feeding-trough/manger}, because there was no place for them in the {a:guest-room/inn}.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — *KATALYMA* ("GUEST-ROOM / INN") — TRANSLATION CRUX**
> - **οὐκ ἦν αὐτοῖς τόπος ἐν τῷ καταλύματι** (*ouk ēn autois topos en tō katalymati*) = "there was no place for them in the *katalyma*." *Katalyma* is a general word for "lodging place," and elsewhere in Luke it means "guest-room" (22:11, the room for the Passover meal). When Luke means a commercial inn he uses a different word, *pandocheion* (10:34). So "guest-room" (an upper or spare room of a house, already full) is a strong reading, and "inn/lodging" the traditional one — both are kept. The TT marks {a:guest-room/inn} and flags this as a known crux for review. → For *katalyma* vs. *pandocheion* and the birth setting, see Companion §D1. **[lexical — PROBABLE]**
>
> 🟡 **"FIRSTBORN SON" — *PRŌTOTOKOS***
> - **τὸν υἱὸν αὐτῆς τὸν πρωτότοκον** (*ton huion autēs ton prōtotokon*) = "her firstborn son." *Prōtotokos* is the legal-cultic term for the firstborn (Ex 13:2), relevant to the redemption rite of vv.22-24. It states birth order without itself implying later children; the TT renders "firstborn" and does not adjudicate the question. **[lexical — PROBABLE]**
>
> 🔵 **"FEEDING-TROUGH / MANGER" — *PHATNĒ***
> - **ἐν φάτνῃ** (*en phatnē*) = "in a *phatnē*." *Phatnē* is an animal's feeding-trough (so "manger," the older English via Latin *manducare*); it can also denote a stall/feeding-place. The TT marks {a:feeding-trough/manger} to keep the plain object visible behind the traditional "manger." **"Strips of cloth"** renders *esparganōsen* (*sparganoō*, "to wrap in swaddling bands") — ordinary infant care, not a special sign in itself.

### **Verse 8**

And *there* were shepherds in the same region, living *in the* fields and keeping *the* watches of *the* night over their flock.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"SHEPHERDS… KEEPING WATCH" — THE LOWLY WITNESSES (DAVID ECHO)**
> - **ποιμένες… ἀγραυλοῦντες καὶ φυλάσσοντες φυλακὰς τῆς νυκτός** (*poimenes… agraulountes kai phylassontes phylakas tēs nyktos*) = "shepherds living in the fields and keeping the watches of the night." Shepherds were a lowly, sometimes suspect, occupation; that the birth is announced to *them* first continues the reversal theme and recalls David the shepherd of Bethlehem (1 Sam 16:11). The TT adds *in the* and *the* in italics for the elliptical phrases.

### **Verse 9**

And a messenger of the Lord stood before them, and *the* glory of the Lord shone around them, and they feared *with* a great fear.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — *ANGELOS KYRIOU* AND *DOXA KYRIOU* (RULE 30 + OPTION C)**
> - **ἄγγελος κυρίου ἐπέστη… καὶ δόξα κυρίου περιέλαμψεν** (*angelos kyriou epestē… kai doxa kyriou perielampsen*) = "a messenger of the Lord stood by… and *the* glory of the Lord shone around." *Angelos kyriou* renders the LXX *malakh YHWH* (as at 1:11); *doxa kyriou* renders the *kavod YHWH*, the visible glory-presence (Ex 24:16-17 LXX). Per Option C, *kyriou* = "the Lord," referent YHWH (Probable). **Important (Rule 30):** the messenger is *angelos*, not God — his speech (vv.10-12) is NOT divine-marked. → For the *malakh*/*kavod* background, see Companion §A6.
>
> 🔵 **"FEARED WITH A GREAT FEAR" — *EPHOBĒTHĒSAN PHOBON MEGAN***
> - **ἐφοβήθησαν φόβον μέγαν** (*ephobēthēsan phobon megan*) = "they feared a great fear" — a Semitic cognate-accusative (cf. Hebrew *yare'u yir'ah gedolah*). The TT adds *with* in italics and keeps the redoubled construction's force.

### **Verse 10**

And the messenger said to them, "Do not be afraid; for see, I bring you good news of a great joy which will be for all the people,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — MESSENGER-SPEECH NOT DIVINE-MARKED (RULE 30)**
> - The messenger's announcement (vv.10-12) is rendered in ordinary quotation marks, NOT the @@…@@ divine-speech markers. Per the editorial decision (luke.md L-001) and the Matthew/Mark precedent, an *angelos* is a messenger, not God; only the direct speech of God or the holy wind/spirit is marked. The TT does not collapse messenger-speech into divine speech.
>
> 🟢 **"I BRING GOOD NEWS… GREAT JOY" — *EUANGELIZOMAI… CHARAN MEGALĒN***
> - **εὐαγγελίζομαι ὑμῖν χαρὰν μεγάλην** (*euangelizomai hymin charan megalēn*) = "I bring you good news of a great joy." The verb (cognate with *euangelion*) and the "joy" keyword tie back to 1:14, 1:44, 1:47. **παντὶ τῷ λαῷ** (*panti tō laō*, "for all the people") — *laos* is Israel as God's people; the wider reach to "the nations" surfaces in Simeon's song (v.32).

### **Verse 11**

because today a savior was born to you in *the* city of David, who is Messiah, Lord.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "A SAVIOR… MESSIAH, LORD" — *SŌTĒR… CHRISTOS KYRIOS***
> - **ἐτέχθη ὑμῖν σήμερον σωτὴρ ὅς ἐστιν Χριστὸς κύριος** (*etechthē hymin sēmeron sōtēr hos estin Christos kyrios*) = "today a savior was born to you, who is Messiah, Lord." The unusual juxtaposition **Χριστὸς κύριος** (*Christos kyrios*, "Messiah, Lord" / "Messiah Lord") is rare and grammatically debated (apposition "Messiah, Lord"; or "the Lord's Messiah," reading a textual/grammatical variant *Christos kyriou*, as at v.26). The TT renders the NA28 nominatives "Messiah, Lord" and notes the crux. *Sōtēr* ("savior") and these titles, applied to a baby, stand against the imperial honorifics of Augustus (*sōtēr*, "lord"). **Χριστός** = "Anointed One" = Hebrew **Mashiach**; the TT renders "Messiah." → see Glossary, *Christos*; Companion §D2. **[grammatical-theological — POSSIBLE]**

### **Verse 12**

### **Verse 14**

"Glory in *the* highest to God, and on earth peace among people of *his* favor."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE GLORIA: *EUDOKIAS* (GENITIVE) — TRANSLATION CRUX**
> - **δόξα ἐν ὑψίστοις θεῷ καὶ ἐπὶ γῆς εἰρήνη ἐν ἀνθρώποις εὐδοκίας** (*doxa en hypsistois theō kai epi gēs eirēnē en anthrōpois eudokias*) = "Glory in *the* highest to God, and on earth peace among people of good will/favor." NA28 reads the **genitive εὐδοκίας** (*eudokias*) — "peace among people *of (his) favor/good pleasure*," i.e., those on whom God's favor rests (a Semitic construction, cf. Qumran "sons of *(God's)* good pleasure"). The older "good will toward men" rests on a variant **nominative εὐδοκία** (*eudokia*). The TT follows the genitive and renders "people of *his* favor," supplying *his* in italics, and flags the case as a known crux. Note also that this is a two-line couplet ("glory… peace"), not three. → For *eudokia(s)* and the textual decision, see Companion §D3. **[text-critical / lexical — PROBABLE]**
>
> 🟢 **RULE 30 — THE GLORIA NOT DIVINE-MARKED; RENDERED AS PROSE**
> - The Gloria is the song of the *heavenly host* (v.13), an *angelos*-class speaker — therefore NOT marked with @@…@@. As a canticle it is authored as flowing prose (luke.md L-002), no line breaks. *Hypsistois* ("the highest *places*") echoes *hypsistos* ("Most High") of chapter 1.

> **Notes**

> 🟡 **"THIS MATTER… WHICH THE LORD MADE KNOWN" — *RHĒMA* (OPTION C)**

### **Verse 43**

and completed the days, while they were returning, the boy Yeshua (Jesus) stayed behind in Yerushalayim (Jerusalem), and his parents did not know *it*.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"THE BOY STAYED BEHIND" — *HYPEMEINEN HO PAIS***
> - **ὑπέμεινεν Ἰησοῦς ὁ παῖς ἐν Ἰερουσαλήμ** (*hypemeinen Iēsous ho pais en Ierousalēm*) = "Jesus the boy stayed behind in Jerusalem." *Pais* = "boy / child / servant"; here "boy." The parents' unawareness sets up the search. The TT adds *it* in italics for the implied object.

---

### **Verse 44**

And thinking *that* he was in the traveling-company, they went a day's journey and were searching for him among the relatives and the acquaintances,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"IN THE TRAVELING-COMPANY" — *EN TĒ SYNODIA***
> - **νομίσαντες αὐτὸν εἶναι ἐν τῇ συνοδίᾳ** (*nomisantes auton einai en tē synodia*) = "thinking he was in the traveling-company." *Synodia* = the caravan/group traveling together (whence "synod"). The large, mingled pilgrim group makes the oversight plausible. The TT adds *that* in italics.

---

### **Verse 45**

and not finding *him*, they returned to Jerusalem searching for him.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"RETURNED… SEARCHING" — *ANEZĒTOUNTES***
> - **ὑπέστρεψαν εἰς Ἰερουσαλὴμ ἀναζητοῦντες αὐτόν** (*hypestrepsan eis Ierousalēm anazētountes auton*) = "they returned to Jerusalem searching for him." The doubled "searching" (vv.44, 45, 48) builds the anxiety the mother voices in v.48. The TT adds *him* in italics.

---

### **Verse 46**

And it happened, after three days, *that* they found him in the temple, sitting in *the* midst of the teachers, both hearing them and questioning them.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"AFTER THREE DAYS… AMONG THE TEACHERS" — *EN MESŌ TŌN DIDASKALŌN***
> - **μετὰ ἡμέρας τρεῖς εὗρον αὐτὸν ἐν τῷ ἱερῷ καθεζόμενον ἐν μέσῳ τῶν διδασκάλων** (*meta hēmeras treis heuron auton en tō hierō kathezomenon en mesō tōn didaskalōn*) = "after three days they found him in the temple, sitting in *the* midst of the teachers." "After three days" is the search-time (some hear an anticipatory echo of later "three days"; the TT does not impose it). The boy is a learner — "hearing and questioning" — not yet teaching. The TT adds *the* in italics.

---

### **Verse 47**

And all who heard him were astonished at his understanding and his answers.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"ASTONISHED AT HIS UNDERSTANDING" — *EXISTANTO… SYNESEI***
> - **ἐξίσταντο… ἐπὶ τῇ συνέσει καὶ ταῖς ἀποκρίσεσιν αὐτοῦ** (*existanto… epi tē synesei kai tais apokrisesin autou*) = "they were astonished at his understanding and his answers." *Existēmi* = "to be amazed, beside oneself." The wonder-reaction again (cf. v.18, 33). His *synesis* ("understanding, insight") ties to the wisdom theme (vv.40, 52).

---

### **Verse 48**

And seeing him, they were astounded, and his mother said to him, "Child, why did you do thus to us? See, your father and I were searching for you, distressed."

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"YOUR FATHER AND I… DISTRESSED" — *ODYNŌMENOI***
> - **ὁ πατήρ σου κἀγὼ ὀδυνώμενοι ἐζητοῦμέν σε** (*ho patēr sou kagō odynōmenoi ezētoumen se*) = "your father and I were searching for you, distressed." Mary calls Joseph "your father" plainly (cf. v.33) — the wording the boy's reply (v.49) implicitly counters with "my Father." *Odynaōmai* = "to be in anguish/pain." The TT keeps the unsmoothed "your father and I." **[lexical — PROBABLE]**

---

### **Verse 49**

And he said to them, @@"Why *is it* that you were searching for me? Did you not know that I must be in the *things* of my Father?"@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — *EN TOIS TOU PATROS MOU* — THE ONE DIVINE-MARKED SAYING (RULE 30) + CRUX**
> - **οὐκ ᾔδειτε ὅτι ἐν τοῖς τοῦ πατρός μου δεῖ εἶναί με;** (*ouk ēdeite hoti en tois tou patros mou dei einai me?*) = "did you not know that I must be in the *things* of my Father?" The phrase **ἐν τοῖς τοῦ πατρός μου** is elliptical — a definite article with no noun — so it can mean "in my Father's **house**" (supply *oikō*/*oikia*) or "in my Father's **affairs/business**" (supply *pragmasin*) or "among my Father's **people**." The TT renders the bare "in the *things* of my Father" with *things* in italics, keeping the ellipsis visible, and flags this as a known crux. The saying is the first recorded speech of Yeshua and the first assertion of a divine sonship that quietly overrides "your father" (v.48). **Per Rule 30, the direct speech of Yeshua is divine-marked with @@…@@** — the sole such span in the chapter. **δεῖ** (*dei*, "it is necessary / I must") is Luke's keyword of divine necessity. → For *en tois tou patros mou* and the *dei* of necessity, see Companion §D5. **[lexical-grammatical — PROBABLE]**

---

### **Verse 50**

And they did not understand the word that he spoke to them.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"DID NOT UNDERSTAND THE WORD" — *OU SYNĒKAN TO RHĒMA***
> - **αὐτοὶ οὐ συνῆκαν τὸ ῥῆμα** (*autoi ou synēkan to rhēma*) = "they did not understand the word." Even Mary and Joseph do not grasp the saying — the narrative resists making them perfect comprehenders. *Rhēma* again "word/matter." This non-understanding stands beside Mary's "keeping" (v.51): she holds what she cannot yet interpret.

---

### **Verse 51**

And he went down with them and came to Natzeret (Nazareth), and was submissive to them. And his mother kept all these matters in her heart.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"WAS SUBMISSIVE… KEPT ALL THESE MATTERS" — *HYPOTASSOMENOS… DIETĒREI***
> - **ἦν ὑποτασσόμενος αὐτοῖς… ἡ μήτηρ αὐτοῦ διετήρει πάντα τὰ ῥήματα** (*ēn hypotassomenos autois… hē mētēr autou dietērei panta ta rhēmata*) = "he was submissive to them… his mother kept all these matters." After the assertion of v.49, the boy returns to ordinary filial submission — the TT keeps both in tension without resolving. *Diatēreō* ("kept, treasured") echoes v.19 (*syntēreō*); *rhēmata* = "matters/words." The TT renders the inward keeping.

---

### **Verse 52**

And Yeshua (Jesus) advanced in wisdom and stature and favor with God and people.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE GROWTH-FORMULA (1 SAM 2:26)**
> - **Ἰησοῦς προέκοπτεν σοφίᾳ καὶ ἡλικίᾳ καὶ χάριτι παρὰ θεῷ καὶ ἀνθρώποις** (*Iēsous proekopten sophia kai hēlikia kai chariti para theō kai anthrōpois*) = "Jesus advanced in wisdom and stature and favor with God and people." Closely echoes 1 Sam 2:26 LXX of Shemu'el ("Samuel advanced… and was in favor with the Lord and with people"). **ἡλικία** (*hēlikia*) = "stature" or "age/maturity" (both possible); *charis* = "favor/grace." The verse closes the infancy narrative and parallels v.40. The TT keeps "stature" with the age-sense noted. **[lexical — PROBABLE]**

## CROSS-CHAPTER TRACKING (HB -> Luke)

**1 Sam 16 / Mic 5:1 -> Luke 2:4-11 — Beit-Lechem (Bethlehem), David, and the shepherds:**

| Element | 1 Samuel 16 / Micah 5 | Luke 2 |
|---------|------------------------|--------|
| Bethlehem | David anointed at Bethlehem (1 Sam 16:1, 13) | birth in "the city of David which is Bethlehem" (2:4, 11) |
| Ruler from Bethlehem | "from you will come one to rule Israel" (Mic 5:1) | "a savior… Messiah, Lord… in the city of David" (2:11) |
| Shepherd | David taken "from following the flock" (1 Sam 16:11) | shepherds the first to hear, by the flock at night (2:8) |

The nativity binds the birthplace, the Davidic descent, and the shepherd witnesses to the Bethlehem prophecy and to David, Israel's shepherd-king.

**Ex 13:2, 12-15 / Lev 12 -> Luke 2:22-24 — the firstborn and the poor person's offering:**

| Element | Exodus / Leviticus | Luke 2 |
|---------|---------------------|--------|
| Firstborn consecrated | "every male opening the womb… holy to YHWH" (Ex 13:2, 12) | quoted at 2:23 |
| Mother's purification | rite after birth (Lev 12:2-8) | "the days of their purification" (2:22) |
| Poor person's offering | "two turtledoves or two pigeons" if she cannot afford a lamb (Lev 12:8) | "a pair of turtledoves or two young pigeons" (2:24) |

The presentation observes Torah exactly and, through the bird-offering, quietly marks the family's poverty.

**Isa 40:1-5 / 42:6 / 49:6 / 52:9-10 -> Luke 2:25-38 — consolation, salvation seen, light to the nations:**

| Element | Isaiah | Luke 2 (Simeon / Anna) |
|---------|--------|------------------------|
| Consolation | "Comfort, comfort my people" (40:1) | "awaiting the consolation of Israel" (2:25) |
| Salvation seen | "all flesh will see the salvation of God" (40:5; 52:10) | "my eyes saw your salvation" (2:30) |
| Light to the nations | "a light of the nations" (42:6; 49:6) | "a light for revelation of the nations" (2:32) |
| Redemption of Zion | "the Lord has redeemed Jerusalem" (52:9) | "awaiting the redemption of Jerusalem" (2:38) |

Simeon and Anna voice the Isaianic hope of comfort and salvation, now stretched explicitly to the nations alongside Israel.

**1 Sam 2:26 / Judg 13:24 -> Luke 2:40, 52 — the growth-formula:**

| Element | 1 Samuel / Judges | Luke 2 |
|---------|--------------------|--------|
| Child grew | "Samuel grew… in favor with YHWH and with people" (1 Sam 2:26) | "advanced in wisdom and stature and favor with God and people" (2:52) |
| Strengthened | "the child grew, and YHWH blessed him" (Judg 13:24, Samson) | "the child grew and was strengthened, filled with wisdom" (2:40) |

The two summary verses (2:40, 52) cast the boy in the mold of Shemu'el and Shimshon — children of promise who grow under divine favor.
