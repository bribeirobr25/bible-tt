## READING GUIDE

**Main Text:** Primary translation — readable but faithful to Greek structure. **Notes:** Essential Greek features immediately below each verse.

**How the text is marked:**
- *Words in italics* — added for English grammar (not in the Greek text)
- {a:wind/spirit} — a word the Greek leaves open to two meanings, both kept
- @@"…"@@ — direct speech of God or the holy wind/spirit

Notes are tagged by type — **Critical · Lexical · Grammatical · Theological** — each shown with its colour (see the legend at the top of the Notes view).

**This chapter opens the public ministry.** Luke 3 dates the appearance of Yochanan (John) by a sixfold synchronism of rulers (vv.1-2) — the most precise chronological frame in the Gospels — then narrates his wilderness proclamation of an immersion of repentance (vv.3-6), framed by a long quotation of Yesha'yahu (Isaiah) 40:3-5. His preaching to the crowds, tax-collectors, and soldiers (vv.7-14), his pointing ahead to a "stronger one" (vv.15-17), and his imprisonment by Herodes (Herod) (vv.18-20) follow. Yeshua (Jesus) is immersed and praying when the skies open, the {a:wind/spirit} descends, and a voice comes from heaven (vv.21-22). The chapter closes with the genealogy (vv.23-38), ascending from Yeshua to Adam to God — ~76 names. **Note on speech-marking (Rule 30):** Yochanan's preaching is *human* prophetic speech and is NOT marked as divine speech; only the **voice from heaven** at 3:22 (the Bat-Qol) is God's direct speech and is marked with `@@…@@`. Proper names follow TT transliteration: Yochanan (not John), Yeshua (not Jesus), Yesha'yahu (not Isaiah), Herodes (not Herod).

## CHAPTER OVERVIEW

**What happens:** A precise dating formula places the events in the fifteenth year of Tiberius Caesar, naming Pontius Pilate, the tetrarchs Herod, Philip, and Lysanias, and the high priests Annas and Caiaphas. The word of God comes to John, son of Zechariah, in the wilderness. He proclaims an immersion of repentance for the release of sins throughout the Jordan region, and the narrator frames his ministry with a long quotation of Isaiah 40:3-5 ("a voice crying in the wilderness… all flesh will see the salvation of God"). John addresses the crowds bluntly — "offspring of vipers" — warning that descent from Abraham counts for nothing, that the axe lies at the root of the trees, and that they must bear fruit worthy of repentance. To specific groups — crowds, tax-collectors, soldiers — he gives concrete ethical instruction: share clothing and food, collect no more than is appointed, extort no one, be content with wages. When the people wonder whether he is the anointed one, John points beyond himself to a stronger one whose sandal-strap he is not worthy to loosen, who will immerse in holy wind/spirit and fire, winnowing the threshing floor. Herod the tetrarch, rebuked by John over Herodias and his other evils, adds to them by shutting John in prison. Jesus, having been immersed and praying, sees the skies opened, the wind/spirit descend in bodily form like a dove, and hears a voice from heaven: "You are my son, the beloved; in you I am well pleased." The chapter closes with the genealogy: Jesus, supposed son of Joseph, traced upward through David and Abraham to Adam, "son of God."

**Key themes:** The sixfold political-religious synchronism dating the ministry to world history; "the word of God came upon John" — the prophetic-call formula (Jer 1:1-2, Hos 1:1); the wilderness as the place of renewal; immersion (*baptisma*) of repentance "for the release of sins"; the Isaiah 40 "voice in the wilderness" and the universal horizon "all flesh will see the salvation of God" (Luke extends the quotation further than Matthew or Mark); ethical repentance with concrete social demands; the relativizing of Abrahamic descent ("God can raise children to Abraham from these stones"); the "stronger one" and the contrast of water-immersion with wind/spirit-and-fire; the baptism scene with the opened skies, the descending wind/spirit, and the voice from heaven; the ascending genealogy reaching past Abraham to Adam, "son of God" — universalizing the lineage beyond Israel.

**Watch for:** The dating formula (vv.1-2) is unique to Luke and historically dense — the "fifteenth year of Tiberius" admits more than one reckoning. "The word of God came upon John" (*egeneto rhēma theou epi Iōannēn*) is the Septuagintal prophetic-call idiom, not narrated as divine direct speech — John's subsequent preaching is *human* prophecy and is NOT divine-marked (Rule 30). At 3:22, the **voice from heaven** is God's direct speech and IS marked with @@…@@ (cf. Mark 1:11, Matt 3:17); a notable Western variant reads Ps 2:7 ("today I have begotten you"). At 3:23, "as was supposed" (*hōs enomizeto*) qualifies Joseph's paternity — the TT preserves the hedge. The genealogy (vv.23-38) ascends to Adam and differs sharply from Matthew's descending list (the divergence at the Joseph line is a known crux — flagged for review). The full genealogy table and cross-book see-stubs live in `luke/PEOPLE.md`.

**Connection:** John's wilderness call stands in the line of the prophetic commissions (Jeremiah, Hosea); the Isaiah 40 quotation roots the whole scene in the return-from-exile proclamation of comfort. The "offspring of vipers" and the axe-at-the-root imagery draw on prophetic judgment oracles. The baptism voice fuses Psalm 2:7 (royal sonship) and Isaiah 42:1 (the chosen servant in whom God delights). The ascending genealogy answers Matthew's descending one: where Matthew roots Jesus in Abraham and David (Israel's covenant), Luke runs the line back through Adam to God, opening the lineage to all humanity — fitting the universal "all flesh will see the salvation of God" of v.6.

## VERSE-BY-VERSE STUDY

### **Verse 1**

Now in *the* fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius Caesar, *while* Pontius Pilate was governing Yehudah (Judea), and Herodes (Herod) was tetrarch of the Galilee, and Philippos (Philip) his brother was tetrarch of the region of Ituraea and Trachonitis, and Lysanias was tetrarch of Abilene,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE SIXFOLD SYNCHRONISM (vv.1-2)**
> - **Ἐν ἔτει δὲ πεντεκαιδεκάτῳ τῆς ἡγεμονίας Τιβερίου Καίσαρος** (*En etei de pentekaidekatō tēs hēgemonias Tiberiou Kaisaros*) = "Now in *the* fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius Caesar." Luke dates the ministry by a sixfold list of rulers (one emperor, one prefect, three tetrarchs, the high priesthood) — the most elaborate chronological frame in the Gospels, modeled on the dating formulae of the HB prophets (cf. Jer 1:1-3, Isa 6:1, Hag 1:1). It anchors the gospel in datable world history. → For the chronology and the disputed reckoning of the "fifteenth year," see Companion §A1.
>
> 🟢 **TT TRANSLITERATIONS — FIRST OCCURRENCES**
> - **Ἡρῴδης** (*Hērōdēs*) = Herodes. Familiar: Herod. Here Herod Antipas, tetrarch of the Galilee (son of Herod the Great).
> - **Φίλιππος** (*Philippos*) = Philippos. Familiar: Philip. Herod Philip, tetrarch of Ituraea and Trachonitis.
> - Tiberius Caesar, Pontius Pilate, and Lysanias are rendered in their familiar Latin/Greek forms (no Hebrew underlay).
>
> 🔵 **"GOVERNING" / "TETRARCH" — *HĒGEMONEUONTOS / TETRAARCHOUNTOS***
> - **ἡγεμονεύοντος Ποντίου Πιλάτου** (*hēgemoneuontos Pontiou Pilatou*) = "*while* Pontius Pilate was governing." *Hēgemōn* = "governor/leader" (Pilate was prefect of Judea, 26-36 CE). **τετρααρχοῦντος** (*tetraarchountos*) = "being tetrarch" (lit. "ruler of a fourth"), the title of a minor client ruler under Rome.

### **Verse 2**

in *the* high priesthood of Chanan (Annas) and Qayafa (Caiaphas), *the* word of God came upon Yochanan (John) the son of Zecharyah (Zechariah) in the wilderness.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "THE WORD OF GOD CAME UPON JOHN" — PROPHETIC-CALL FORMULA**
> - **ἐγένετο ῥῆμα θεοῦ ἐπὶ Ἰωάννην** (*egeneto rhēma theou epi Iōannēn*) = "*the* word of God came upon John." This is the Septuagintal prophetic-commission idiom (cf. Jer 1:1-2; Hos 1:1; Joel 1:1 LXX, "the word of YHWH that came to…"). **ῥῆμα** (*rhēma*) = "word, utterance, matter" (Semitic *davar*). **Important (Rule 30):** this is *narrated* commissioning, not God's quoted direct speech; John's subsequent preaching (vv.7-17) is *human* prophetic speech and is therefore NOT marked with the @@…@@ divine-speech markers. → For the prophetic-call formula and John as prophet, see Companion §A1.
>
> 🟢 **TT TRANSLITERATIONS**
> - **Ἅννας** (*Hannas*) = Chanan (Hebrew: **Chanan/Chananyah**, "YHWH has shown favor"). Familiar: Annas.
> - **Καϊάφας** (*Kaiaphas*) = Qayafa (Aramaic). Familiar: Caiaphas. Annas was deposed high priest; Caiaphas the serving one — Luke names both, reflecting Annas's continuing influence.
> - **Ἰωάννης** (*Iōannēs*) = Yochanan (Hebrew: **Yochanan**, "YHWH is gracious"). Familiar: John. Son of the priest Zecharyah of Luke 1.

### **Verse 3**

And he went into all the region around the Yarden (Jordan), proclaiming an immersion of repentance for *the* release of sins,

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"IMMERSION OF REPENTANCE FOR THE RELEASE OF SINS" — *BAPTISMA METANOIAS***
> - **βάπτισμα μετανοίας εἰς ἄφεσιν ἁμαρτιῶν** (*baptisma metanoias eis aphesin hamartiōn*) = "an immersion of repentance for *the* release of sins." **βάπτισμα** (*baptisma*) = "dipping, immersion, washing" — the TT renders "immersion" (per locked glossary), keeping the physical act rather than the loanword "baptism." **μετάνοια** (*metanoia*) = "change of mind, turning, repentance." **ἄφεσις** (*aphesis*) = "release, letting-go, forgiveness" (cf. 1:77). The phrase echoes the Benedictus (1:77).
>
> 🟢 **YARDEN — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Ἰορδάνης** (*Iordanēs*) = Yarden (Hebrew: **Yarden**, "*the* descender"). Familiar: Jordan. The river's region (*perichōros*) is the setting of John's whole ministry.

### **Verse 4**

as it is written in *the* book of *the* words of Yesha'yahu (Isaiah) the prophet: "A voice of *one* crying in the wilderness: 'Prepare the way of the Lord, make his paths straight.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — ISAIAH 40:3-5 QUOTATION (vv.4-6)**
> - **φωνὴ βοῶντος ἐν τῇ ἐρήμῳ** (*phōnē boōntos en tē erēmō*) = "a voice of *one* crying in the wilderness." The quotation is Isaiah 40:3-5 LXX. The Hebrew accents read "a voice crying: 'In the wilderness prepare…'"; the LXX (which Luke follows) attaches "in the wilderness" to the voice. The TT follows the Greek word-order Luke uses. Per Option C, *kyriou* in the quotation renders YHWH (the original Isaiah text reads "the way of YHWH"). Referent of *kyrios*: YHWH (Probable); the Gospel fuses the preparation toward Yeshua. → For the Isaiah 40 quotation and its return-from-exile setting, see Companion §A6 and the PROPHECY companion.
>
> 🟢 **YESHA'YAHU — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Ἠσαΐας** (*Ēsaias*) = Yesha'yahu (Hebrew: **Yesha'yahu**, "YHWH is salvation"). Familiar: Isaiah.

### **Verse 5**

Every ravine will be filled and every mountain and hill will be made low, and the crooked *places* will become straight and the rough *ways* into smooth roads;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"RAVINE… MOUNTAIN… CROOKED… ROUGH" — LEVELLING IMAGERY**
> - **πᾶσα φάραγξ πληρωθήσεται** (*pasa pharanx plērōthēsetai*) = "every ravine will be filled." The road-building imagery (filling valleys, lowering hills) pictures preparing a highway for a royal procession — the return of YHWH leading the exiles home (Isa 40). The TT adds *places* and *ways* in italics for the substantival adjectives ("the crooked *things*," "the rough *things*").

---

### **Verse 6**

and all flesh will see the salvation of God.'"

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "ALL FLESH WILL SEE THE SALVATION OF GOD" — LUKE'S EXTENSION**
> - **ὄψεται πᾶσα σὰρξ τὸ σωτήριον τοῦ θεοῦ** (*opsetai pasa sarx to sōtērion tou theou*) = "all flesh will see the salvation of God." Luke extends the Isaiah quotation to 40:5 — further than Matthew (3:3) or Mark (1:3), which stop at 40:3. The universal horizon ("all flesh," **πᾶσα σάρξ**, Semitic *kol basar*) suits Luke's reach beyond Israel and anticipates the genealogy's ascent to Adam (v.38). **σωτήριον** (*sōtērion*) = "the saving / means of salvation" (neuter), cognate with *sōtēria* in the canticles (1:69, 71, 77; cf. 2:30). → For Luke's universalizing redaction of the Isaiah citation, see Companion §A6.

### **Verse 7**

So he was saying to the crowds going out to be immersed by him, "Offspring of vipers, who warned you to flee from the coming wrath?

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"OFFSPRING OF VIPERS" — *GENNĒMATA ECHIDNŌN***
> - **γεννήματα ἐχιδνῶν** (*gennēmata echidnōn*) = "offspring/brood of vipers." A prophetic invective (cf. the serpent imagery of judgment). John addresses the *crowds* generally (Luke), where Matthew 3:7 directs it at Pharisees and Sadducees. **Rule 30:** this is John's *human* prophetic speech — not divine-marked. "The coming wrath" (*tēs mellousēs orgēs*) = the eschatological judgment.

### **Verse 8**

Therefore bear fruits worthy of repentance, and do not begin to say among yourselves, 'We have Avraham (Abraham) *as* father.' For I say to you that God is able from these stones to raise up children to Abraham.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — DESCENT FROM AVRAHAM (ABRAHAM) RELATIVIZED**
> - **μὴ ἄρξησθε λέγειν ἐν ἑαυτοῖς· πατέρα ἔχομεν τὸν Ἀβραάμ** (*mē arxēsthe legein en heautois: patera echomen ton Abraam*) = "do not begin to say among yourselves, 'We have Abraham *as* father.'" John denies that physical descent from Avraham secures standing — "God is able from these stones (*lithōn*) to raise up children to Abraham." There may be a wordplay in the Aramaic substrate between "stones" (*avnayya*) and "children/sons" (*benayya*). The TT keeps the literal "stones." → For the Abrahamic-descent polemic and the possible *banim/avanim* wordplay, see Companion §A3.
>
> 🟢 **AVRAHAM — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Ἀβραάμ** (*Abraam*) = Avraham (Hebrew: **Avraham**, "father of a multitude," Gen 17:5). Familiar: Abraham.

### **Verse 9**

And already the axe is laid at the root of the trees; therefore every tree not producing good fruit is cut down and thrown into *the* fire."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"THE AXE AT THE ROOT" — *HĒ AXINĒ PROS TĒN RHIZAN***
> - **ἤδη δὲ καὶ ἡ ἀξίνη πρὸς τὴν ῥίζαν τῶν δένδρων κεῖται** (*ēdē de kai hē axinē pros tēn rhizan tōn dendrōn keitai*) = "already the axe is laid at the root of the trees." The present "is laid/lies" (*keitai*) conveys imminence — judgment is poised, not merely future. The fruit-tree-and-fire imagery is prophetic judgment language (cf. Isa 10:33-34; Jer 11:16). The TT adds *the* before "fire" in italics (anarthrous *pyr*).

### **Verse 10**

And the crowds were asking him, saying, "What then should we do?"

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"WHAT SHOULD WE DO?" — *TI POIĒSŌMEN***
> - **τί οὖν ποιήσωμεν** (*ti oun poiēsōmen*) = "what then should we do?" The deliberative subjunctive recurs three times (vv.10, 12, 14), structuring John's ethical instruction by group. The question reappears at Pentecost (Acts 2:37) — a Lukan linking motif.

### **Verse 11**

And answering he said to them, "Let the *one* having two tunics share with the *one* having none, and let the *one* having food do likewise."

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"TWO TUNICS… SHARE" — CONCRETE REPENTANCE**
> - **ὁ ἔχων δύο χιτῶνας μεταδότω** (*ho echōn dyo chitōnas metadotō*) = "let the *one* having two tunics share." *Chitōn* = the inner garment/tunic. John's repentance is not ritual but social: redistribution to those in need. The instruction to crowds, tax-collectors (v.13), and soldiers (v.14) is distinctive to Luke and fits his emphasis on wealth and the poor.

---

### **Verse 12**

And tax-collectors also came to be immersed, and they said to him, "Teacher, what should we do?"

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"TAX-COLLECTORS" — *TELŌNAI***
> - **τελῶναι** (*telōnai*) = "tax/toll-collectors." Contractors who collected tolls for Rome, despised as collaborators and often extortionate. Their coming to John, and his accepting them, anticipates Luke's recurring concern with tax-collectors (5:27-32; 19:1-10).

---

### **Verse 13**

And he said to them, "Collect nothing more than what has been appointed to you."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"NOTHING MORE THAN APPOINTED" — *MĒDEN PLEON***
> - **μηδὲν πλέον παρὰ τὸ διατεταγμένον ὑμῖν πράσσετε** (*mēden pleon para to diatetagmenon hymin prassete*) = "exact nothing more than what has been appointed to you." John does not demand they abandon the profession, only that they cease the extortion (*prassō* = "exact/collect") built into it. The reform is ethical, within the role.

---

### **Verse 14**

And soldiers also were asking him, saying, "And we, what should we do?" And he said to them, "Extort no one and accuse *no one* falsely, and be content with your wages."

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"EXTORT NO ONE… BE CONTENT WITH WAGES" — *DIASEISĒTE… OPSŌNIOIS***
> - **μηδένα διασείσητε μηδὲ συκοφαντήσητε** (*mēdena diaseisēte mēde sykophantēsēte*) = "extort/intimidate no one and accuse *no one* falsely." *Diaseiō* (lit. "shake violently") = extort by intimidation; *sykophanteō* = bring false charges for gain (English "sycophant"). **ὀψωνίοις** (*opsōniois*) = "wages, soldier's pay/rations." Likely auxiliary troops in Herod's or the prefect's service. The TT adds *no one* in italics for the second verb's object.

### **Verse 15**

And *while* the people were in expectation and all were reasoning in their hearts about Yochanan (John), whether perhaps he might be the anointed one,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "WHETHER HE MIGHT BE THE ANOINTED ONE" — *HO CHRISTOS***
> - **μήποτε αὐτὸς εἴη ὁ χριστός** (*mēpote autos eiē ho christos*) = "whether perhaps he might be the anointed one." **χριστός** (*christos*) = "anointed one" (per locked glossary, lowercase as title/descriptor; maps to HB *mashiach*). The optative *eiē* conveys their tentative wondering. John's denial (v.16) redirects the expectation to the "stronger one." → For messianic expectation in the period and John's self-effacement, see Companion §A3.

### **Verse 16**

John answered, saying to all, "I indeed immerse you in water; but *the one* stronger than I is coming, of whom I am not worthy to loosen the strap of his sandals; he will immerse you in *the* holy {a:wind/spirit} and fire.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "THE STRONGER ONE… WIND/SPIRIT AND FIRE"**
> - **ὁ ἰσχυρότερός μου** (*ho ischyroteros mou*) = "the *one* stronger than I." John contrasts his water-immersion with the coming one's immersion **ἐν πνεύματι ἁγίῳ καὶ πυρί** (*en pneumati hagiō kai pyri*) = "in *the* holy wind/spirit and fire." *Pneuma* = wind/spirit (slash per locked glossary; anarthrous, *the* added in italics). "Fire" may be purgative, judgmental, or both (cf. the chaff burned in v.17). The TT preserves the unresolved pairing. "Loosen the strap of the sandals" = the task of the lowest slave — radical self-subordination. → For the wind/spirit-and-fire immersion, see Companion §D1. **[lexical-theological — PROBABLE]**
>
> 🟢 **"HOLY WIND/SPIRIT" — *PNEUMA HAGION***
> - **πνεύματι ἁγίῳ** (*pneumati hagiō*) = "holy wind/spirit." Anarthrous; slash per locked glossary, avoiding the imported title "the Holy Spirit." The TT adds *the* in italics.

### **Verse 17**

His winnowing-fork *is* in his hand to clear out his threshing floor and to gather the wheat into his storehouse, but the chaff he will burn up with unquenchable fire."

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"WINNOWING-FORK… UNQUENCHABLE FIRE" — *PTYON… PYRI ASBESTŌ***
> - **οὗ τὸ πτύον ἐν τῇ χειρὶ αὐτοῦ** (*hou to ptyon en tē cheiri autou*) = "whose winnowing-fork *is* in his hand." *Ptyon* = the fork used to toss threshed grain so wind separates wheat from chaff — a judgment image (cf. Mal 3:19 LXX; Ps 1:4). **πυρὶ ἀσβέστῳ** (*pyri asbestō*) = "unquenchable fire" (*asbestos* = "not extinguished"). The TT adds *is* in italics (no copula).

### **Verse 18**

So with many other exhortations also he proclaimed good news to the people.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"PROCLAIMED GOOD NEWS" — *EUĒNGELIZETO***
> - **πολλὰ μὲν οὖν καὶ ἕτερα παρακαλῶν εὐηγγελίζετο τὸν λαόν** (*polla men oun kai hetera parakalōn euēngelizeto ton laon*) = "with many other exhortations also he proclaimed good news to the people." Luke summarizes John's preaching — including the stern warnings — as *euangelizomai* ("announce good news"). The verb (cf. 1:19) frames repentance-preaching itself as good news.

### **Verse 19**

But Herodes (Herod) the tetrarch, being reproved by him concerning Herodias the wife of his brother and concerning all the evils that Herod had done,

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"REPROVED CONCERNING HERODIAS" — *ELENCHOMENOS***
> - **ἐλεγχόμενος ὑπ' αὐτοῦ περὶ Ἡρῳδιάδος** (*elenchomenos hyp' autou peri Hērōdiados*) = "being reproved by him concerning Herodias." Herod Antipas had taken Herodias, his brother's wife. Luke places John's imprisonment here, before the baptism account, as a narrative wrap-up of the Baptist's role — the actual execution is told later (9:9 alludes back). The TT keeps the prolepsis as Luke arranges it.

### **Verse 20**

added this also to *them* all: he shut up John in prison.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"SHUT UP JOHN IN PRISON" — *KATEKLEISEN***
> - **κατέκλεισεν τὸν Ἰωάννην ἐν φυλακῇ** (*katekleisen ton Iōannēn en phylakē*) = "he shut up John in prison." The verb *katakleiō* = "shut up, confine." Luke removes John from the scene before narrating the baptism, so that the spotlight falls on Yeshua. The TT adds *them* in italics for the elliptical "to all" (his other evils).

### **Verse 21**

And it happened, when all the people were immersed, *that* Yeshua (Jesus) also having been immersed and praying, the sky was opened,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE BAPTISM: JOHN NOT NAMED AS BAPTIZER**
> - **ἐγένετο δὲ… καὶ Ἰησοῦ βαπτισθέντος καὶ προσευχομένου ἀνεῳχθῆναι τὸν οὐρανόν** (*egeneto de… kai Iēsou baptisthentos kai proseuchomenou aneōchthēnai ton ouranon*) = "and it happened… *that* Jesus also having been immersed and praying, the sky was opened." Luke, having already imprisoned John (vv.19-20), does not name John as the baptizer; the immersion is reported as a completed participle, and the emphasis falls on Yeshua **praying** (a Lukan motif at key moments) and the opening of the sky. **Rule 30:** what follows in v.22 (the voice) is God's direct speech and IS divine-marked. **οὐρανός** (*ouranos*) = "sky/heaven." The TT keeps the *egeneto* + infinitive Septuagintism.
>
> 🟢 **YESHUA — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Ἰησοῦς** (*Iēsous*) = Yeshua (Hebrew: **Yeshua**, "YHWH saves"). Familiar: Jesus.

### **Verse 22**

and the holy {a:wind/spirit} descended in bodily form like a dove upon him, and a voice came from heaven: @@"You are my son, the beloved; in you I am well pleased."@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE VOICE FROM HEAVEN (BAT-QOL) — DIVINE SPEECH (RULE 30)**
> - **φωνὴν ἐξ οὐρανοῦ γενέσθαι· σὺ εἶ ὁ υἱός μου ὁ ἀγαπητός, ἐν σοὶ εὐδόκησα** (*phōnēn ex ouranou genesthai: sy ei ho huios mou ho agapētos, en soi eudokēsa*) = "a voice came from heaven: 'You are my son, the beloved; in you I am well pleased.'" This is **God's direct speech** — the *Bat-Qol* ("daughter of a voice," the rabbinic term for the heavenly voice) — and is therefore **marked with @@…@@** per Rule 30 (cf. Mark 1:11; Matt 3:17, which has the third-person "This is…"). The words fuse Psalm 2:7 (royal sonship: "you are my son") with Isaiah 42:1 (the chosen servant in whom God delights: "my chosen, in whom my soul delights"). **εὐδόκησα** (*eudokēsa*) = "I took pleasure / was well pleased" (aorist). → For Ps 2:7 / Isa 42:1 fusion and the *Bat-Qol*, see the PROPHECY companion. **[theological — PROBABLE]**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — WESTERN VARIANT: PSALM 2:7 IN FULL**
> - A notable Western reading (Codex Bezae, D, and some Old Latin/patristic witnesses) has the voice quote Psalm 2:7 in full: "You are my son; today I have begotten you" (*huios mou ei sy, egō sēmeron gegennēka se*). NA28 reads "in you I am well pleased" (with Mark/Matthew). The TT follows NA28 and flags the variant for Hellenist review (Rule 13). **[text-critical — POSSIBLE]**
>
> 🟢 **"HOLY WIND/SPIRIT… LIKE A DOVE" — *SŌMATIKŌ EIDEI***
> - **τὸ πνεῦμα τὸ ἅγιον σωματικῷ εἴδει ὡς περιστερὰν** (*to pneuma to hagion sōmatikō eidei hōs peristeran*) = "the holy wind/spirit in bodily form like a dove." Only Luke adds **σωματικῷ εἴδει** ("in bodily form"), heightening the visibility of the descent. *Pneuma* = wind/spirit (slash per locked glossary). The simile "like a dove" qualifies the manner of descent, not necessarily the form.

### **Verse 23**

And Yeshua (Jesus) himself was beginning *to be* about thirty years *old*, being a son — as was supposed — of Yosef (Joseph), of Eli,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "AS WAS SUPPOSED" — *HŌS ENOMIZETO* (CRUX)**
> - **ὢν υἱός, ὡς ἐνομίζετο, Ἰωσὴφ** (*ōn huios, hōs enomizeto, Iōsēph*) = "being a son — as was supposed — of Joseph." The parenthetical **ὡς ἐνομίζετο** (*hōs enomizeto*, "as was supposed/reckoned") qualifies Joseph's paternity: Jesus was *legally* reckoned Joseph's son, the narrator hedging the biological claim (cf. 1:34-35). The phrase's exact syntactic scope — and whether the genealogy that follows is Joseph's or (on one ancient and modern proposal) Mary's via Eli — is disputed. The TT preserves the hedge "as was supposed" without resolving the line's owner. → For *hōs enomizeto* and the Joseph/Mary-line question, see Companion §A4 and `luke/PEOPLE.md`. **[grammatical-historical — POSSIBLE]**
>
> 🔵 **"ABOUT THIRTY YEARS" — *HŌSEI ETŌN TRIAKONTA***
> - **ἀρχόμενος ὡσεὶ ἐτῶν τριάκοντα** (*archomenos hōsei etōn triakonta*) = "beginning, *being* about thirty years *old*." *Hōsei* ("about") makes the age approximate. "Thirty" may echo the age of priestly/Levitical service (Num 4:3) and of David (2 Sam 5:4). The construction is awkward; the TT adds *to be* and *old* in italics. The genealogy that follows ascends (son of… son of…) rather than descends.

### **Verse 24**

of Mattat, of Levi, of Melchi, of Yannai, of Yosef,

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **THE ASCENDING GENEALOGY (vv.23-38) — STRUCTURE AND DIVERGENCE FROM MATTHEW**
> - The genealogy ascends — "son of X, of Y, of Z…" — each verse a cluster of names, running back from Yeshua through David (v.31), Avraham (v.34), Noach (v.36), to Adam and God (v.38). This is the reverse of Matthew's descending list (Matt 1:1-17, Abraham → Yeshua) and **diverges sharply from it**, most notably at the generation immediately above Joseph (Luke: "of Eli"; Matthew: "Jacob fathered Joseph") and across the whole post-Davidic line — a long-standing crux. Proposed harmonizations (Luke gives Mary's line; levirate marriage; legal vs. biological descent) are all contested. The TT renders Luke's list as the Greek gives it and flags the divergence for Hellenist review (Rule 13). The **full genealogy table, generation count, and cross-book see-stubs** (to `genesis` for Adam → the patriarchs, and to `matthew` for the David line) live in **`luke/PEOPLE.md`** per the v3.3.2 cross-book convention. **[historical — POSSIBLE]**
>
> 🟢 **GENEALOGY NAME-RENDERING POLICY**
> - Names with a familiar English form receive the "Transliterated (Familiar)" gloss at first occurrence in the genealogy (e.g. David, Avraham (Abraham), Noach (Noah), Adam). Names without a distinct familiar form are given in transliteration only (e.g. Mattat, Yannai, Neri). The Greek's repeated **τοῦ** (*tou*, genitive "of") is rendered "of" throughout; the implied "son" is not repeated each time.

### **Verse 31**

of Mal'ah, of Mena, of Mattata, of Natan, of David,

> **Notes**

> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE DAVIDIC LINE VIA NATAN, NOT SHELOMOH (SOLOMON)**

Luke's line runs through David's son **Natan** (2 Sam 5:14; Zech 12:12), whereas Matthew's runs through **Shelomoh (Solomon)** (Matt 1:6). This is the structural root of the two genealogies' divergence after David. ### **Verse 32**

of Yishai, of Oved, of Bo'az, of Salah, of Nachshon,

> 🟢 **THE RUTH-BOOK LINE — CONVERGENCE WITH MATTHEW**

From David back to Nachshon the line matches Ruth 4:18-22 and Matthew 1:3-6 (the two genealogies converge here, between David and Abraham). ### **Verse 33**

> - The names between Amminadav and Chetsron are textually unstable: NA28 reads **Ἀδμίν… Ἀρνί** (Admin… Arni), where other witnesses read "Aram" (= Matthew 1:3-4; Ruth 4:19). The TT follows NA28's Admin/Arni and notes the variant. **Ἰούδα** (*Iouda*) here reaches the patriarch Yehudah (Judah), son of Ya'aqov.

### **Verse 34**

of Ya'aqov (Jacob), of Yitzchak (Isaac), of Avraham (Abraham), of Terach, of Nachor,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE PATRIARCHS AND BEYOND ABRAHAM**
> - **Ἰακὼβ… Ἰσαὰκ… Ἀβραάμ** (*Iakōb… Isaak… Abraam*) = Ya'aqov, Yitzchak, Avraham — the patriarchs (cf. Gen 21, 25). Where **Matthew stops at Abraham** (the covenant root of Israel), **Luke continues past Abraham** through Terach and Nachor (Gen 11:24-26) — the decisive structural difference: Luke universalizes the lineage beyond Israel toward all humanity (v.38). The TT gives the familiar glosses (Jacob, Isaac, Abraham) at first occurrence here. → For the Abraham-and-beyond ascent and its universal theology, see Companion §A4.

> 🟢 **TT TRANSLITERATIONS**
> - **Ἰακώβ** = Ya'aqov (Hebrew: **Ya'aqov**, "heel-grabber"). Familiar: Jacob.
> - **Ἰσαάκ** = Yitzchak (Hebrew: **Yitzchak**, "he laughs"). Familiar: Isaac.
> - Terach and Nachor (Gen 11) have no distinct familiar English form; transliteration only.

of Metushelach, of Chanokh (Enoch), of Yered, of Mahalal'el, of Qenan,

> **Notes**

> 🟡 **THE ANTEDILUVIAN LINE (GEN 5)**
> - **Μαθουσαλά… Ἑνὼχ… Ἰάρετ… Μαλελεὴλ… Καϊνάν** (*Mathousala… Henōch… Iaret… Maleleēl… Kainan*) = Metushelach, Chanokh, Yered, Mahalal'el, Qenan. This is the pre-flood line of Genesis 5. **Chanokh (Enoch)** "walked with God, and was not, for God took him" (Gen 5:24) — a figure of large Second Temple interest. ### **Verse 38**

of Enosh, of Shet, of Adam, of God.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "OF ADAM, OF GOD" — THE CLIMAX OF THE ASCENT**
> - **τοῦ Ἐνὼς τοῦ Σὴθ τοῦ Ἀδὰμ τοῦ θεοῦ** (*tou Enōs tou Sēth tou Adam tou theou*) = "of Enosh, of Shet, of Adam, of God." The genealogy ends not with a human ancestor but with **τοῦ θεοῦ** ("of God"): Adam is "son of God" by creation (Gen 1:27; 5:1). The same genitive construction (*tou theou*) that has chained the whole list now binds Adam to God — framing Yeshua's sonship (v.22, "you are my son") against the backdrop of Adam's. The universal reach (back past Abraham to the first human and to God) answers the universal horizon of v.6 ("all flesh"). The TT keeps the bare "of God," matching the unbroken genitive chain. → For the Adam-sonship climax and its theology, see Companion §A4 and §D2. **[theological — PROBABLE]**
>
> 🟢 **SHET AND ADAM — TT TRANSLITERATIONS**
> - **Σήθ** = Shet (Hebrew: **Shet**, "appointed/granted," Gen 4:25). Familiar: Seth. **Ἀδάμ** = Adam (Hebrew: **Adam**, "human/earth-creature," from *adamah*, "ground"). Familiar form identical: Adam (no redundant gloss). Enosh (Hebrew: **Enosh**, "man/mortal," Gen 4:26).

## CROSS-CHAPTER TRACKING (HB -> Luke)

**Isa 40:3-5 -> Luke 3:4-6 — "a voice in the wilderness":**

| Element | Isaiah 40:3-5 (LXX) | Luke 3:4-6 |
|---------|----------------------|-------------|
| The voice | "A voice of *one* crying in the wilderness: prepare the way of YHWH" (40:3) | "A voice of *one* crying in the wilderness: prepare the way of the Lord" (3:4) |
| Levelling | "every valley filled, every mountain and hill made low" (40:4) | "every ravine filled… mountain and hill made low" (3:5) |
| Universal end | "all flesh will see the salvation of God" (40:5) | "all flesh will see the salvation of God" (3:6) |

Luke quotes Isaiah 40 further than Matthew or Mark, reaching v.5's "all flesh will see the salvation of God" — the universal horizon that the genealogy's ascent to Adam (3:38) and "of God" mirror.

**Jer 1:1-2 / Hos 1:1 -> Luke 3:2 — the prophetic-call formula:**

| Element | Jeremiah / Hosea (LXX) | Luke 3:2 |
|---------|-------------------------|----------|
| Dating | "in the days of… king of Judah" (Jer 1:2-3) | "in the fifteenth year of Tiberius…" (3:1) |
| Word-event | "the word of YHWH that came to Jeremiah" (Jer 1:2) | "*the* word of God came upon John" (3:2) |
| Recipient | the prophet, son of a priest (Jer 1:1) | John, son of the priest Zecharyah (3:2) |

John is cast in the mould of the called prophets — the word-event idiom and the priestly-son detail mark his commission; his preaching is *human* prophecy (not divine-marked, Rule 30).

**Ps 2:7 / Isa 42:1 -> Luke 3:22 — the voice from heaven (Bat-Qol):**

| Element | Psalm 2:7 / Isaiah 42:1 | Luke 3:22 |
|---------|--------------------------|-----------|
| Sonship | "You are my son" (Ps 2:7) | @@"You are my son, the beloved"@@ |
| Delight | "my chosen, in whom my soul delights" (Isa 42:1) | @@"in you I am well pleased"@@ |
| Wind/spirit | "I have put my wind/spirit upon him" (Isa 42:1) | the {a:wind/spirit} descends upon him (3:22) |

The heavenly voice — God's direct speech, divine-marked — fuses the royal sonship of Ps 2 with the chosen servant of Isa 42. A Western variant (Codex Bezae) quotes Ps 2:7 in full ("today I have begotten you"); NA28 reads with Mark/Matthew.

**Gen 5 / Gen 11 / Ruth 4 -> Luke 3:31-38 — the ascending genealogy:**

| Element | Genesis / Ruth | Luke 3:31-38 |
|---------|-----------------|---------------|
| David's line | David → Solomon (1 Chr 3) | David → **Natan** (3:31), diverging from Matthew's Solomon |
| Patriarchs | Abraham → Isaac → Jacob (Gen 21-25) | reached at 3:34, then continued *past* Abraham |
| Shem's line | Shem → Arpakhshad → … Ever (Gen 11:10-26) | 3:35-36, including the LXX-only "Qenan" |
| First human | Adam, made in God's image (Gen 1:27; 5:1) | "of Adam, of God" (3:38) — the climax |

Where Matthew descends from Abraham (Israel's covenant root) through Solomon, Luke ascends past Abraham through Natan to Adam and to God — opening the lineage to all humanity. The post-Davidic divergence and the LXX-only Qenan are known cruxes, flagged for Hellenist review (Rule 13). The full table and cross-book see-stubs live in `luke/PEOPLE.md`.
