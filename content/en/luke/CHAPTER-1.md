## READING GUIDE

**Main Text:** Primary translation — readable but faithful to Greek structure. **Notes:** Essential Greek features immediately below each verse.

**How the text is marked:**
- *Words in italics* — added for English grammar (not in the Greek text)
- {a:wind/spirit} — a word the Greek leaves open to two meanings, both kept
- @@"…"@@ — direct speech of God or the holy wind/spirit

Notes are tagged by type — **Critical · Lexical · Grammatical · Theological** — each shown with its colour (see the legend at the top of the Notes view).

**This is the longest opening chapter in the Gospels.** Luke 1 begins with a formal literary prologue (vv.1-4) — a single periodic Greek sentence, the most "classical" Greek in the four Gospels — addressed to one Theophilos. The narrative then shifts into a Septuagintal register modeled on the HB. Two annunciations run in parallel: the messenger Gavriel announces the birth of Yochanan to the priest Zecharyah (vv.5-25), then the birth of Yeshua to Miryam (vv.26-38). Miryam visits Elisheva (vv.39-45) and sings the Magnificat (vv.46-55). Yochanan is born and named (vv.57-66), and Zecharyah sings the Benedictus (vv.67-79). **Note on speech-marking (Rule 30):** Gavriel is a *messenger* (*angelos*), not God — his words are NOT marked as divine speech. The two canticles (Magnificat, Benedictus) are *human* speech and are rendered as flowing prose. Proper names follow TT transliteration: Yeshua (not Jesus), Yochanan (not John), Zecharyah (not Zechariah), Elisheva (not Elizabeth), Miryam (not Mary), Gavriel (not Gabriel).

## CHAPTER OVERVIEW

**What happens:** After a formal prologue dedicating the work to Theophilus (vv.1-4), the narrative opens in the days of Herod, king of Judea. The aged priest Zechariah and his wife Elizabeth are righteous but childless. While serving in the temple, Zechariah is visited by the messenger Gabriel, who announces that Elizabeth will bear a son to be named John, who will go before the Lord in the spirit and power of Elijah. Zechariah doubts and is struck mute as a sign. Elizabeth conceives. Six months later Gabriel is sent to a virgin in Nazareth, Mary, betrothed to Joseph of David's house, and announces that she will conceive a son, Jesus, by the holy wind/spirit — "the power of the Most High will overshadow" her. Mary consents. She visits Elizabeth, whose unborn child leaps; Elizabeth blesses her, and Mary sings the Magnificat, magnifying the Lord for lifting the lowly and scattering the proud. Mary stays three months and returns. Elizabeth bears her son; on the eighth day, against the family's wish to name him Zechariah, both parents insist on "John," and Zechariah's speech returns. He sings the Benedictus, blessing the Lord for raising a horn of salvation in David's house and prophesying that his own son will be the prophet of the Most High preparing the Lord's way. The child grows in the wilderness.

**Key themes:** The classical literary prologue and the dedication to Theophilus ("friend/beloved of God"); the parallel-annunciation structure (John / Jesus) that invites comparison; the strongly Septuagintal style echoing the births of Isaac, Samson, and Samuel; barrenness reversed by divine initiative (Sarah, Hannah); the messenger Gabriel (Dan 8-9) as announcer; *charis* ("favor/grace") as the keynote of the annunciation to Mary; the holy wind/spirit and "overshadowing" as the mode of conception; the canticles (Magnificat, Benedictus) saturated with HB allusion (Hannah's song, the Psalms, the prophets); the reversal motif — lowly lifted, proud scattered; the naming controversies (John, the unexpected name) and Zechariah's silence-then-speech.

**Watch for:** The prologue's single periodic sentence (vv.1-4) is the most refined Greek in the Gospels — the TT keeps its subordinated structure. Gabriel is *angelos* ("messenger"), an angel — his speech is NOT marked as divine speech (only God's/the wind-spirit's direct speech is). The verb at 1:28, *kecharitōmenē* ("having-been-favored"), is a perfect passive participle, not the later title "full of grace." At 1:35, *episkiasei* ("will overshadow") echoes the cloud over the wilderness dwelling (Ex 40:35 LXX) — the TT preserves the verb without resolving its sense. *Kyrios* shifts referent across the chapter — YHWH in the HB-styled passages (Option C: "the Lord"), but human "master/lord" in address (e.g. 1:43). At 1:78, *anatolē* ("rising / dawn / shoot/branch") is genuinely ambiguous — the TT flags it. The two canticles are human poetry rendered as prose.

**Connection:** The infancy narrative is built on HB precedent: the barren-then-blessed mothers (Sarah in Gen 18, Hannah in 1 Sam 1), the annunciation type-scene, and Gabriel from Daniel. The Magnificat reworks Hannah's song (1 Sam 2:1-10). The Benedictus draws on the Psalms and the covenant promises to Abraham and David. Elijah (Mal 3-4) stands behind John's commissioning "in the spirit and power of Elijah." The overshadowing wind/spirit recalls Genesis 1:2 and the glory-cloud of Exodus 40.

## VERSE-BY-VERSE STUDY

### **Verse 1**

Since many *people* have undertaken to compile a narrative concerning the matters that have been brought to fullness among us,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE LITERARY PROLOGUE (vv.1-4)**
> - **Ἐπειδήπερ πολλοὶ ἐπεχείρησαν ἀνατάξασθαι διήγησιν** (*Epeidēper polloi epecheirēsan anataxasthai diēgēsin*) = "Since many undertook to draw up a narrative." Verses 1-4 form a single periodic sentence — the most polished, classical Greek in the four Gospels, modeled on Hellenistic historiographical and scientific prefaces (cf. Josephus, *Against Apion*). The TT preserves the subordinated, suspended structure rather than breaking it into short clauses. → For the genre and parallels of the Lukan prologue, see Companion §A1.
>
> 🔵 **"BROUGHT TO FULLNESS" — *PEPLĒROPHORĒMENŌN***
> - **πεπληροφορημένων** (*peplērophorēmenōn*) = perfect passive participle of *plērophoreō*, "to bring to full measure, fulfill, accomplish fully." It can mean events "fully accomplished/come to pass" or matters "fully believed/established." The TT renders "brought to fullness," keeping the completed-action sense.

---

### **Verse 2**

just as those who from *the* beginning *were* eyewitnesses and became servants of the word handed *them* down to us,

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"EYEWITNESSES AND SERVANTS OF THE WORD" — *AUTOPTAI… HYPĒRETAI***
> - **αὐτόπται καὶ ὑπηρέται τοῦ λόγου** (*autoptai kai hypēretai tou logou*) = "eyewitnesses and servants of the word." A single article governs both nouns — likely the same group are both eyewitnesses and ministers. *Autoptēs* ("eyewitness," lit. "self-seer") is a term of historiography. The author distinguishes himself from this first generation: he received, not witnessed.
>
> 🔵 **"FROM THE BEGINNING" — *AP' ARCHĒS***
> - **ἀπ' ἀρχῆς** (*ap' archēs*) = "from *the* beginning." The TT adds *the* in italics (anarthrous in Greek). The phrase marks the witnesses' presence from the start of the events narrated.

---

### **Verse 3**

it seemed *good* also to me, having followed all *things* carefully from the start, to write *them* to you in order, most excellent Theophilos (Theophilus),

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THEOPHILOS ("FRIEND/BELOVED OF GOD")**
> - **κράτιστε Θεόφιλε** (*kratiste Theophile*) = "most excellent Theophilos." The name Θεόφιλος = "friend of God" or "loved by God" (*theos* + *philos*) — possibly a real patron, possibly a symbolic ideal reader; the text does not decide. **κράτιστος** (*kratistos*, "most excellent") is an honorific used for officials of rank (cf. Acts 23:26, 24:3, 26:25). → For Theophilos's identity and the dedication convention, see Companion §A2.
>
> 🔵 **"IN ORDER" — *KATHEXĒS***
> - **καθεξῆς** (*kathexēs*) = "in order, in sequence." Whether this means strict chronological order or an orderly literary arrangement is debated; the TT keeps the neutral "in order."

---

### **Verse 4**

so that you may know the certainty concerning the words *about* which you were instructed.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"THE CERTAINTY" — *ASPHALEIAN***
> - **τὴν ἀσφάλειαν** (*tēn asphaleian*) = "the certainty, security, firm truth" (lit. "non-slipping"). It stands emphatically at the end of the sentence — the goal of the whole work.
>
> 🔵 **"YOU WERE INSTRUCTED" — *KATĒCHĒTHĒS***
> - **κατηχήθης** (*katēchēthēs*) = aorist passive of *katēcheō*, "to instruct, inform" (English "catechize" derives from it). It may indicate formal instruction in the *logoi* (accounts/words) or simply being informed about events.

### **Verse 5**

*There* was, in the days of Herodes (Herod) king of Yehudah (Judea), a certain priest named Zecharyah (Zechariah), of the division of Aviyah, and his wife *was* of the daughters of Aharon (Aaron), and her name *was* Elisheva (Elizabeth).

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — SHIFT TO SEPTUAGINTAL STYLE**
> - From the classical prologue, the narrative shifts abruptly into a Hebraic, Septuagintal Greek. **Ἐγένετο ἐν ταῖς ἡμέραις** (*Egeneto en tais hēmerais*, "It happened in the days") imitates the HB narrative opening (cf. 1 Sam 1:1, Ruth 1:1 LXX). The infancy account is deliberately styled to echo the births of Yitschaq (Isaac), Shimshon (Samson), and Shemu'el (Samuel). → For the Septuagintal register and its function, see Companion §A1.
>
> 🟢 **TT TRANSLITERATIONS — FIRST OCCURRENCES**
> - **Ζαχαρίας** (*Zacharias*) = Zecharyah (Hebrew: **Zecharyah**, "YHWH remembers"). Familiar: Zechariah.
> - **Ἐλισάβετ** (*Elisabet*) = Elisheva (Hebrew: **Elisheva**, "my God *is an* oath / God *is* fullness"; the name of Aharon's wife, Ex 6:23). Familiar: Elizabeth.
> - **Ἡρῴδης** (*Hērōdēs*) = Herodes. Familiar: Herod. Here Herod the Great (d. 4 BCE).
>
> 🔵 **"DIVISION OF AVIYAH" — PRIESTLY COURSE**
> - **ἐφημερίας Ἀβιά** (*ephēmerias Abia*) = "division/course of Aviyah" — the eighth of the twenty-four priestly divisions (1 Chr 24:10), each serving in rotation. Both Zechariah and Elizabeth are of priestly descent (Aharon's line).

---

### **Verse 6**

And both were righteous before God, walking blamelessly in all the commandments and ordinances of the Lord.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"RIGHTEOUS… BLAMELESS" — *DIKAIOI… AMEMPTOI***
> - **δίκαιοι… ἄμεμπτοι** (*dikaioi… amemptoi*) = "righteous… blameless." The pair, with "walking in all the commandments," is HB covenant language (cf. Noach, Gen 6:9; the description echoes Iyov/Job 1:1). It establishes that their childlessness is *not* a sign of sin — sharpening the reversal to come.
>
> 🟢 **"THE LORD" — *KYRIOU* (OPTION C)**
> - **τοῦ κυρίου** (*tou kyriou*) = "of the Lord." In this HB-styled context, *kyrios* renders the underlying YHWH. Per GS Option C, the TT reads "the Lord" and tracks the referent as YHWH. Referent: YHWH (Probable).

---

### **Verse 7**

And they had no child, because Elizabeth was barren, and both were advanced in their days.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE BARRENNESS MOTIF**
> - **στεῖρα** (*steira*) = "barren." The barren-then-blessed wife is a recurring HB type: Sarah (Gen 11:30, 18), Rivqah (Gen 25:21), Rachel (Gen 29:31), the wife of Manoach (Judg 13), and Channah (Hannah, 1 Sam 1:2). Luke signals that this birth belongs to that lineage of divine reversals. → For the barren-mother type-scene, see Companion §A3.
>
> 🔵 **"ADVANCED IN THEIR DAYS" — *PROBEBĒKOTES***
> - **προβεβηκότες ἐν ταῖς ἡμέραις** (*probebēkotes en tais hēmerais*) = "advanced in their days" — a Semitic idiom (cf. Gen 18:11 LXX of Avraham and Sarah). The TT keeps the idiom rather than smoothing to "elderly."

---

### **Verse 8**

And it happened, while he was serving as priest in the order of his division before God,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"AND IT HAPPENED" — *EGENETO* + INFINITIVE**
> - **Ἐγένετο δὲ… τοῦ ἱερατεύειν** (*Egeneto de… tou hierateuein*) = "And it happened… to serve as priest." The *kai/de egeneto* construction with a following clause is a hallmark Septuagintism, recurring throughout the chapter. The TT preserves the formula.

---

### **Verse 9**

according to the custom of the priesthood, *that* it fell to him by lot to burn incense, having entered into the sanctuary of the Lord.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"BY LOT… TO BURN INCENSE" — *ELACHE… TOU THYMIASAI***
> - **ἔλαχε τοῦ θυμιᾶσαι** (*elache tou thymiasai*) = "it fell *to him* by lot to burn incense." The incense offering (twice daily, Ex 30:7-8) in the *naos* (sanctuary/holy place) was assigned by lot; a priest might receive this privilege only once in a lifetime. **ναός** (*naos*) = the inner sanctuary, distinct from the wider temple complex (*hieron*).

---

### **Verse 10**

And the whole multitude of the people was praying outside at the hour of the incense *offering*.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"THE HOUR OF THE INCENSE" — PRAYER AND INCENSE**
> - **τῇ ὥρᾳ τοῦ θυμιάματος** (*tē hōra tou thymiamatos*) = "at the hour of the incense." The people pray outside while the priest offers incense within; incense and prayer are linked (Ps 141:2). The TT adds *offering* in italics for the elliptical genitive.

---

### **Verse 11**

And a messenger of the Lord appeared to him, standing at *the* right of the altar of incense.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — *ANGELOS KYRIOU* ("MESSENGER OF THE LORD")**
> - **ἄγγελος κυρίου** (*angelos kyriou*) = "a messenger of the Lord." This is the standard LXX rendering of the Hebrew **malakh YHWH** (Gen 16:7, Ex 3:2, Judg 13:3). Per GS Option C, the Greek *kyrios* is rendered "the Lord"; the underlying HB tradition refers to YHWH. **Important (Rule 30):** the messenger is *angelos*, an angelic agent — his speech (vv.13-20) is therefore NOT marked as direct divine speech. Referent of *kyriou*: YHWH (Probable). → For the *malakh YHWH* tradition, see Companion §A6.

---

### **Verse 12**

And Zecharyah (Zechariah) was troubled, seeing *this*, and fear fell upon him.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"FEAR FELL UPON HIM" — *PHOBOS EPEPESEN***
> - **φόβος ἐπέπεσεν ἐπ' αὐτόν** (*phobos epepesen ep' auton*) = "fear fell upon him." The reaction of dread before a heavenly messenger is a fixed feature of the annunciation type-scene (cf. Dan 8:17, 10:7-9), answered by the standard "do not be afraid" (v.13).

### **Verse 13**

But the messenger said to him, "Do not be afraid, Zecharyah (Zechariah), because your petition was heard, and your wife Elisheva (Elizabeth) will bear you a son, and you will call his name Yochanan (John).

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — GAVRIEL'S SPEECH NOT DIVINE-MARKED (RULE 30)**
> - The messenger's words (vv.13-20) are rendered in ordinary quotation marks, NOT the @@…@@ divine-speech markers. Per the editorial decision (luke.md L-001) and the Matthew/Mark precedent, an *angelos* is a messenger, not God; only the direct speech of God or the holy wind/spirit is marked. The TT does not collapse messenger-speech into divine speech.
>
> 🟢 **YOCHANAN — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Ἰωάννης** (*Iōannēs*) = Yochanan (Hebrew: **Yochanan**, "YHWH has shown favor / YHWH is gracious"). Familiar: John. The name's meaning ("YHWH is gracious") resonates with *charis* in the chapter.
>
> 🔵 **"YOUR PETITION WAS HEARD" — *EISĒKOUSTHĒ***
> - **εἰσηκούσθη ἡ δέησίς σου** (*eisēkousthē hē deēsis sou*) = "your petition was heard." The aorist passive likely refers to long prayer for a child; some read it of prayer for Israel's redemption. The TT keeps "petition" without specifying its object.

### **Verse 14**

And he will be joy and exultation to you, and many will rejoice at his birth.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"JOY AND EXULTATION" — *CHARA KAI AGALLIASIS***
> - **χαρὰ καὶ ἀγαλλίασις** (*chara kai agalliasis*) = "joy and exultation." *Agalliasis* is intense, often liturgical, rejoicing — the same root returns at v.44 (the baby leaps "in exultation") and v.47 (Mary's spirit "exulted").

### **Verse 15**

For he will be great before the Lord, and he will not drink wine or strong drink, and he will be filled with *the* holy {a:wind/spirit} *even* from his mother's womb.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"WINE OR STRONG DRINK" — NAZIRITE ECHO**
> - **οἶνον καὶ σίκερα οὐ μὴ πίῃ** (*oinon kai sikera ou mē piē*) = "he will certainly not drink wine or strong drink." *Sikera* (a Semitic loanword, *shekar*) = fermented drink. The abstention recalls the Nazirite vow (Num 6:3) and the annunciation of Shimshon (Samson, Judg 13:4-7). → For the Nazirite and Samson background, see Companion §A3.
>
> 🟢 **"HOLY WIND/SPIRIT" — *PNEUMATOS HAGIOU***
> - **πνεύματος ἁγίου** (*pneumatos hagiou*) = "holy wind/spirit." Anarthrous; the TT adds *the* in italics and *even* for the emphatic "from the womb." Per locked glossary, *pneuma* = wind/spirit (slash policy), avoiding the imported title "the Holy Spirit."

### **Verse 16**

And many of the sons of Israel he will turn back to the Lord their God.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"THE LORD THEIR GOD" — *KYRION… THEON* (OPTION C)**
> - **ἐπὶ κύριον τὸν θεὸν αὐτῶν** (*epi kyrion ton theon autōn*) = "to the Lord their God." Standard LXX covenant phrasing for YHWH-Elohim. Per Option C, "the Lord" renders *kyrios*; referent: YHWH (Probable). "Turn back" (*epistrepsei*) is prophetic repentance language.

### **Verse 17**

And he will go before him in *the* spirit and power of Eliyahu (Elijah), to turn back *the* hearts of fathers to children and *the* disobedient to *the* mindset of *the* righteous, to prepare for the Lord a people made ready."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE ELIYAHU (ELIJAH) COMMISSION (MAL 3:23-24 / 4:5-6)**
> - **ἐν πνεύματι καὶ δυνάμει Ἠλίου** (*en pneumati kai dynamei Ēliou*) = "in *the* spirit and power of Elijah." The phrase "to turn back the hearts of fathers to children" quotes Malachi 3:24 (Eng. 4:6 LXX), where Eliyahu is sent before "the great and terrible day of YHWH." John is cast as the returning-Elijah figure. → For the Elijah-redivivus expectation, see Companion §A6 and the PROPHECY companion.
>
> 🟢 **"BEFORE HIM" — REFERENT AMBIGUITY**
> - **προελεύσεται ἐνώπιον αὐτοῦ** (*proeleusetai enōpion autou*) = "he will go before him." The antecedent of "him" is "the Lord their God" (v.16) — i.e., YHWH; the Gospel later applies the preparation to Yeshua, fusing the referents. The TT keeps "him" without resolving the fusion. Referent: YHWH / Yeshua (Possible).

### **Verse 18**

And Zecharyah (Zechariah) said to the messenger, "By what will I know this? For I am old, and my wife *is* advanced in her days."

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **ZECHARIAH'S DOUBT — CONTRAST WITH MIRYAM (MARY)**
> - **κατὰ τί γνώσομαι τοῦτο;** (*kata ti gnōsomai touto?*) = "by what will I know this?" Zechariah asks for proof, echoing Avraham (Gen 15:8). His question is treated as unbelief (v.20), whereas Miryam's "how will this be?" (v.34) is treated as inquiry, not doubt — the narrative draws the contrast deliberately.

### **Verse 19**

And the messenger, answering, said to him, "I am Gavriel (Gabriel), the *one* standing before God, and I was sent to speak to you and to bring you this good news.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — GAVRIEL (GABRIEL) FROM DANIEL**
> - **ἐγώ εἰμι Γαβριὴλ ὁ παρεστηκὼς ἐνώπιον τοῦ θεοῦ** (*egō eimi Gabriēl ho parestēkōs enōpion tou theou*) = "I am Gabriel, the one standing before God." Gavriel (Hebrew: **Gavriel**, "God *is* my strength / man of God") is the interpreting messenger of Daniel 8:16 and 9:21. His self-identification places the annunciation within the apocalyptic tradition. He is an *angelos* — his speech is not divine-marked (Rule 30). → For Gabriel in Daniel and Second Temple angelology, see Companion §A6.
>
> 🔵 **"GOOD NEWS" — *EUANGELISASTHAI***
> - **εὐαγγελίσασθαί σοι** (*euangelisasthai soi*) = "to bring you good news." The verb (cognate with *euangelion*, "good news/gospel") is frequent in Luke; here it is the messenger's announcement, not yet the proclaimed gospel of the kingdom.

### **Verse 20**

And see, you will be silent and not able to speak until the day these *things* happen, because you did not believe my words, which will be fulfilled in their time."

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"SILENT AND NOT ABLE TO SPEAK" — SIGN AND DISCIPLINE**
> - **σιωπῶν καὶ μὴ δυνάμενος λαλῆσαι** (*siōpōn kai mē dynamenos lalēsai*) = "silent and not able to speak." The muteness is both the requested "sign" (v.18) and a consequence of unbelief. Whether he is also deaf is hinted by the signs made to him in v.62. The TT keeps the surface description.

### **Verse 21**

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"MARVELING AT HIS DELAY" — *ECHRONIZEN***
> - **ἐθαύμαζον ἐν τῷ χρονίζειν** (*ethaumazon en tō chronizein*) = "they were marveling at his delaying." A prolonged stay in the sanctuary was unusual and could cause concern; the imperfect tenses convey ongoing waiting.

### **Verse 22**

And coming out, he was not able to speak to them, and they realized that he had seen a vision in the sanctuary; and he was making signs to them, and remained mute.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"VISION" — *OPTASIAN***
> - **ὀπτασίαν** (*optasian*) = "vision, appearance." The crowd infers the encounter from his muteness and gestures. The word denotes a visual revelatory experience.

### **Verse 23**

And it happened, when the days of his service were fulfilled, *that* he went away to his house.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"SERVICE" — *LEITOURGIAS***
> - **λειτουργίας** (*leitourgias*) = "public service, liturgy" — here the priestly temple duty (the source of English "liturgy"). His week of service complete, he returns home.

### **Verse 24**

And after these days Elisheva (Elizabeth) his wife conceived, and she kept herself hidden five months, saying

> **Notes**

> 🔵 **"KEPT HERSELF HIDDEN" — *PERIEKRYBEN***
> - **περιέκρυβεν ἑαυτὴν μῆνας πέντε** (*periekryben heautēn mēnas pente*) = "she hid herself five months." The reason is unstated — modesty, awe, or awaiting the public reversal of her disgrace (v.25). The TT does not supply a motive.

### **Verse 25**

that "Thus the Lord has done for me in *the* days in which he looked upon *me*, to take away my disgrace among people."

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"TAKE AWAY MY DISGRACE" — *ONEIDOS* (RACHEL ECHO)**
> - **ἀφελεῖν ὄνειδός μου** (*aphelein oneidos mou*) = "to take away my disgrace/reproach." Childlessness as social "reproach" echoes Rachel's words at Yosef's birth (Gen 30:23 LXX, "God has taken away my reproach"). Elizabeth's speech is woven from HB diction. Referent of *kyrios*: YHWH (Probable).

### **Verse 26**

And in the sixth month the messenger Gavriel (Gabriel) was sent from God to a town of the Galilee named Natseret (Nazareth),

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE PARALLEL ANNUNCIATION BEGINS**
> - The second annunciation (vv.26-38) deliberately parallels the first (Gabriel sent; "do not be afraid"; a miraculous conception; a name given; a sign), inviting comparison between John and Yeshua and between Zechariah's doubt and Miryam's faith. → For the parallel-annunciation structure, see Companion §A1.
>
> 🟢 **NATSERET — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Ναζαρέθ** (*Nazareth*) = Natseret. Familiar: Nazareth. An obscure Galilean village, unmentioned in the HB — the lowliness of the setting is part of Luke's reversal theme.

### **Verse 27**

to a virgin betrothed to a man whose name *was* Yosef (Joseph), of the house of David, and the name of the virgin *was* Miryam (Mary).

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"VIRGIN" — *PARTHENON***
> - **παρθένον** (*parthenon*) = "virgin." Unlike Matthew 1:23 (a quotation of Isa 7:14, where *parthenos* renders Hebrew *almah*), here Luke uses *parthenos* in plain narrative, twice, with no OT citation. The TT renders "virgin" without the slash, since no underlying *almah* ambiguity is in play. Mary's own words in v.34 ("I do not know a man") confirm the sense.
>
> 🟢 **TT TRANSLITERATIONS**
> - **Ἰωσήφ** (*Iōsēph*) = Yosef (Hebrew: **Yosef**, "he adds"). Familiar: Joseph.
> - **Μαριάμ** (*Mariam*) = Miryam (Hebrew: **Miryam**). Familiar: Mary. The Davidic descent here attaches to Joseph ("of the house of David").

### **Verse 28**

And coming in to her, he said, "Greetings, *you* having-been-favored *one*, the Lord *is* with you."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — *KECHARITŌMENĒ* ("HAVING-BEEN-FAVORED")**
> - **κεχαριτωμένη** (*kecharitōmenē*) = perfect passive participle of *charitoō* ("to favor, bestow grace upon"), used as a vocative address: "*you* who have been favored." It is a state resulting from God's prior gracious action — NOT the later Latin-derived title "full of grace" (*gratia plena*), which imports a fullness the Greek does not state. The perfect tense indicates a completed bestowal with continuing effect. The TT renders the participle transparently and flags this as a known translation crux. → For *charis*/*charitoō* and the "full of grace" rendering, see Companion §D1. **[lexical-historical — PROBABLE]**
>
> 🟢 **"THE LORD IS WITH YOU" — *HO KYRIOS META SOU***
> - **ὁ κύριος μετὰ σοῦ** (*ho kyrios meta sou*) = "the Lord *is* with you." The phrase echoes HB commissioning assurances (Judg 6:12 to Gid'on; Ruth 2:4). Per Option C, "the Lord" renders *kyrios*; referent: YHWH (Probable). The TT adds *is* in italics (no copula in Greek).

### **Verse 29**

But she was troubled at the word, and was considering what kind of greeting this might be.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"TROUBLED… CONSIDERING" — *DIETARACHTHĒ… DIELOGIZETO***
> - **διεταράχθη… διελογίζετο** (*dietarachthē… dielogizeto*) = "she was troubled… was reckoning/pondering." Like Zechariah (v.12), Mary is troubled — but her reaction is reflective ("what kind of greeting"), not fearful doubt. The imperfect *dielogizeto* conveys ongoing inward deliberation (cf. 2:19).

### **Verse 30**

And the messenger said to her, "Do not be afraid, Miryam (Mary), for you found favor with God.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"FOUND FAVOR" — *HEURES CHARIN***
> - **εὗρες χάριν παρὰ τῷ θεῷ** (*heures charin para tō theō*) = "you found favor with God." *Charis* ("favor, grace") is the key term of the annunciation (cf. *kecharitōmenē*, v.28). "To find favor with/before God" is HB idiom (Noach, Gen 6:8; Mosheh, Ex 33:17 LXX). The TT renders *charis* as "favor" in narrative.

### **Verse 31**

And see, you will conceive in *the* womb and will bear a son, and you will call his name Yeshua (Jesus).

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — NAME GIVEN: YESHUA**
> - **καλέσεις τὸ ὄνομα αὐτοῦ Ἰησοῦν** (*kaleseis to onoma autou Iēsoun*) = "you will call his name Jesus." **Ἰησοῦς** (*Iēsous*) renders Hebrew **Yeshua** ("YHWH saves"), a shortened form of Yehoshua. Unlike Matthew 1:21, Luke does not here supply the etymology — but it surfaces in the canticles (*sōtēria*, "salvation," vv.69-71, 77). The annunciation language ("conceive… bear a son… call his name") echoes Isaiah 7:14 and the Hagar/Yishmael annunciation (Gen 16:11). The TT uses "Yeshua."

### **Verse 32**

This *one* will be great and will be called Son of *the* Most High, and the Lord God will give to him the throne of David his father,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — DAVIDIC SONSHIP (2 SAM 7)**
> - **υἱὸς ὑψίστου κληθήσεται… τὸν θρόνον Δαυὶδ** (*huios hypsistou klēthēsetai… ton thronon Dauid*) = "he will be called Son of *the* Most High… the throne of David." The language draws on the Davidic covenant (2 Sam 7:12-16; Ps 89). **ὕψιστος** (*hypsistos*, "Most High") = LXX *Elyon*, a divine title. "His father David" attaches the Davidic line to Yeshua through Yosef. → For the Davidic-covenant background, see the PROPHECY companion. **[theological — PROBABLE]**

> 🟢 **"THE LORD GOD" — *KYRIOS HO THEOS* (OPTION C)**
> - **κύριος ὁ θεός** (*kyrios ho theos*) = "the Lord God." LXX rendering of YHWH-Elohim. Per Option C, "the Lord"; referent: YHWH (Probable).

### **Verse 33**

and he will reign over the house of Ya'aqov (Jacob) into the ages, and of his kingdom there will be no end."

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"HOUSE OF YA'AQOV (JACOB)… NO END" — DAN 7 / ISA 9 ECHO**
> - **βασιλεύσει… εἰς τοὺς αἰῶνας… οὐκ ἔσται τέλος** (*basileusei… eis tous aiōnas… ouk estai telos*) = "he will reign… into the ages… there will be no end." The everlasting, never-ending kingdom echoes Daniel 7:14 and Isaiah 9:6 (Eng. 9:7). "House of Jacob" = all Israel. The TT keeps "into the ages" rather than smoothing to "forever."

### **Verse 34**

And Miryam (Mary) said to the messenger, "How will this be, since I do not know a man?"

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"HOW WILL THIS BE?" — INQUIRY, NOT DOUBT**
> - **πῶς ἔσται τοῦτο** (*pōs estai touto*) = "how will this be?" Distinguished from Zechariah's "by what will I know?" (v.18): Mary asks the *manner*, not a *proof*, and is not rebuked. The contrast is structural.
>
> 🔵 **"I DO NOT KNOW A MAN" — *ANDRA OU GINŌSKŌ***
> - **ἄνδρα οὐ γινώσκω** (*andra ou ginōskō*) = "I do not know a man" — present tense, the Semitic sexual sense of *ginōskō* (cf. Gen 4:1 LXX). It confirms her present virginity; the TT preserves the idiom rather than paraphrasing.

### **Verse 35**

And answering, the messenger said to her, "*The* holy {a:wind/spirit} will come upon you, and *the* power of *the* Most High will overshadow you; therefore also the *one* being born will be called holy, *the* Son of God.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — *EPISKIASEI* ("WILL OVERSHADOW")**
> - **ἐπισκιάσει σοι** (*episkiasei soi*) = "will overshadow you." The verb *episkiazō* recurs in the LXX for the glory-cloud that "overshadowed" the wilderness dwelling (Ex 40:35) and at the transfiguration (Luke 9:34). It evokes divine presence and enveloping power, not a crudely physical image. The TT renders "overshadow" and flags this as a known crux for review. → For *episkiazō* and the glory-cloud background, see Companion §D2. **[lexical-theological — PROBABLE]**
>
> 🟢 **"WILL BE CALLED HOLY, THE SON OF GOD" — SYNTAX**
> - **τὸ γεννώμενον ἅγιον κληθήσεται υἱὸς θεοῦ** (*to gennōmenon hagion klēthēsetai huios theou*) = the words can parse as "the holy one being born will be called Son of God" or "the one being born will be called holy, *the* Son of God." The placement of *hagion* ("holy") is debated. The TT keeps the looser apposition and notes the ambiguity. **[grammatical — POSSIBLE]**
>
> 🟢 **"HOLY WIND/SPIRIT"**
> - **πνεῦμα ἅγιον** (*pneuma hagion*) = "holy wind/spirit." Anarthrous; slash per locked glossary. Paired here with "power of the Most High" in synonymous parallelism.

### **Verse 36**

And see, Elisheva (Elizabeth) your relative — she also has conceived a son in her old age, and this *is the* sixth month for her who was called barren;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"YOUR RELATIVE" — *SYNGENIS***
> - **ἡ συγγενίς σου** (*hē syngenis sou*) = "your relative/kinswoman." The exact relationship is unstated; "cousin" is later tradition, not the text. Elizabeth is of priestly (Aharon) descent (v.5), which has prompted discussion about Mary's lineage; the TT keeps the unspecified "relative."

### **Verse 37**

because nothing will be impossible with God."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — GEN 18:14 ECHO**
> - **οὐκ ἀδυνατήσει παρὰ τοῦ θεοῦ πᾶν ῥῆμα** (*ouk adynatēsei para tou theou pan rhēma*) = "nothing/no word will be impossible with God." This closely echoes the LXX of Genesis 18:14 spoken to Sarah ("Is anything impossible with God?"). **ῥῆμα** (*rhēma*) means both "word" and "thing/matter" (Semitic *davar*) — "no word/thing." The TT renders "nothing" while the note preserves the *rhēma* double sense and the Sarah allusion. → see Companion §A3.

### **Verse 38**

And Miryam (Mary) said, "See, the *female* servant of the Lord; may it happen to me according to your word." And the messenger went away from her.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"FEMALE SERVANT OF THE LORD" — *DOULĒ KYRIOU***
> - **ἡ δούλη κυρίου** (*hē doulē kyriou*) = "the female servant/slave of the Lord." *Doulē* is the feminine of *doulos* ("slave, bondservant"); the TT marks *female* in italics for the gendered noun and resists softening to "handmaid." It returns at v.48 ("the lowliness of his female servant"). Referent of *kyrios*: YHWH (Probable).
>
> 🔵 **"MAY IT HAPPEN" — *GENOITO***
> - **γένοιτό μοι** (*genoito moi*) = "may it happen to me" — optative of *ginomai*, expressing acceptance/consent (not a passive resignation). Mary's response contrasts with Zechariah's doubt.

### **Verse 39**

And Miryam (Mary), rising up in those days, went into the hill country with haste, to a town of Yehudah (Judah),

> **Notes**

> 🔵 **"A TOWN OF YEHUDAH (JUDAH)" — UNNAMED**

and she entered into the house of Zecharyah (Zechariah) and greeted Elisheva (Elizabeth).

> - **ἠσπάσατο** (*ēspasato*) = "she greeted/embraced." The greeting (*aspasmos*, v.41, 44) is the trigger for the unborn child's leap — the narrative pivot of the visitation scene.

### **Verse 41**

And it happened, when Elisheva (Elizabeth) heard the greeting of Miryam (Mary), *that* the baby leaped in her womb, and Elizabeth was filled with *the* holy {a:wind/spirit},

### **Verse 42**

and she called out with a great cry and said, "Blessed *are* you among women, and blessed *is* the fruit of your womb.

> 🟢 **"BLESSED ARE YOU AMONG WOMEN" — *EULOGĒMENĒ***

### **Verse 43**

And from where *is* this to me, that the mother of my lord should come to me?

> **Notes**

> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "MY LORD" — *HO KYRIOS MOU* (HUMAN REFERENT)**

Per Option C, this is the non-divine "lord" usage, NOT capitalized as the divine "Lord." The TT renders "my lord" and tracks the referent shift. ### **Verse 44**

> - **ἐν ἀγαλλιάσει** (*en agalliasei*) = "in exultation." The same intense-joy root as v.14 and v.47, tying John's prenatal leap to the chapter's joy motif. Elizabeth interprets the leap as the child's joy.

### **Verse 45**

> **Notes**

"From the Lord" (*para kyriou*): referent YHWH (Probable). The clause may also read "that there will be a fulfillment of what was spoken to her *from the Lord*."

### **Verse 46**

And Miryam (Mary) said, "My soul magnifies the Lord,

> 🟢 **"MAGNIFIES" — *MEGALYNEI* (PS 34:4 ECHO)**

### **Verse 47**

and my spirit exulted in God my savior,

> **Notes**

> 🟡 **"GOD MY SAVIOR" — *SŌTĒRI***

### **Verse 48**

because he looked upon the lowliness of his *female* servant. For see, from now *on* all generations will call me blessed,

### **Verse 49**

because the Mighty *One* did great *things* for me, and holy *is* his name,

> - **ὁ δυνατός… ἅγιον τὸ ὄνομα αὐτοῦ** (*ho dynatos… hagion to onoma autou*) = "the Mighty One… holy *is* his name." Echoes Ps 111:9 (Eng. 110:9 LXX, "holy and awesome is his name") and Deut 10:21. The TT supplies *One* and *is* in italics. Referent: YHWH (Probable).

### **Verse 50**

and his mercy *is* for generations and generations to those who fear him.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"MERCY… GENERATIONS" — PS 103:17 ECHO**
> - **τὸ ἔλεος… εἰς γενεὰς καὶ γενεάς** (*to eleos… eis geneas kai geneas*) = "mercy… for generations and generations." *Eleos* ("mercy") translates HB *chesed* (covenant-loyalty); the phrasing echoes Ps 103:17 (Eng. 102:17 LXX). The Semitic doubling "generations and generations" is preserved.

### **Verse 51**

He did *a* mighty *deed* with his arm; he scattered *the* proud in *the* thought of their heart.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"MIGHTY DEED WITH HIS ARM" — *KRATOS EN BRACHIONI***
> - **ἐποίησεν κράτος ἐν βραχίονι αὐτοῦ** (*epoiēsen kratos en brachioni autou*) = "he did a mighty *deed* with his arm." "The arm of YHWH" is HB imagery for saving power (Ex 6:6, Ps 89:11). The series of aorists (vv.51-53) may be gnomic (timeless truths) or prophetic-past (the reversal as already accomplished); the TT keeps the past tense and notes the ambiguity. **[grammatical — POSSIBLE]**

### **Verse 52**

He pulled down rulers from thrones and lifted up *the* lowly;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE REVERSAL MOTIF**
> - **καθεῖλεν δυνάστας ἀπὸ θρόνων καὶ ὕψωσεν ταπεινούς** (*katheilen dynastas apo thronōn kai hypsōsen tapeinous*) = "he pulled down rulers from thrones and lifted up *the* lowly." The great-reversal theme — central to Luke (cf. 6:20-26) — draws on 1 Sam 2:7-8 (Hannah) and Ps 113:7-8. *Tapeinous* ("lowly") ties back to *tapeinōsis* (v.48). → For the reversal theme across Luke, see Companion §F.

### **Verse 53**

*the* hungry he filled with good *things*, and *the* rich he sent away empty.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"HUNGRY… RICH" — PS 107:9 ECHO**
> - **πεινῶντας… πλουτοῦντας** (*peinōntas… ploutountas*) = "hungry… rich." Echoes Ps 107:9 (Eng. 106:9 LXX) and 1 Sam 2:5. The economic reversal is concrete, not only spiritual; the TT does not spiritualize "hungry"/"rich."

### **Verse 54**

He helped Israel his servant, to remember mercy,

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"ISRAEL HIS SERVANT" — *PAIDOS AUTOU* (ISA 41:8)**
> - **ἀντελάβετο Ἰσραὴλ παιδὸς αὐτοῦ** (*antelabeto Israēl paidos autou*) = "he helped Israel his servant/child." *Pais* = "servant" or "child"; "Israel my servant" is Isaian (Isa 41:8-9, 42:1). "To remember mercy" (*mnēsthēnai eleous*) closes the song's covenant theme.

### **Verse 55**

just as he spoke to our fathers, to Avraham (Abraham) and to his seed, into the age."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE ABRAHAMIC PROMISE**
> - **τῷ Ἀβραὰμ καὶ τῷ σπέρματι αὐτοῦ εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα** (*tō Abraam kai tō spermati autou eis ton aiōna*) = "to Abraham and to his seed, into the age." The Magnificat grounds the reversal in the covenant with Avraham (Gen 17:7, 22:17-18). **σπέρμα** (*sperma*, "seed") = offspring/posterity. "Into the age" (*eis ton aiōna*) is preserved rather than smoothed to "forever." → see the PROPHECY companion.

### **Verse 56**

And Miryam (Mary) remained with her about three months, and returned to her house.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"ABOUT THREE MONTHS" — TIMING**
> - **ὡς μῆνας τρεῖς** (*hōs mēnas treis*) = "about three months." This brings the visit near to Elizabeth's delivery (the sixth month at the annunciation, v.36). Whether Mary stayed through the birth is left open; the narrative reports her departure before recounting it (v.57).

### **Verse 57**

And the time was fulfilled for Elisheva (Elizabeth) to give birth, and she bore a son.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"THE TIME WAS FULFILLED" — *EPLĒSTHĒ HO CHRONOS***
> - **ἐπλήσθη ὁ χρόνος τοῦ τεκεῖν** (*eplēsthē ho chronos tou tekein*) = "the time was fulfilled for her to bear." Septuagintal phrasing (cf. Gen 25:24 LXX of Rivqah). The fulfillment motif (*plēthō*/*plēroō*) runs through the chapter.

### **Verse 58**

And her neighbors and relatives heard that the Lord had magnified his mercy with her, and they were rejoicing with her.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"MAGNIFIED HIS MERCY" — *EMEGALYNEN TO ELEOS***
> - **ἐμεγάλυνεν… τὸ ἔλεος αὐτοῦ** (*emegalynen… to eleos autou*) = "magnified his mercy." *Megalynō* echoes Mary's "magnifies" (v.46); *eleos* ("mercy/chesed") ties to the canticles. Referent of *kyrios*: YHWH (Probable).

### **Verse 59**

And it happened on the eighth day *that* they came to circumcise the child, and they were calling him by the name of his father, Zecharyah (Zechariah).

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"ON THE EIGHTH DAY… CIRCUMCISE" — GEN 17:12**
> - **τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῇ ὀγδόῃ… περιτεμεῖν** (*tē hēmera tē ogdoē… peritemein*) = "on the eighth day… to circumcise." The eighth-day circumcision fulfills the Avrahamic covenant command (Gen 17:12; Lev 12:3). Naming at circumcision is the cultural setting of the dispute that follows.

### **Verse 60**

And his mother, answering, said, "No; rather he will be called Yochanan (John)."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"NO; RATHER… JOHN" — THE NAME INSISTED**
> - **οὐχί, ἀλλὰ κληθήσεται Ἰωάννης** (*ouchi, alla klēthēsetai Iōannēs*) = "No; rather he will be called John." Elizabeth knows the name commanded to the (mute) Zechariah (v.13) — how is unstated. The TT keeps the abrupt "No; rather."

### **Verse 61**

And they said to her, "*There* is no one of your kindred who is called by this name."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"NO ONE OF YOUR KINDRED" — CUSTOM**
> - **οὐδείς ἐστιν ἐκ τῆς συγγενείας σου** (*oudeis estin ek tēs syngeneias sou*) = "no one of your kindred." Naming after a relative was customary; "John" breaks the family pattern, intensifying the surprise. The TT adds *There* in italics.

### **Verse 62**

And they were making signs to his father *as to* what he might want him to be called.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"MAKING SIGNS" — ZECHARIAH ALSO DEAF?**
> - **ἐνένευον** (*eneneuon*) = "they were making signs/nodding." That they gesture rather than speak to Zechariah suggests he was deaf as well as mute (cf. v.20, *kōphos* can mean both). The TT keeps the surface action; the inference is noted.

### **Verse 63**

And asking for a writing tablet, he wrote, saying, "Yochanan (John) is his name." And they all marveled.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"WRITING TABLET… JOHN IS HIS NAME" — *PINAKIDION***
> - **πινακίδιον** (*pinakidion*) = "small writing tablet" (wax-coated). **Ἰωάννης ἐστὶν ὄνομα αὐτοῦ** = "John is his name" — present tense, as if already settled by divine decree. Zechariah's written agreement with Elizabeth resolves the dispute and prompts amazement.

### **Verse 64**

And his mouth was opened immediately, and his tongue *loosed*, and he was speaking, blessing God.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"MOUTH OPENED… BLESSING GOD" — SIGN COMPLETED**
> - **ἀνεῴχθη… τὸ στόμα αὐτοῦ… καὶ ἡ γλῶσσα αὐτοῦ** (*aneōchthē… to stoma autou… kai hē glōssa autou*) = "his mouth was opened… and his tongue." The verb governs "mouth"; "tongue" is supplied with an implied verb — the TT adds *loosed* in italics for English. His first restored words are praise, framing the Benedictus that follows.

### **Verse 65**

And fear came upon all who lived around them, and in the whole hill country of Yehudah (Judea) all these matters were being talked about,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"FEAR… TALKED ABOUT" — *PHOBOS… DIELALEITO***
> - **ἐγένετο… φόβος… διελαλεῖτο πάντα τὰ ῥήματα ταῦτα** (*egeneto… phobos… dielaleito panta ta rhēmata tauta*) = "fear came… all these matters were being talked about." *Phobos* ("fear, awe") is the proper response to divine action (cf. v.12). *Rhēmata* = "words/matters" (Semitic *devarim*). The TT keeps "matters."

### **Verse 66**

and all who heard *them* laid *them* up in their heart, saying, "What then will this child be?" For *the* hand of *the* Lord was with him.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"THE HAND OF THE LORD" — *CHEIR KYRIOU***
> - **χεὶρ κυρίου ἦν μετ' αὐτοῦ** (*cheir kyriou ēn met' autou*) = "*the* hand of *the* Lord was with him." HB idiom for divine power and protection (cf. 1 Sam 3:19 of Shemu'el). Per Option C, "the Lord" = YHWH (Probable). "Laid up in their heart" echoes the same verb used of Mary (2:19, 2:51).

---

### **Verse 67**

And Zecharyah (Zechariah) his father was filled with *the* holy {a:wind/spirit} and prophesied, saying,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE BENEDICTUS (vv.68-79): HUMAN PROPHECY, RENDERED AS PROSE**
> - Verse 67 is the narrative introduction; the canticle proper begins at v.68. Zechariah's song is **human** prophetic speech "filled with *the* holy wind/spirit" — but spoken by Zechariah, not God; it is therefore NOT divine-marked (Rule 30). Per editorial decision (luke.md L-002), it is authored as flowing prose. → For the Benedictus structure, see the PROPHECY companion. **[theological — PROBABLE]**
>
> 🟢 **"FILLED… AND PROPHESIED" — *EPLĒSTHĒ… EPROPHĒTEUSEN***
> - **ἐπλήσθη πνεύματος ἁγίου καὶ ἐπροφήτευσεν** (*eplēsthē pneumatos hagiou kai eprophēteusen*) = "was filled with holy wind/spirit and prophesied." The same "filled" verb describes Elizabeth (v.41) and is anticipated for John (v.15) — the wind/spirit empowers human speech, which remains human (not divine-marked).

### **Verse 68**

"Blessed *be the* Lord, the God of Israel, because he looked upon and made redemption for his people,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — *EULOGĒTOS KYRIOS* — HB BERAKHAH OPENING**
> - **εὐλογητὸς κύριος ὁ θεὸς τοῦ Ἰσραήλ** (*eulogētos kyrios ho theos tou Israēl*) = "Blessed *be the* Lord, the God of Israel" — the standard HB blessing-formula (Ps 41:14; 72:18; 106:48). Referent of *kyrios*: YHWH (Probable, Option C). The TT adds *be the* in italics for the elliptical *eulogētos* (no copula or article in Greek).
>
> 🔵 **"MADE REDEMPTION" — *EPOIĒSEN LYTRŌSIN***
> - **ἐποίησεν λύτρωσιν τῷ λαῷ αὐτοῦ** (*epoiēsen lytrōsin tō laō autou*) = "made redemption for his people." *Lytrōsis* ("ransom, redemption") evokes the Exodus deliverance (cf. Ps 111:9). "Looked upon" (*epeskepsato*) is HB visitation language for God's saving intervention.

### **Verse 69**

and raised up a horn of salvation for us in *the* house of David his servant,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "HORN OF SALVATION" — *KERAS SŌTĒRIAS* (PS 18:3)**
> - **ἤγειρεν κέρας σωτηρίας ἡμῖν** (*ēgeiren keras sōtērias hēmin*) = "raised up a horn of salvation for us." The "horn" is an HB symbol of strength and victorious power (Ps 18:3 [Eng. 18:2]; 132:17, "I will make a horn sprout for David"); *sōtēria* = "salvation, deliverance." The image is martial/royal, not merely spiritual. "House of David his servant" anchors the messianic hope in the Davidic line. → see Glossary, *sōtēria*; PROPHECY companion.

### **Verse 70**

just as he spoke through *the* mouth of his holy prophets from of old,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"THROUGH THE MOUTH OF HIS HOLY PROPHETS" — PROPHETIC FULFILLMENT**
> - **διὰ στόματος τῶν ἁγίων ἀπ' αἰῶνος προφητῶν αὐτοῦ** (*dia stomatos tōn hagiōn ap' aiōnos prophētōn autou*) = "through *the* mouth of his holy prophets from of old (lit. 'from *the* age')." *Dia* ("through") makes the prophets the instruments of God's prior speech. The TT keeps "from of old" for *ap' aiōnos*.

### **Verse 71**

salvation from our enemies and from *the* hand of all who hate us,

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"SALVATION FROM OUR ENEMIES" — POLITICAL DELIVERANCE**
> - **σωτηρίαν ἐξ ἐχθρῶν ἡμῶν** (*sōtērian ex echthrōn hēmōn*) = "salvation from our enemies." The deliverance is framed in concrete, national-political terms (Ps 106:10; 18:18), continuing the Davidic-victory imagery. The TT does not spiritualize "enemies." Referent of the salvation's author: YHWH.

---

### **Verse 72**

to do mercy with our fathers and to remember his holy covenant,

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"DO MERCY… REMEMBER COVENANT" — *POIĒSAI ELEOS… DIATHĒKĒS***
> - **ποιῆσαι ἔλεος μετὰ τῶν πατέρων ἡμῶν καὶ μνησθῆναι διαθήκης ἁγίας αὐτοῦ** (*poiēsai eleos meta tōn paterōn hēmōn kai mnēsthēnai diathēkēs hagias autou*) = "to do mercy with our fathers and to remember his holy covenant." *Diathēkē* = "covenant" (LXX for *berit*). The doublet "mercy… covenant" restates the *chesed* theme of the Magnificat (v.50).

### **Verse 73**

*the* oath that he swore to Avraham (Abraham) our father, to grant to us

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE OATH TO AVRAHAM (GEN 22:16-18)**
> - **ὅρκον ὃν ὤμοσεν πρὸς Ἀβραὰμ τὸν πατέρα ἡμῶν** (*horkon hon ōmosen pros Abraam ton patera hēmōn*) = "*the* oath that he swore to Abraham our father." The reference is the sworn covenant of Gen 22:16-18 (after the binding of Yitschaq). Like the Magnificat (v.55), the Benedictus grounds salvation in the Avrahamic oath, not only the Davidic. → see PROPHECY companion.

### **Verse 74**

that, rescued from *the* hand of enemies, *we* might serve him without fear,

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"SERVE HIM WITHOUT FEAR" — *LATREUEIN APHOBŌS***
> - **ἀφόβως… λατρεύειν αὐτῷ** (*aphobōs… latreuein autō*) = "to serve him without fear." *Latreuō* = cultic/religious service, worship. The goal of deliverance is unhindered worship — the political and the cultic held together. The TT supplies *we* in italics for the implied subject of the infinitive.

### **Verse 75**

in holiness and righteousness before him all our days.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"HOLINESS AND RIGHTEOUSNESS" — *HOSIOTĒTI KAI DIKAIOSYNĒ***
> - **ἐν ὁσιότητι καὶ δικαιοσύνῃ** (*en hosiotēti kai dikaiosynē*) = "in holiness and righteousness." A standard pairing for covenant faithfulness; *dikaiosynē* ("righteousness") ties back to the description of Zechariah and Elizabeth as *dikaioi* (v.6). "All our days" closes the purpose clause.

### **Verse 76**

And you, child, will be called *a* prophet of *the* Most High, for you will go before *the* Lord to prepare his ways,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — JOHN AS PROPHET; "BEFORE THE LORD" (MAL 3:1 / ISA 40:3)**
> - **προφήτης ὑψίστου κληθήσῃ… προπορεύσῃ… ἐνώπιον κυρίου ἑτοιμάσαι ὁδοὺς αὐτοῦ** (*prophētēs hypsistou klēthēsē… proporeusē… enōpion kyriou hetoimasai hodous autou*) = "you will be called *a* prophet of *the* Most High… you will go before *the* Lord to prepare his ways." This fuses Malachi 3:1 ("I send my messenger to prepare the way before me") and Isaiah 40:3 ("prepare the way of YHWH"). In the source texts "the Lord" = YHWH; the Gospel applies the preparation to Yeshua, fusing the referents — the TT preserves the fusion. Referent of *kyriou*: YHWH / Yeshua (Possible). → see PROPHECY companion. **[theological — PROBABLE]**

### **Verse 77**

to give knowledge of salvation to his people in *the* release of their sins,

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"RELEASE OF THEIR SINS" — *APHESEI HAMARTIŌN***
> - **ἐν ἀφέσει ἁμαρτιῶν αὐτῶν** (*en aphesei hamartiōn autōn*) = "in *the* release of their sins." *Aphesis* = "release, letting-go, forgiveness" (per locked glossary, "release" preserving the root sense, cf. *apolyō* in Matt 1:19). Salvation is here defined as the release of sins — anticipating John's ministry (3:3). The TT keeps "release."

### **Verse 78**

through *the* tender mercy of our God, in which *the* {a:rising/dawn} from on high will look upon us,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — *ANATOLĒ* ("RISING / DAWN / SHOOT") — TRANSLATION CRUX**
> - **ἀνατολὴ ἐξ ὕψους** (*anatolē ex hypsous*) = "*the* rising/dawn from on high." *Anatolē* carries two distinct senses: (1) astronomical "rising / dawn / daybreak" (the rising sun or star, fitting v.79 "to shine upon those in darkness"), and (2) botanical "shoot / branch / sprout" — the LXX rendering of the messianic *tsemach* ("Branch," Jer 23:5; Zech 3:8, 6:12). The TT marks {a:rising/dawn} as the primary preserved ambiguity (the light imagery of v.79 favors "dawn") while the note flags the "Branch" reading. This is a known crux for Hellenist review. → For *anatolē* and the *tsemach*/Branch background, see Companion §D3. **[lexical — PROBABLE]**
>
> 🔵 **"TENDER MERCY" — *SPLANCHNA ELEOUS***
> - **διὰ σπλάγχνα ἐλέους θεοῦ ἡμῶν** (*dia splanchna eleous theou hēmōn*) = "through *the* tender mercy (lit. 'bowels of mercy') of our God." *Splanchna* = the inner organs, the seat of compassion in Semitic idiom (cf. *rachamim*). The TT renders the sense ("tender mercy") and notes the visceral idiom. → see Glossary, *splanchna*.

### **Verse 79**

to shine upon those sitting in darkness and *in the* shadow of death, to guide our feet into *the* way of peace."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — ISA 9:1 / PS 107:10 ECHO**
> - **ἐπιφᾶναι τοῖς ἐν σκότει καὶ σκιᾷ θανάτου καθημένοις** (*epiphanai tois en skotei kai skia thanatou kathēmenois*) = "to shine upon those sitting in darkness and *in the* shadow of death." This echoes Isaiah 9:1 (Eng. 9:2) and Ps 107:10 (Eng. 106:10). "Shadow of death" (*skia thanatou*) renders HB *tsalmavet* (cf. Ps 23:4). "Way of peace" (*hodon eirēnēs*) = HB *derech shalom* (Isa 59:8). The TT preserves the layered allusion.

### **Verse 80**

And the child grew and was strengthened in spirit, and he was in the wilderness *places* until *the* day of his showing to Israel.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"GREW… IN THE WILDERNESS" — GROWTH-FORMULA + ELIYAHU SETTING**
> - **τὸ δὲ παιδίον ηὔξανεν καὶ ἐκραταιοῦτο πνεύματι** (*to de paidion ēuxanen kai ekrataiouto pneumati*) = "the child grew and was strengthened in spirit." The growth-formula echoes Shimshon (Judg 13:24-25) and Shemu'el (1 Sam 2:26), and recurs for Yeshua (2:40, 52). The wilderness setting prefigures John's prophetic ministry (3:2) and the Eliyahu (Elijah) typology. *En tais erēmois* = "in the wilderness *places*"; the TT adds *places* in italics for the plural. "Showing/manifestation" (*anadeixis*) = his public appearance.

## CROSS-CHAPTER TRACKING (HB -> Luke)

**1 Sam 1-2 -> Luke 1 — Channah (Hannah) and the Magnificat:**

| Element | 1 Samuel 1-2 (Hannah) | Luke 1 (Elizabeth / Mary) |
|---------|------------------------|----------------------------|
| Barrenness | Hannah barren, reproached (1:2-7) | Elizabeth barren, "disgrace" removed (1:7, 25) |
| Vow / petition | "look upon the lowliness of your female servant" (1:11) | "looked upon the lowliness of his female servant" (1:48) |
| Song of reversal | "the bows of the mighty are broken… the hungry cease *to hunger*" (2:4-5) | "pulled down rulers… filled the hungry" (1:52-53) |
| Child dedicated | Shemu'el given to YHWH, grows before him (2:21, 26) | John grows and is strengthened in spirit (1:80) |

The Magnificat is modeled on Hannah's song; both celebrate a divine reversal centered on a child given against the odds.

**Gen 18 -> Luke 1:37 — "nothing impossible with God":**

| Element | Gen 18 (Sarah) | Luke 1:37 |
|---------|----------------|-----------|
| Aged, barren | Sarah past childbearing (18:11) | Elizabeth "advanced in days," barren (1:7, 18) |
| Divine word | "Is anything impossible (*adynatēsei*) with God?" (18:14 LXX) | "Nothing/no word will be impossible (*adynatēsei*) with God" (1:37) |
| Promise of a son | Yitschaq promised | John and Yeshua announced |

The Gabriel oracle to Mary quotes the Sarah promise nearly verbatim from the LXX, binding the new births to the Abrahamic line.

**Mal 3:1, 3:23-24 / Isa 40:3 -> Luke 1:17, 76 — John as the returning Eliyahu (Elijah):**

| Element | Malachi / Isaiah | Luke 1 |
|---------|-------------------|--------|
| Forerunner | "I send my messenger to prepare the way before me" (Mal 3:1) | "you will go before *the* Lord to prepare his ways" (1:76) |
| Elijah | "I send Eliyahu… he will turn the hearts of fathers to children" (Mal 3:23-24) | "in *the* spirit and power of Elijah… to turn back hearts of fathers to children" (1:17) |
| Way prepared | "prepare the way of YHWH" (Isa 40:3) | "to prepare for the Lord a people made ready" (1:17) |

Luke casts John as the prophesied Elijah-figure; the source texts speak of YHWH, while the Gospel fuses the referent toward Yeshua — the TT preserves the fusion (Rule 13: Possible).

**2 Sam 7 / Ps 18, 132 -> Luke 1:32-33, 69 — Davidic throne and "horn of salvation":**

| Element | 2 Sam 7 / Psalms | Luke 1 |
|---------|-------------------|--------|
| Eternal throne | "I will establish his throne forever" (2 Sam 7:13) | "the throne of David his father… no end" (1:32-33) |

Both annunciation and Benedictus root the child's destiny in the Davidic covenant and its psalms of royal victory.

**Gen 22:16-18 / Gen 17:7 -> Luke 1:55, 72-73 — the oath to Avraham (Abraham):**

| Element | Genesis | Luke 1 |

| Covenant kept | everlasting covenant (17:7) | "to remember his holy covenant" (1:72) |

Both canticles ground the salvation they celebrate in the Abrahamic covenant and its sworn oath, not only the Davidic promise.
