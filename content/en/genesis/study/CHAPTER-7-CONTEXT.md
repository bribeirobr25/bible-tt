## A. Hebrew Text Features Exposed by TT

### A1. "Seven seven" — distributive ambiguity preserved
**[TEXTUAL — VERIFIED]**

The TT renders שִׁבְעָה שִׁבְעָה (*shiv'ah shiv'ah*) as "seven seven" — copying how the Hebrew repeats the number. Most English translations smooth this to "seven pairs" (NIV, ESV) or "seven of each" (NRSV). But the Hebrew does not say "pairs" — it simply repeats the number. Whether this means seven individual animals or seven pairs (fourteen) is genuinely ambiguous. The TT preserves the repetition and notes the ambiguity rather than resolving it.

### A2.  "Two from all" vs. "seven seven" — the central tension
**[TEXTUAL — VERIFIED]**

Gen 6:19: "two from all you shall bring." Gen 7:2: "seven seven" of the clean, "two" of the unclean. Gen 7:9: "two two they came." The TT translates all three as the MT reads them, without harmonization (Rule 22). This is the most visible numerical tension in the flood narrative. Traditional harmonization reads 6:19 as a general statement refined by 7:2. Source-critical analysis assigns the passages to different documentary traditions. The TT presents the text without choosing between these frameworks.

### A3. Clean/unclean distinction — before Sinai
**[TEXTUAL — VERIFIED]**

The distinction between clean and unclean animals appears at 7:2 — centuries before the detailed laws of Leviticus 11 and Deuteronomy 14. The text assumes the reader already knows the distinction or that it predates Moses. This is a textual feature the TT preserves without importing later Jewish legal categories.

### A4. Cosmological mechanism — Day 2 reversed
**[TEXTUAL — VERIFIED]**

Gen 7:11: "all the fountains of the great deep burst open, and the windows of the sky were opened." This precisely reverses Gen 1:6–7, where the raqia (the solid dome of the sky) separated waters above from waters below. The flood dismantles the structure built on Day 2: the *tehom* (the deep, present since 1:2) breaks through from below, while the waters above the raqia pour down through sky-windows. The flood is not merely "a lot of rain" — it is the undoing of creation's water-boundary.

### A5. "Standing thing" (*yequm*) — rare vocabulary
**[TEXTUAL — VERIFIED]**

Gen 7:4,23: הַיְקוּם (*ha-yequm*) = "the standing thing" — everything that stands or exists. This word appears only here and at Deut 11:6 in the entire Hebrew Bible. Its rarity suggests it is either an old or specialized term for "all living things on dry ground."

### A6. "Breath of the spirit of life" — triple compound
**[TEXTUAL — VERIFIED]**

Gen 7:22: נִשְׁמַת רוּחַ חַיִּים (*nishmat ruach chayyim*) = "breath of the spirit/wind of life." This three-word combination appears nowhere else. It combines Gen 2:7 (*nishmat chayyim*, the breath of life blown into the first human) with Gen 6:17 (*ruach chayyim*, the spirit of life in all flesh). At the moment of total destruction, the text reaches for the fullest possible way to name what is being lost.

### A7. YHWH shuts the tebah — mid-verse name shift
**[TEXTUAL — VERIFIED]**

Gen 7:16: "as God commanded him; and YHWH shut behind him." Both divine names in a single verse, performing different functions. Elohim commands (cosmic authority); YHWH seals (personal protector). The act of closing the tebah is attributed to the personal, intimate divine name. The TT preserves both names exactly as the MT reads.

### A8. Obedience formula — four occurrences
**[TEXTUAL — VERIFIED]**

The phrase "Noach did according to all that [God/YHWH] commanded" appears at 6:22, 7:5, 7:9, 7:16 — with alternating divine names. The repetition is a deliberate pattern: Noach's response to every command is silent obedience. He does not speak a recorded word until 9:25.

## B. Ancient Near Eastern Parallels

### B1. Mesopotamian flood — boarding and sealing
**[COMPARATIVE PARALLEL — VERIFIED]**

In Gilgamesh XI, Utnapishtim boards his vessel, and the craftsman Puzur-Amurri seals the door. In Atrahasis, the god Enki gives the boarding command. In Genesis, God/YHWH commands entry AND personally seals the vessel (7:16). The divine sealing is unique to the biblical account — no craftsman intermediary.

**Source:** George, A.R., *The Babylonian Gilgamesh Epic*, Oxford, 2003, Tablet XI:93-96.

### B2. The flood mechanism — above and below
**[COMPARATIVE PARALLEL — VERIFIED]**

Mesopotamian flood traditions also describe water from multiple cosmic sources. Atrahasis and Gilgamesh reference storms, wind, and rising waters. But only Genesis specifies a precise cosmological mechanism: "fountains of the great deep" (subterranean) + "windows of the sky" (celestial) — directly reversing the creation cosmology of Gen 1:6-7. The structural relationship between creation and de-creation is unique to the biblical text.

**Source:** Walton, J.H., *The Lost World of the Flood*, IVP Academic, 2018.

### B3. Duration — 7, 40, and 150
**[COMPARATIVE PARALLEL — PROBABLE]**

The Gilgamesh flood lasts 7 days (Tablet XI:127-131). Genesis uses multiple overlapping durations: 7-day warning (7:4), 40-day rain (7:12), 150-day prevailing (7:24). The number 7 appears in both traditions; 40 and 150 are distinctive to the biblical account. Whether the different durations reflect different source traditions or a single complex chronology is debated.

**Source:** Finkel, I., *The Ark Before Noah*, Hodder & Stoughton, 2014.

### B4. Clean/unclean animals in ANE context
**[COMPARATIVE PARALLEL — POSSIBLE]**

Mesopotamian flood traditions do not distinguish between clean and unclean animals aboard the vessel. The clean/unclean distinction in Gen 7:2 reflects a specifically Israelite cultic category. Some scholars argue this indicates a later (Priestly) addition to the flood narrative. Others argue the distinction is pre-Mosaic and independent. The TT does not adjudicate.

**Source:** Milgrom, J., *Leviticus 1-16*, Anchor Bible Commentary, 1991.

### B5. The Eridu Genesis (Ziusudra) — the oldest flood tradition
**[COMPARATIVE PARALLEL — VERIFIED]**

The oldest surviving flood narrative is the Sumerian Eridu Genesis (c. 1600 BCE for the extant tablet, originating earlier). Ziusudra ("Life of Long Days") is the Sumerian flood hero, corresponding to the Akkadian Utnapishtim ("he found life") and Atrahasis ("exceeding wise"). Key features: the flood lasts seven days (matching Gen 7:4,10); after the flood Utu (the sun) appears and Ziusudra opens a window and offers sacrifice — structurally parallel to Noach opening the *challon* (8:6) and building the altar (8:20); Ziusudra is taken to live forever in Dilmun (the Sumerian paradise) — Genesis gives Noach no immortality; he dies at 950. These parallels show a shared ancient flood-discourse. The direction of dependence and the mechanism of transmission are debated; the TT does not adjudicate.

**Source:** Jacobsen, T., *The Harps That Once: Sumerian Poetry in Translation*, Yale, 1987; ETCSL, University of Oxford, "The Flood Story" (1.7.4).

### B6. Atrahasis — closer parallel than Gilgamesh
**[COMPARATIVE PARALLEL — VERIFIED]**

Recent scholarship argues that Genesis is more directly related to the Atrahasis tradition than to Gilgamesh: (1) both Atrahasis and Genesis place the flood a few generations after creation; Gilgamesh embeds the flood in an immortality narrative; (2) both use third-person narration; Gilgamesh uses first-person retrospective; (3) in Atrahasis (3.1.37) the flood is announced "for the seventh night," directly paralleling Gen 7:4 ("in seven more days"); (4) Atrahasis covers creation → population growth → divine frustration → flood, the same macro-sequence as Genesis 1–9. The current companions' implicit framing of Gilgamesh as the primary parallel should be understood as the more famous but not the closest textual relationship.

**Source:** Lambert, W.G. & Millard, A.R., *Atra-ḫasīs: The Babylonian Story of the Flood*, Oxford, 1969; Hendel, R.S., *The Text of Genesis 1–11*, Oxford, 1998.

## C. Historical and Archaeological Context

### C1. Flood chronology and ancient calendars
**[HISTORICAL / ARCHAEOLOGICAL — PROBABLE]**

The flood narrative's precise chronology (month/day markers at 7:11, 8:4, 8:5, 8:13, 8:14) totals approximately one solar year (370-371 days). Some scholars argue this reflects a 364-day calendar (known from Jubilees and Qumran texts) adjusted to a solar year. Others see it as a literary framework. The precision itself is a textual feature regardless of calendrical system.

**Source:** Cassuto, U., *A Commentary on the Book of Genesis*, vol. 2, 1964, pp. 50-55.

## D. Linguistic and Philological Deep Dives

### D1. *Arubbot ha-shamayim* — windows of the sky
**[TEXTUAL — VERIFIED]**

אֲרֻבֹּת הַשָּׁמַיִם = "windows/sluices of the sky." The noun *arubbah* means a latticed opening or sluice-gate. It implies an architectural feature in the sky — not metaphor, but the cosmological model in which the raqia is a solid structure with openings. The same phrase appears at 2 Kgs 7:2,19 and Mal 3:10 ("windows of the sky" for abundant rain/blessing).

### D2. *Ma'yenot tehom rabbah* — fountains of the great deep
**[TEXTUAL — VERIFIED]**

מַעְיְנֹת תְּהוֹם רַבָּה = "fountains/springs of the great deep." *Tehom* is the primordial deep from Gen 1:2, related to the Akkadian *Tiamat* (though not derived from it). *Rabbah* = "great/abundant." The subterranean waters are not ordinary groundwater — they are the primordial *tehom* beneath the earth's surface, held back since creation.

**Source:** Tsumura, D.T., *Creation and Destruction*, Eisenbrauns, 2005. Tsumura demonstrates that *tehom* is a common Semitic word for "deep waters," not a personified deity borrowed from Babylonian *Tiamat*. Genesis has demythologized the concept.

### D3. Clean/unclean as pre-Mosaic cultic category
**[TEXTUAL — VERIFIED]**

The distinction between "clean" (*tahor*) and "unclean" animals appears at Gen 7:2, long before the detailed legislation of Leviticus 11 and Deuteronomy 14. The text presents this as an already-known category, not as a new revelation. The primary purpose at this stage is sacrificial rather than dietary: clean animals are required for the burnt offerings Noach will offer after the flood (8:20). This explains the numerical asymmetry — "seven seven" of clean animals provides the surplus needed for sacrifice while preserving breeding pairs. The TT notes the distinction without importing later halakhic categories.

**Source:** Milgrom, J., *Leviticus 1-16*, Anchor Bible Commentary, 1991, pp. 718-736.

## E. Scientific Correspondence and Non-Correspondence

### E1. The flood mechanism and geological observation
**[SCIENTIFIC COMPARISON — POSSIBLE]**

Genesis 7:11 describes the flood as coming from two sources simultaneously: "all the fountains of the great deep (*tehom rabbah*) burst open, and the windows of the skies were opened." This is a cosmological reversal — the waters separated on Day 2 (Gen 1:6–7) reunite. The text presents this as a total event: waters cover "all the high mountains under all the skies" (7:19), with specific dates (second month, seventeenth day). Modern geology does not find evidence of a global flood in the stratigraphic record. There is no single depositional layer covering all continents from one event. However, significant regional flood events in Mesopotamia are well-attested archaeologically (see Chapter 6, C1). The text's cosmological mechanism (waters above + waters below reuniting) reflects the three-tier cosmology described in Gen 1, not a hydrological model. What the text describes is a theological narrative of de-creation; what geology observes is evidence for devastating regional floods that likely gave rise to the flood tradition. Neither discipline answers the other's question.

### E2. The tebah's dimensions — naval architecture
**[SCIENTIFIC COMPARISON — POSSIBLE]**

Genesis 6:15 gives the tebah's dimensions: 300 cubits long, 50 wide, 30 high (approximately 137 x 23 x 14 meters using the standard cubit of ~45.7 cm). The length-to-beam ratio of 6:1 is within the range used by modern cargo vessels for stability. A 1993 study by the Korea Research Institute of Ships and Ocean Engineering (KRISO) found the proportions viable for flotation stability in open water, though not for self-propulsion — consistent with the text, which includes no sail, rudder, or steering mechanism. The word *tebah* itself means "box" or "chest" (see Chapter 6, A9), not "ship." The text describes a container designed to float and preserve, not to navigate. Whether these proportions reflect ancient engineering knowledge, literary convention, or something else is not resolved by the text.

**Source:** Hong, S.W., et al., "Safety Investigation of Noah's Ark in a Seaway," *Journal of Creation* 8:1, 1994.

### E3. The "vapor canopy" hypothesis
**[SCIENTIFIC COMPARISON — SPECULATIVE]**

Some young-earth creationist popularizers have proposed that a "vapor canopy" — a layer of water, vapor, or ice above the pre-flood atmosphere — created higher atmospheric pressure and oxygen levels, causing giant animal sizes and human longevity, and that the flood occurred when this canopy collapsed. The hypothesis is rejected by mainstream science (a vapor canopy of the required magnitude would produce a runaway greenhouse effect and toxic atmospheric pressure). It is also rejected by mainstream young-earth creationism: Answers in Genesis, the largest YEC organization, has explicitly disavowed the hypothesis on physical grounds. The Carboniferous-era data sometimes cited (oxygen levels reaching ~35%, giant arthropods with wingspans up to 71 cm) is genuine — but those measurements are from ~300 million years ago in mainstream geologic chronology, not from any biblical-flood timeframe.

**Source:** Faulkner, D., "Did the Pre-Flood World Have a Vapor Canopy?", Answers in Genesis (ACADEMIC POPULAR, denominational refutation); Beerling, D., *The Emerald Planet*, Oxford University Press, 2007 (PEER-REVIEWED, on Carboniferous oxygen).

## F. Later Reception

### F1. Jewish reception — the righteous remnant
**[LATER RECEPTION — DOCUMENTED]**

Rabbinic tradition (Genesis Rabbah 32:8) debates whether Noach was righteous absolutely or only relative to his corrupt generation. The phrase "in his generations" (6:9) is read as either praise (righteous even in a wicked age) or qualification (righteous only by comparison). The TT translates the phrase without adjudicating.

Midrash emphasizes the 7-day waiting period (7:4) as a week of mourning — God waited seven days for possible repentance before sending the flood.

**Source:** Freedman, H. & Simon, M. (trans.), *Midrash Rabbah: Genesis*, Soncino Press, 1939.

### F2. Christian reception — typological readings
**[LATER RECEPTION — DOCUMENTED]**

1 Peter 3:20-21 reads the flood waters as a type of baptism. Early Church Fathers' interpretation (Augustine, *City of God* XV.26-27) reads the tebah as a type of the Church. The number of people saved (eight) is connected to resurrection symbolism (the eighth day). These readings are post-biblical and not in the Hebrew text.

**Source:** Augustine, *City of God*, Book XV, chs. 26-27.

### F3. Islamic reception — Nuh in the Quran
**[LATER RECEPTION — DOCUMENTED]**

The Quran (Surahs 11:25-49, 23:23-30, 71:1-28) presents Nuh as a prophet who preaches to his people before the flood. A key difference: in the Quranic account, one of Nuh's sons refuses to board and drowns (11:42-43). The Genesis text records no such refusal — all three sons enter the tebah.

**Source:** Tottoli, R., *Biblical Prophets in the Qur'an and Muslim Literature*, 2002.

## G. Curiosities and Open Questions

### G1. Did fish survive?
**[TEXTUAL — VERIFIED]**

Gen 7:21-22 specifies destruction of "all flesh that moves **upon the land**" and "everything which had breath of the spirit of life in its nostrils, from all which was on the **dry ground**." Fish and marine creatures are conspicuously absent from the destruction list. The flood destroys land-based life; the waters themselves are the instrument, not the target. Aquatic life appears to survive by omission.

### G2. "Two two" after "seven seven" — execution vs. instruction
**[TEXTUAL — VERIFIED]**

At 7:2, YHWH instructs "seven seven" of clean animals. At 7:9, the execution describes "two two" entering. This could mean: (1) the "two two" is a summary description of the boarding process; (2) only two of each kind actually entered, with the "seven seven" instruction from a different tradition; (3) the "two two" refers to the unclean animals specifically. The TT translates both without reconciliation.

### G3. The date — second month, seventeenth day
**[TEXTUAL — POSSIBLE]**

Gen 7:11: the flood begins on Month 2, Day 17. Gen 8:4: the tebah rests on Month 7, Day 17 — exactly 150 days later (5 months × 30 days). Gen 8:14: the earth is fully dry on Month 2, Day 27 — almost exactly one year after the flood began. The symmetry suggests deliberate calendrical design, though whether this is literary or historical precision is UNCERTAIN.

### G4. The flood chronology — calendrical precision
**[TEXTUAL — PROBABLE]**

The flood narrative provides exact calendar dates: onset on the second month, seventeenth day (7:11); waters prevail 150 days (7:24); ark rests on mountains of Ararat on the seventh month, seventeenth day (8:4); mountaintops visible on the tenth month, first day (8:5); earth dry on the second month, twenty-seventh day of Noah's 601st year (8:14). The total duration from onset to dry earth spans exactly one solar year plus ten days — or precisely one lunar year (354 days) plus one additional month, depending on the calendar system used. This level of calendrical precision in an ancient narrative intersects with the history of timekeeping: whether the flood chronology preserves a solar calendar (365 days), a lunar calendar (354 days), or a schematic 360-day calendar (attested in Mesopotamia and in the later Book of Jubilees) is debated. The precision itself is the textual feature — the narrative is not vague about time.
