**Genre note (Rule 23):** This chapter is a **genealogy** — a structured list governed by a repeating formula. The formula is locked per Rule 7; deviations from the pattern are textually significant and noted.

**Adam is proper name throughout this chapter** (confirmed at Gen 4:25; locked at 5:1).

## CHAPTER OVERVIEW

**What happens:** The *toledot* of Adam — a ten-generation genealogy from Adam to Noach (Noah), structured by a locked formula: "X lived Y years, begot Z, lived W more years, begot sons and daughters, all his days were N years, and he died." Two entries break the formula in distinct ways: Chanokh (Enoch) breaks the death refrain — instead of "and he died," the text reads "he walked with God and was not, for God took him"; Noah breaks the pattern differently — Lemekh (Lamech) names him with a speech (a name-etymology rather than a death notice), and v.32 names three sons rather than one, launching the flood narrative rather than closing with death.

**Key themes:** The *sefer toledot* (book of generations — *sefer* = "book") — the only *toledot* with the prefix "book"; the image/likeness chain (God → Adam → Shet (Seth)); the relentless "and he died" refrain; Enoch's departure without dying — "walked with God" and "was not" (traditionally called a "translation," meaning God took him before death); Noah's name-etymology (*nacham*, comfort/regret); extraordinary lifespans (365–969 years).

**Watch for:** Enoch is the seventh from Adam (parallel to Enmeduranki, seventh pre-flood king in Sumerian tradition); Enoch lives 365 years (solar-year number, shortest in the list); Metushelach (Methuselah)'s death year coincides exactly with the flood year by the text's own arithmetic; the chapter uses *Elohim* throughout except v.29, where Lamech's speech names YHWH.

**Connection:** Lamech names Noah with a hope tied to the cursed ground (5:29, echoing 3:17). The *nacham* root in Noah's name reappears at 6:6 as divine "regret" — comfort and regret sharing a root.

## VERSE-BY-VERSE STUDY

### **Verse 1**

This *is* the book of the generations of Adam. In the day God created *a* human, in the likeness of God he made him.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **TOLEDOT FORMULA RETURNS**
> - **סֵפֶר תּוֹלְדֹת אָדָם** (*sefer toledot Adam*) = "the book of the generations of Adam." Second *toledot* marker in Genesis (first at 2:4). This one adds **סֵפֶר** (*sefer*, "book/document") — the only *toledot* with this prefix. Signals a structured written record.
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — ADAM IS NOW PROPER NAME**
> - **אָדָם** (*Adam*) — anarthrous (no article), genealogical context. Proper name locked from this verse forward (per editorial-log entry 081). The same verse then uses "human" (*adam*) generically in the creation-recall phrase: "in the day God created *a* human."
>
> 🔵 **IMAGE/LIKENESS RECALLED**
> - "In the likeness of God he made him" — echoes Gen 1:26–27. Uses *demut* (likeness) and *asah* (made). Cross-reference to v.3 where the language inverts: Adam begets Shet (Seth) "in his likeness, according to his image."

---

### **Verse 2**

Male and female he created them, and he blessed them, and he called their name Human in the day they were created.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔵 **"HE CALLED THEIR NAME HUMAN"**
> - **וַיִּקְרָא אֶת־שְׁמָם אָדָם** = "and he called their name *adam* (Human)." God names humanity collectively *adam*. This is the text's own statement: the word *adam* is a divine name for the species, not just a personal name.

---

### **Verse 3**

And Adam lived one hundred and thirty years, and he begot in his likeness, according to his image, and he called his name Shet (Seth).

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **IMAGE/LIKENESS INVERTED**
> - Gen 1:26–27: God creates human "in our image, according to our likeness." Gen 5:3: Adam begets Seth "in **his** likeness, according to **his** image." The divine-to-human creation language is **chained to human-to-human descent**. The *tselem/demut* pair continues through the generations.
>
> 🟢 **130 YEARS**
> - Gen 2:17 warned "dying you shall die." Gen 3:19 stated "dust you are." Now Adam lives 130 years before Seth — then dies at 930 (v.5). The "in the day" tension (2:17 vs. 5:5) is textually present. Cross-reference to editorial-log entry for *beyom* interpretive options.

### **Verse 5**

And all the days of Adam which he lived were nine hundred and thirty years, and he died.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **"AND HE DIED" — THE REFRAIN**
> - **וַיָּמֹת** (*vayyamot*) = "and he died." This phrase closes each genealogical entry as a **locked formula** (Rule 7). It repeats 8 times in this chapter (vv. 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 27, 31). The ONE exception is Chanokh (Enoch) (v.24) — where the formula is conspicuously absent.
>
> 🔵 **"WERE" IS NOT ITALICIZED (Rule 11 note)**
> - The formula "all the days of X *were* N years" contains the Hebrew verb **וַיִּהְיוּ** (*vayyiheyu*) = "and they were" — a fully expressed Hebrew verb from root **ה-י-ה**. "Were" translates this verb directly; it is not an English addition for grammar. Therefore it is not in italics throughout this chapter, unlike copula added to nominal clauses elsewhere (where Hebrew has no expressed verb).
>
> 🟡 **930 YEARS**
> - The ages in this chapter are extraordinary by modern standards. The text states them as fact within its narrative world. The TT presents them without harmonization or explanation — neither defending nor explaining them. The study companion file discusses the ages in context (Section E, G).

### **Verse 22**

And Chanokh (Enoch) walked with God after he begot Metushelach (Methuselah) three hundred years, and he begot sons and daughters.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **"WALKED WITH GOD" — FORMULA BREAK**
> - The standard formula is "X lived... and he begot." For Enoch, the formula shifts: "Enoch **walked with God** (*vayyithalekh Chanokh et-ha-Elohim*)." This breaks the pattern — the first time anyone is said to "walk with" God. The only other person who receives this phrase is Noach (Noah) (6:9).
>
> 🔵 **"WITH GOD" (ELOHIM, NOT YHWH)**
> - Note: Enoch walks with *Elohim*, not *YHWH*. This chapter uses only *Elohim* except at v.29 (Lemekh (Lamech)'s speech mentions "the ground which YHWH cursed"). The divine-name distribution matters.

---

### **Verse 23**

And all the days of Chanokh (Enoch) were three hundred and sixty-five years.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **365 YEARS — SHORTEST LIFESPAN**
> - Enoch's 365 years is by far the shortest in this genealogy (others range from 777 to 969). Yet he is the one who "walked with God." The number 365 = days in a solar year; whether this is significant is POSSIBLE but unresolved.

---

### **Verse 24**

And Chanokh (Enoch) walked with God, and he was not, for God took him.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "AND HE DIED" FORMULA ABSENT**
> - Every other entry in this genealogy ends "and he died." Enoch's entry ends: **"and he was not, for God took him"** (*ve-einennu ki laqach oto Elohim*). The death formula is **conspicuously replaced**. The text does not say he died, does not say he went to heaven, does not explain where he went. It says God **took** him. Anything beyond that is later interpretation.
>
> 🟢 **"HE WAS NOT"**
> - **אֵינֶנּוּ** (*einennu*) = "he was not" / "he is no more." The same word used of something that has ceased to be present. The simplicity is deliberate — the text refuses to elaborate.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **OLDEST RECORDED AGE**
> - Methuselah at 969 years is the longest-lived person in the biblical record. By the chronological arithmetic, his death year coincides approximately with the year of the flood (Gen 7). The text does not make this connection explicit.

### **Verse 29**

And he called his name Noach (Noah), saying, "This *one* shall comfort us from our work and from the toil of our hands, from the ground which YHWH cursed."

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **NOACH / NACHAM WORDPLAY**
> - **נֹחַ** (*Noach*) = Noah. **נָחַם** (*nacham*) = comfort/relent. The name-etymology connects Noah to comfort/relief. Rule 14 wordplay flagged — the sonic link is approximate (n-ch vs. n-ch-m), not exact.
>
> 🔴 **"THE GROUND WHICH YHWH CURSED"**
> - Lemekh (Lamech)'s speech explicitly references Gen 3:17 (ground cursed because of the human). This is the ONLY verse in Gen 5 that uses **YHWH** (the rest use *Elohim*). The curse of the ground is tied to the YHWH-name register.
>
> 🔵 **ITSABON — SAME ROOT AS 3:16–17**
> - **עִצָּבוֹן** (*itsabon*) = "toil" — same word used for both the woman's toil (3:16) and the human's toil (3:17). Lamech hopes Noah will provide relief from the same *itsabon* that was declared in the garden consequences.

### **Verse 31**

And all the days of Lemekh (Lamech) were seven hundred and seventy-seven years, and he died.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **777 — ECHOING 4:24**
> - Lamech son of Methuselah lives 777 years. The OTHER Lamech (Gen 4:24, Cainite line) claimed "seventy-seven" (*shiv'im ve-shiv'ah*) fold vengeance. The 777 / 77 numerical echo across the two Lamechs is textually present — whether intentional structural mirroring or coincidence is POSSIBLE but unresolved.

> - The standard formula lists one son per entry. Noah's entry names **three sons** — breaking the pattern. This sets up the Table of Nations (Gen 10) and the post-flood narrative structure.

## CROSS-CHAPTER TRACKING (Gen 4 → Gen 5)

**Two genealogies, two lines:**
- Gen 4:17–22: Cainite line (Qayin (Cain) → Chanokh (Enoch) → ... → Lemekh (Lamech) → Yaval/Yuval/Tuval-Cain)
- Gen 5:1–32: Sethite line (Adam → Shet (Seth) → ... → Lamech → Noach (Noah))
- Shared names across lines: Enoch, Lamech (different individuals, same names)

**Image/likeness chain:**
- 1:26–27: God creates human in divine image/likeness (*tselem/demut*)
- 5:1: Recalls "in the likeness of God he made him"
- 5:3: Adam begets Seth "in his likeness, according to his image" — language inverted from divine-to-human to human-to-human

**"And he died" as mortality confirmation:**
- 2:17: "dying you shall die"
- 3:19: "dust you are, and to dust you shall return"
- 5:5,8,11,14,17,20,27,31: "and he died" (8× refrain)
- 5:24: Enoch — "and he was not, for God took him" (formula conspicuously absent)

**Toledot formula chain:**
- 2:4: "generations of the skies and the land"
- 5:1: "book of the generations of Adam"
- Next: 6:9 "generations of Noah"

**YHWH vs. Elohim distribution:**
- Gen 5 uses *Elohim* throughout — EXCEPT v.29 (Lamech's speech: "the ground which **YHWH** cursed")
- This mirrors Gen 1's exclusive *Elohim* usage vs. Gen 2–4's *YHWH Elohim* and *YHWH*

**Itsabon thread:**
- 3:16: woman's toil (*itsabon*)
- 3:17: human's toil from cursed ground (*itsabon*)
- 5:29: Lamech hopes Noah will bring relief from "our work and the toil (*itsabon*) of our hands"
