**This chapter marks the transition from primeval to patriarchal history.** YHWH calls Avram (Abram) out of Charan with a command that names no destination and a promise that will shape the rest of the Torah. The five-fold blessing (12:2–3) introduces *berakhah* as a narrative engine. Abram journeys through Kenaan (Canaan) — Shekhem (Shechem), the oak of Moreh, Beyt-El (Bethel) — building altars and calling on the name of YHWH. Famine drives him to Egypt, where the wife-sister episode introduces a pattern that recurs at 20:2 and 26:7. Pharaoh is struck with plagues (*nega'im*) — the same root that will return in Exodus.

---

## CHAPTER OVERVIEW

**What happens:** YHWH commands Abram to leave his land, his birthplace, and his father's house for an unnamed destination — three separations ascending in intimacy. The promise is five-fold: great nation, blessing, great name, blessing to others, and all families of the ground blessed/blessing themselves in him. Abram goes, taking Sarai, Lot, possessions, and the persons they had made in Charan. He passes through Canaan to Shechem, to the oak of Moreh, where YHWH appears and promises the land to his seed. Abram builds an altar. He moves to the hill country between Bethel and Ai, builds another altar, and calls on the name of YHWH. He journeys toward the Negev. A famine sends him to Egypt. He tells Sarai to say she is his sister. Pharaoh takes Sarai; Abram prospers. YHWH strikes Pharaoh with great plagues. Pharaoh rebukes Abram and sends him away.

**Key themes:** The "go, yourself" command (an emphatic Hebrew construction that folds the verb back on the subject); the unnamed destination (trust precedes knowledge); the five-fold blessing as the seed of Israel's vocation; the "ground" (adamah) chain continues — "families of the ground" (12:3); the blessing verb is genuinely ambiguous — passive "be blessed" or reflexive "bless themselves"; altar-building as a claim-by-worship, not by conquest; "the Canaanites were then in the land" — the narrator's aside; the first descent to Egypt; the wife-sister motif; Pharaoh's plagues foreshadowing Exodus.

**Watch for:** The three-part separation structure in 12:1 (land → birthplace → father's house); the shift from Elohim speech (primeval) to YHWH speech (patriarchal); "the persons they had made in Charan" (12:5) — the word for a living being used for people, and the verb "made" for acquiring them; the narrator's comment "the Canaanites were then in the land" (12:6) — noted by Ibn Ezra as possibly post-Mosaic; the two different curse words in 12:3 — a light curse and a heavy curse, from different roots; Pharaoh's rebuke echoes the question-pattern of divine confrontations (3:13, 4:10).

**Connection:** Gen 11:31–32 establishes Abram's family in Charan after leaving Ur. Gen 12 fulfills the departure hinted in 11:31. The blessing of 12:2–3 answers the curse of 3:17 and 5:29 — blessing now enters as a counter-force to the curse on the ground. The altar-building at Shechem and Bethel establishes sites that recur throughout Genesis (33:18–20, 28:10–22, 35:1–7). The Egypt descent foreshadows the Exodus pattern: famine → descent → plagues → departure with wealth.

## VERSE-BY-VERSE STUDY

### **Verse 1**

And YHWH said to Avram (Abram), "Go, yourself, from your land and from your birthplace and from your father's house, to the land which I will show you.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — *LEKH LEKHA* (GO, YOURSELF)**
> - **לֶךְ־לְךָ** (*lekh lekha*) = "Go, yourself" — emphatic doubled form (imperative + reflexive pronoun), rare in the Hebrew Bible. Appears only here and at Gen 22:2 (binding of Yitschaq), bracketing Abram/Abraham's narrative arc. The TT renders "Go, yourself" to preserve the doubling without importing a single interpretation of the reflexive force. For fuller discussion, see companion Section A1.
>
> 🔴 **THREE SEPARATIONS — ASCENDING INTIMACY**
> - "From your land → from your birthplace → from your father's house": broad to narrow, public to intimate. Each separation is more costly than the last. The unnamed destination ("the land which I will show you") intensifies the demand: Abram is told what to leave but not where he is going. For fuller discussion, see companion Section A2.
>
> 🟡 **YHWH SPEAKS — NOT ELOHIM**
> - The primeval history (Gen 1–11) uses both divine names, but the patriarchal call begins with YHWH — the personal, relational name. The shift marks the transition from cosmic to covenantal narrative.

---

### **Verse 2**

And I will make you into a great nation, and I will bless you, and I will make your name great — and be a blessing.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **FIVE-FOLD PROMISE (12:2–3)**
> - The promise unfolds across vv.2–3 in five clauses: (1) "I will make you into a great nation"; (2) "I will bless you"; (3) "I will make your name great"; (4) "be a blessing"; (5) "all the families of the ground will be blessed / will bless themselves in you." The structure shifts at (4) from declarative ("I will...") to imperative ("be a blessing") — Abram becomes the agent, not merely the recipient.
>
> 🟢 **"GREAT NAME" (*gadol*)**
> - **וַאֲגַדְּלָה שְׁמֶךָ** = "and I will make your name great." Echoes Gen 11:4 where the Babel builders say "let us make for ourselves a name (*shem*)." What they seized, God now grants. The *shem* (name) wordplay continues from Noah's son Shem (9:26).

---

### **Verse 3**

And I will bless those who bless you, and the one who curses you I will curse, and all the families of the ground will be blessed / will bless themselves in you."

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — TWO DIFFERENT CURSE WORDS**
> - "The one who curses you (*meqallelekha*) I will curse (*a'or*)." Two different roots: **קָלַל** (*qalal*) = to treat lightly, disparage, curse (lighter); **אָרַר** (*arar*) = to curse (heavy, formal, the same root as Gen 3:14, 3:17, 4:11, 9:25). Those who treat Abram lightly receive the heavier curse. The asymmetry is deliberate: the response exceeds the provocation.
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — *NIVREKHU* (PASSIVE OR REFLEXIVE?)**
> - **וְנִבְרְכוּ בְךָ כֹּל מִשְׁפְּחֹת הָאֲדָמָה** = "and all the families of the ground *nivrekhu* in you." The niphal form *nivrekhu* is genuinely ambiguous: (1) passive — "will be blessed" (God blesses all families through Abram); (2) reflexive — "will bless themselves" (all families will use Abram as a standard of blessing). Both readings have ancient support. The TT preserves the ambiguity with a slash per Rule 22: "will be blessed / will bless themselves."
>
> 🟢 **"FAMILIES OF THE GROUND" (*mishpechot ha-adamah*)**
> - *Adamah* — not *erets* (land/earth). "Ground" — the same *adamah* from which *adam* was formed (2:7), which was cursed (3:17), which cried out with Hevel's blood (4:10), which Noah was to comfort (5:29). The blessing to "all families of the *adamah*" is a direct counter to the curse on the *adamah*. The chain continues.

---

### **Verse 4**

And Abram went as YHWH had spoken to him, and Lot went with him; and Abram *was* seventy-five years, a son of, when he went out from Charan.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔵 **"AND ABRAM WENT" — NO DIALOGUE**
> - **וַיֵּלֶךְ אַבְרָם** = "And Abram went." YHWH speaks five clauses of promise; Abram responds with zero words. He simply goes. Like Noah (6:22: "and Noah did"), the response is action, not speech.
>
> 🔵 **AGE FORMULA**
> - **וְאַבְרָם בֶּן־חָמֵשׁ שָׁנִים וְשִׁבְעִים שָׁנָה** = "and Abram was a son of five and seventy years." Terah was 70 when he began fathering (11:26); he lived to 205 (11:32). If Abram left at 75, the chronology raises questions about whether Terah was still alive. The text does not harmonize — it records.

---

### **Verse 5**

And Abram took Sarai his wife, and Lot the son of his brother, and all their possessions which they had gathered, and the persons which they had made in Charan, and they went out to go to the land of Kenaan (Canaan), and they came to the land of Canaan.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **"THE PERSONS WHICH THEY HAD MADE" (*ha-nephesh asher asu*)**
> - **וְאֶת־הַנֶּפֶשׁ אֲשֶׁר־עָשׂוּ בְחָרָן** = "and the persons (*nephesh*) which they had made (*asu*) in Charan." *Nephesh* here = persons/people. *Asah* = made/acquired. The verb "made" for acquiring people is striking — it likely refers to servants or household members gained in Charan. Targum Onkelos: "souls they had converted." The text says "made."
>
> 🔵 **"AND THEY CAME TO THE LAND OF CANAAN"**
> - The repetition — "they went out to go to the land of Canaan, and they came to the land of Canaan" — brackets the journey. 11:31: Terah "went out... to go to the land of Canaan, and they came to Charan and settled there." Terah stopped short. Abram completes what Terah did not.

---

### **Verse 6**

And Abram passed through the land to the place of Shekhem (Shechem), to the oak of Moreh; and the Kenaani *was* then in the land.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **SHECHEM**
> - **שְׁכֶם** (*Shekhem*) = first named location in Abram's Canaan journey. The site recurs: Yaakov buys land there (33:18–19), Yosef's bones are buried there (Josh 24:32), and Yehoshua's covenant renewal occurs there (Josh 24:1). Abram's first stop becomes a critical site in Israel's memory.
>
> 🟢 **"OAK OF MOREH" (*elon Moreh*)**
> - **אֵלוֹן מוֹרֶה** = "oak of Moreh" or "great tree of Moreh." *Elon* = oak or terebinth (large tree). *Moreh* = teacher/teaching/oracle (from root י-ר-ה, to teach/direct). Whether "Moreh" is a personal name, a place name, or a description ("the teaching tree" / "the oracle oak") is UNCERTAIN. The TT transliterates and notes.
>
> 🔴 **"THE CANAANITES WERE THEN IN THE LAND"**
> - **וְהַכְּנַעֲנִי אָז בָּאָרֶץ** = "and the Kenaani was *then* (*az*) in the land." The word *az* (then, at that time) implies a contrast with the narrator's own time, when the Canaanites were presumably no longer in the land. Ibn Ezra flagged this as evidence of post-Mosaic authorship. The TT translates what the text says and notes the implication in the narrator's *az*.

---

### **Verse 7**

And YHWH appeared to Abram and said, "To your seed I will give this land." And he built there an altar to YHWH who had appeared to him.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **FIRST DIVINE APPEARANCE (*vayyera*)**
> - **וַיֵּרָא יהוה אֶל־אַבְרָם** = "And YHWH appeared to Abram." First recorded theophany to Abram. In 12:1, YHWH "said" (*vayyomer*); here YHWH "appeared" (*vayyera*) — a visual component. The verb ראה (see) in niphal = was seen / appeared.
>
> 🔴 **"TO YOUR SEED I WILL GIVE THIS LAND"**
> - **לְזַרְעֲךָ אֶתֵּן אֶת־הָאָרֶץ הַזֹּאת** = "To your seed I will give this land." The land promise is made while the Canaanites are in the land (v.6). The promise is to the *seed* (zera), not to Abram himself. "This land" — now named, now visible, now promised.
>
> 🟢 **ALTAR (*mizbeach*)**
> - **מִזְבֵּחַ** (*mizbeach*) = altar. From root ז-ב-ח (*z-b-ch*), to slaughter/sacrifice. First appeared at 8:20 (Noah's post-flood altar). Abram builds an altar at Shechem — worship as the first response to the land promise. No sacrifice is described; the act of building is the response.

---

### **Verse 8**

And he moved from there to the hill country east of Beyt-El (Bethel) and pitched his tent — Bethel *was* to the west and Ai *was* to the east — and he built there an altar to YHWH and called on the name of YHWH.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **BETHEL (BEYT-EL)**
> - **בֵּית־אֵל** (*Beyt-El*) = "House of God." The name anticipates Yaakov's naming in 28:19 — but the text uses the name here proleptically (the narrator uses the later name). The site becomes a major worship center.
>
> 🟢 **"CALLED ON THE NAME OF YHWH"**
> - **וַיִּקְרָא בְּשֵׁם יהוה** = "and he called on the name of YHWH." Same phrase as Gen 4:26 ("then *it was* begun to call on the name of YHWH") and 13:4 (Abram returns to this altar). The phrase links Abram's worship to the earliest human worship recorded in Genesis.
>
> 🔵 **TWO ALTARS**
> - Shechem (v.7): altar built in response to YHWH's appearance. Bethel (v.8): altar built, and Abram calls on YHWH's name. Two altars, two acts of worship — Abram marks the land with worship before any claim of possession.

---

### **Verse 9**

And Abram journeyed, going and journeying toward the Negev.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔵 **"GOING AND JOURNEYING" (*halokh ve-nasoa*)**
> - **הָלוֹךְ וְנָסוֹעַ** = "going and journeying." Infinitive absolute construction — continuous, progressive movement. Abram is not settling; he is passing through. The land is promised but not yet possessed.
>
> 🟢 **NEGEV (*negev*)**
> - **הַנֶּגְבָּה** (*ha-negbah*) = "toward the Negev." *Negev* = south/dry land. The directional *-ah* suffix indicates movement toward. The Negev is the arid southern region of Canaan — Abram moves from north (Shechem) through center (Bethel) to south (Negev).

---

### **Verse 10**

And there was a famine in the land, and Abram went down to Egypt to sojourn there, for the famine *was* heavy in the land.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **FIRST FAMINE, FIRST DESCENT TO EGYPT**
> - **וַיְהִי רָעָב בָּאָרֶץ** = "And there was a famine in the land." First famine in Genesis. The promised land cannot sustain Abram. The irony: God promises the land (v.7), then famine expels him from it. This becomes a pattern: famine drives the patriarchs (26:1, Yitschaq; 42:1–2, Yaakov's sons).
>
> 🔵 **"WENT DOWN" (*vayyered*)**
> - **וַיֵּרֶד** = "and he went down." Egypt is always "down" from Canaan — geographically and theologically. *Yarad* (descend) for going to Egypt; *alah* (ascend) for returning. The first descent to Egypt foreshadows the great descent (Yaakov, Gen 46) and the Exodus.
>
> 🟢 **"TO SOJOURN" (*lagur*)**
> - **לָגוּר** = "to sojourn." A *ger* (sojourner) is a temporary resident without land rights. Abram goes not to settle but to survive. The word *ger* will define Israel's identity in Egypt (Ex 22:20, 23:9).

---

### **Verse 11**

And it was when he drew near to enter Egypt, and he said to Sarai his wife, "See, I know that you *are* a woman beautiful of appearance.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔵 **"WOMAN BEAUTIFUL OF APPEARANCE" (*yefat mar'eh*)**
> - **אִשָּׁה יְפַת־מַרְאֶה** = "woman beautiful of appearance." *Yefat* from *yafeh* (beautiful); *mar'eh* = appearance/sight. The beauty is noted by Abram as a threat, not a compliment. The description is functional — it sets up the danger.

---

### **Verse 12**

And it will be when the Egyptians see you, and they will say, 'This *is* his wife,' and they will kill me, and you they will let live.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔵 **ABRAM'S FEAR**
> - The logic is explicit: the Egyptians will kill the husband to take the wife. Abram anticipates murder. The text does not evaluate whether his fear is justified — it records his reasoning.

---

### **Verse 13**

Say, please, *that* you *are* my sister, so that it may go well for me on your account, and my life will live because of you."

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🟡 **TIER 2 — RECEPTION NOTE**
> - This passage has documented reception history. See companion Section F for context on the wife-sister episode, including its use to excuse deception and its implications for Sarai's agency.
>
> 🔴 **THE WIFE-SISTER REQUEST**
> - **אִמְרִי־נָא אֲחֹתִי אָתְּ** = "Say, please, my sister you *are*." The *na* (please) is a polite particle — he requests, not commands. The stated motive is self-preservation. This pattern recurs: Gen 20:2 (Avraham and Avimelekh), Gen 26:7 (Yitschaq and Rivqah). The text reports without moral commentary. For fuller discussion, see companion Section F4.
>
> 🟡 **SARAI'S SILENCE**
> - Sarai does not speak in this chapter. She is spoken about (v.11), spoken to (v.13), taken (v.15), and returned (v.19) — but her words are not recorded. The text does not provide her perspective.

### **Verse 14**

And it was when Abram came to Egypt, and the Egyptians saw the woman, that she *was* very beautiful.

---

### **Verse 15**

And the officials of Pharaoh saw her and praised her to Pharaoh, and the woman was taken *into* the house of Pharaoh.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔵 **PASSIVE — "THE WOMAN WAS TAKEN"**
> - **וַתֻּקַּח הָאִשָּׁה** = "and the woman was taken." Hophal (causative passive) of *laqach* (take). Sarai is the grammatical object throughout: seen, praised, taken. The passive construction emphasizes her lack of agency in this sequence.
>
> 🔵 **"OFFICIALS OF PHARAOH" (*sarey Par'oh*)**
> - **שָׂרֵי פַרְעֹה** = "officials/princes of Pharaoh." *Sarey* = plural of *sar* (official, prince). Pharaoh is unnamed — no personal name, only the title. This contrasts with later Exodus narratives where Pharaoh is similarly unnamed but the encounter is far longer.

---

### **Verse 16**

And to Abram he did well on her account, and he had flock and herd and male donkeys and male servants and female servants and female donkeys and camels.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🟡 **ABRAM PROSPERS FROM THE DECEPTION**
> - **וּלְאַבְרָם הֵיטִיב בַּעֲבוּרָהּ** = "And to Abram he did well on her account." The wealth Abram receives is explicitly linked to Sarai's presence in Pharaoh's house. "On her account" (*ba'avurah*) — the benefit comes through Sarai's situation. The text reports the enrichment without evaluating its morality.
>
> 🟢 **LIVESTOCK LIST**
> - Seven items: flock (*tson*), herd (*baqar*), male donkeys (*chamorim*), male servants (*avadim*), female servants (*shefachot*), female donkeys (*atonot*), camels (*gemalim*). The list signals substantial wealth. The camels are sometimes noted as anachronistic for the early second millennium — the text includes them without qualification.

---

### **Verse 17**

And YHWH struck Pharaoh *with* great plagues, and his house, on account of Sarai, the wife of Abram.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — *NEGA'IM* (PLAGUES)**
> - **וַיְנַגַּע יהוה אֶת־פַּרְעֹה נְגָעִים גְּדֹלִים** = "And YHWH struck Pharaoh with great plagues (*nega'im*)." Root נ-ג-ע = to strike/afflict — same root as the Exodus plagues. The root connection is the basis for rendering *nega'im* as "plagues" rather than "afflictions" or "blows" — the same vocabulary carries this charge in the Exodus account. YHWH intervenes to protect Sarai where Abram's plan has placed her at risk; the text does not describe what the plagues were. For fuller discussion, see companion Section A8.
>
> 🟡 **YHWH ACTS — ABRAM DOES NOT**
> - Abram created the situation; YHWH resolves it. The pattern: human failure → divine rescue. The text does not record Abram praying, repenting, or acting. YHWH intervenes without being asked.
>
> 🔵 **"ON ACCOUNT OF SARAI, THE WIFE OF ABRAM"**
> - **עַל־דְּבַר שָׂרַי אֵשֶׁת אַבְרָם** = "on account of Sarai, the wife of Abram." The narrator restores her identity — she is "the wife of Abram," not Pharaoh's acquisition. The full designation corrects the situation's distortion.

---

### **Verse 18**

And Pharaoh called Abram and said, "What *is* this you have done to me? Why did you not tell me that she *is* your wife?

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **PHARAOH'S REBUKE**
> - **מַה־זֹּאת עָשִׂיתָ לִּי** = "What is this you have done to me?" Pharaoh — a foreign king — rebukes the patriarch. The question-pattern echoes God's confrontations: "What is this you have done?" (3:13 to the woman). The moral clarity comes from Pharaoh, not from Abram. The text does not soften this.

---

### **Verse 19**

Why did you say, 'She *is* my sister,' and I took her for myself as a wife? And now, see, your wife — take *her* and go."

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔵 **"TAKE AND GO" (*qach va-lekh*)**
> - **הִנֵּה אִשְׁתְּךָ קַח וָלֵךְ** = "See, your wife — take *her* and go." Pharaoh returns Sarai and expels Abram. The command is curt — imperative: take, go. No negotiation, no penalty beyond expulsion. Pharaoh acts with more straightforward justice than Abram did with his deception.

---

### **Verse 20**

And Pharaoh commanded men concerning him, and they sent him away, and his wife, and all which *was* his.

> **📝 NOTES**
>
> 🔵 **EXPULSION — NOT PUNISHMENT**
> - Pharaoh commands an escort — not execution, not imprisonment. "They sent him away" (*vayeshallechu*) — piel of *shalach* (send). Abram leaves Egypt with his wife and all his possessions (including those gained in v.16). He departs wealthier than he arrived. The Exodus echo: Israel will also leave Egypt with Egyptian wealth (Ex 12:35–36).
>
> 🟡 **ABRAM'S SILENCE**
> - Abram does not respond to Pharaoh's rebuke. No defense, no apology, no explanation. The chapter ends with Abram being sent away — passive, silent, and wealthy.

## CROSS-CHAPTER TRACKING (Gen 11 → Gen 12)

**Gen 11:31 vs Gen 12:5 — completing the journey:**

| Element | Gen 11:31 | Gen 12:5 |
|---------|----------|----------|
| Departure | Terah takes Abram, Lot, Sarai from Ur | Abram takes Sarai, Lot from Charan |
| Destination stated | "to go to the land of Kenaan" | "to go to the land of Kenaan" |
| Arrival | "they came to Charan and settled there" | "they came to the land of Kenaan" |
| Completion | Stopped short | Journey completed |

Terah intended Canaan but settled in Charan. Abram completes the journey his father did not finish. The verbal echoes are deliberate: identical destination language, different outcomes.

**Blessing and curse chain:**

| Reference | Curse (*arar*) | Blessing (*barakh*) |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| 3:14 | Serpent cursed (*arur*) | — |
| 3:17 | Ground (*adamah*) cursed | — |
| 4:11 | Cain cursed from the ground | — |
| 5:29 | — | Noah: "comfort us from the ground which YHWH has cursed" |
| 9:25 | Canaan cursed | — |
| 9:26 | — | "Blessed be YHWH, God of Shem" |
| 12:2–3 | "The one who curses you I will curse (*a'or*)" | "I will bless you... be a blessing... all families of the *adamah* blessed" |

Gen 12:2–3 introduces *berakhah* as a counter-force to the *arar* chain that dominates Gen 3–11. The blessing explicitly targets "families of the *adamah*" — the same ground that was cursed. The curse is not revoked; the blessing is set against it.

**Altar chain:**

| Reference | Builder | Location | Context |
|-----------|---------|----------|---------|
| 8:20 | Noah | Post-flood | First altar; burnt offerings; YHWH smells the pleasing aroma |
| 12:7 | Abram | Shechem | Response to YHWH's appearance and land promise |
| 12:8 | Abram | Between Bethel and Ai | Calls on the name of YHWH |

Abram's altar-building echoes Noah's. But Noah built one altar after the flood; Abram builds two in a single chapter. He marks the land with worship — not with walls or weapons.

**Egypt foreshadowing — Gen 12 and the Exodus:**

| Element | Gen 12 | Exodus |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Cause | Famine in the land | Famine (Gen 42–47 → settlement → slavery) |
| Direction | "went down (*yarad*) to Egypt" | Israel goes down to Egypt |
| Foreign ruler | Pharaoh (unnamed) | Pharaoh (unnamed) |
| Woman at risk | Sarai taken into Pharaoh's house | Israel enslaved in Egypt |
| Divine intervention | YHWH strikes Pharaoh with *nega'im* (plagues) | YHWH strikes Egypt with plagues (*makkot*) |
| Outcome | Pharaoh sends Abram away | Pharaoh sends Israel away |
| Departure with wealth | Abram leaves with flock, servants, possessions | Israel leaves with silver, gold, clothing (Ex 12:35–36) |

The parallels are extensive enough to suggest that Gen 12:10–20 is a narrative anticipation of the Exodus. Whether this is authorial design or editorial shaping, the pattern is present in the text.

**Divine name distribution:**
- 12:1: YHWH speaks the call
- 12:4: YHWH (Abram obeys what YHWH spoke)
- 12:7: YHWH appears, promises land
- 12:8: YHWH (altar, calling on the name)
- 12:17: YHWH strikes Pharaoh

Gen 12 uses YHWH exclusively — no Elohim. The patriarchal narrative begins under the personal name. The cosmic Elohim of Gen 1 gives way to the covenantal YHWH of the Abraham cycle.

**Name chain:**
- 11:4: Babel builders: "let us make for ourselves a name (*shem*)"
- 12:2: YHWH to Abram: "I will make your name (*shimkha*) great"
- What Babel seized by construction, Abram receives by obedience. The name that human ambition could not secure, divine promise grants.
