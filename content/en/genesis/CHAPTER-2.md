## READING GUIDE

**Main Text:** Primary translation—readable but faithful to Hebrew structure
**Notes:** Essential Hebrew features immediately below each verse
***Italics:*** Words added for English grammar (not in Hebrew text)
**"Quotes":** Direct divine speech or human speech
**YHWH:** Tetragrammaton rendered consonantally (Rule 25, Option A)
**raqia, yatzar, etc.:** Transliterated Hebrew terms (explained in notes)

**This chapter follows Rule 23 (Genre Sensitivity):** Genesis 1's locked formulas do NOT mechanically apply here. Genesis 2 is narrative, not liturgical rhythm.

**Adam / Human Policy (Rule 17 standing note):** Hebrew אָדָם (*adam*) oscillates between generic *"human / humanity"* and, potentially, the proper name *Adam*. The definite article (*ha-adam* = "the human") signals generic use; article absence is contextually ambiguous. **This translation renders "the human" throughout Genesis 2–3**, even in anarthrous occurrences (2:20, 3:17, 3:21), to preserve the ongoing wordplay with *adamah* (ground) and avoid premature proper-name commitment. Transition to proper-name *Adam* is formally resolved in Genesis 4.

## CHAPTER OVERVIEW

**What happens:** A second creation account begins at 2:4 with a different sequence and focus. YHWH God forms the human from dust, plants a garden in Eden with two named trees, places the human there to work and keep it, gives the command about the tree of knowledge, declares "not good" for the human to be alone, forms animals, and builds the woman from the human's side.

**Key themes:** The "generations" formula (2:4); YHWH God (combined divine name); formation from the ground — the wordplay between the human and the ground (adam / adamah); the garden as bounded space; the tree of knowledge and the tree of life; the command and its consequence ("dying you shall die"); naming as ordering; "one flesh."

**Watch for:** The divine name shifts to YHWH Elohim (2:4 onward); the different creation order from Chapter 1 (human before animals in 2:7, 19); the "not good" — the only negative assessment in creation; the deep sleep; "bone of my bones" as the first human speech.

**Connection:** The trees introduced here — knowledge and life — drive the narrative of Chapter 3. The relationship between the human and the ground becomes the basis for the curse in 3:17–19.

## VERSE-BY-VERSE STUDY

### **Verse 1**

And were finished the skies and the land and all their host.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **HOST**
> - **צָבָא** (*tsava*) = army/host—military metaphor applied to the totality of creation; often used of angelic or stellar hosts elsewhere, but here plainly "everything in them"
>
> 🔵 **PASSIVE**
> - **וַיְכֻלּוּ** (*vayekhullu*) = passive "were finished"—agent (God) not named here; agent appears in v.2

---

### **Verse 2**

And God finished on the seventh day his work which he had made, and he rested on the seventh day from all his work which he had made.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL - TEXTUAL VARIANT**
> - MT reads **בַּיּוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי** (*bayom hashvi'i*) = "on the seventh day" for the *finishing*. The LXX (the ancient Greek translation) and Samaritan Pentateuch read "on the sixth day" (likely harmonizing, since finishing and resting on the same day is theologically awkward).
> - **Per Rule 22:** translate MT in main text; note variant. Do not silently adopt LXX/SP.
> - **PROBABLE** resolution of the apparent contradiction: *kalah* here means "brought to completion" as an ongoing state rather than a final stroke of work on day 7.
>
> 🟢 **REST**
> - **וַיִּשְׁבֹּת** (*vayishbot*) = "and he rested/ceased"—root **שׁ-ב-ת** (*sh-b-t*), source of *Shabbat* (Sabbath). Not yet the noun *Shabbat*; it is the verbal action that gives the day its name.

---

### **Verse 3**

And God blessed the seventh day and sanctified it, for on it he rested from all his work which God created to make.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **FIRST SANCTIFICATION**
> - **וַיְקַדֵּשׁ** (*vayeqaddesh*) = "and he sanctified/set apart"—first use of the root **ק-ד-שׁ** (*q-d-sh*) in the Bible. The first thing called holy is a *time*, not a place or object.
>
> 🔵 **"CREATED TO MAKE"**
> - **אֲשֶׁר־בָּרָא אֱלֹהִים לַעֲשׂוֹת** (*asher bara elohim la'asot*) = "which God created to make/do"—pairs both creation-verbs from Genesis 1 (*bara*, *asah*) in a single compressed phrase. Infinitive construct *la'asot* = "to make"; interpretation **POSSIBLE**: "that he had created by making" or "created for the purpose of making/doing."

---

### **Verse 4**

These *are* the generations of the skies and the land when they were created, in the day YHWH Elohim made land and skies.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL - FIRST YHWH (Tetragrammaton)**
> - **יהוה** (YHWH) appears here for the first time in the Bible.
> - **Per Rule 25, Option A** (declared in front matter): rendered **consonantally as YHWH**, without supplying reconstructed vowels or substituting "LORD." Alternatives considered but not chosen: *Yahweh* (vocalized reconstruction), *the LORD* (traditional substitution), *Adonai* (Jewish reading tradition).
> - First occurrence of the compound **יהוה אֱלֹהִים** (*YHWH Elohim*) = "YHWH Elohim," a distinctive pairing used heavily in Gen 2–3. **Never collapsed to "Lord God"** — that would conceal two distinct lexemes.
>
> 🔴 **TOLEDOT FORMULA**
> - **אֵלֶּה תּוֹלְדוֹת** (*eleh toledot*) = "these *are* the generations/origins/accounts"—major structural marker in Genesis. Appears 11× across the book and divides it into sections.
> - Root **י-ל-ד** (*y-l-d*) = to give birth; *toledot* literally = "begettings." Meaning extends beyond genealogy to "origin-account" or "things that came forth."
>
> 🔵 **ORDER REVERSAL**
> - Gen 1:1 order: **skies and land**. Gen 2:4b order: **land and skies**. **POSSIBLE** marker of genre/perspective shift: Gen 1 is cosmic-top-down; Gen 2 is earth-up-close.
>
> 🔵 **"IN THE DAY" (idiom)**
> - **בְּיוֹם** (*beyom*) = "in the day" — Hebrew temporal idiom meaning *"when / at the time when"*, not a literal 24-hour marker. Applies wherever *beyom* appears in this chapter and the next (2:4, 2:17, 3:5). The translation keeps "in the day" literally; the idiom is flagged here and at 2:17.

---

### **Verse 5**

And every shrub of the field not yet was on the land, and every herb of the field not yet sprouted, for YHWH Elohim had not caused rain upon the land, and a human there was not to work the ground.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🔵 **NEGATIVE-PRECREATION SERIES**
> - Chain of "not yet" clauses sets a pre-creation state before the narrative acts. Hebrew **טֶרֶם** (*terem*) = "not yet/before."
> - **Note on "was":** The Hebrew **יִהְיֶה** (*yihyeh*, from root **ה-י-ה**) is the expressed verb here — "not yet was/shall be." "Was" translates the Hebrew verb directly and is therefore not in italics (contrast with nominal clauses elsewhere where no Hebrew verb is present).
>
> 🟢 **WORK THE GROUND**
> - **לַעֲבֹד אֶת־הָאֲדָמָה** (*la'avod et-ha'adamah*) = "to work/serve the ground"—first mention of human *work* precedes the human's creation. Root **ע-ב-ד** (*'-b-d*) = serve/work (same root as "servant" and "worship").

---

### **Verse 6**

And *a* mist went up from the land and watered all the face of the ground.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🟡 **UNCERTAIN - *ed***
> - **אֵד** (*ed*) = rare word; appears twice in the Hebrew Bible (here + Job 36:27). Meaning **UNCERTAIN** — **POSSIBLE** glosses: mist, vapor, spring, stream. → For the Akkadian cognate *edû* and ANE water-source tradition, see chapter companion §B.

---

### **Verse 7**

And YHWH Elohim formed the human, dust from the ground, and breathed into his nostrils breath of life, and the human became *a* living being.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL - *adam* / *adamah* WORDPLAY**
> - **אָדָם** (*adam*) = human; **אֲדָמָה** (*adamah*) = ground. Same consonantal root.
> - Preserved in main text through *human* + *ground* pairing; the sonic link is lost in English. **Note per Rule 14:** Hebrew wordplay cannot be fully rendered in English.
>
> 🟢 **NEW VERB - *yatzar***
> - **וַיִּיצֶר** (*vayyitzer*) = "and he formed"—root **י-צ-ר** (*y-tz-r*), potter's verb. Distinct from *bara* (create, Gen 1:1) and *asah* (make, Gen 1:7).
> - Added to glossary for Genesis 2+. *bara* is used distinctively for divine creative action; its precise scope (including whether it implies creation from nothing) is debated. *yatzar* describes hands-on shaping/forming — a different verb for a different action. *asah* = make/fabricate.
>
> 🟢 **NEFESH CHAYAH RETURNS**
> - **נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה** (*nefesh chayah*) = "living being"—previously applied in Gen 1:20, 21, 24, 30 to animals. Here applied to the human. The same category; humans share it with other living creatures.
> - **NOT "soul"** in later philosophical sense (see glossary).

---

### **Verse 8**

And YHWH Elohim planted a garden in Eden from east, and placed there the human whom he had formed.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **EDEN**
> - **עֵדֶן** (*eden*) = a place name here; homophonous with a common noun meaning "delight/pleasure." **POSSIBLE** resonance, not forced in translation.
>
> 🔵 **"FROM EAST"**
> - **מִקֶּדֶם** (*miqqedem*) = "from east" / "from of old" — ambiguous. Spatial reading ("in the east") dominant; temporal reading ("from ancient time") also **POSSIBLE**.

---

### **Verse 9**

And YHWH Elohim caused to sprout from the ground every tree pleasant to the sight and good for food, and the tree of the life in the midst of the garden, and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **TWO TREES**
> - **עֵץ הַחַיִּים** (*etz ha-chayyim*) = "the tree of the life" (with article both places). **עֵץ הַדַּעַת טוֹב וָרָע** (*etz hada'at tov va-ra*) = "the tree of the knowledge-of-good-and-evil." Two distinct trees introduced in one verse; both become central in Gen 3.
>
> 🟢 **GOOD AND EVIL**
> - **טוֹב וָרָע** (*tov va-ra*) — the pairing "good/evil" may be a merism ("everything from X to Y"), i.e., comprehensive knowledge. Meaning debated; preserve ambiguity.

---

### **Verse 10**

And *a* river going out from Eden to water the garden, and from there it divided and became four heads.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🔵 **PARTICIPLE - ONGOING**
> - **יֹצֵא** (*yotze*) = "going out" (participle)—ongoing/habitual action, not completed past.

---

### **Verse 11**

The name of the one *is* Pishon; it *is* the one going around all the land of Havilah, where *the* gold *is*.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **PROPER NAMES**
> - **פִּישׁוֹן** (*Pishon*), **חֲוִילָה** (*Havilah*)—transliterated; geographical identification **UNCERTAIN** in modern terms.

---

### **Verse 12**

And the gold of that land *is* good; there *is* the bdellium and the stone shoham.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🟡 **UNCERTAIN MATERIALS**
> - **בְּדֹלַח** (*bedolach*) = "bdellium"—plant resin or possibly a gemstone; identification debated.
> - **שֹׁהַם** (*shoham*) = "shoham stone"—likely onyx, carnelian, or beryl; identification **UNCERTAIN**. Transliterated here to avoid false precision.

---

### **Verse 13**

And the name of the second river *is* Gihon; it *is* the one going around all the land of Cush.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **CUSH**
> - **כּוּשׁ** (*Kush*) = region south of Egypt (modern Sudan/Ethiopia), though the geography here is debated.

---

### **Verse 14**

And the name of the third river *is* Hiddeqel; it *is* the one going east of Asshur. And the fourth river, that *is* Perat.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **IDENTIFIABLE RIVERS**
> - **חִדֶּקֶל** (*Hiddeqel*) = Tigris; **פְּרָת** (*Perat*) = Euphrates. Rivers 1–2 (Pishon, Gihon) **UNCERTAIN**. → For Mesopotamian river geography and ANE context, see chapter companion §B.

---

### **Verse 15**

And YHWH Elohim took the human and placed him in the garden of Eden to work it and to keep it.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **TO WORK AND TO KEEP**
> - **לְעָבְדָהּ וּלְשָׁמְרָהּ** (*le'ovdah ulshomrah*) = "to work it and to keep it"—feminine suffixes (antecedent **UNCERTAIN** — **POSSIBLE**: *gan* or *adamah*). Paired infinitives: **ע-ב-ד** (serve/work), **שׁ-מ-ר** (keep/guard/observe). → For the Tabernacle echo (Num 3:7–8, 8:26) and priestly-vocation intertext, see chapter companion §C.

---

### **Verse 16**

And YHWH Elohim commanded upon the human, saying, @@"From every tree of the garden eating you shall eat.@@

> **NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **INFINITIVE ABSOLUTE**
> - **אָכֹל תֹּאכֵל** (*akhol tokhel*) = "eating you shall eat"—Hebrew **infinitive absolute** construction used for emphasis. Traditional smooth renderings: "you may freely eat" / "you shall surely eat."
> - **Preserved structurally** per Rule 5; awkwardness is intentional. Paired with the negative counterpart in v.17.

---

### **Verse 17**

@@But from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, you shall not eat from it, for in the day of your eating from it dying you shall die."@@

> **NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **INFINITIVE ABSOLUTE (NEGATIVE)**
> - **מוֹת תָּמוּת** (*mot tamut*) = "dying you shall die"—same emphatic infinitive-absolute construction as v.16. Traditional smooth rendering: "you shall surely die."
> - The parallel form (positive in v.16, negative/lethal here) heightens the contrast. Preservation is required.
>
> 🟡 **"IN THE DAY"**
> - **בְּיוֹם** (*beyom*) = "in the day"—can mean literal day or idiom for "at the time that." The apparent tension with Gen 3 (they do not die the same day they eat) is an old interpretive crux; preserve the Hebrew, do not resolve.
> - Gen 5:5 reports Adam lived 930 years before dying — much later than "the day" of eating. Multiple interpretive solutions are all **POSSIBLE**, none imposed: *beyom* as idiomatic "when"; death as process activated at eating but consummated later; "day" as divine-scale time (cf. Ps 90:4); spiritual death preceding physical death. For fuller discussion, see companion Section D4.

---

### **Verse 18**

And YHWH Elohim said, @@"Not good the being of the human alone; I will make for him *a* helper as his counterpart."@@

> **NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL - *ezer kenegdo***
> - **עֵזֶר כְּנֶגְדּוֹ** (*ezer kenegdo*) = "helper as his counterpart." **NOT "helpmate,"** which imports a KJV-era compression absent from the Hebrew.
> - **עֵזֶר** (*ezer*) = help/helper — appears ~21× in the Hebrew Bible, ~16 of those referring to God as Israel's helper in military/rescue contexts; only 2 refer to the woman (Gen 2:18, 2:20). The statistical profile of *ezer* is **predominantly superior-help**, not subordinate-help. **POSSIBLE** meanings: "a helper corresponding to him" / "a counterpart for him." Mutual, not subordinate. For fuller discussion, see companion Section A6.
> - **כְּנֶגְדּוֹ** (*kenegdo*) = "as opposite him" / "as his counterpart" — preposition **נֶגֶד** (*neged*) = "in front of, opposite, corresponding." **PROBABLE**: mutual and complementary, not hierarchical.
>
> 🔵 **FIRST "NOT GOOD"**
> - Contrasts sharply with Gen 1's repeated "good" and "very good." First *לֹא־טוֹב* (*lo tov*) in the Bible.

---

### **Verse 19**

And YHWH Elohim formed from the ground every living thing of the field and every bird of the sky, and brought *them* to the human to see what he would call to it; and all that the human called to *each* living being, that *is* its name.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **yatzar AGAIN**
> - *yatzar* (formed) is used here for animals — same verb as v.7 used for the human. **NOT *bara*** (which Gen 1 used at 1:21 for animals and 1:27 for humans). Gen 2 prefers the potter's verb.
>
> 🔵 **PLURAL-TO-SINGULAR**
> - Hebrew shifts between "them" and "it/each": the animals are brought collectively, but the naming is individual. Preserved in translation by the *each* italic.

---

### **Verse 20**

And the human called names to all the livestock, and to the bird of the sky, and to every living thing of the field, but for the human he did not find a helper as his counterpart.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **NAMING**
> - Naming in the ancient Near East is an act of dominion/classification — echoes Gen 1:26, 28 *radah* (dominion) but executed in concrete form. Not theological import; note structurally.
>
> 🔵 **"FOR THE HUMAN"**
> - **וּלְאָדָם** (*ul'adam*) without the article here—**POSSIBLE** transition toward proper-name use. Most translations restore the article; Hebrew permits both readings.

---

### **Verse 21**

And YHWH Elohim caused a deep sleep to fall upon the human, and he slept; and he took one of his {a:sides/ribs}, and closed flesh beneath it.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🟢 **DEEP SLEEP**
> - **תַּרְדֵּמָה** (*tardemah*) = "deep sleep"—distinct from ordinary sleep; used later for divinely induced sleep (e.g., Abraham, Gen 15:12). Not trance, not death.
>
> 🔴 **AMBIGUITY - *tsela***
> - **צֵלָע** (*tsela*) = "side / rib / flank." The word appears **~41× in the Hebrew Bible. The large majority (~35) refer to architectural side** — Tabernacle walls, Ark panels, Temple chambers (Exod 25–27, 36–38; 1 Kgs 6–7; Ezek 41). Only ~2 refer to human anatomy (here and the parallel in v.22). "Rib" is the traditional but **statistically marginal** rendering.
> - **Both preserved** in main text via slash (Rule 2). The woman is built from a *side* (substantial) or a *rib* (anatomical) of the human; Hebrew permits both.

---

### **Verse 22**

And YHWH Elohim built the {a:side/rib} which he had taken from the human into *a* woman, and brought her to the human.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **NEW VERB - *banah***
> - **וַיִּבֶן** (*vayyiven*) = "and he built"—root **ב-נ-ה** (*b-n-h*), construction verb. The only place in the creation narratives where God's action is described as **building**. Distinct from *bara*, *asah*, *yatzar*.
> - **POSSIBLE** resonance: later, the same root is used of architectural and familial "building" (e.g., "to build a house" = to establish a family line).

---

### **Verse 23**

And the human said, "This *one*, this time, bone from my bones and flesh from my flesh; to this *one* shall be called *ishah* (woman), for from *ish* (man) was this *one* taken."

> **NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL - *ish* / *ishah* WORDPLAY**
> - **אִישׁ** (*ish*) = man; **אִשָּׁה** (*ishah*) = woman. The phonetic pair is preserved in transliteration in the main text so the Hebrew wordplay is visible. Etymologically the two words are **not** from the same root (despite appearances), but the text plays on the sound.
> - **The first recorded human speech** in the Bible.
>
> 🔵 **"THIS ONE, THIS TIME"**
> - **זֹאת הַפַּעַם** (*zot hapa'am*) = "this *one*, this time"—emphatic; contrasts with the animals of vv. 19–20 that did not qualify.

---

### **Verse 24**

Therefore shall *a* man leave his father and his mother and cling to his woman, and they shall become one flesh.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🔵 **NARRATOR'S ASIDE**
> - Shift from character speech (v.23) to narrator's commentary (v.24). Hebrew **עַל־כֵּן** (*al-ken*) = "therefore / for this reason"—etiological marker explaining a present-day practice.
>
> 🟢 **"ONE FLESH"**
> - **בָּשָׂר אֶחָד** (*basar echad*) = "one flesh"—same adjective *echad* (one) as Gen 1:5 *"day one"*; here describes union, not enumeration.

---

### **Verse 25**

And they were both of them naked, the human and his woman, and they were not ashamed.

> **NOTES**
>
> 🔴 **FORESHADOWING WORDPLAY**
> - **עֲרוֹם** (*arom*) = naked. Gen 3:1 opens with **עָרוּם** (*arum*) = crafty/shrewd (of the serpent). Same consonants, different vocalization. Hebrew wordplay foreshadowing the next scene.
> - **Note per Rule 14:** cannot be rendered in English; flag for reader.

## CROSS-CHAPTER TRACKING (Gen 1 → Gen 2)

**Verb-of-creation shift:**
- Gen 1: *bara* (1:1, 1:21, 1:27), *asah* (1:7, 1:16, 1:25)
- Gen 2 introduces: *yatzar* (2:7 human, 2:19 animals), *banah* (2:22 woman)
- Gen 2 also *bara* + *asah* paired in 2:3 summary ("created to make")

**Genre shift per Rule 23:**
- Gen 1: liturgical rhythm, locked formulas ("And God said... and it was so... evening and morning...")
- Gen 2: narrative prose; no locked formulas. The Gen 1 formula structure is **not** imposed here.

**Order reversal:**
- Gen 1:1 — "skies and land"
- Gen 2:4b — "land and skies"

**Article on *adam*:**
- Gen 1: *adam* (anarthrous) at 1:26; *ha'adam* (with article) at 1:27 — generic humanity
- Gen 2: *ha'adam* nearly throughout — still generic "the human"; no proper-name use yet

**First occurrences in Gen 2:**
- First YHWH (v.4)
- First "not good" (v.18)
- First human speech (v.23)
- First sanctification / holy category (v.3)
- First commandment (v.16–17)
