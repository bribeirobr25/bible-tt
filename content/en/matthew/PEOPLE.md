# Matthew — People (Chapters 1--3)
## The Transparent Translation (TT)

**Ruleset:** v3.0 (Rule 29 governs this file)
**Status:** provisional

**Note:** Figures who appear in both Genesis and Matthew are cross-referenced to `genesis/PEOPLE.md` rather than duplicated. New entries follow the same field structure. Transliterations follow TT convention (Rules 4, 25).

## Avraham (Abraham)
**See:** genesis/PEOPLE.md
**In Matthew:** First in the genealogy (1:2); the genealogy begins "son of Avraham" (1:1). Matthew structures Yeshua's lineage as descending from Avraham through David — establishing Avraham as the covenantal origin point of the Messianic line.

---

## Yitschaq (Isaac)
**See:** genesis/PEOPLE.md
**In Matthew:** Named in the genealogy (1:2) as son of Avraham and father of Ya'aqov. No narrative role in Matthew 1--3.

---

## Ya'aqov (Jacob)
**See:** genesis/PEOPLE.md
**In Matthew:** Named in the genealogy (1:2) as son of Yitschaq and father of Yehudah and his brothers. No narrative role in Matthew 1--3.

---

## Yehudah (Judah)
**See:** genesis/PEOPLE.md
**In Matthew:** Named in the genealogy (1:2--3) as son of Ya'aqov and father of Perets and Zerach by Tamar. Matthew specifies "Yehudah and his brothers" — the only genealogical entry to mention siblings, marking the selection of the Judahite line.

---

## Tamar (Tamar)
**See:** genesis/PEOPLE.md
**In Matthew:** Named in the genealogy (1:3) as the mother of Perets and Zerach by Yehudah. One of four women (plus Miryam) included in the genealogy — an unusual feature in Jewish patrilineal genealogies. Her story (Gen 38) involves deception and sexual irregularity; her inclusion may signal that God's purposes work through unexpected and socially marginal figures.

---

## Rachav (Rahab)
**Meaning:** uncertain (possibly "broad" or "wide")
**Origin:** Canaanite woman of Yericho (Josh 2:1--21; 6:22--25)
**First mention in Matthew:** 1:5
**In Matthew:** Named in the genealogy (1:5) as wife of Salmon and mother of Boaz. The identification of this Rachav with the Rachav of Joshua is not stated in the OT genealogies (cf. Ruth 4:20--21; 1 Chr 2:11) — it is a tradition reflected in Matthew's genealogy. Second of four women named before Miryam.
**Note:** The Hebrew Bible spells the name רָחָב (*Rachav*). The Greek is Ῥαχάβ (*Rhachab*). Not to be confused with the sea monster Rahab (רַהַב, *Rahav*, different vowel pattern).

---

## Rut (Ruth)
**Meaning:** uncertain (possibly "friend" or "companion")
**Origin:** Moabite woman (Ruth 1:4)
**First mention in Matthew:** 1:5
**In Matthew:** Named in the genealogy (1:5) as wife of Boaz and mother of Oved. Third of four women named before Miryam. A Moabite — her inclusion in the Davidic lineage is significant given Deut 23:3's exclusion of Moabites from the assembly.
**Note:** The TT renders the name as Rut (Hebrew רוּת, *Rut*). Greek: Ῥούθ (*Rhouth*).

---

## Uriyah (Uriah)
**Meaning:** "my light is YHWH" (*uri* + *Yah*)
**Origin:** Hittite soldier, husband of Bat-Sheva (2 Sam 11)
**First mention in Matthew:** 1:6
**In Matthew:** Not named directly — Matthew refers to Shelomoh's mother as "the *one* of Uriyah" (τῆς τοῦ Οὐρίου, 1:6), identifying Bat-Sheva by her first husband rather than by her own name. Fourth of four women (or references to women) before Miryam. The circumlocution points to the adultery and murder in 2 Sam 11 without narrating it.
**Note:** The Greek reads Οὐρίου (*Ouriou*). The TT renders from Hebrew אוּרִיָּה (*Uriyah*). The Hittite designation marks him as a non-Israelite — another outsider in the Messianic lineage.

---

## David (David)
**Meaning:** "beloved" (possibly; etymology debated)
**Origin:** Son of Yishai (Jesse) of Bet-Lechem (Bethlehem), tribe of Yehudah
**First mention in Matthew:** 1:1
**In Matthew:** Named in the genealogy heading (1:1 — "son of David"), at 1:6 as the king who begot Shelomoh by the one of Uriyah, and at 1:17 as the structural pivot of the genealogy (Avraham to David = 14 generations; David to the deportation = 14; deportation to the anointed one = 14). "Son of David" becomes a recurring Messianic title throughout Matthew (9:27; 12:23; 15:22; 20:30--31; 21:9, 15; 22:42).
**Cross-reference:** Full entry will appear in samuel/PEOPLE.md or kings/PEOPLE.md when those books are translated.

---

## Shelomoh (Solomon)
**Meaning:** "his peace" or "peaceful" (from *shalom*)
**Origin:** Son of David and Bat-Sheva (2 Sam 12:24)
**First mention in Matthew:** 1:6--7
**In Matthew:** Named in the genealogy (1:6--7) as son of David (by the one of Uriyah) and father of Rechav'am (Rehoboam). Through Shelomoh the royal line continues to the Babylonian deportation.
**Cross-reference:** Full entry will appear in kings/PEOPLE.md when that book is translated.

## Yeshua (Jesus)
**Meaning:** "YHWH saves" or "YHWH is salvation" (Hebrew יֵשׁוּעַ, *Yeshua*, a late form of יְהוֹשֻׁעַ, *Yehoshua*)
**Origin:** BORN
**Father:** legally — Yosef son of Ya'aqov (Matt 1:16, 20); biologically — "from the holy spirit" per the text (1:18, 20)
**Mother:** Miryam (1:16, 18)
**Spouse(s):** none stated in Matthew 1--3
**Children:** none stated in Matthew 1--3
**Location(s):** born in Bet-Lechem of Yehudah (2:1); taken to Egypt (2:14); settled in Natseret of the Galil (2:23); came to the Yarden for immersion (3:13); led into the wilderness (4:1)
**First mention:** Matt 1:1 (genealogy heading); 1:16 (birth); 1:21 (naming)
**Mentioned in:** Matt 1:1, 16, 18, 21, 25; 2:1; 3:13, 15--16
**Key events in Matthew 1--3:** Named in the genealogy as the anointed one, son of David, son of Avraham (1:1); conceived "from the holy spirit" (1:18, 20); named Yeshua by Yosef per angelic instruction — "for he will save his people from their sins" (1:21); born in Bet-Lechem (2:1); visited by the magi (2:11); taken to Egypt to escape Herodes (2:14); returned after Herodes's death and settled in Natseret (2:23); came to Yochanan for immersion in the Yarden (3:13); immersed, the skies opened, the spirit of God descended like a dove, a voice from the skies said "This is my son, the beloved, in whom I am well pleased" (3:16--17)

**Note:** The name etymology is stated in the text itself (1:21). The Greek Ἰησοῦς (*Iēsous*) derives from Hebrew/Aramaic יֵשׁוּעַ (*Yeshua*). The TT renders the name in its Hebrew form per transliteration policy.

## Miryam (Mary)
**Meaning:** uncertain (possibly "bitter," "beloved," or "rebellious"; etymology debated since antiquity)
**Origin:** BORN (no parents named in Matthew)
**Father:** not stated in Matthew
**Mother:** not stated in Matthew
**Spouse(s):** Yosef (betrothed, 1:18; married, 1:24)
**Children:** Yeshua (1:25); brothers and sisters mentioned later (13:55--56)
**Location(s):** Bet-Lechem (2:1, 11); Egypt (2:14); Natseret (2:23)
**First mention:** Matt 1:16
**Mentioned in:** Matt 1:16, 18, 20; 2:11, 13--14, 20--21
**Key events in Matthew 1--3:** Named in the genealogy as the one from whom Yeshua was born (1:16); found to be pregnant "from the holy spirit" before coming together with Yosef (1:18); identified by the angel as the one whose child is from the holy spirit (1:20); bore a son, named Yeshua (1:25); present when the magi visited and offered gifts (2:11); fled to Egypt with Yosef and the child (2:14); returned to the land of Israel after Herodes's death (2:21)

**Note:** Matthew's birth narrative is told from Yosef's perspective (contrast Luke 1--2, which centers on Miryam). Miryam does not speak or act independently in Matthew 1--2. The genealogy's phrasing at 1:16 shifts from the active pattern ("X begot Y") to passive ("from whom was born Yeshua") — a grammatical break that the TT preserves.

---

## Yosef (Joseph)
**Meaning:** "he will add" or "may he add" (Gen 30:24; from *yasaf*)
**Origin:** BORN
**Father:** Ya'aqov (Matt 1:16; contrast Luke 3:23 — "of Eli")
**Mother:** not stated
**Spouse(s):** Miryam (1:18--20, 24)
**Children:** Yeshua (legally, 1:25); further children implied later (13:55--56)
**Location(s):** Bet-Lechem (2:1); Egypt (2:14); Natseret (2:23)
**First mention:** Matt 1:16
**Mentioned in:** Matt 1:16, 18--20, 24; 2:13--15, 19--23
**Key events in Matthew 1--3:** Named as husband of Miryam and legal father of Yeshua in the genealogy (1:16); described as "righteous" (*dikaios*), planned to release Miryam quietly (1:19); received angelic instruction in a dream to take Miryam as wife (1:20); named the child Yeshua (1:25); warned in a dream to flee to Egypt (2:13); took the child and Miryam to Egypt (2:14); after Herodes's death, called in a dream to return (2:19--21); warned in a dream about Archelaos, withdrew to the Galil, settled in Natseret (2:22--23)

**Note:** Yosef receives four dreams with divine instruction in Matthew 1--2 (1:20; 2:13; 2:19; 2:22) — a pattern that echoes the patriarch Yosef of Genesis, who was also a dreamer and also went to Egypt. Whether Matthew intends this typology or whether it is a later reader's observation is debated. The TT does not state the typology in the main text.

---

## Herodes (Herod)
**Meaning:** "heroic" (Greek name, Ἡρῴδης, *Hērōdēs*)
**Origin:** BORN (c. 73/72 BCE)
**Full designation:** Herod the Great (Herodes I), king of Judea under Roman authority
**Father:** Antipater the Idumaean
**Mother:** Cypros (Nabataean)
**Spouse(s):** ten wives recorded by Josephus, including Mariamne I (Hasmonean) and Mariamne II
**Location(s):** Yerushalayim (2:1, 3, 7)
**First mention:** Matt 2:1
**Mentioned in:** Matt 2:1, 3, 7, 12, 13, 15, 16, 19, 22
**Key events in Matthew 1--3:** Reigning as king when Yeshua was born in Bet-Lechem (2:1); troubled by the magi's report of a newborn "king of the Yehudim" (2:2--3); assembled chief priests and scribes to learn the birthplace of the anointed one (2:4); secretly summoned the magi and sent them to Bet-Lechem (2:7--8); furious when the magi did not return, ordered the killing of all male children in Bet-Lechem two years old and under (2:16); died (2:19) — Yosef was then instructed to return

**Note:** Historical Herodes ruled 37--4 BCE (Josephus, *AJ* 17.191; *BJ* 1.665). His death in 4 BCE places Yeshua's birth before that date — the traditional "1 CE" dating is a later calculation error by Dionysius Exiguus (6th c.). The TT does not harmonize the Matthean narrative with external chronology in the main text; discrepancies are noted per Rule 13.

## Yochanan the Immerser (John the Baptist)
**Meaning:** "YHWH is gracious" (Hebrew יוֹחָנָן, *Yochanan*); "the Immerser" renders βαπτιστής (*baptistēs*) per locked glossary (immersion, not transliterated "baptism")
**Origin:** BORN (no birth narrative in Matthew; cf. Luke 1)
**Father:** not stated in Matthew (Luke 1:5 — Zechariah/Zekaryah)
**Mother:** not stated in Matthew (Luke 1:5 — Elizabeth/Elisheva)
**Spouse(s):** none stated
**Children:** none stated
**Location(s):** the wilderness of Yehudah (3:1); the Yarden (3:6, 13)
**First mention:** Matt 3:1
**Mentioned in:** Matt 3:1--14
**Key events in Matthew 1--3:** Appeared in the wilderness of Yehudah proclaiming "Turn back, for the kingdom of the skies has drawn near" (3:1--2); identified by the text as the one spoken of by Yeshayahu the prophet — "a voice crying in the wilderness" (3:3, quoting Isa 40:3); wore camel's hair with a leather belt, ate locusts and wild honey (3:4); people from Yerushalayim, all Yehudah, and the Yarden region came for immersion, confessing their sins (3:5--6); warned the Perushim and Tsedoqim coming for immersion — "offspring of vipers" (3:7); proclaimed the coming of one stronger than himself whose sandals he was not worthy to carry, who would immerse in the holy spirit and fire (3:11); immersed Yeshua in the Yarden despite initial reluctance — "I need to be immersed by you" (3:13--14); Yeshua's reply: "Let it be so now, for thus it is fitting for us to fulfill all righteousness" (3:15)

**Note:** Matthew does not narrate Yochanan's birth or family background (contrast Luke 1). His introduction at 3:1 is abrupt — he simply "appeared." The description of his clothing (3:4) echoes the description of Eliyahu in 2 Kings 1:8 (leather belt, hairy garment). Matthew later makes the Eliyahu identification explicit (11:14; 17:12--13), but the TT does not import later passages into the chapter 3 translation.
