## Isaiah 7:14 — the virgin/young woman will conceive (Matt 1:22–23)

**Verse:** Matthew 1:22–23
**Text says:** "And all this happened so that what was spoken by the Lord through the prophet might be fulfilled, saying: 'See, the virgin/young woman will conceive and will bear a son, and they will call his name Immanu'el' — which is, being translated, 'God with us.'"
**Context:** The narrator interrupts the dream scene (1:18–25) with the first of Matthew's fulfillment formulas (ἵνα πληρωθῇ). The formula introduces Isaiah 7:14, applied to Miryam's conception of Yeshua. This is the inaugural fulfillment-formula citation in the book — it establishes the pattern that recurs at 2:5–6, 2:15, 2:17–18, 2:23, and 3:3.
**Subject:** The "virgin/young woman" (*parthenos*/*almah*) — Matthew applies the figure to Miryam (Mary); Isaiah's original context addresses a sign given to King Achaz during the Syro-Ephraimite crisis (c. 735 BCE). The Greek of Matthew quotes the LXX (*parthenos*, more strongly implying virginity); the Hebrew of Isaiah uses *almah* ("young woman of marriageable age"). The TT renders the slash form "virgin/young woman" per Rule 2 (Ambiguity Preservation).
**Fulfillment status:** CLAIMED
**Fulfillment notes:** Matthew uses the formula ἵνα πληρωθῇ ("so that it might be fulfilled") — a direct fulfillment formula per Entry M-001 §Direct. Matthew quotes the LXX text (*parthenos*) rather than the Hebrew source (*almah*); the choice predetermines the virginity reading. Isaiah 7:14's original context is the Syro-Ephraimite crisis: a sign to Achaz that within the time a young woman bears a son and weans him, the threatening northern coalition will collapse. The near-fulfillment in Isaiah's day is widely accepted by historical-critical scholarship; Matthew's typological reapplication to Yeshua's conception is the interpretive move Matthew explicitly claims. The doubled naming (Yeshua / Immanu'el) in Matt 1:21–23 is itself part of Matthew's claim. → See `docs/editorial-log/matthew.md` Entry M-002 for the full *parthenos*/*almah* slash-policy reasoning and the Hebrew *almah* vs. Hebrew *betulah* (the standard term for "virgin") distinction.
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: Isaiah 7:14 is read in its near-fulfillment context — a sign to King Achaz about the imminent failure of the Syro-Ephraimite coalition. *Almah* means "young woman," not specifically a virgin (the Hebrew word for virgin is *betulah*). The verse is not understood as a messianic prophecy in mainstream Jewish interpretation. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: Matthew's citation has been read since the second century as fulfillment prophecy of Yeshua's virgin conception. The LXX's *parthenos* (which more strongly implies virginity than Hebrew *almah*) was treated by patristic interpreters (Justin Martyr, *Dialogue* 43; Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 3.21) as evidence that the Greek translators had preserved the original prophetic intent. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: The Quran affirms Miryam's virginal conception of Isa (Quran 19:20; 3:47) but does not derive this from Isaiah 7:14 specifically. The Islamic reading affirms the miracle of conception without engaging Matthew's typological framework or the LXX-versus-Hebrew translation question. [DOCUMENTED]
