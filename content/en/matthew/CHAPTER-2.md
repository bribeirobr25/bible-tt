**This chapter is the most OT-heavy chapter in the birth narrative.** Four formula quotations ("so that what was spoken might be fulfilled") drive the narrative: Micah 5:1 (Beyt-Lechem), Hosea 11:1 (out of Mitsrayim), Jeremiah 31:15 (Rachel weeping), and a mysterious "He shall be called a Natsri" with no single OT source. Each quotation uses kyrios Option C with the OT YHWH connection noted. Proper names follow TT transliteration: Herodes (not Herod), Beyt-Lechem (not Bethlehem), Mitsrayim (not Egypt), Natseret (not Nazareth).

## CHAPTER OVERVIEW

**What happens:** Wise men (magi) from the east arrive in Jerusalem asking where the king of the Yehudim has been born, having seen his star at its rising. Herod the king is disturbed and gathers the chief priests and scribes, who identify Bethlehem from Micah 5:1. Herod secretly summons the magi, learns the time of the star's appearance, and sends them to Bethlehem. The star leads them to the child; they offer gold, frankincense, and myrrh, then depart by another way, warned in a dream. A messenger of the Lord appears to Joseph in a dream: flee to Egypt with the child and his mother. Joseph obeys. The narrator cites Hosea 11:1: "Out of Egypt I called my son." Herod, realizing the magi have outwitted him, orders the killing of all male children in Bethlehem and its vicinity, two years old and under. The narrator cites Jeremiah 31:15: Rachel weeping for her children. After Herod dies, a messenger appears again in a dream: return to the land of Yisra'el. Joseph returns but, fearing Herod's son Archelaos in Judah, withdraws to the Galilee and settles in Nazareth. The narrator's final formula: "so that what was spoken through the prophets might be fulfilled: 'He shall be called a Natsri.'"

**Key themes:** Four OT fulfillment formulas structuring the narrative; the magi as Gentile worshippers recognizing what Jerusalem does not; Herod as threatened king (Moses/Pharaoh parallel); the flight to and return from Egypt (Exodus typology); the mysterious "Natsri" designation with no identifiable single OT source; Joseph as dreamer receiving divine direction (four dreams total: 1:20, 2:13, 2:19, 2:22).

**Watch for:** The magi are not numbered and not called kings — "three" comes from the three gifts, not the text; the star's behavior (it "goes before them" and "stands over" the place) is narrated without scientific explanation; Herod's calculation of "two years and under" implies the star appeared some time before the magi arrive; the Hosea 11:1 quotation in its original context refers to Israel's exodus from Egypt, not to a future messiah — Matthew reads it typologically; the "Natsri" quotation cites "the prophets" (plural), not a single prophet, and no known OT verse matches exactly.

**Connection:** The four dreams to Joseph continue the patriarch-Joseph typology from chapter 1. The flight to Egypt and return recapitulates the Exodus narrative. The formula quotations establish Matthew's pattern of reading Jesus's life as a re-enactment of Israel's story. The magi from the east anticipate the Gospel's later movement toward Gentile inclusion.

## VERSE-BY-VERSE STUDY

### **Verse 1**

Now *after* Yeshua (Jesus) was born in Beyt-Lechem (Bethlehem) of Yehudah (Judah) in the days of Herodes (Herod) the king, see, *magoi* from *the* east came to Yerushalayim (Jerusalem),

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 ***MAGOI* — TRANSLITERATED**
> - **μάγοι** (*magoi*) = plural of *magos*: Persian priestly caste, astrologers, wise men, interpreters of dreams and stars (Herodotus, 1.101). The text does not number them, does not call them kings, and does not name them — "three" is inferred from the three gifts (v.11), not from the text itself. The TT transliterates *magoi* at first occurrence, preserving the Greek term's breadth rather than selecting one meaning.
>
> *For fuller discussion of the *magoi*'s identity and the Persian priestly-caste parallel, see companion Section B.*
>
> 🟢 **BEYT-LECHEM — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Βηθλέεμ** (*Bēthleem*) = Beyt-Lechem (Hebrew: **בֵּית לֶחֶם**, "house of bread/food"). Familiar: Bethlehem. Located about 9 km south of Jerusalem. The specification "of Judah" distinguishes from the Bethlehem in the territory of Zevulun (Josh 19:15).
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — HERODES**
> - **Ἡρῴδης** (*Hērōdēs*) = Herodes (Herod the Great), king of Judah under Roman authority (r. c. 37-4 BCE). The Greek form is transliterated as Herodes per TT policy. The dating of Herod's death to 4 BCE means the birth of Jesus is placed before the common "year 1" — a known chronological discrepancy in the later calendar system.

---

### **Verse 2**

saying, "Where is the one born king of the Yehudim? For we saw his star at *its* rising and came to bow down to him."

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"AT *ITS* RISING" — *EN TĒ ANATOLĒ***
> - **ἐν τῇ ἀνατολῇ** (*en tē anatolē*) = "at its rising" (singular). The same word in the plural (*anatolai*) means "the east" (v.1). The singular form here likely refers to the star's heliacal rising (its first appearance above the horizon), not to a geographic direction. The TT adds *its* in italics.
>
> 🟢 **"BOW DOWN" — *PROSKYNEŌ***
> - **προσκυνῆσαι** (*proskynēsai*) = to bow down, do obeisance, prostrate oneself. The word covers the full range from political homage to divine worship. The TT renders "bow down" to preserve the ambiguity — the *magoi* may intend royal homage, divine worship, or both. The same verb recurs at vv.8, 11.
>
> 🟢 **"KING OF THE YEHUDIM"**
> - **βασιλεὺς τῶν Ἰουδαίων** (*basileus tōn Ioudaiōn*) = "king of the Yehudim." This title appears again at the crucifixion (Matt 27:11, 29, 37). The *magoi* use it; Herodes (Herod) hears it as a threat. The title frames the entire Gospel.

---

### **Verse 3**

And Herodes (Herod) the king, having heard, was troubled, and all Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) with him.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"WAS TROUBLED" — *ETARACHTHĒ***
> - **ἐταράχθη** (*etarachthē*) = aorist passive, "was troubled/disturbed/shaken." The same verb describes the disciples' fear at seeing Jesus walk on water (Matt 14:26). The passive may suggest an external force of disturbance. "All Jerusalem" troubled with Herod — the city shares the king's anxiety rather than welcoming the news.

---

### **Verse 4**

And having gathered all the chief priests and scribes of the people, he inquired of them where the anointed one is born.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"THE ANOINTED ONE" — HERODES' QUESTION**
> - The *magoi* asked about "the king of the Yehudim" (v.2); Herodes (Herod) reformulates as "the anointed one" (*ho christos*). The shift moves the question from political to messianic language. The chief priests and scribes know the scriptural answer but do not act on it — they provide the information and disappear from the narrative.

---

### **Verse 5**

And they said to him, "In Beyt-Lechem (Bethlehem) of Yehudah (Judah), for thus it has been written through the prophet:

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"IT HAS BEEN WRITTEN" — *GEGRAPTAI***
> - **γέγραπται** (*gegraptai*) = perfect passive, "it has been written" — standard scripture-citation formula indicating completed action with ongoing authority. The quotation that follows (v.6) draws primarily from Micah 5:1 [5:2 in English Bibles].

---

### **Verse 6**

'And you, Beyt-Lechem (Bethlehem), land of Yehudah (Judah), are by no means least among the rulers of Judah; for out of you will come a ruler who will shepherd my people Yisra'el.'"

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — MICAH 5:1 QUOTATION (MODIFIED)**
> - The quotation draws from **Micah 5:1 [5:2 in English]** but modifies it: the MT reads "too small to be among the clans" but Matthew reverses to "by no means least among the rulers." The addition "who will shepherd my people Yisra'el" blends in **2 Samuel 5:2** (David as shepherd-king), making this a composite quotation — a common Second Temple citation practice. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL].
>
> *For the full comparison of Matthew's text against the MT and the LXX (the ancient Greek translation), and the hermeneutical method of composite quotation, see companion Section A.*
>
> 🟡 **"SHEPHERD MY PEOPLE" — DAVIDIC ECHO**
> - **ποιμανεῖ τὸν λαόν μου** (*poimanei ton laon mou*) = "will shepherd my people." The shepherd metaphor for kingship is pervasive in the HB (2 Sam 5:2, Ps 78:70-72, Ezek 34). The phrase "my people Yisra'el" is YHWH's language — the underlying speaker is YHWH even though the Greek says "my people" without the divine name.

---

### **Verse 7**

Then Herodes (Herod), having secretly called the *magoi*, determined from them the exact time of the appearing star.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"SECRETLY" — *LATHRA***
> - **λάθρᾳ** (*lathra*) = secretly, privately. Herod's secrecy contrasts with the *magoi*'s openness. The detail sets up the deception of v.8 and the rage of v.16.
>
> 🔵 **"DETERMINED" — *ĒKRIBŌSEN***
> - **ἠκρίβωσεν** (*ēkribōsen*) = determined precisely, ascertained exactly. The verb emphasizes precision — Herod wants the exact chronology. This detail explains the "two years and under" calculation in v.16.

---

### **Verse 8**

And sending them to Beyt-Lechem (Bethlehem), he said, "Go, search carefully for the child, and when you find *him*, report to me, so that I also may come and bow down to him."

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **HERODES' DECEPTION**
> - Herodes (Herod) uses the *magoi*'s own word — **προσκυνήσω** (*proskynēsō*) = "I may bow down" — echoing their stated purpose (v.2). His intention is destruction (v.13), not worship. The narrative expects the reader to hear the irony.

---

### **Verse 9**

And having heard the king, they went, and see, the star which they saw at *its* rising went before them until it came and stood over *the place* where the child was.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE STAR'S BEHAVIOR**
> - The star **προῆγεν** (*proēgen*) = "went before/led" and **ἐστάθη** (*estathē*) = "stood" over the place. The text narrates this guiding-and-stopping behavior without explanation or astronomical comment. The TT translates what the text says without scientific rationalization.
>
> *For a survey of modern astronomical identifications (conjunction, comet, supernova) and their limitations, see companion Section G.*

---

### **Verse 10**

And seeing the star, they rejoiced *with* exceedingly great joy.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **EMPHATIC JOY — COGNATE CONSTRUCTION**
> - **ἐχάρησαν χαρὰν μεγάλην σφόδρα** (*echarēsan charan megalēn sphodra*) = literally "they joyed *with* great joy exceedingly." The cognate accusative (verb + noun from the same root) is a Semitic construction common in the LXX. The TT preserves the emphatic redundancy.

---

### **Verse 11**

And coming into the house, they saw the child with Miryam (Mary) his mother, and falling down, they bowed down to him, and opening their treasures, they offered to him gifts: gold and frankincense and myrrh.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "THE HOUSE," NOT A MANGER**
> - **τὴν οἰκίαν** (*tēn oikian*) = "the house." Matthew says house, not stable or manger. The manger (*phatnē*) appears only in Luke 2:7. The *magoi* find the child in a house — either the family has moved since the birth, or Matthew's account differs from Luke's in setting. The TT translates each Gospel on its own terms without harmonizing.
>
> 🟢 **THREE GIFTS — GOLD, FRANKINCENSE, MYRRH**
> - **χρυσὸν καὶ λίβανον καὶ σμύρναν** (*chryson kai libanon kai smyrnan*). Three gifts (the text does not specify a number of *magoi*): gold = royal tribute; frankincense (*libanos*) = priestly/liturgical incense (cf. Lev 2:1); myrrh (*smyrna*) = burial spice (cf. John 19:39). → For the symbolic-significance reception tradition (Origen and later Christian readings), see chapter companion §F.
>
> 🔵 **"THE CHILD" — *TO PAIDION***
> - **τὸ παιδίον** (*to paidion*) = "the child" (diminutive of *pais*). Not "baby" (*brephos*, Luke 2:12). The term suggests a young child, not a newborn — consistent with the time elapsed implied by Herodes (Herod)'s "two years and under" (v.16).

---

### **Verse 12**

And having been warned in a dream not to return to Herodes (Herod), they departed to their own country by another way.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"WARNED" — *CHRĒMATISTHENTES***
> - **χρηματισθέντες** (*chrēmatisthentes*) = "having been warned/instructed by divine communication." The verb *chrēmatizō* in the passive carries the specific sense of a divine oracle or warning. The *magoi* receive divine guidance — the third dream-revelation in the narrative (after 1:20 and before 2:13).

---

### **Verse 13**

Now *after* they departed, see, a messenger of the Lord appears to Yosef (Joseph) in a dream, saying, "Rise, take the child and his mother, and flee to Mitsrayim (Egypt), and be there until I tell you, for Herodes (Herod) is about to seek the child to destroy him."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — *ANGELOS KYRIOU* ("MESSENGER OF THE LORD") — SECOND APPEARANCE**
> - As in 1:20, *angelos kyriou* renders the HB *malakh YHWH* tradition. Per GS Option C: "the Lord" in main text; YHWH connection noted. This is the second of Joseph's four dream-revelations (1:20, 2:13, 2:19, 2:22).
>
> 🟢 **MITSRAYIM — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Αἴγυπτον** (*Aigypton*) = Mitsrayim (Hebrew: **מִצְרַיִם**, *Mitsrayim*). The Greek name derives from a different tradition than the Hebrew. The TT uses the Hebrew form to maintain the Exodus echo — "flee to Egypt" immediately evokes Israel's sojourn in Egypt. Familiar: Egypt.
>
> 🟡 **MOSES TYPOLOGY — THREATENED CHILD**
> - The pattern — a king orders the death of children; one child is saved; the child goes to/from Egypt — echoes the Moses narrative (Ex 1:15-2:10). Matthew presents Jesus's early life as recapitulating the Exodus story. Whether this is historical memory or theological shaping or both, the text does not resolve. The TT translates the text as presented.

---

### **Verse 14**

And he rose *and* took the child and his mother by night, and departed to Mitsrayim (Egypt),

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **IMMEDIATE OBEDIENCE — NIGHT DEPARTURE**
> - As in 1:24, Yosef (Joseph) acts immediately on the divine instruction. "By night" (**νυκτός**, *nyktos*) adds urgency. The pattern of instant compliance continues the patriarch-Joseph typology.

---

### **Verse 15**

and was there until the death of Herodes (Herod), so that what was spoken by the Lord through the prophet might be fulfilled, saying, "Out of Mitsrayim (Egypt) I called my son."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — HOSEA 11:1 QUOTATION**
> - **ἐξ Αἰγύπτου ἐκάλεσα τὸν υἱόν μου** = "Out of Mitsrayim (Egypt) I called my son." The quotation is from **Hosea 11:1**, where in its original context YHWH's "son" is the nation of Israel — a retrospective statement about the Exodus, not a messianic prophecy. Matthew reads it typologically: as Israel was called out of Egypt, so Yeshua (Jesus) is. The TT translates Matthew's use without adjudicating the hermeneutical method. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL].
>
> The fulfillment formula here uses ἵνα πληρωθῇ (*hina plērōthē*) — a purpose clause: the flight to Egypt is presented as occurring *so that* the Hosea word might be fulfilled. This differs from the τότε (*tote*) formula at v.17, where the massacre is not presented as divinely intended but as retrospectively corresponding to prophecy. The TT preserves both formulas exactly since the theological distinction is textual.
>
> *For the full typological analysis and the distinction between the *hina* and *tote* fulfillment formulas, see companion Sections A1 and A3.*
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "BY THE LORD" — KYRIOS/YHWH**
> - **ὑπὸ κυρίου** (*hypo kyriou*) = "by the Lord." The underlying speaker in Hosea 11:1 is YHWH. Per GS Option C: "the Lord" in main text; YHWH as original speaker noted. The formula "what was spoken by the Lord through the prophet" maintains the distinction: the Lord (YHWH) is the ultimate author; the prophet is the channel.

---

### **Verse 16**

Then Herodes (Herod), seeing that he was outwitted by the *magoi*, was exceedingly angry, and he sent *and* killed all the male children in Beyt-Lechem (Bethlehem) and in all its vicinity, from two years old and under, according to the time which he had determined from the *magoi*.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — SLAUGHTER OF THE INNOCENTS**
> - **ἀνεῖλεν πάντας τοὺς παῖδας** (*aneilen pantas tous paidas*) = "he killed all the male children." The massacre is unattested outside Matthew. Josephus, who catalogues Herod's crimes extensively (*Antiquities* 14-17), does not mention it. Given Bethlehem's small population (estimated 300-1000), the number of male children under two would have been small (perhaps 10-20) — a tragedy that might not register in Josephus's broader account of Herod's atrocities. The historicity is debated; the text narrates it as event.
>
> 🔵 **"TWO YEARS AND UNDER"**
> - **ἀπὸ διετοῦς καὶ κατωτέρω** (*apo dietous kai katōterō*) = "from two years old and under." This implies the star appeared up to two years before the *magoi* arrive in Yerushalayim (Jerusalem). The *magoi*'s journey took time; the child is no longer a newborn.

---

### **Verse 17**

Then was fulfilled what was spoken through Yirmeyahu the prophet, saying:

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **FULFILLMENT FORMULA — VARIANT**
> - **τότε ἐπληρώθη** (*tote eplērōthē*) = "then was fulfilled" — a simple temporal past tense, unlike the purpose clause **ἵνα πληρωθῇ** (*hina plērōthē*) used at 1:22 and 2:15. The massacre is not presented as divinely intended to fulfill prophecy; it is presented as an event that, having occurred, corresponds to the prophetic lament. The distinction is theologically significant.
>
> *For extended discussion of the two formula types and their theological implications, see companion Section A3.*
>
> 🟢 **YIRMEYAHU — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Ἰερεμίου** (*Ieremiou*) = Yirmeyahu (Hebrew: **יִרְמְיָהוּ**, *Yirmeyahu*). Familiar: Jeremiah.

---

### **Verse 18**

"A voice in Ramah was heard, weeping and much lamentation — Rachel weeping for her children, and she would not be comforted, because they are not."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — JEREMIAH 31:15 QUOTATION**
> - The quotation is from **Jeremiah 31:15**, where Rachel weeps for her descendants being taken into exile. Matthew applies the lament to the slaughter of children in Beyt-Lechem (Bethlehem). The consolation verses immediately following in Jeremiah (31:16-17 — "there is hope for your future... your children shall return") are not quoted, but a reader who knows Jeremiah may hear the unquoted hope. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL].
>
> *For the full table comparing Jeremiah 31:15 with Matthew's application and discussion of Rachel's burial location, see companion Section A2.*
>
> 🟡 **"BECAUSE THEY ARE NOT" — *HOTI OUK EISIN***
> - **ὅτι οὐκ εἰσίν** (*hoti ouk eisin*) = "because they are not" — euphemism for death. The phrase is stark. The children simply "are not."

---

### **Verse 19**

Now *after* Herodes (Herod) died, see, a messenger of the Lord appears in a dream to Yosef (Joseph) in Mitsrayim (Egypt),

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **THIRD DREAM — PATTERN CONTINUES**
> - This is the third of four dream-revelations to Yosef (Joseph) (1:20, 2:13, 2:19, 2:22). The *angelos kyriou* formula repeats. The historical present tense **φαίνεται** (*phainetai*) = "appears" (present tense narrating a past event) adds vividness.

---

### **Verse 20**

saying, "Rise, take the child and his mother, and go to the land of Yisra'el, for those seeking the life of the child have died."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — EXODUS 4:19 ECHO**
> - The wording closely parallels **Exodus 4:19** (LXX): "Go, return to Mitsrayim (Egypt), for all those seeking your life have died" — YHWH's instruction to Mosheh (Moses). Matthew reverses the direction (from Egypt to Yisra'el) but preserves the verbal pattern almost exactly, continuing the Moses-Jesus typology. Confidence: VERIFIED [STRONG INFERENCE].
>
> *For the full verbal comparison and Moses typology table, see companion Section A (Cross-Chapter Tracking).*
>
> 🟢 **"THOSE SEEKING" — PLURAL**
> - **οἱ ζητοῦντες** (*hoi zētountes*) = "those seeking" (plural), though only Herod has been named as a threat. The plural may follow the Exodus 4:19 wording, or it may indicate that Herod's agents are also meant.

---

### **Verse 21**

And he rose *and* took the child and his mother, and came into the land of Yisra'el.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **IMMEDIATE OBEDIENCE — CONTINUED**
> - The formula repeats from v.14: "rose, took the child and his mother." Yosef (Joseph)'s response pattern is consistent throughout — divine instruction followed by instant compliance.

---

### **Verse 22**

But hearing that Archelaos reigns over Yehudah (Judah) in place of his father Herodes (Herod), he was afraid to go there, and having been warned in a dream, he withdrew to the regions of the Galil (Galilee).

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **ARCHELAOS**
> - **Ἀρχέλαος** (*Archelaos*) = Archelaos (Archelaus), son of Herod the Great. He ruled Judah, Shomron, and Idumea (4 BCE - 6 CE) with the title ethnarch, not king. Josephus reports his brutality (*Antiquities* 17.13.2) — Augustus eventually removed him. Yosef (Joseph)'s fear is historically plausible.
>
> 🔵 **FOURTH DREAM**
> - **χρηματισθεὶς κατ᾽ ὄναρ** (*chrēmatistheis kat' onar*) = "having been warned in a dream." This is the fourth and final dream-revelation to Joseph. The verb *chrēmatizō* (as in v.12) indicates divine communication. Galilee, ruled by Herod Antipas (a less threatening figure), becomes the setting for most of the Gospel narrative.

### **Verse 23**

And coming, he settled in a city called Natseret (Nazareth), so that what was spoken through the prophets might be fulfilled: "He shall be called a Natsri."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "HE SHALL BE CALLED A NATSRI" — NO SINGLE OT SOURCE**
> - **Ναζωραῖος κληθήσεται** (*Nazōraios klēthēsetai*) = "he shall be called a Natsri." No known OT verse contains this phrase. Uniquely, the formula cites "the prophets" (plural, **τῶν προφητῶν**), not a single prophet — every other formula quotation in Matthew names a single prophet. The TT translates the text's claim without resolving the source question; the mystery is part of the text. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL].
>
> Proposed OT connections: (1) נֵצֶר (*netser*) = "branch" (Isa 11:1; also Jer 23:5, Zech 3:8/6:12 use *tsemach*) — the phonetic similarity is the most commonly proposed link; (2) נָזִיר (*nazir*) = "Nazirite/consecrated one" (Judg 13:5, 7) — partial phonetic overlap; (3) a general theme of lowliness associated with Nazareth (cf. John 1:46). No consensus has been reached; the TT transliterates "Natsri" because no single rendering captures the claim.
>
> *For the full list of proposed OT connections (netser/branch, nazir/Nazirite, despised-Natseret theme), the reception history, and scholarly discussion, see companion Sections A4, G1, and F2.*
>
> 🟢 **NATSERET — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Ναζαρέτ** (*Nazaret*) = Natseret (Hebrew: **נָצְרַת**, *Natseret*). Familiar: Nazareth. A small village in the lower Galil (Galilee), unmentioned in the HB, Josephus, or rabbinic literature before the Christian era.

## CROSS-CHAPTER TRACKING (HB -> Matthew)

**Micah 5:1 [5:2] -> Matt 2:6 — Beyt-Lechem:**

| Element | Micah 5:1 | Matt 2:6 |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| Location | "Beyt-Lechem Ephrathah, too small to be among the clans" | "Beyt-Lechem, land of Yehudah, by no means least among the rulers" |
| Ruler | "One who will be ruler in Yisra'el" | "A ruler who will shepherd my people Yisra'el" |
| Modification | — | Reverses "too small" to "by no means least"; adds "shepherd my people" from 2 Sam 5:2 |

Matthew's quotation is a composite: Micah 5:1 modified and blended with 2 Samuel 5:2. The reversal of "too small" to "by no means least" may reflect a different textual tradition or a deliberate interpretive adjustment.

**Hosea 11:1 -> Matt 2:15 — "Out of Mitsrayim I called my son":**

| Element | Hosea 11:1 | Matt 2:15 |
|---------|------------|-----------|
| Speaker | YHWH | "The Lord" (kyrios, Option C) |
| "My son" | Israel as YHWH's son (the nation) | Yeshua as YHWH's son (typological reading) |
| Context | Retrospective: recalling the Exodus | Prospective: the return from Mitsrayim |
| Hermeneutic | Historical statement about a past event | Typological fulfillment in a new event |

**Jeremiah 31:15 -> Matt 2:18 — Rachel weeping:**

| Element | Jeremiah 31:15 | Matt 2:18 |
|---------|----------------|-----------|
| Rachel | Weeps for descendants taken into exile | Weeps for children killed by Herodes |
| Location | Ramah (deportation staging point) | Beyt-Lechem vicinity |
| Context | Exile — followed by hope (31:16-17) | Slaughter — the hope verses are not quoted |

**Exodus 4:19 -> Matt 2:20 — "Those seeking the life... have died":**

| Element | Exodus 4:19 | Matt 2:20 |
|---------|-------------|-----------|
| Speaker | YHWH to Mosheh | Messenger of the Lord to Yosef |
| Direction | Return to Mitsrayim | Return from Mitsrayim to Yisra'el |
| Threat removed | "Those seeking your life" | "Those seeking the life of the child" |

The verbal echo is nearly exact. The reversal of direction (to Mitsrayim / from Mitsrayim) reinforces the Moses-Yeshua typology.
