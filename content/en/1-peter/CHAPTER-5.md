Notes are tagged by type — **Critical · Lexical · Grammatical · Theological** — each shown with its colour (see the legend at the top of the Notes view).

**The closing chapter: leadership, humility, vigilance, and the epistolary farewell.** Peter, as "fellow-elder," exhorts the elders to shepherd the flock willingly and as examples, not for gain or by domineering — for the chief Shepherd will give an unfading crown (vv.1-4); the younger submit, and all clothe themselves with humility, "for God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble" (vv.5-7); be vigilant against the adversary, resist firm in faith, and the God of all grace will restore you after a little suffering (vv.8-11). The letter closes: written "through Silvanus," with greetings from "she in Babylon" and "Mark my son," and a kiss of love and peace (vv.12-14). **Note on speech-marking (Rule 30):** no narrated divine speech; the Proverbs 3:34 citation (5:5) is the sage's voice — unmarked. Names: Silvanus (Silas), Markos (Mark), "Babylon."

## CHAPTER OVERVIEW

**What happens:** Peter exhorts the elders among the churches — writing as a "fellow-elder" and witness of the Messiah's sufferings and sharer in the glory to be revealed — to shepherd the flock of God among them: overseeing not by compulsion but willingly, as God wills; not for shameful gain but eagerly; not lording it over those in their charge but becoming examples to the flock; and when the chief Shepherd appears, they will receive the unfading crown of glory (vv.1-4). Likewise the younger are to submit to the elders, and all are to clothe themselves with humility toward one another, "because God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble" (v.5). So humble yourselves under God's mighty hand, that he may lift you up in due time, casting all your anxiety on him, because he cares for you (vv.6-7). Be sober, be watchful: your adversary the slanderer prowls like a roaring lion seeking someone to devour; resist him, firm in faith, knowing the same sufferings are being borne by the brotherhood throughout the world (vv.8-9). And the God of all grace, who called you into his eternal glory in the Messiah, after you have suffered a little, will himself restore, support, strengthen, and establish you; to him be the power forever (vv.10-11). Through Silvanus, the faithful brother, Peter has written briefly, exhorting and testifying that this is the true grace of God — stand firm in it (v.12). "She who is in Babylon, chosen together with you, greets you, and Mark my son" (v.13). Greet one another with a kiss of love; peace to all who are in the Messiah (v.14).

**Key themes:** The **pastoral charge** — Peter as "fellow-elder" (not "chief," despite his stature); shepherding willingly, not for gain, not domineering, but as examples (echoing the Messiah's *hypogrammos*, 2:21); the **chief Shepherd** (*archipoimēn*) and the "unfading crown" (tying to the unfading inheritance, 1:4); mutual **humility** grounded in Proverbs 3:34 ("God opposes the proud, gives grace to the humble"); **humbling under God's mighty hand** (Exodus language) with the promise of exaltation "in due time"; **casting anxiety on God** (Ps 55:22) because "he cares for you"; **vigilance** against the **adversary/slanderer** (the *diabolos*), the roaring lion; resistance "firm in faith," in solidarity with a suffering worldwide brotherhood; the **God of all grace** who restores after "a little" suffering — the letter's hope-through-suffering arc resolved; and the **epistolary close** — Silvanus the bearer, the greeting from "**Babylon**" (a likely cipher for Rome), "**Mark** my son," the kiss of love, and peace.

**Watch for:** **5:1** — Peter calls himself *sympresbyteros* ("**fellow**-elder"), a notable self-lowering. **5:5** quotes Proverbs 3:34 (LXX: "God opposes the proud, gives grace to the humble" — same citation as James 4:6; the sage's voice — unmarked). **5:7** echoes Psalm 55:22 ("cast your burden on the LORD"). **5:8** "the **adversary** (*antidikos*), the **slanderer** (*diabolos*), like a roaring lion" — *diabolos* (lit. "slanderer/accuser") is the term behind "devil"; the lion may echo Psalm 22:13. **5:12** "**through Silvanus** I wrote" — the bearer/amanuensis (the authorship-by-secretary question of the Introduction). **5:13** "**she in Babylon**" = the co-elect church, with "Babylon" widely read as a cipher for **Rome** (Tier-2); "**Mark** my son" links to the gospel of Mark (cross-book).

**Connection:** "Shepherd the flock of God" (5:2) draws on Ezekiel 34 (the LORD against false shepherds, promising a true one) and the Messiah as shepherd (2:25; Jn 10). The "chief Shepherd" + "crown" (5:4) echo the shepherd-king imagery (Ps 23; the messianic shepherd). "God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble" (5:5) quotes Proverbs 3:34 (LXX). "Humble yourselves under the mighty hand of God" (5:6) uses the Exodus "mighty hand" (Ex 3:19; Deut 9:26). "Cast all your anxiety on him" (5:7) echoes Psalm 55:22. The roaring lion (5:8) recalls Psalm 22:13 and the predator-imagery of the Psalms.

## VERSE-BY-VERSE STUDY

### **Verse 1**

*The* elders therefore among you I exhort — the fellow-elder and witness of the sufferings of the Messiah, the *one* also *who is* a sharer of the glory about to be revealed:

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "THE FELLOW-ELDER" — *SYMPRESBYTEROS***
> - **ὁ συμπρεσβύτερος καὶ μάρτυς τῶν τοῦ Χριστοῦ παθημάτων** = "the **fellow-elder** and **witness** of the sufferings of the Messiah." Peter addresses the elders not as a superior but as **σύμπρεσβύτερος** (*sympresbyteros*, "co-elder") — a striking self-lowering (cf. the humility theme, v.5). **μάρτυς** ("witness") of the sufferings — and "sharer (*koinōnos*) of the glory about to be revealed" (the sufferings-then-glory pattern, 1:11; 4:13).

---

### **Verse 2**

shepherd the flock of God among you, overseeing not by compulsion but willingly, according to God; nor for shameful gain but eagerly;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "SHEPHERD THE FLOCK OF GOD" (Ezekiel 34)**
> - **ποιμάνατε τὸ… ποίμνιον τοῦ θεοῦ ἐπισκοποῦντες** = "**shepherd** the **flock** of God, **overseeing**." The pastoral verb (*poimainō*) + "overseeing" (*episkopountes*, the *episkopos*-root, cf. 2:25) define eldership as shepherding, echoing Ezekiel 34 (the LORD against self-serving shepherds). The first contrast: "not by compulsion (*anankastōs*) but willingly… nor for **shameful gain** (*aischrokerdōs*) but eagerly." → PROPHECY (Ezek 34 → 1 Pet 5:2).

---

### **Verse 3**

nor as lording *it* over those allotted *to you*, but becoming examples of the flock.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "NOT LORDING IT OVER… BUT EXAMPLES" (*TYPOI*)**
> - **μηδ' ὡς κατακυριεύοντες τῶν κλήρων ἀλλὰ τύποι γινόμενοι** = "nor as **lording it over** (*katakyrieuontes*) those allotted *to you*, but becoming **examples** (*typoi*)." Directly echoes Jesus's teaching against domineering leadership (Mk 10:42-45). **κλῆροι** ("those allotted / portions") = the congregations in the elders' charge. Leadership by *typos* (model), like the Messiah's *hypogrammos* (2:21).

---

### **Verse 4**

And when the chief Shepherd is revealed, you will receive the unfading crown of glory.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"THE CHIEF SHEPHERD" + "UNFADING CROWN" — *ARCHIPOIMĒN / AMARANTINON***
> - **τοῦ ἀρχιποίμενος… τὸν ἀμαράντινον τῆς δόξης στέφανον** = "the **chief Shepherd**… the **unfading** crown of glory." *Archipoimēn* (only here in the GS) — the Messiah over the under-shepherds (the "shepherd and overseer," 2:25). *Amarantinon* ("unfading," cf. the inheritance "unfading," *amaranton*, 1:4) — the reward outlasts the perishable. The crown (*stephanos*) is the victor's wreath, not a royal diadem.

---

### **Verse 5**

Likewise, younger *ones*, submit to *the* elders; and all *of you*, clothe yourselves with humility toward one another, because "God opposes *the* proud, but to *the* humble he gives grace."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — PROVERBS 3:34: "GOD OPPOSES THE PROUD"**
> - **ὁ θεὸς ὑπερηφάνοις ἀντιτάσσεται, ταπεινοῖς δὲ δίδωσιν χάριν** = "**God opposes *the* proud, but to *the* humble he gives grace**" — Proverbs 3:34 (LXX, which reads "God/Lord" where the Hebrew has "the LORD"; the same citation in James 4:6). The sage's voice (not God's first-person — unmarked). **ἐγκομβώσασθε** ("clothe/tie-on," as an apron-knot) — gird on humility as a garment, perhaps recalling Jesus girding to wash feet (Jn 13). → PROPHECY (Prov 3:34 → 1 Pet 5:5).

---

### **Verse 6**

Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God, so that he may lift you up in *due* time,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "UNDER THE MIGHTY HAND OF GOD" (Exodus)**
> - **ταπεινώθητε… ὑπὸ τὴν κραταιὰν χεῖρα τοῦ θεοῦ** = "Humble yourselves… under the **mighty hand** of God." The "mighty hand" is the Exodus deliverance idiom (Ex 3:19; Deut 9:26 — God's strong hand that both judges and saves). The pattern of v.5 (grace to the humble) becomes personal: humble now, "**lifted up** (*hypsōsē*) in *due* time" — the suffering-then-glory shape again.

---

### **Verse 7**

having cast all your anxiety upon him, because it matters to him concerning you.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — PSALM 55:22: "CAST YOUR ANXIETY ON HIM"**
> - **πᾶσαν τὴν μέριμναν ὑμῶν ἐπιρίψαντες ἐπ' αὐτόν** = "having **cast all your anxiety** upon him" — echoing Psalm 55:22 ("Cast your burden on the LORD"). Grammatically a participle dependent on "humble yourselves" (v.6): humbling *includes* handing one's cares to God. **"because it matters to him concerning you" (αὐτῷ μέλει περὶ ὑμῶν)** — God's care is the ground. → PROPHECY (Ps 55:22 → 1 Pet 5:7).

---

### **Verse 8**

Be sober, be watchful. Your adversary, *the* slanderer, walks about like a roaring lion, seeking *someone* to devour;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "THE ADVERSARY, THE SLANDERER… A ROARING LION"**
> - **ὁ ἀντίδικος ὑμῶν διάβολος ὡς λέων ὠρυόμενος** = "Your **adversary** (*antidikos*, a legal opponent), *the* **slanderer** (*diabolos*), like a **roaring lion**." *Diabolos* (lit. "accuser/slanderer") is the term behind "devil"; the TT renders the meaning "slanderer" (with the familiar association noted). The roaring lion seeking to "devour" (*katapiein*) echoes Psalm 22:13. "Be sober, be watchful" (*nēpsate, grēgorēsate*) — the vigilance the imminent end requires (4:7).

---

### **Verse 9**

whom resist, firm in the faith, knowing that the same *kinds* of sufferings are being accomplished for your brotherhood in the world.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"RESIST… FIRM IN THE FAITH" + WORLDWIDE SOLIDARITY**
> - **ᾧ ἀντίστητε στερεοὶ τῇ πίστει** = "whom resist, **firm** (*stereoi*) in the faith." Resistance is steadfastness, not combat. **τῇ… ἀδελφότητι** ("for your **brotherhood**," cf. 2:17) "in the world" — the readers' suffering is shared by believers everywhere; they are not alone. **ἐπιτελεῖσθαι** ("being accomplished/completed") frames suffering as a measured, bounded thing.

---

### **Verse 10**

And the God of all grace, the *One* who called you into his eternal glory in *the* Messiah, *after you have* suffered a little, will himself restore, support, strengthen, *and* establish *you*.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "THE GOD OF ALL GRACE… WILL RESTORE" (the arc resolved)**
> - **ὁ… θεὸς πάσης χάριτος… ὀλίγον παθόντας αὐτὸς καταρτίσει, στηρίξει, σθενώσει, θεμελιώσει** = "the **God of all grace**… *after you have* **suffered a little**, will himself **restore, support, strengthen, *and* establish** *you*." Four future verbs pile up the promise (*katartisei / stērixei / sthenōsei / themeliōsei*). "Suffered a little" (cf. 1:6) sets present trial against "**eternal glory**" — the letter's hope-through-suffering arc resolved. **αὐτός** ("**himself**") emphasizes God as the agent.

---

### **Verse 11**

To him *be* the power into the ages. Amen.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **THE CLOSING DOXOLOGY**
> - **αὐτῷ τὸ κράτος εἰς τοὺς αἰῶνας· ἀμήν** = "To him *be* the **power** (*kratos*) into the ages. Amen." A briefer doxology than 4:11 (here "the power" alone), sealing the body of the letter before the epistolary close.

---

### **Verse 12**

Through Silvanus (Silas), the faithful brother — as I consider *him* — I wrote to you briefly, exhorting and testifying *that* this is *the* true grace of God; in which stand.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "THROUGH SILVANUS… I WROTE" (the bearer/amanuensis)**
> - **Διὰ Σιλουανοῦ… ἔγραψα** (*Dia Silouanou… egrapsa*) = "**Through Silvanus**… I wrote." *Dia* + genitive may mark Silvanus (Silas) as the **letter-carrier** or the **secretary (amanuensis)** — bearing on the authorship/Greek-style question (see Introduction §B). "**Briefly**" (*di' oligōn*) is the conventional letter-closing modesty. The letter's summary purpose: "**testifying that this is the true grace of God; in which stand** (*stēte*)." → Companion §A; Introduction §B.

---

### **Verse 13**

She *who is* in Babylon (Bavel), chosen together *with you*, greets you, and Markos (Mark) my son.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "SHE IN BABYLON" + "MARK MY SON"**
> - **ἡ ἐν Βαβυλῶνι συνεκλεκτὴ καὶ Μᾶρκος ὁ υἱός μου** = "**She** *who is* in **Babylon**, **chosen together** *with you*, *greets you*, and **Markos** (Mark) **my son**." "She… chosen-together" (*syneklektē*) = the **church** in Babylon (feminine; cf. 2 Jn 1, 13), sending greetings. "**Babylon**" is widely read as a **cipher for Rome** (the exile-empire, as in Revelation; Rome as the place of Peter's later ministry) — the TT keeps "Babylon" and notes it (Tier-2). "**Mark** my son" — John Mark (Acts 12:12; 2 Tim 4:11), linking to the gospel of Mark (cross-book). → Companion §B (Babylon = Rome); People (Markos → mark; Silvanus).

---

### **Verse 14**

Greet one another with a kiss of love. Peace to all of you who *are* in *the* Messiah.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **THE EPISTOLARY FAREWELL — "KISS OF LOVE… PEACE"**
> - **ἀσπάσασθε ἀλλήλους ἐν φιλήματι ἀγάπης. εἰρήνη ὑμῖν πᾶσιν** = "Greet one another with a **kiss of love**. **Peace** to all of you." The "holy kiss" greeting (cf. Rom 16:16; here "kiss of **love**," *agapē*) + the peace-benediction (*eirēnē*, Hebrew *shalom*) close the letter, inclusio with the opening "grace and peace" (1:2). "In *the* Messiah" frames the whole community.

## Glossary — 1 Peter 5

| Term | Greek | Meaning in context |
|------|-------|--------------------|
| **fellow-elder** | συμπρεσβύτερος (*sympresbyteros*) | Peter's self-designation among the elders (5:1) — a self-lowering. |

| **adversary / slanderer** | ἀντίδικος / διάβολος | The legal opponent / "slanderer" (the devil), a roaring lion (5:8). |
