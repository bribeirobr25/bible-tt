**How the text is marked:**

- @@"…"@@ — direct speech of God or the holy wind/spirit

Notes are tagged by type — **Critical · Lexical · Grammatical · Theological** — each shown with its colour (see the legend at the top of the Notes view).

**Chapter 4 turns to living in the light of the end and to suffering for the name.** Arm yourselves with the Messiah's mindset; you have done with the old life of the nations, who will give account; for this the good news was even preached "to the dead" (vv.1-6) — the chapter's crux. The end of all things is near, so be sober, love earnestly (love covers a multitude of sins), be hospitable, and serve with your gifts to God's glory (vv.7-11). Do not be surprised at the fiery trial; rejoice as you share the Messiah's sufferings; if reviled for the name, you are blessed — suffer as a Christian, not as a wrongdoer; for judgment begins at God's house, and the righteous are scarcely saved (vv.12-19). **Note on speech-marking (Rule 30):** no narrated divine speech; the Proverbs 11:31 citation (4:18) is the sage's voice — unmarked. **High-restraint verse:** 4:6 ("the gospel was preached even to the dead") is rendered literally and its readings flagged, resolving none (Rule 3; editorial-log P-010).

## CHAPTER OVERVIEW

**What happens:** Since the Messiah suffered in the flesh, the readers are to arm themselves with the same resolve — whoever has suffered in the flesh is done with sin — to live the rest of their time not by human passions but by God's will (vv.1-2). Enough time was already spent in the way of the nations — debauchery, drunkenness, lawless idolatry — at which the outsiders are surprised the believers no longer join, and they malign them; but they will give account to the One ready to judge the living and the dead (vv.3-5). For this reason the good news was preached even to the dead, so that, though judged in the flesh as humans, they might live to God in the spirit (v.6). The end of all things has drawn near: be sober and clear-headed for prayers; above all keep your love for one another fervent, for love covers a multitude of sins; be hospitable without grumbling; as each received a gift, serve one another as good stewards of God's varied grace — whoever speaks, as oracles of God; whoever serves, by the strength God supplies — so that in all things God may be glorified through Jesus the Messiah, to whom belong the glory and the power (vv.7-11). Beloved, do not be surprised at the fiery ordeal among you, as though something strange were happening, but rejoice insofar as you share the sufferings of the Messiah, so that you may also rejoice exulting at the revelation of his glory (vv.12-13). If you are reviled for the name of the Messiah, you are blessed, because the spirit of glory and of God rests on you (v.14). Let none suffer as a murderer, thief, evildoer, or meddler; but if as a "Christian," let him not be ashamed but glorify God in this name (vv.15-16). For it is time for the judgment to begin at the house of God; and if first at us, what is the end of those who disobey the gospel? And "if the righteous is scarcely saved, where will the ungodly and sinner appear?" (vv.17-18). So let those suffering by God's will entrust their souls to a faithful Creator while doing good (v.19).

**Key themes:** Arming with the Messiah's suffering-mindset; the break with the "way of the nations" and the outsiders' bafflement and slander; God as judge of living and dead; the crux of **4:6** (the good news preached "to the dead," with its link to 3:19); the imminent **end** as motive for sober prayer and fervent love; "**love covers a multitude of sins**" (Prov 10:12); hospitality and gift-stewardship ("whoever speaks, as oracles of God"); the **fiery trial** as participation in the Messiah's sufferings, not something strange; suffering "**for the name**" / "as a **Christian**" as honor not shame (one of three NT uses of *Christianos*); "the spirit of glory and of God" resting on the persecuted; **judgment beginning at the house of God** (Ezek 9; Mal 3); the righteous "scarcely saved" (Prov 11:31); entrusting the soul to "a faithful **Creator**."

**Watch for:** **4:6** "the gospel was preached even to the dead" — the chapter's crux (linked to 3:19); rendered literally, readings flagged, resolved none (Rule 3; P-010). The flesh/spirit antithesis returns (4:6 "judged in flesh… live in {a:wind/spirit}") — same crux as 3:18. **4:14** "the spirit of glory and of God rests on you" echoes Isaiah 11:2 — the {a:wind/spirit}. **4:16** *Christianos* ("Christian") — a rare term, likely an outsiders' label (cf. Acts 11:26; 26:28) the letter reclaims. 4:8 quotes Proverbs 10:12 ("love covers… sins"); 4:18 quotes Proverbs 11:31 LXX (the sage's voice — unmarked). "Judgment begins at the house of God" (4:17) draws on Ezekiel 9:6 / Malachi 3:1-5.

**Connection:** "Love covers a multitude of sins" (4:8) quotes Proverbs 10:12. "The spirit of glory and of God rests on you" (4:14) echoes Isaiah 11:2 (the spirit of the LORD resting on the messianic shoot). "Judgment begins at the house of God" (4:17) draws on Ezekiel 9:6 (judgment starting at the sanctuary) and Malachi 3:1-5 (the Lord coming to his temple to refine). "If the righteous is scarcely saved, where will the ungodly… appear?" (4:18) quotes Proverbs 11:31 (LXX). "A faithful Creator" (4:19) reaches back to Genesis 1 and the Creator-faithfulness of Isaiah 40-45.

## VERSE-BY-VERSE STUDY

### **Verse 1**

Therefore, *the* Messiah having suffered in *the* flesh, you also arm yourselves with the same resolve — because the *one* having suffered in *the* flesh has ceased from sin —

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"ARM YOURSELVES" — *HOPLISASTHE***
> - **τὴν αὐτὴν ἔννοιαν ὁπλίσασθε** (*tēn autēn ennoian hoplisasthe*) = "arm yourselves with the same resolve/intent" (a military image, *hoplon* = weapon). The Messiah's flesh-suffering (3:18) is the readers' weapon. **"The *one* having suffered in *the* flesh has ceased from sin"** is itself debated (the believer who suffers / the Messiah / a baptismal break with sin) — the TT renders the maxim as the Greek gives it.

---

### **Verse 2**

so as to live the rest of *the* time in *the* flesh no longer by human desires but by *the* will of God.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"THE REST OF THE TIME… BY THE WILL OF GOD"**
> - **τὸν ἐπίλοιπον… χρόνον… θελήματι θεοῦ** (*ton epiloipon chronon… thelēmati theou*) = "the rest of *the* time… by *the* will of God." The two governing measures of life — "human desires (*epithymiai*)" vs. "the will of God" — restate 1:14; the readers' remaining time is reframed.

---

### **Verse 3**

For the time *that has* passed *is* sufficient *for* having worked out the will of the nations, having walked in debaucheries, desires, drunkenness, carousing, drinking-bouts, and lawless idolatries.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **THE VICE-CATALOG OF "THE NATIONS"**
> - **τὸ βούλημα τῶν ἐθνῶν** (*to boulēma tōn ethnōn*) = "the will of the nations." The six-fold vice-list (debauchery, lust, drunkenness, revels, carousing, "lawless idolatries," *athemitois eidōlolatriais*) marks the pre-conversion past; "sufficient" (*arketos*) is dry understatement — enough of that.

---

### **Verse 4**

In *this* they are surprised that you do not run with *them* into the same flood of dissipation, slandering *you*;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"THE FLOOD OF DISSIPATION" — *ANACHYSIN ASŌTIAS***
> - **τῆς ἀσωτίας ἀνάχυσιν** (*tēs asōtias anachysin*) = "the flood/outpouring of dissipation" (*asōtia*, the "un-savedness/wastefulness" of the prodigal, Lk 15:13). The believers' withdrawal provokes the outsiders' bafflement and slander (**βλασφημοῦντες**) — the social friction behind the letter.

---

### **Verse 5**

who will give account to the *One* ready to judge *the* living and *the* dead.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"TO JUDGE THE LIVING AND THE DEAD"**
> - **τῷ ἑτοίμως ἔχοντι κρῖναι ζῶντας καὶ νεκρούς** = "to the *One* ready to judge *the* living and *the* dead." The slanderers face God's account; "living and dead" sets up v.6 (the dead who were evangelized) — a creedal-sounding phrase (cf. Acts 10:42).

---

### **Verse 6**

For to this *end* the good news was preached even to *the* dead, so that they might be judged according to humans in *the* flesh but live according to God in *the* {a:wind/spirit}.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "THE GOOD NEWS PREACHED EVEN TO THE DEAD" (the crux, P-010)**
> - **καὶ νεκροῖς εὐηγγελίσθη** (*kai nekrois euēngelisthē*) = "the good news was preached even to *the* dead." A crux, linked to 3:19. Major readings: (a) the **now-dead Christians** who heard the gospel while alive (and were "judged… in flesh" by dying); (b) those who heard the proclamation of 3:19 (the dead of Noah's day); (c) a general descent-proclamation. The flesh/spirit antithesis returns — "judged according to humans **in *the* flesh** but live according to God **in *the* {a:wind/spirit}**" (cf. 3:18). The TT renders literally and **resolves none** (Rule 3 / Rule 13). → editorial-log P-010; Companion §A; cross-ref 3:19 (P-006).

---

### **Verse 7**

And the end of all *things* has drawn near. Be sober therefore, and clear-headed for prayers;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "THE END OF ALL THINGS HAS DRAWN NEAR"**
> - **πάντων δὲ τὸ τέλος ἤγγικεν** (*pantōn de to telos ēngiken*) = "And the end of all *things* has drawn near" (*ēngiken*, perfect — "has come near and is near"; cf. Mk 1:15). The eschatological horizon is the motive for the ethics that follow: sobriety, clear-headedness "for prayers" (**νήψατε εἰς προσευχάς**). Restates 1:13; 4:17.

---

### **Verse 8**

above all *things*, having earnest love among yourselves, because love covers a multitude of sins;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — PROVERBS 10:12: "LOVE COVERS A MULTITUDE OF SINS"**
> - **ἀγάπη καλύπτει πλῆθος ἁμαρτιῶν** (*agapē kalyptei plēthos hamartiōn*) = "love covers a multitude of sins" — Proverbs 10:12 ("love covers all transgressions," not the LXX wording but the Hebrew sense). "**Above all things**" (*pro pantōn*) + "**earnest/stretched-out** (*ektenē*) love" (cf. 1:22) — love as the community's first priority before the end. → PROPHECY (Prov 10:12 → 1 Pet 4:8).

---

### **Verse 9**

*be* hospitable to one another without grumbling;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"HOSPITABLE WITHOUT GRUMBLING" — *PHILOXENOI***
> - **φιλόξενοι… ἄνευ γογγυσμοῦ** (*philoxenoi… aneu gongysmou*) = "hospitable (lit. love-of-strangers) without grumbling." In a house-church movement of traveling believers, hospitality is concrete love; "without grumbling" guards its sincerity.

---

### **Verse 10**

each, as *he* received a gift, serving it among yourselves as good stewards of *the* varied grace of God;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"STEWARDS OF THE VARIED GRACE" — *POIKILĒS CHARITOS***
> - **καλοὶ οἰκονόμοι ποικίλης χάριτος θεοῦ** = "good stewards of *the* varied/many-colored grace of God." Each **χάρισμα** ("gift," grace-gift) is for serving others (cf. Rom 12; 1 Cor 12); *poikilēs* ("varied") echoes the "varied trials" of 1:6 — grace as many-colored as the trials.

---

### **Verse 11**

if anyone speaks, *let it be* as oracles of God; if anyone serves, as out of *the* strength which God supplies, so that in all *things* God may be glorified through Yeshua *the* Messiah (Jesus), to whom belong the glory and the power into the ages of the ages. Amen.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"AS ORACLES OF GOD" + THE DOXOLOGY**
> - **ὡς λόγια θεοῦ** (*hōs logia theou*) = "as oracles/utterances of God" — speaking gifts exercised as God's own words. The two gift-types (speaking / serving) both aim "**so that in all things God may be glorified**." The doxology (glory + power "into the ages of the ages, Amen") may close the letter's first major movement.

---

### **Verse 12**

Beloved, do not be surprised at the burning *trial* among you, occurring to you for a testing, as *though* a strange *thing* were happening to you,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "THE BURNING TRIAL" — *PYRŌSIS***
> - **τῇ ἐν ὑμῖν πυρώσει πρὸς πειρασμὸν** (*tē en hymin pyrōsei pros peirasmon*) = "the burning/fiery *trial* among you, for a testing." *Pyrōsis* ("burning, conflagration") recalls the gold-refined-by-fire of 1:7 — suffering as assay, not anomaly. "Do not be surprised… as *though* a strange *thing*" reframes persecution as expected, even normal, for those united to the suffering Messiah (v.13).

---

### **Verse 13**

but insofar as you share in the sufferings of the Messiah, rejoice, so that also at the revelation of his glory you may rejoice, exulting.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"SHARE IN THE SUFFERINGS… REJOICE"**
> - **καθὸ κοινωνεῖτε τοῖς τοῦ Χριστοῦ παθήμασιν χαίρετε** = "insofar as you **share** (*koinōneō*) in the sufferings of the Messiah, rejoice." The sufferings-then-glory pattern (1:11) becomes participatory: present sharing in his sufferings → future rejoicing "at the revelation of his glory" (1:7).

---

### **Verse 14**

If you are reviled in *the* name of *the* Messiah, *you are* blessed, because the {a:wind/spirit} of glory and of God rests upon you.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "THE SPIRIT OF GLORY… RESTS ON YOU" (Isaiah 11:2)**
> - **τὸ τῆς δόξης καὶ τὸ τοῦ θεοῦ πνεῦμα… ἀναπαύεται** = "the {a:wind/spirit} of glory and of God **rests** (*anapauetai*) upon you" — echoing Isaiah 11:2 (the spirit of the LORD resting on the messianic shoot), now resting on the reviled. The beatitude form ("**blessed**," *makarioi*, cf. Mt 5:11) reframes the reviling-for-the-name as honor. The {a:wind/spirit} slash kept. → PROPHECY (Isa 11:2 → 1 Pet 4:14).

---

### **Verse 15**

For let none of you suffer as a murderer or thief or evildoer or as a meddler *in others' affairs*;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"MEDDLER" — *ALLOTRIEPISKOPOS***
> - **ἀλλοτριεπίσκοπος** (*allotriepiskopos*) = "meddler / overseer-of-others'-affairs" — a rare word (perhaps coined here), the odd one in a list of crimes (murderer, thief, evildoer). Suffering for wrongdoing earns no honor (cf. 2:20); only suffering "for the name" (v.14, 16) is blessed.

---

### **Verse 16**

but if as a Christian, let him not be ashamed, but let him glorify God in this name.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "AS A CHRISTIAN" — *CHRISTIANOS***
> - **εἰ δὲ ὡς Χριστιανός** (*ei de hōs Christianos*) = "but if as a **Christian**." One of only three NT uses of *Christianos* (Acts 11:26; 26:28) — likely an outsiders' label ("partisan of Christus") here **reclaimed** as a name to glorify God in, not be ashamed of. The TT keeps "Christian" (the established English form of the term). → Companion §D (the term's origin).

---

### **Verse 17**

Because *it is* the time for the judgment to begin from the house of God; and if first from us, what *is* the end of those disobeying the good news of God?

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "JUDGMENT BEGINS AT THE HOUSE OF GOD" (Ezek 9; Mal 3)**
> - **ἄρξασθαι τὸ κρίμα ἀπὸ τοῦ οἴκου τοῦ θεοῦ** = "the judgment to begin from the **house of God**." Draws on Ezekiel 9:6 (judgment beginning at the sanctuary) and Malachi 3:1-5 (the Lord coming to refine his temple). The believers' present suffering *is* this purifying judgment beginning with God's household; the unbelieving face worse. **"House of God"** ties back to the "spiritual house" of 2:5. → PROPHECY (Ezek 9:6 / Mal 3 → 1 Pet 4:17).

---

### **Verse 18**

And if the righteous *is* scarcely saved, where will the ungodly and *the* sinner appear?

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **PROVERBS 11:31 (LXX) — "THE RIGHTEOUS SCARCELY SAVED"**
> - **εἰ ὁ δίκαιος μόλις σῴζεται** (*ei ho dikaios molis sōzetai*) = "if the righteous *is* scarcely saved" — Proverbs 11:31 LXX (which differs from the Hebrew: the LXX reads "scarcely saved"). The sage's voice (not God's first-person — unmarked). The lesser-to-greater argument (if even the righteous barely, then the ungodly?) reinforces v.17. → PROPHECY (Prov 11:31 → 1 Pet 4:18).

---

### **Verse 19**

So then, *let* also those suffering according to *the* will of God entrust their souls to a faithful Creator in doing good.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "ENTRUST THEIR SOULS TO A FAITHFUL CREATOR"**
> - **πιστῷ κτίστῃ παρατιθέσθωσαν τὰς ψυχὰς αὐτῶν** = "entrust their souls to a **faithful Creator**." **κτίστης** (*ktistēs*, "Creator," only here in the GS) grounds endurance in God's creative faithfulness (cf. Isa 40-45; the "faithful Creator" who keeps his world). **παρατίθημι** ("entrust/deposit") echoes the Messiah's "into your hands I entrust my spirit" (Lk 23:46). Suffering "according to God's will" + "doing good" (the keyword *agathopoiia*) — the chapter's resolution.
