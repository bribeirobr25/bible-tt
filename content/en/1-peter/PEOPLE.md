This file presents biographical information about figures appearing in 1 Peter. All claims are labeled by type and confidence. This material is contextual study content governed by Rule 29.

---

**Note:** 1 Peter is an **epistle**, not a narrative — so this is a **thin PEOPLE file**: it covers only the people named in the letter's frame (the author, the named co-senders/bearer, and the addressee communities), not a cast of narrative characters. **There is no genealogy table** — an epistle has no genealogy to render (contrast the gospels and Acts; e.g. Luke 3). Figures whose canonical biography already lives in another book are cross-referenced (`**See:** {book}/PEOPLE.md`) rather than duplicated, with a 1-Peter-specific role note: the author **Kefa (Peter)** carries his canonical entry in `mark/PEOPLE.md` (whose Twelve-roster is the fullest in the corpus). Figures whose canonical home is 1 Peter — **Silvanus (Silas)** the bearer (5:12) and **Markos (Mark)** "my son" (5:13) — carry full (brief) entries here, since `mark/PEOPLE.md` has no John-Mark person entry. The addressee communities ("the elect exiles of the dispersion," 1:1) and "she in Babylon" (5:13) are described as collectives, not persons. Transliterations follow the locked proper-name table (RULES-GS.md / RULES-HB.md); see `docs/editorial-log/1-peter.md` for the name and Rule-30 decisions.

## Silvanus (Silas)
**Meaning:** Greek Σιλουανός (*Silouanos*), a Latinized form (the Latin cognomen *Silvanus*, "of the woods / forest"); the shorter Greek Σίλας (*Silas*, Acts) is the same man, an Aramaic-derived hypocoristic (possibly from שְׁאִילָא, "asked-for")
**Origin:** A leading member of the Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) congregation; a Roman citizen (Acts 16:37) and a "leading man among the brothers" (Acts 15:22)
**Familiar name:** Silvanus (also Silas)
**Profession:** Prophet and teacher in the early community (Acts 15:32); a traveling co-worker; here, the letter's bearer and/or secretary (5:12)
**Hometown:** not stated; based in Yerushalayim (Jerusalem)
**First mention:** 1 Peter 5:12 (in this letter)
**Mentioned in:** 1 Peter 5:12 (as Silvanus); also, on the Silas identification, Acts 15:22, 27, 32, 40; 16:19-40; 17:4-15; 18:5; and the Pauline letters (2 Cor 1:19; 1 Thess 1:1; 2 Thess 1:1, all "Silvanus")
**Key events in 1 Peter:** Named only in the closing: "**Through Silvanus**, the faithful brother — as I consider *him* — I wrote to you briefly" (5:12). The Greek *dia* + genitive (διὰ Σιλουανοῦ) marks him as the letter's **bearer** (the one who carried and delivered it on the Asia-Minor circuit) and/or as its **secretary/amanuensis** — a question that bears on the letter's polished Greek style (see Introduction §B). Peter's commendation "the faithful brother" vouches for him to the recipient churches.
**Spouse(s):** not stated
**Children:** not stated
**Archaeological evidence:** No direct archaeological evidence for the individual.
**Extra-biblical mentions:** Not securely attested outside the NT. Later tradition (e.g. lists of the Seventy) names a Silas/Silvanus but is unreliable. The identification of the "Silvanus" of 5:12 with the "Silas" of Acts and the "Silvanus" of Paul's letters is widely held but not certain (the name was common).
**Historicity status:** PROBABLE (the Silas of Acts and Paul's "Silvanus" are well attested; the identification with 1 Peter's Silvanus is probable but not certain)
**Books appearing in:** 1 Peter; (on the identification) Acts; 1–2 Thessalonians; 2 Corinthians
**Character arc:** In this letter he is a frame figure only — the trusted brother through whom the letter reaches its readers, whose reliability Peter underwrites. If he is the Silas of Acts, his wider arc (the Jerusalem-council delegate, Paul's companion through the second journey, imprisoned and singing at Philippi) lies outside 1 Peter and is not developed here.

**Note:** "Silvanus" (the Latinate form Peter uses) and "Silas" (Luke's shorter Greek form in Acts) are the same name for the same man — the TT glosses "(Silas)" once to make the identification visible, then uses "Silvanus" with the letter. Whether *dia* + genitive here means "carried by" (courier) or "written down by" (secretary) is debated and is one input to the authorship question; the TT renders the Greek and notes the options rather than deciding (Rule 13).

## Markos (Mark)
**Meaning:** Greek Μᾶρκος (*Markos*), from the Latin praenomen *Marcus* (associated with *Mars*); traditionally identified with the "John whose other name was Mark" (Yochanan Markos / John Mark), whose Hebrew given name is Yochanan (יוֹחָנָן, "YHWH is gracious")
**Origin:** Traditionally a Jerusalemite — son of a Miryam (Mary) at whose house the early community gathered (Acts 12:12); cousin of Bar-Naba (Barnabas) (Col 4:10)
**Familiar name:** Mark (also John Mark)
**Profession:** A traveling assistant to the early missionaries; in Christian tradition, the author of the Gospel of Mark
**Hometown:** Yerushalayim (Jerusalem), by tradition
**First mention:** 1 Peter 5:13 (in this letter)
**Mentioned in:** 1 Peter 5:13 (as Markos "my son"); on the John-Mark identification, Acts 12:12, 25; 13:5, 13; 15:37-39; and the Pauline letters (Col 4:10; Philem 24; 2 Tim 4:11)
**Key events in 1 Peter:** Named only in the closing greeting: "She *who is* in Babylon, chosen together *with you*, greets you, and **Markos** (Mark) **my son**" (5:13). "My son" (ὁ υἱός μου) is almost certainly a relationship of spiritual fatherhood/discipleship, not biological — Mark is with Peter at the place the letter is sent from ("Babylon," likely Rome). This association is the traditional ground for linking the Gospel of Mark to Petrine testimony (cf. Papias, in Eusebius, *Hist. eccl.* 3.39.15).
**Spouse(s):** not stated
**Children:** not stated
**Archaeological evidence:** No direct archaeological evidence for the individual.
**Extra-biblical mentions:** Papias (early 2nd c., via Eusebius, *Hist. eccl.* 3.39.15) calls Mark "the interpreter of Peter," who recorded Peter's preaching — the tradition behind the gospel's attribution. Later traditions (Mark as founder of the Alexandrian church) are less securely attested.
**Historicity status:** PROBABLE (John Mark is well attested in Acts and the Pauline corpus; the identification of 5:13's Markos with that John Mark, and with the gospel's traditional author, is the standard reading but is a tradition-based inference)
**Books appearing in:** 1 Peter; (on the identification) Acts; Colossians; Philemon; 2 Timothy; (by attribution) the Gospel of Mark
**Character arc:** In this letter, a frame figure only — present with Peter and sending greetings, his closeness signaled by "my son." His wider arc (the assistant who turned back at Perga, the cause of the Paul–Barnabas split, later "useful for ministry," 2 Tim 4:11) lies outside 1 Peter and is not developed here.

**Note:** `mark/PEOPLE.md` carries no John-Mark person entry (its entries are Yeshua, the Twelve, and their immediate circle — the gospel's *narrative* cast, not its traditional author), so Markos receives a brief full entry here rather than a cross-book stub. "My son" is read as discipleship language; the TT does not assert a biological relationship. The Mark↔Peter association is the patristic basis for the Gospel of Mark as Petrine testimony — noted, not pressed (Rule 3).

## The elect exiles of the dispersion (the recipient communities)
**Meaning:** Greek ἐκλεκτοὶ παρεπίδημοι διασπορᾶς (*eklektoi parepidēmoi diasporas*) = "elect sojourners/exiles of the dispersion" (1:1; cf. 1:17 *paroikia*, "time of your sojourning"; 2:11 "resident-aliens and sojourners")
**Origin:** Christian congregations scattered across five Roman provinces of Asia Minor — **Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia** (1:1); the order suggests the letter-carrier's circuit. The communities are largely (though not only) gentile in background (cf. 1:14, 18; 4:3), addressed in Israel's exile-identity terms.
**Familiar name:** the recipient churches (the "elect exiles")
**First mention:** 1 Peter 1:1
**Mentioned in:** addressed throughout the letter (1:1, 17; 2:11; and the second-person plural address everywhere)
**Key events in 1 Peter:** The addressees of the whole letter. They are "chosen according to the foreknowledge of God the Father, by the sanctifying work of the {a:wind/spirit}, for obedience and the sprinkling of the blood of Yeshua" (1:2); given a new birth into a living hope and an imperishable inheritance (1:3-5); grieved now by "various trials" (1:6) and social hostility (2:12; 3:16; 4:4, 12-16) — the letter's pastoral occasion. They are exhorted to holiness, to honorable conduct among the nations, to submission within the household and the empire, and to steadfastness under suffering, in solidarity with "the brotherhood in the world" (5:9).
**Historicity status:** PROBABLE (the existence of Christian communities in these provinces in the mid-to-late 1st c. is historically secure; their precise composition is reconstructed from the letter)
**Books appearing in:** 1 Peter (the named addressees); the five provinces overlap the Pentecost pilgrim list of Acts 2:9-10
**Note (collective entry):** Not a person — the addressee community, included here because the letter's "people" are chiefly its readers. "Dispersion" (*diaspora*) and "sojourners/exiles" (*parepidēmoi / paroikia*) cast these mostly-gentile believers in **Israel's exile identity** — the letter's controlling self-description (kept literal; Tier-2). For the recipient communities and the epistolary form, see the Introduction (§B Recipient Community, §D Epistolary Conventions).

## "She in Babylon" (the co-elect sending church)
**Meaning:** Greek ἡ ἐν Βαβυλῶνι συνεκλεκτή (*hē en Babylōni syneklektē*) = "she *who is* in Babylon, chosen-together" (5:13) — a feminine designation for the **church** sending the greeting (cf. "the elect lady," 2 John 1, 13), not a woman
**Origin:** The Christian community at the place from which the letter is written, named "Babylon" (Bavel)
**Familiar name:** the church in "Babylon"
**First mention:** 1 Peter 5:13
**Mentioned in:** 1 Peter 5:13
**Key events in 1 Peter:** Sends greetings to the recipient churches in the closing — "She *who is* in Babylon, chosen together *with you*, greets you" (5:13) — alongside Markos (Mark). "Chosen-together" (*syneklektē*) binds the sending community to the addressees as fellow-elect.
**Historicity status:** PROBABLE (a sending community existed; the location's identity is debated)
**Books appearing in:** 1 Peter
**Note (collective entry):** Not a person — a **community** personified as feminine "she" (the church). "**Babylon**" is **widely read as a cipher for Rome** (the archetypal exile-empire, as in Revelation 17–18; consonant with the tradition of Peter's later Roman ministry); a minority hold for literal Babylon in Mesopotamia or the Egyptian Babylon. The TT keeps "Babylon (Bavel)" in the text and notes the Rome reading (Tier-2); see Companion §B and CHAPTER-5 v.13.

## Kefa (Peter)
**See:** mark/PEOPLE.md
**In 1 Peter:** author/apostle, writing to the exiles of the dispersion. He names himself in the opening — "Kefa (Peter), an apostle of Yeshua *the* Messiah, to *the* elect sojourners of *the* dispersion" (1:1) — and writes the whole letter in his own voice: a circular epistle of exhortation to scattered, suffering believers across five provinces of Asia Minor. He grounds their hope in the new birth, the living hope, and the imperishable inheritance (ch. 1); calls them to holy conduct, to honor within the household and the empire, and to endure unjust suffering after the pattern of the suffering Messiah (chs. 2–4); and closes as a "**fellow-elder**" (συμπρεσβύτερος, 5:1) — a notable self-lowering — charging the elders to shepherd the flock not for gain or by domineering, and urging humility, vigilance, and steadfastness (ch. 5). He signs off "through Silvanus" with greetings from "Babylon" and "Mark my son" (5:12-13). The canonical bio fields (the Galilean fisherman Shimon, the renaming Kefa/Peter, his place in the Twelve, his role in the gospels and Acts, later tradition) live in mark/PEOPLE.md; this entry adds the 1-Peter-specific role.
