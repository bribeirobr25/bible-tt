## READING GUIDE

**Main Text:** Primary translation — readable but faithful to Greek structure. **Notes:** Essential Greek features immediately below each verse.

**How the text is marked:**
- *Words in italics* — added for English grammar (not in the Greek text)
- {a:wind/spirit} — a word the Greek leaves open to two meanings, both kept
- @@"…"@@ — direct speech of God or the holy wind/spirit

Notes are tagged by type — **Critical · Lexical · Grammatical · Theological** — each shown with its colour (see the legend at the top of the Notes view).

**This is the most interpretively loaded chapter of the letter.** It completes the **household code** with wives and husbands (vv.1-7) — a high-restraint passage — then turns to the conduct of all (vv.8-12, closing with Psalm 34), to suffering for righteousness and "always ready for a defense" (vv.13-17), and finally to the **most debated paragraph in the letter**: the Messiah's suffering, his being "made alive in spirit," his proclamation "to the spirits in prison," Noah's ark, and baptism as antitype (vv.18-22). **Note on speech-marking (Rule 30):** there is no narrated divine speech here; the Psalm 34 citation (vv.10-12) is the psalmist's voice, not God's first person — unmarked. **High-restraint passages** (the household code vv.1-7; the spirits-in-prison vv.19-20; baptism v.21): the TT renders the Greek and flags the interpretive options in Tier-2, resolving none (Rule 3; editorial-log P-006, P-007, P-009). Names: Sarah (Sara), Avraham (Abraham), Noach (Noah).

## CHAPTER OVERVIEW

**What happens:** The household code continues: wives are to submit to their own husbands, so that even husbands who disobey the word may be won "without a word" through their wives' pure, reverent conduct (vv.1-2); their adornment should be not the outward braiding of hair and gold and clothing but "the hidden person of the heart" — a gentle and quiet spirit, precious before God (vv.3-4); so the holy women of old adorned themselves, submitting to their husbands, as Sarah obeyed Abraham, "calling him lord" — and the readers are her children if they do good and fear no terror (vv.5-6). Husbands, likewise, are to dwell with their wives "according to knowledge," honoring them as the "weaker vessel" and as co-heirs of the grace of life, so that their prayers not be hindered (v.7). Then to all: be of one mind, sympathetic, loving, tenderhearted, humble; do not repay evil for evil or reviling for reviling, but bless, for you were called to inherit a blessing (vv.8-9) — grounded in Psalm 34: let the one who would love life keep the tongue from evil, turn from evil and do good, seek peace; for the eyes of the Lord are on the righteous, but his face against evildoers (vv.10-12). Who will harm you if you are zealous for good? Even if you suffer for righteousness, you are blessed; do not fear their fear, but sanctify the Messiah as Lord in your hearts, always ready to give a defense to anyone who asks for the reason for your hope — with gentleness and reverence, keeping a good conscience, so that those who revile your good conduct are shamed (vv.13-16); for it is better to suffer for doing good, if God so wills, than for doing evil (v.17). Because the Messiah also suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, to bring you to God — put to death in flesh but made alive in spirit (v.18); in which he went and proclaimed to the spirits in prison, who disobeyed in the days of Noah while the ark was being built, in which eight souls were brought safely through water (vv.19-20); which water, as an antitype, now saves you — baptism, not the removal of bodily dirt but an appeal of a good conscience toward God, through the resurrection (v.21); who is at God's right hand, with angels, authorities, and powers subjected to him (v.22).

**Key themes:** The **household code's** second panel (wives/husbands) — won "without a word" by conduct; "the hidden person of the heart" over outward adornment; Sarah as exemplar; husbands honoring wives as "co-heirs of the grace of life" (a striking dignity-claim within the code); the ethic of **non-retaliation** ("not evil for evil… but bless"), grounded in Psalm 34; suffering for righteousness as blessing; "sanctify the Messiah as Lord" (Isaiah 8:13 reapplied to Christ); the **apologia** — readiness to give the reason for hope with gentleness and reverence; and the dense, debated **vv.18-22**: the once-for-all righteous-for-unrighteous suffering; the flesh/spirit antithesis; the **proclamation to the spirits in prison**; **Noah's ark and the eight souls saved through water**; **baptism as antitype** ("not the removal of dirt but an appeal of a good conscience"); the Messiah's exaltation over all powers.

**Watch for:** The **household code (vv.1-7)** is a high-restraint passage — the TT renders the Greek (wives submit; "weaker vessel"; Sarah "calling him lord") and flags the reception history in the notes, taking no position (Rule 3; P-009). Note the **mutuality** the code already carries: husbands honor wives as "co-heirs," "so that your prayers not be hindered." **3:15** "sanctify the Messiah as Lord" reapplies Isaiah 8:13 ("the LORD of hosts… sanctify him") to the Messiah — a κύριος referent-shift, flagged. **Vv.18-22** are the letter's exegetical storm-center: the dative **πνεύματι** (3:18, "in/by spirit") is the flesh/spirit crux; **"the spirits in prison"** (3:19) admits several readings (the dead of Noah's day / the disobedient angels of Genesis 6 / a descent proclamation) — the TT renders literally and resolves none (P-006); **baptism "now saves you… as antitype"** (3:21) with "appeal/pledge of a good conscience" is rendered without importing a sacramental theology (P-007). The Psalm 34 citation (vv.10-12) is the psalmist's voice — unmarked; *kyrios* there = YHWH (Option C).

**Connection:** Sarah and Abraham (vv.5-6) draw on Genesis (18:12, Sarah's deference); "fear no terror" echoes Proverbs 3:25. The non-retaliation ethic and Psalm 34:12-16 (vv.10-12) extend the Psalm-34 thread begun at 2:3. "Do not fear their fear… sanctify [the Lord]" (vv.14-15) quotes Isaiah 8:12-13 — where "the LORD of hosts" is the one to be sanctified, here reapplied to the Messiah. The spirits-in-prison and the ark (vv.19-20) draw on Genesis 6-8 (the flood, the eight in the ark) and the Second-Temple Enochic tradition of the imprisoned "watchers" (1 Enoch). Baptism "through water" as the antitype of the flood (v.21) reads the deluge typologically. The exaltation "at God's right hand" with powers subjected (v.22) echoes Psalm 110:1 (the most-cited HB verse, as in Acts 2).

## VERSE-BY-VERSE STUDY

### **Verse 1**

Likewise, wives, *be* submitting to *your* own husbands, so that even if some disobey the word, through the conduct of the wives, without a word, they may be won,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE HOUSEHOLD CODE: WIVES (high restraint, P-009)**
> - **γυναῖκες, ὑποτασσόμεναι τοῖς ἰδίοις ἀνδράσιν** (*gynaikes, hypotassomenai tois idiois andrasin*) = "wives, *be* submitting to *your* own husbands." "**Likewise** (*homoiōs*)" links to the servants of 2:18 — the household-code's second panel. The pastoral aim is missionary: an "**unbelieving**" husband (one who "disobeys the word") "may be won (**κερδηθήσονται**) **without a word**" — by the wife's conduct (*anastrophē*), not argument. The TT renders the Greek and **flags the interpretive history in Tier-2 / the companion, taking no position** (Rule 3). → editorial-log P-009; Companion §F (reception).

---

### **Verse 2**

having observed your pure conduct in fear.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"PURE CONDUCT IN FEAR"**
> - **τὴν ἐν φόβῳ ἁγνὴν ἀναστροφήν** (*tēn en phobō hagnēn anastrophēn*) = "your pure conduct in fear." **ἐποπτεύσαντες** ("having observed/watched closely," cf. 2:12) — the husband is won by *seeing*. "Fear" here = reverence (toward God; cf. 1:17; 3:15), not dread of the husband.

---

### **Verse 3**

Let your adornment not be the outward *one* — of braiding of hair and wearing of gold *ornaments* or putting on of garments —

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"ADORNMENT" — *KOSMOS***
> - **ὁ… κόσμος** (*ho kosmos*) here = "adornment / ordering" (the root sense of *kosmos*; cf. "cosmetic"). The triad — braided hair, gold, fine clothes — is the conventional moralists' list (cf. 1 Tim 2:9); the point is not prohibition of all adornment but its relocation (v.4).

---

### **Verse 4**

but the hidden person of the heart, in the imperishable *beauty* of a gentle and quiet spirit, which is precious before God.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "THE HIDDEN PERSON OF THE HEART"**
> - **ὁ κρυπτὸς τῆς καρδίας ἄνθρωπος** (*ho kryptos tēs kardias anthrōpos*) = "the hidden person of the heart." The true *kosmos* is interior — **τοῦ πραέως καὶ ἡσυχίου πνεύματος** ("of a gentle and quiet spirit"). *Pneuma* here = the human disposition/temperament (no "wind" sense is live), so it is rendered "spirit" plain, **not** the {a:wind/spirit} slash. **ἄφθαρτος** ("imperishable") links to the imperishable inheritance/seed (1:4, 23). "Precious (**πολυτελές**) before God" inverts the value-scale of v.3.

---

### **Verse 5**

For thus also formerly the holy women, the *ones* hoping in God, adorned themselves, submitting to *their* own husbands,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"THE HOLY WOMEN… HOPING IN GOD"**
> - **αἱ ἅγιαι γυναῖκες αἱ ἐλπίζουσαι εἰς θεόν** (*hai hagiai gynaikes hai elpizousai eis theon*) = "the holy women, the *ones* hoping in God." The matriarchs are the precedent; their adornment was the inner *kosmos* + the submission of v.1. "Hope" (*elpis*) is the letter's keynote (1:3, 13, 21; 3:15).

---

### **Verse 6**

as Sarah (Sara) obeyed Avraham (Abraham), calling him lord; of whom you became children, doing good and not fearing any terror.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — SARAH "CALLING HIM LORD" (Gen 18:12)**
> - **ὡς Σάρρα ὑπήκουσεν τῷ Ἀβραάμ, κύριον αὐτὸν καλοῦσα** = "as Sarah obeyed Avraham, calling him **lord**." Draws on Genesis 18:12 (Sarah's inward "my **lord** [*adoni*] *being* old"). Here *kyrios* = a human honorific ("lord/master"), not the divine name — the TT renders "lord" lowercase. The readers are "**her children**" by **doing good** (*agathopoieō*) and "not fearing any terror" (**πτόησιν**, echoing Prov 3:25 LXX). → Companion §A.

---

### **Verse 7**

Husbands, likewise, dwelling together *with them* according to knowledge, as with a weaker vessel — the feminine *one* — assigning honor, as also *to* co-heirs of *the* grace of life, so that your prayers may not be hindered.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — HUSBANDS: "WEAKER VESSEL" + "CO-HEIRS" (P-009)**
> - **ὡς ἀσθενεστέρῳ σκεύει τῷ γυναικείῳ, ἀπονέμοντες τιμήν** = "as with a **weaker vessel** — the feminine *one* — assigning **honor**." The code addresses husbands too: dwell "according to knowledge (**κατὰ γνῶσιν**)," assign honor, recognizing the wife as a **co-heir (συγκληρονόμος) of the grace of life** — a striking dignity-and-equality claim *within* the code. "**Weaker vessel**" (*asthenesteron skeuos*) is rendered literally (likely socially/physically situated, not a worth-claim — the same sentence calls her a co-heir); the interpretive history is flagged, no position taken (Rule 3, P-009). The sanction is severe: **"so that your prayers may not be hindered (**ἐγκόπτεσθαι**)."** → editorial-log P-009; Companion §F.

---

### **Verse 8**

And finally, *be* all of one mind, sympathetic, loving the brothers, tenderhearted, humble-minded,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **THE FIVE-FOLD COMMUNITY VIRTUE LIST**
> - **ὁμόφρονες, συμπαθεῖς, φιλάδελφοι, εὔσπλαγχνοι, ταπεινόφρονες** = "of one mind, sympathetic, loving the brothers, tenderhearted, humble-minded" — five compound adjectives (the last, *tapeinophrōn*, "humble-minded," a virtue the culture despised). Addressed to "all," closing the household section with mutual community ethics.

---

### **Verse 9**

not repaying evil for evil or reviling for reviling, but on the contrary blessing, because for this you were called, so that you might inherit a blessing.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — NON-RETALIATION: "BLESS, … INHERIT A BLESSING"**
> - **μὴ ἀποδιδόντες κακὸν ἀντὶ κακοῦ… τοὐναντίον δὲ εὐλογοῦντες** = "not repaying evil for evil… but on the contrary blessing." The Messiah's own non-retaliation (2:23) becomes the readers' calling; the wordplay: they were called to *bless* (*eulogeō*) so as to *inherit a blessing* (*eulogia*). Echoes Jesus's teaching (Lk 6:28). Grounds the Psalm 34 citation following.

---

### **Verse 10**

For "the *one* desiring to love life and to see good days, let him stop his tongue from evil and *his* lips from speaking deceit,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — PSALM 34:12-16 (the citation begins; κύριος = YHWH)**
> - Begins the quotation of **Psalm 34:12-16** (LXX 33:13-17), the longest OT citation in the letter (vv.10-12), grounding the non-retaliation ethic. The Psalm-34 thread runs from 2:3. This is the psalmist's voice (wisdom exhortation), **not** God's first-person — so it is **not** divine-marked (Rule 30). → PROPHECY (Ps 34:12-16 → 1 Pet 3:10-12).

---

### **Verse 11**

and let him turn away from evil and do good, let him seek peace and pursue it.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"SEEK PEACE AND PURSUE IT"**
> - **ζητησάτω εἰρήνην καὶ διωξάτω αὐτήν** (*zētēsatō eirēnēn kai diōxatō autēn*) = "let him seek peace and pursue it" (Ps 34:14). Active peacemaking, not mere avoidance of evil — the positive face of the non-retaliation ethic.

---

### **Verse 12**

Because *the* eyes of *the* Lord *are* on *the* righteous, and his ears to their petition, but *the* face of *the* Lord *is* against *those* doing evil."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **"THE EYES OF THE LORD" (κύριος = YHWH, Option C)**
> - **ὀφθαλμοὶ κυρίου ἐπὶ δικαίους** (*ophthalmoi kyriou epi dikaious*) = "*the* eyes of *the* Lord *are* on *the* righteous" (Ps 34:15-16). *Kyrios* = **YHWH** (Option C: "the Lord"). The Psalm's assurance (God attends the righteous, opposes evildoers) undergirds the call to bless rather than retaliate. Closes the citation.

---

### **Verse 13**

And who *is* the *one* who will harm you if you become zealots of the good?

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"ZEALOTS OF THE GOOD" — *ZĒLŌTAI***
> - **τοῦ ἀγαθοῦ ζηλωταί** (*tou agathou zēlōtai*) = "zealots of the good." A rhetorical question (cf. Ps 34's protection) transitioning to the suffering-for-righteousness theme; even if harm comes (v.14), the principle holds.

---

### **Verse 14**

But even if you should suffer for righteousness, *you are* blessed. And do not fear their fear, nor be troubled,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — ISAIAH 8:12 + THE BEATITUDE**
> - **εἰ καὶ πάσχοιτε διὰ δικαιοσύνην, μακάριοι** = "even if you should suffer for righteousness, *you are* **blessed**" (the beatitude form, cf. Mt 5:10). **τὸν δὲ φόβον αὐτῶν μὴ φοβηθῆτε μηδὲ ταραχθῆτε** ("do not fear their fear nor be troubled") quotes **Isaiah 8:12** — setting up the reapplication of Isaiah 8:13 in v.15.

---

### **Verse 15**

but sanctify *the* Messiah as Lord in your hearts, *being* always ready for a defense to everyone asking you a word concerning the hope in you,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — ISAIAH 8:13 REAPPLIED + THE *APOLOGIA***
> - **κύριον δὲ τὸν Χριστὸν ἁγιάσατε** (*kyrion de ton Christon hagiasate*) = "sanctify *the* Messiah **as Lord**." Isaiah 8:13 reads "**the LORD of hosts** (YHWH), **him** you shall sanctify"; 1 Peter substitutes "the Messiah" for the object — a **κύριος referent-shift** applying a YHWH-text to Christ (Rule 13 + Tier-2; cf. the same move at Acts 2:36). **ἀπολογία** (*apologia*) = "defense, reasoned answer" (legal-sounding) — the readers are "**always ready**" to give the **reason** for their **hope** (*elpis*), the source of the English "apologetics." → Companion §A; PROPHECY (Isa 8:12-13 → 1 Pet 3:14-15).

---

### **Verse 16**

but with gentleness and fear, having a good conscience, so that in *the matter* in which you are spoken against, those reviling your good conduct in *the* Messiah may be put to shame.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"WITH GENTLENESS AND FEAR" — THE MANNER OF THE DEFENSE**
> - **μετὰ πραΰτητος καὶ φόβου, συνείδησιν ἔχοντες ἀγαθήν** = "with gentleness and fear, having a good **conscience**." The *apologia* (v.15) is bounded by manner: gentleness + reverent fear, not combativeness. **συνείδησις** ("conscience") recurs at v.21 — the good conscience is the through-line.

---

### **Verse 17**

For *it is* better, if the will of God should will *it*, to suffer doing good than doing evil.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"BETTER TO SUFFER DOING GOOD" — *KREITTON***
> - **κρεῖττον γὰρ ἀγαθοποιοῦντας… πάσχειν** = "better… to suffer doing good." Restates 2:20; "**if the will of God should will *it***" (the optative) keeps suffering within God's permission, not as inevitable — and sets up the supreme example (v.18).

---

### **Verse 18**

Because *the* Messiah also suffered once for sins, *the* righteous for *the* unrighteous, so that he might bring you to God — put to death in flesh but made alive in {a:wind/spirit};

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "ONCE FOR SINS… PUT TO DEATH IN FLESH, MADE ALIVE IN SPIRIT" (the crux opens, P-006)**
> - **ἅπαξ περὶ ἁμαρτιῶν ἔπαθεν, δίκαιος ὑπὲρ ἀδίκων** = "suffered **once** (*hapax*) for sins, *the* righteous for *the* unrighteous" — the vicarious, once-for-all suffering (cf. 2:24; Isa 53). The crux is the parallel pair of anarthrous datives: **θανατωθεὶς μὲν σαρκὶ ζῳοποιηθεὶς δὲ πνεύματι** = "put to death **in flesh** (*sarki*) but made alive **in spirit** (*pneumati*)." Read as spheres ("in the flesh / in the spirit") or as agency ("by the Spirit"); the TT keeps the {a:wind/spirit} slash and the flesh/spirit parallel, **resolving neither** (Rule 13). → editorial-log P-006; Companion §A.

---

### **Verse 19**

in which also, having gone, he proclaimed to the spirits in prison,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "PROCLAIMED TO THE SPIRITS IN PRISON" (the descent crux, P-006)**
> - **ἐν ᾧ καὶ τοῖς ἐν φυλακῇ πνεύμασιν πορευθεὶς ἐκήρυξεν** = "in which also, having gone, he proclaimed (*ekēryxen*) to the **spirits in prison**." The most debated clause in the letter. Major readings: (a) the **disobedient dead of Noah's generation** (v.20); (b) the **disobedient angels/"watchers" of Genesis 6** imprisoned (the Enochic tradition, 1 Enoch 6-21; cf. 2 Pet 2:4; Jude 6); (c) the **pre-incarnate Christ** preaching through Noah. "Spirits" (*pneumata*) = the imprisoned beings (no "wind" sense — rendered plain "spirits," not the slash). *Ekēryxen* ("proclaimed/heralded") does not specify content (good news vs. judgment). The TT renders literally and **resolves none** (Rule 13, Rule 3). → editorial-log P-006; Companion §A; §F (reception: the "harrowing of hell").

---

### **Verse 20**

who once disobeyed, when the patience of God was waiting in *the* days of Noach (Noah), *while* an ark was being prepared, into which a few — that is, eight souls — were brought safely through water.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — NOAH'S ARK: EIGHT SOULS SAVED THROUGH WATER (Gen 6-8)**
> - **ἐν ἡμέραις Νῶε κατασκευαζομένης κιβωτοῦ… ὀκτὼ ψυχαί, διεσώθησαν δι' ὕδατος** = "in *the* days of Noach, *while* an ark was being prepared… eight souls were brought safely **through water**." Genesis 6-8: God's patience (*makrothymia*) waited; the eight (Noah's household) were saved **δι' ὕδατος** ("through water") — the flood both judged and, for the eight, was the medium of rescue. This sets up the baptism antitype (v.21). **ψυχαί** = "souls/persons" (the whole people; cf. 1:9). → PROPHECY (Gen 6-8 → 1 Pet 3:20).

---

### **Verse 21**

Which also, *as* an antitype, now saves you — immersion, not a removal of dirt of *the* flesh but an appeal of a good conscience toward God — through *the* resurrection of Yeshua *the* Messiah (Jesus),

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — BAPTISM "NOW SAVES YOU… AS ANTITYPE" (P-007)**
> - **ὃ… ἀντίτυπον νῦν σῴζει βάπτισμα** = "Which also, *as* an **antitype** (*antitypon*), now saves you — **immersion** (*baptisma*)." The flood-water is the type; baptism the antitype. The verse immediately qualifies: **οὐ σαρκὸς ἀπόθεσις ῥύπου ἀλλὰ συνειδήσεως ἀγαθῆς ἐπερώτημα εἰς θεόν** = "not a removal of dirt of *the* flesh but an **appeal/pledge** (*eperōtēma*) of a good conscience toward God." *Eperōtēma* is debated ("appeal/request" vs. "pledge/answer" — a baptismal-formula term); the TT renders "appeal" and notes the option. The saving is **"through *the* resurrection"** — not the water itself. Rendered without importing a sacramental theology (Rule 3). → editorial-log P-007; Companion §A.

### **Verse 22**

who is at *the* right *hand* of God, having gone into heaven, *with* angels and authorities and powers having been subjected to him.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — EXALTATION: PSALM 110:1 + POWERS SUBJECTED**
> - **ὅς ἐστιν ἐν δεξιᾷ θεοῦ… ὑποταγέντων αὐτῷ ἀγγέλων καὶ ἐξουσιῶν καὶ δυνάμεων** = "who is at *the* **right *hand*** of God… *with* angels and authorities and powers **having been subjected** to him." The exaltation echoes **Psalm 110:1** (at God's right hand — the verse behind Acts 2:34-35). The "**subjection**" (*hypotagentōn*, the household-code verb *hypotassō*) of all spirit-powers answers the "spirits in prison" (v.19): the Messiah who proclaimed to them now reigns over every power. → PROPHECY (Ps 110:1 → 1 Pet 3:22); cross-ref Acts 2.
