## READING GUIDE

**Main Text:** Primary translation — readable but faithful to Greek structure. **Notes:** Essential Greek features immediately below each verse.

**How the text is marked:**
- *Words in italics* — added for English grammar (not in the Greek text)
- {a:wind/spirit} — a word the Greek leaves open to two meanings, both kept
- @@"…"@@ — direct speech of God or the holy wind/spirit

Notes are tagged by type — **Critical · Lexical · Grammatical · Theological** — each shown with its colour (see the legend at the top of the Notes view).

**Chapter 2 turns from new birth to new identity and conduct.** Put away malice and long for pure milk (vv.1-3); come to the living stone and be built a spiritual house, a holy priesthood — the **living-stone catena** weaving Isaiah 28:16, Psalm 118:22, and Isaiah 8:14 (vv.4-8); you are "a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation" (vv.9-10, Exodus 19:6 / Hosea). Then the conduct of exiles: honorable among the nations (vv.11-12); submission to human authorities (vv.13-17); and the first panel of the **household code** — household servants and the suffering Messiah as the pattern, framed by Isaiah 53 (vv.18-25). **Note on speech-marking (Rule 30):** the only divine first-person word here is inside the Isaiah 28:16 citation (2:6, "behold, I lay in Zion…") — marked @@…@@; the other OT quotations (Ps 118:22; Isa 8:14; Ps 34:8; Isa 53) are not God's first-person speech and are unmarked. **The household code (2:18-25) is a high-restraint passage** — the TT renders the Greek (household servants submit, the Messiah's example) and flags the interpretive history in the notes, importing nothing (Rule 3; editorial-log P-009).

## CHAPTER OVERVIEW

**What happens:** Having been born again through the living word (ch.1), the readers are to put away all malice, deceit, hypocrisy, envy, and slander, and like newborn infants long for the pure spiritual milk, since "you tasted that the Lord is good" (vv.1-3, Ps 34:8). Coming to the Messiah as a living stone — rejected by people but chosen and precious to God — they themselves are built as living stones into a spiritual house, a holy priesthood offering spiritual sacrifices (vv.4-5). Scripture is woven into a catena about the stone: the chosen cornerstone in Zion (Isa 28:16), the stone the builders rejected become the head of the corner (Ps 118:22), and a stone of stumbling for those who disbelieve, who stumble "as they were appointed" (vv.6-8, Isa 8:14). But the readers are "a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for God's possession" — once not a people, now God's people, once unpitied, now pitied (vv.9-10, Ex 19:6; Hosea). As sojourners and exiles they are to abstain from fleshly desires and keep their conduct honorable among the nations, so that those who malign them may glorify God (vv.11-12). They are to submit, for the Lord's sake, to every human institution — emperor and governors — living as free yet as God's servants: honor everyone, love the brotherhood, fear God, honor the emperor (vv.13-17). Household servants are to submit to their masters, even harsh ones, for it is a gracious thing to suffer unjustly while doing good — because the Messiah suffered for you, leaving an example to follow (vv.18-21). Then the chapter folds in Isaiah 53: he committed no sin, no deceit was found in his mouth; reviled, he did not revile back; he bore our sins in his body on the tree, that we might live to righteousness; "by his wound you were healed," for "you were straying like sheep," but now returned to the shepherd and overseer of your souls (vv.22-25).

**Key themes:** Newborn longing for the "pure spiritual milk"; the **living stone** and the community as a spiritual house / holy priesthood; the threefold stone-catena (cornerstone / rejected-stone-become-head / stone of stumbling); the staggering identity transfer — Israel's titles ("chosen race, royal priesthood, holy nation, people for possession," Ex 19:6) applied to the scattered believers, with Hosea's reversal ("not my people → my people"); the exile ethic — honorable conduct among the nations as witness; submission to governing authorities "for the Lord's sake" within freedom; the **household code's** first panel (servants) grounded not in social theory but in the **suffering Messiah** as *hypogrammos* (a writing-copy to trace); the dense weave of **Isaiah 53** (no sin, no deceit, silent under reviling, bore our sins, "by his wound you were healed"); the Messiah as **shepherd and overseer of souls**.

**Watch for:** The stone-catena (vv.6-8) stacks three OT texts — the TT keeps each recognizable. At 2:6 the citation is God's first-person word ("I lay in Zion…") — marked @@…@@. **2:8** — "they stumble… as they were appointed" (*etethēsan*) — election/destiny language; the TT renders it without resolving (Rule 13). **2:9-10** transfers Israel's covenant titles to the church — rendered as the Greek has it, no supersessionist gloss added (Rule 3). The **household code begins at 2:18** (*oiketai*, household servants) — a high-restraint passage; render the Greek, flag the history, import nothing (editorial-log P-009). 2:21 *hypogrammos* ("writing-copy/example") is a rare, vivid word. The Isaiah 53 weave (vv.22-25) is allusive (not formula-cited) — the densest NT use of the fourth Servant Song.

**Connection:** The chapter is a tapestry of the Hebrew Bible. "Taste that the Lord is good" (2:3) quotes Psalm 34:8 (a psalm Peter returns to at 3:10-12). The stone-catena draws Isaiah 28:16 (the cornerstone in Zion), Psalm 118:22 (the rejected stone), and Isaiah 8:14 (the stone of stumbling) — the same stone-texts the gospels apply to the Messiah (Mk 12:10 par.; Lk 20:17). The identity titles (2:9) reproduce Exodus 19:6 ("a kingdom of priests and a holy nation") and Isaiah 43:20-21 ("my chosen people… for myself"); "not a people → people of God" (2:10) is Hosea 1:9-10; 2:23. The servant-portrait (2:22-25) is Isaiah 53:9, 4-6, 12 — "he bore our sins," "by his wound healed," "all we like sheep went astray" — the Servant Song already sounded in Acts 3 (the *pais*).

## VERSE-BY-VERSE STUDY

### **Verse 1**

Therefore, having put away all malice and all deceit and hypocrisy and envies and all slanders,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"HAVING PUT AWAY" — *APOTHEMENOI* (the vice-list)**
> - **ἀποθέμενοι** (*apothemenoi*) = "having put away/off" (a clothing metaphor, as of stripping a garment). The five vices (malice, deceit, hypocrisy, envies, slanders) are the social sins that corrode community — the opposite of the "sincere brotherly love" of 1:22. Sets up the "long for milk" of v.2.

---

### **Verse 2**

like newborn infants, long for the pure {a:milk *of the* word/spiritual milk}, so that by it you may grow into salvation,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "THE PURE *LOGIKON* MILK" (an ambiguity)**
> - **τὸ λογικὸν ἄδολον γάλα** (*to logikon adolon gala*) = "the pure *logikon* milk." **λογικός** (*logikos*) is genuinely double: "of the word" (*logos* — tying to the living word of 1:23-25) *and* "rational/spiritual" (cf. Rom 12:1). The TT keeps the slash {a:milk *of the* word/spiritual milk}. **ἄδολον** ("pure/unadulterated," lit. "without deceit," echoing the *dolos* of v.1). → Companion §D.

---

### **Verse 3**

if *indeed* you tasted that the Lord *is* good.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — PSALM 34:8 (κύριος = YHWH, reapplied)**
> - **ἐγεύσασθε ὅτι χρηστὸς ὁ κύριος** (*egeusasthe hoti chrēstos ho kyrios*) = "you tasted that the Lord *is* good" (Ps 34:8 LXX). In the psalm *kyrios* = **YHWH** (Option C: "the Lord"); 1 Peter applies it in a context where "the Lord" of v.4 is the Messiah — a κύριος referent-flow the TT keeps "the Lord" and notes. **χρηστός** ("good/kind") puns on **Χριστός** ("Messiah") — a likely intended wordplay. → PROPHECY (Ps 34:8 → 1 Pet 2:3).

---

### **Verse 4**

Coming to him, a living stone — rejected by people but chosen *and* precious before God —

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"A LIVING STONE" — *LITHON ZŌNTA***
> - **λίθον ζῶντα** (*lithon zōnta*) = "a living stone" — a striking oxymoron introducing the stone-catena (vv.6-8). The Messiah is "rejected (**ἀποδεδοκιμασμένον**) by people but chosen and precious (**ἔντιμον**) before God" — the rejection/election contrast that the Psalm 118 and Isaiah 28 citations will ground.

---

### **Verse 5**

you yourselves also, as living stones, are being built *as* a spiritual house into a holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Yeshua *the* Messiah (Jesus).

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "A SPIRITUAL HOUSE… A HOLY PRIESTHOOD"**
> - **οἶκος πνευματικὸς εἰς ἱεράτευμα ἅγιον** (*oikos pneumatikos eis hierateuma hagion*) = "a spiritual house into a holy priesthood." The believers are both the temple (house) and its priests, offering "spiritual sacrifices." Temple + priesthood imagery transferred to the community (anticipating the Exodus-19 titles of v.9). **πνευματικός** here = "spiritual" (of the {a:wind/spirit}); the TT keeps "spiritual" in this adjectival use.

---

### **Verse 6**

Because it is contained in scripture: @@"Behold, I lay in Zion (Tsiyon) a stone, a chosen, precious cornerstone, and the *one* believing in him will never be put to shame."@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — ISAIAH 28:16 (divine first-person word → marked)**
> - **ἰδοὺ τίθημι ἐν Σιὼν λίθον… ἀκρογωνιαῖον** (*idou tithēmi en Siōn lithon akrogōniaion*) = "Behold, I lay in Zion a stone… a cornerstone" (Isa 28:16 LXX). This is **God's first-person word** ("I lay"), so it is marked **@@…@@** (Rule 30 / A-004). **ἀκρογωνιαῖος** = "cornerstone / capstone." "Will never be put to shame" (*ou mē kataischynthē*) — the LXX reading. First stone of the catena. → PROPHECY (Isa 28:16 → 1 Pet 2:6).

---

### **Verse 7**

To you therefore who believe *is* the preciousness, but to those disbelieving, "*the* stone which the builders rejected — this *one* became *the* head of *the* corner,"

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — PSALM 118:22 (the rejected stone)**
> - **λίθος ὃν ἀπεδοκίμασαν οἱ οἰκοδομοῦντες, οὗτος ἐγενήθη εἰς κεφαλὴν γωνίας** (*lithos hon apedokimasan… eis kephalēn gōnias*) = "*the* stone which the builders rejected — this *one* became *the* head of *the* corner" (Ps 118:22). The psalm's rejected-then-vindicated stone, applied to the Messiah (as in Mk 12:10 par.; Acts 4:11). This is the psalmist's voice, not God's first-person — **not** divine-marked. → PROPHECY (Ps 118:22 → 1 Pet 2:7).

---

### **Verse 8**

and "a stone of stumbling and a rock of offense"; *those* who stumble *at* the word, disbelieving — to which also they were appointed.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — ISAIAH 8:14 + "AS THEY WERE APPOINTED" (*ETETHĒSAN*)**
> - **λίθος προσκόμματος καὶ πέτρα σκανδάλου** (*lithos proskommatos kai petra skandalou*) = "a stone of stumbling and a rock of offense" (Isa 8:14). The third stone-text. The clause **εἰς ὃ καὶ ἐτέθησαν** ("to which also they were appointed/set") attaches the appointing to the *stumbling* — election/destiny language the TT renders without resolving its scope (Rule 13). The wordplay: the same *lithos* is honor to believers, stumbling to the disbelieving. **[theological — the referent of "appointed" (stumbling vs. disbelief): Uncertain; rendered, not resolved.]** → Companion §A; PROPHECY (Isa 8:14 → 1 Pet 2:8).

---

### **Verse 9**

But you *are* a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for *God's* possession, so that you may declare the excellencies of the *One* who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — EXODUS 19:6 / ISAIAH 43 — ISRAEL'S TITLES TRANSFERRED**
> - **γένος ἐκλεκτόν, βασίλειον ἱεράτευμα, ἔθνος ἅγιον, λαὸς εἰς περιποίησιν** (*genos eklekton, basileion hierateuma, ethnos hagion, laos eis peripoiēsin*) = "a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for *God's* possession." Four titles of **Israel** — Exodus 19:6 ("a kingdom of priests and a holy nation") + Isaiah 43:20-21 ("my chosen race… a people I formed for myself") — applied to the scattered believers. The TT renders the transfer **as the Greek has it**, adding no supersessionist gloss (Rule 3). → PROPHECY (Ex 19:6 / Isa 43 → 1 Pet 2:9).

---

### **Verse 10**

*you* who once *were* not a people but now *are* a people of God, the *ones* not having received mercy but now having received mercy.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — HOSEA: "NOT A PEOPLE → PEOPLE OF GOD"**
> - **οἵ ποτε οὐ λαὸς νῦν δὲ λαὸς θεοῦ** (*hoi pote ou laos nyn de laos theou*) = "who once *were* not a people but now *are* a people of God" — Hosea 1:9-10; 2:23 (the reversal of "Lo-Ammi / not my people" and "Lo-Ruhamah / not pitied"). Hosea's word for unfaithful Israel's restoration is applied to the readers' inclusion. → PROPHECY (Hosea 1-2 → 1 Pet 2:10).

---

### **Verse 11**

Beloved, I urge *you* as sojourners and exiles to abstain from the fleshly desires, which war against the soul,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "SOJOURNERS AND EXILES" (the identity keyword)**
> - **παροίκους καὶ παρεπιδήμους** (*paroikous kai parepidēmous*) = "sojourners and exiles / resident-aliens and visiting-strangers." The letter's identity keyword (cf. 1:1, 17), now the basis of the ethic: as those who do not belong to the present order, abstain from "the fleshly desires (**ἐπιθυμίαι**), which war against the soul." → Companion §I (the *paroikoi* social identity).

---

### **Verse 12**

keeping your conduct among the nations honorable, so that, in *the* *matter* in which they speak against you as evildoers, observing *your* good works they may glorify God in *the* day of visitation.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"HONORABLE CONDUCT… DAY OF VISITATION"**
> - **τὴν ἀναστροφὴν… καλήν** (*tēn anastrophēn kalēn*) = "honorable conduct" (the *anastrophē* keyword, 1:15). The aim is missional: the maligning outsiders, **seeing** the good works, may "glorify God in *the* day of **visitation** (**ἐπισκοπή**)" — God's visiting, whether in mercy or judgment (the term is open). Echoes Mt 5:16.

---

### **Verse 13**

Submit to every human institution for the Lord's sake — whether to a king as supreme,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "SUBMIT… FOR THE LORD'S SAKE" (the civic code)**
> - **ὑποτάγητε πάσῃ ἀνθρωπίνῃ κτίσει διὰ τὸν κύριον** (*hypotagēte pasē anthrōpinē ktisei dia ton kyrion*) = "Submit to every human institution/creation for the Lord's sake." **ὑποτάσσω** ("submit/be subordinate") is the household-code verb (2:18; 3:1, 5); here applied to civic order. "**βασιλεύς** as supreme" = the emperor. Grounded "for the Lord's sake," not in the state's intrinsic authority. → editorial-log P-009 (the submission word-field).

---

### **Verse 14**

or to governors as *those* sent through him for *the* punishment of evildoers and *the* praise of those doing good.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"GOVERNORS… PUNISHMENT AND PRAISE"**
> - **ἡγεμόσιν** (*hēgemosin*) = "governors" (the provincial term, as of Pilate). The state's God-permitted function: punish evil, praise good (cf. Rom 13:3-4). The frame is limited and functional, not absolute.

---

### **Verse 15**

Because thus is the will of God: doing good to silence the ignorance of foolish people —

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"TO SILENCE… BY DOING GOOD" — *PHIMOUN***
> - **ἀγαθοποιοῦντας φιμοῦν** (*agathopoiountas phimoun*) = "by doing good to silence/muzzle." The answer to slander is conduct, not argument; *agathopoieō* ("do good") is a 1 Peter keyword (2:20; 3:6, 17).

---

### **Verse 16**

as free, and not having freedom as a covering for evil, but as servants of God.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"AS FREE… AS SERVANTS OF GOD" — THE PARADOX**
> - **ὡς ἐλεύθεροι… ὡς θεοῦ δοῦλοι** (*hōs eleutheroi… hōs theou douloi*) = "as free… as servants of God." Christian freedom is not license (**ἐπικάλυμμα τῆς κακίας**, "a covering for evil") but slavery to God — the paradox that frames the whole submission ethic.

---

### **Verse 17**

Honor everyone, love the brotherhood, fear God, honor the king.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **THE FOURFOLD MAXIM**
> - **πάντας τιμήσατε, τὴν ἀδελφότητα ἀγαπᾶτε, τὸν θεὸν φοβεῖσθε, τὸν βασιλέα τιμᾶτε** = "Honor everyone, love the brotherhood, fear God, honor the king." A crisp four-clause summary. Note the calibration: **fear** is for God; the king receives **honor** (*timē*), the same verb as "everyone" — the emperor is not feared as God (cf. Prov 24:21).

---

### **Verse 18**

*Household* servants, *be* submitting in all fear to *your* masters, not only to the good and gentle but also to the crooked.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE HOUSEHOLD CODE BEGINS (*OIKETAI*) — high restraint (P-009)**
> - **οἱ οἰκέται, ὑποτασσόμενοι… τοῖς δεσπόταις** (*hoi oiketai, hypotassomenoi… tois despotais*) = "*Household* servants, *be* submitting… to *your* masters." **οἰκέται** = household slaves/servants (not the general *douloi*). This opens the **household code** (2:18–3:7), the book's highest-restraint passage. The TT renders the Greek — the submission, even "to the crooked (**σκολιοῖς**)" — and **flags the interpretive history (ancient slavery; the modern reception) in Tier-2 and the companion, taking no position in the main text** (Rule 3). The address is to the servants as moral agents, and the ground (vv.21-25) is the Messiah's own unjust suffering, not an endorsement of the institution. → editorial-log P-009; Companion §F (reception history).

---

### **Verse 19**

For this *is* grace, if because of conscience *toward* God someone bears up under griefs, suffering unjustly.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"THIS IS GRACE" — *CHARIS***
> - **τοῦτο… χάρις** (*touto charis*) = "this *is* grace/favor" (vv.19, 20) — enduring *unjust* suffering "because of conscience toward God" is what is gracious; *charis* here ≈ "a commendable thing / what finds favor with God."

---

### **Verse 20**

For what credit *is it* if, sinning and being beaten, you endure? But if, doing good and suffering, you endure, this *is* grace before God.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"WHAT CREDIT" — *KLEOS***
> - **ποῖον κλέος** (*poion kleos*) = "what credit/glory." Suffering for wrongdoing earns nothing; suffering *for doing good* (the readers' situation, ch.1) is "grace before God." The pivot to the Messiah's example (v.21).

---

### **Verse 21**

For to this you were called, because the Messiah also suffered for you, leaving you an example, so that you might follow in his footsteps;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "AN EXAMPLE" — *HYPOGRAMMOS***
> - **ὑπογραμμὸν** (*hypogrammon*) = "a writing-copy / tracing-pattern" (a model for a child to trace letters over) — only here in the GS. The Messiah's unjust suffering is the *hypogrammos* the readers "follow in his footsteps (**ἴχνεσιν**)." This grounds the household-code submission not in social order but in the *imitatio* of the suffering Messiah — opening the Isaiah-53 weave.

---

### **Verse 22**

who committed no sin, nor was deceit found in his mouth;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — ISAIAH 53:9 (the sinless Servant)**
> - **ὃς ἁμαρτίαν οὐκ ἐποίησεν οὐδὲ εὑρέθη δόλος ἐν τῷ στόματι αὐτοῦ** = "who committed no sin, nor was deceit found in his mouth" — Isaiah 53:9 ("he had done no violence, nor was deceit in his mouth"). The Isaiah-53 weave (vv.22-25) is **allusive/woven**, not formula-cited — the densest NT use of the fourth Servant Song (the same Servant as Acts 3's *pais*). → PROPHECY (Isa 53 → 1 Pet 2:22-25).

---

### **Verse 23**

who, being reviled, did not revile in return; suffering, he did not threaten, but handed *himself* over to the *One* judging justly;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"HANDED HIMSELF OVER TO THE ONE JUDGING JUSTLY"**
> - **παρεδίδου δὲ τῷ κρίνοντι δικαίως** (*paredidou de tō krinonti dikaiōs*) = "but handed *himself* over to the *One* judging justly." The Messiah's silence under reviling (Isa 53:7) becomes the model for the suffering servants/readers — entrusting judgment to God rather than retaliating. The object of *paredidou* ("himself"/"his cause") is unexpressed; the TT supplies *himself* in italics.

---

### **Verse 24**

who himself bore up our sins in his body on the tree, so that, having died to sins, we might live to righteousness; by whose wound you were healed.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — ISAIAH 53:4-5, 12 — "BORE OUR SINS… BY HIS WOUND HEALED"**
> - **ὃς τὰς ἁμαρτίας ἡμῶν αὐτὸς ἀνήνεγκεν ἐν τῷ σώματι αὐτοῦ ἐπὶ τὸ ξύλον** = "who himself bore up our sins in his body on the tree" — Isaiah 53:4, 12 ("he bore the sins of many"). **ξύλον** ("tree/wood") for the cross (cf. Acts 5:30; Deut 21:23). **οὗ τῷ μώλωπι ἰάθητε** = "by whose **wound** (*mōlōps*, welt/bruise) you were healed" — Isaiah 53:5. The vicarious, sin-bearing reading of Isaiah 53 (a Jewish/Christian interpretive crux — see Readings in PROPHECY). → PROPHECY (Isa 53:4-6,12 → 1 Pet 2:24).

### **Verse 25**

For you were straying like sheep, but now you returned to the shepherd and overseer of your souls.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — ISAIAH 53:6 + THE SHEPHERD/OVERSEER**
> - **ἦτε γὰρ ὡς πρόβατα πλανώμενοι** (*ēte gar hōs probata planōmenoi*) = "you were straying like sheep" — Isaiah 53:6 ("all we like sheep have gone astray"). The return is "to the **shepherd** (*poimēn*) and **overseer** (*episkopos*) of your souls" — pastoral titles for the Messiah (cf. 5:4 "chief shepherd"; *episkopos*, "overseer," the same root as the "visitation" of 2:12). → PROPHECY (Isa 53:6 → 1 Pet 2:25).
