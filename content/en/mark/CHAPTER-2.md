## READING GUIDE

**Main Text:** Primary translation — readable but faithful to Greek structure. **Notes:** Essential Greek features immediately below each verse.

**How the text is marked:**
- *Words in italics* — added for English grammar (not in the Greek text)
- {a:wind/spirit} — a word the Greek leaves open to two meanings, both kept
- @@"…"@@ — direct speech of God or Yeshua (Jesus)

Notes are tagged by type — **Critical · Lexical · Grammatical · Theological** — each shown with its colour (see the legend at the top of the Notes view).

**This chapter gathers five controversies.** Mark 2:1–3:6 is a connected series of conflict stories: the paralytic and the authority to forgive, the call of Levi and eating with sinners, the question of fasting, and two sabbath disputes. The "Son of Man" (*ho huios tou anthrōpou*) appears for the first time (2:10, 2:28), claiming authority to forgive and lordship over the sabbath. The TT keeps Mark's concrete verbs and the Aramaic-flavoured idioms.

## CHAPTER OVERVIEW

**What happens:** Back in Kfar Nachum, a paralytic is lowered through the dug-open roof; Yeshua tells him his sins are forgiven, drawing the charge of blasphemy from the scribes — "who can forgive sins but God?" He answers that "the Son of Man has authority to forgive sins on the land," and heals the man as proof. He calls Levi the tax collector and dines with tax collectors and sinners, answering critics: "I did not come to call the righteous but sinners." Asked why his disciples do not fast, he speaks of the bridegroom, and of new wine in fresh wineskins. Passing through grainfields on the sabbath, his disciples pluck heads of grain; challenged, he cites David eating the loaves of presentation and declares, "The sabbath came into being for the human, not the human for the sabbath; so the Son of Man is lord even of the sabbath."

**Key themes:** "Son of Man" introduced (authority to forgive; lord of the sabbath); forgiveness of sins as a divine prerogative claimed; the gathering of outsiders (Levi, tax collectors, sinners); the bridegroom and the newness that bursts old containers; sabbath as gift for humanity; the first hardening of opposition that culminates at 3:6.

**Watch for:** "Son of Man" is literally "the son of the human" — an idiom that ranges from "a human being" to the Daniel 7:13 figure; the TT keeps "Son of Man" and notes the range. The reference to "Evyatar (Abiathar) the high priest" (2:26) names a different priest than 1 Samuel 21 (Ahimelech) — a well-known crux, flagged not smoothed. *Pneuma* at 2:8 is Yeshua's own inner spirit (no wind/spirit slash, unlike the divine spirit of chapter 1).

**Connection:** The forgiveness controversy presupposes the HB conviction that only God forgives sin (e.g. Isa 43:25). The David episode draws on 1 Samuel 21:1–6 (the loaves of presentation, Lev 24:5–9). "Son of Man" reaches toward Daniel 7:13. The bridegroom imagery echoes the HB marriage metaphor for God and Israel (Hos 2; Isa 62:5).

## VERSE-BY-VERSE STUDY

### **Verse 1**

And entering again into Kfar Nachum (Capernaum) after *some* days, it was heard that he is in *a* house.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"IT WAS HEARD THAT HE IS IN A HOUSE"**
> - **ἠκούσθη ὅτι ἐν οἴκῳ ἐστίν** (*ēkousthē hoti en oikō estin*) = "it was heard that he is in a house." The anarthrous *en oikō* may mean "at home" (Shimon's house, the base of 1:29). Mark's historical present "is" (*estin*) inside a past report is preserved.

---

### **Verse 2**

And many gathered together, so that there was no longer room, not even at the door, and he was speaking the word to them.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"THE WORD" — *TON LOGON***
> - **τὸν λόγον** (*ton logon*) = "the word" — Mark's shorthand for the message/proclamation (cf. 1:45; 4:14–20). The crowd-pressure motif (no room) continues from 1:33,45.

---

### **Verse 3**

And they come, bringing to him a paralytic, carried by four.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"CARRIED BY FOUR" — VIVID DETAIL**
> - **αἰρόμενον ὑπὸ τεσσάρων** (*airomenon hypo tessarōn*) = "carried by four." The concrete number is typical of Mark's vivid narration; Matthew and Luke omit it.

---

### **Verse 4**

And not being able to bring *him* to him because of the crowd, they unroofed the roof where he was, and having dug *it* out, they lower the mat where the paralytic was lying.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"UNROOFED THE ROOF … DUG IT OUT"**
> - **ἀπεστέγασαν τὴν στέγην … ἐξορύξαντες** (*apestegasan tēn stegēn … exoryxantes*) = "unroofed the roof … having dug out." The verbs fit a flat earthen-and-thatch roof that could be dug through. "They lower" (**χαλῶσι**, *chalōsi*) is a historical present. **κράβαττον** (*krabatton*) = a poor person's pallet/mat.

---

### **Verse 5**

And Yeshua (Jesus), seeing their trust, says to the paralytic, @@"Child, your sins are forgiven."@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "YOUR SINS ARE FORGIVEN"**
> - **ἀφίενταί σου αἱ ἁμαρτίαι** (*aphientai sou hai hamartiai*) = "your sins are forgiven/released." The verb (from *aphiēmi*, "release / let go / forgive") is the verbal counterpart of *aphesis* in 1:4; the TT keeps the conventional "forgiven" here with the release sense noted. The passive ("are forgiven") may be a divine passive (God forgives), which is precisely what the scribes contest in v.7. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL].
>
> 🟢 **"SEEING THEIR TRUST" — *PISTIS***
> - **ἰδὼν … τὴν πίστιν αὐτῶν** (*idōn … tēn pistin autōn*) = "seeing their trust." *Pistis* = trust/faith; "their" includes the four carriers. **τέκνον** (*teknon*) = "child" — an address of affection. Marked as divine speech (Rule 30 — Yeshua speaking).

---

### **Verse 6**

But some of the scribes were sitting there and reasoning in their hearts,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"REASONING IN THEIR HEARTS"**
> - **διαλογιζόμενοι ἐν ταῖς καρδίαις αὐτῶν** (*dialogizomenoi en tais kardiais autōn*) = "reasoning in their hearts" — internal, unspoken; the narrator gives the reader access to their thoughts, setting up v.8. (Scribes' inner speech; not divine.)

---

### **Verse 7**

"Why does this *one* speak thus? He blasphemes! Who is able to forgive sins except one — God?"

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "WHO CAN FORGIVE SINS EXCEPT GOD?"**
> - **τίς δύναται ἀφιέναι ἁμαρτίας εἰ μὴ εἷς ὁ θεός** (*tis dynatai aphienai hamartias ei mē heis ho theos*) = "who is able to forgive sins except one — God?" The scribes voice the HB conviction that forgiveness of sin is God's prerogative (cf. Isa 43:25). The charge of "blasphemy" (**βλασφημεῖ**) frames the central question of the episode — by what authority Yeshua forgives. The TT renders the objection without resolving it. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL].

---

### **Verse 8**

And immediately Yeshua (Jesus), knowing in his spirit that they reason thus among themselves, says to them, @@"Why do you reason these things in your hearts?@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"IN HIS SPIRIT" — *PNEUMA* (NO SLASH)**
> - **τῷ πνεύματι αὐτοῦ** (*tō pneumati autou*) = "in his spirit." Here *pneuma* is Yeshua's own inner spirit/perception — "wind" is not a live sense, so the TT does **not** use the wind/spirit slash (contrast 1:8,10,12). The phrase describes inward, immediate knowledge of the scribes' thoughts.

---

### **Verse 9**

Which is easier, to say to the paralytic, 'Your sins are forgiven,' or to say, 'Rise and take up your mat and walk'?

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **THE TEST QUESTION**
> - The rhetorical question pairs the unverifiable (forgiveness) with the verifiable (healing): the visible act will testify to the authority behind the invisible one. The quoted commands are within Yeshua's speech (Rule 30 span continues from v.8 through v.11).

---

### **Verse 10**

But that you may know that the Son of Man has authority to forgive sins on the land" — he says to the paralytic —

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "SON OF MAN" — FIRST OCCURRENCE**
> - **ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου** (*ho huios tou anthrōpou*) = literally "the son of the human/man." The idiom ranges from a generic "human being" (Aramaic *bar enash*) to the exalted figure of **Daniel 7:13** ("one like a son of man" coming with the clouds). Mark's first use already claims a divine prerogative (authority to forgive), tilting toward the exalted sense. The TT keeps the established "Son of Man" and notes the range; it does not collapse the ambiguity. **ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς** (*epi tēs gēs*) = "on the land/earth." Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL].
>
> 🔵 **BROKEN SYNTAX — NARRATOR ASIDE**
> - The sentence breaks off ("— he says to the paralytic —") and resumes in v.11; Mark embeds the narrator's aside inside Yeshua's speech. The TT preserves the interruption with dashes; the divine-speech marking pauses for the aside and resumes at v.11.

---

### **Verse 11**

@@"To you I say, rise, take up your mat, and go to your house."@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **THREE IMPERATIVES**
> - **ἔγειρε … ἆρον … ὕπαγε** (*egeire … aron … hypage*) = "rise … take up … go." The healing command resumes Yeshua's speech (Rule 30). The mat that carried him he now carries.

---

### **Verse 12**

And he rose and immediately, taking up the mat, went out before all, so that all were amazed and glorified God, saying, "We never saw *anything* like this."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"GLORIFIED GOD"**
> - The crowd's response (**ἐξίστασθαι** + **δοξάζειν τὸν θεόν**, "be amazed" + "glorify God") credits God, not directly resolving the authority question the scribes raised. *Euthys* ("immediately") again. (Crowd speech; not divine.)

---

### **Verse 13**

And he went out again alongside the sea; and all the crowd was coming to him, and he was teaching them.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **TEACHING BY THE SEA**
> - A transitional summary (cf. 1:45; 2:2). The sea of Galilee remains the recurring setting; the imperfects ("was coming," "was teaching") mark ongoing action.

---

### **Verse 14**

And passing by, he saw Levi the *son* of Chalfai (Alphaeus) sitting at the tax booth, and he says to him, @@"Follow me."@@ And rising, he followed him.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **LEVI; CHALFAI — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Λευί** (*Leui*) = Levi (Hebrew **לֵוִי**). **τὸν τοῦ Ἁλφαίου** (*ton tou Halphaiou*) = "the *son* of Chalfai" (Alphaeus; a Semitic name). Levi sits at the **τελώνιον** (*telōnion*, "tax booth") — a toll-collector, a despised profession. "Follow me" (**ἀκολούθει μοι**) parallels the call of the fishermen (1:17). Marked as divine speech (Rule 30 — Yeshua speaking).

---

### **Verse 15**

And it happens *that* he reclines in his house, and many tax collectors and sinners were reclining with Yeshua (Jesus) and his disciples, for there were many, and they were following him.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **EATING WITH "TAX COLLECTORS AND SINNERS"**
> - **τελῶναι καὶ ἁμαρτωλοί** (*telōnai kai hamartōloi*) = "tax collectors and sinners." Table fellowship implied acceptance; reclining (**κατακεῖσθαι**) is the posture of a formal meal. Whose house ("his") is ambiguous — Levi's or Yeshua's; the TT preserves the ambiguity.

---

### **Verse 16**

And the scribes of the Perushim (Pharisees), seeing that he eats with the sinners and tax collectors, were saying to his disciples, "Why does he eat with the tax collectors and sinners?"

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **PERUSHIM — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Φαρισαῖοι** (*Pharisaioi*) = Perushim (Hebrew **פְּרוּשִׁים**, "separated ones"). A movement emphasizing Torah purity; their objection concerns ritual/social boundaries around the table. (Scribes' question; not divine.)

---

### **Verse 17**

And hearing *it*, Yeshua (Jesus) says to them, @@"Those who are strong have no need of a physician, but those having *it* badly. I did not come to call *the* righteous but sinners."@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "NOT THE RIGHTEOUS BUT SINNERS"**
> - **οὐκ ἦλθον καλέσαι δικαίους ἀλλὰ ἁμαρτωλούς** (*ouk ēlthon kalesai dikaious alla hamartōlous*) = "I did not come to call *the* righteous but sinners." The physician proverb frames sinners as the sick who need care. Whether "the righteous" is genuine or ironic (no one is truly righteous) is left open. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL]. Marked as divine speech (Rule 30 — Yeshua speaking).

---

### **Verse 18**

And the disciples of Yochanan (John) and the Perushim (Pharisees) were fasting. And they come and say to him, "Why do the disciples of John and the disciples of the Perushim (Pharisees) fast, but your disciples do not fast?"

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **THE FASTING QUESTION**
> - Both Yochanan (John)'s disciples and the Perushim (Pharisees) practiced voluntary fasts beyond the required Day of Atonement. The question contrasts their practice with that of Yeshua's followers. (Questioners' speech; not divine.)

---

### **Verse 19**

And Yeshua (Jesus) said to them, @@"Can the sons of the bridal-chamber fast while the bridegroom is with them? *As* long *as* they have the bridegroom with them, they cannot fast.@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"SONS OF THE BRIDAL-CHAMBER" — *NUMPHŌN***
> - **οἱ υἱοὶ τοῦ νυμφῶνος** (*hoi huioi tou nymphōnos*) = "the sons of the bridal-chamber" — a Semitic idiom for the bridegroom's wedding companions/guests. The bridegroom imagery evokes the HB marriage metaphor for God and Israel (Hos 2; Isa 62:5). Marked as divine speech (Rule 30 — Yeshua speaking, vv.19–22).

---

### **Verse 20**

But days will come when the bridegroom is taken away from them, and then they will fast in that day.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"TAKEN AWAY" — FORESHADOWING**
> - **ἀπαρθῇ** (*aparthē*) = "is taken away" — a passive that early readers heard as foreshadowing Yeshua's death; the text states only that fasting will then be fitting. The TT renders without expanding the hint.

---

### **Verse 21**

No one sews a patch of unshrunk cloth on an old garment; otherwise the fullness pulls away from it — the new from the old — and a worse tear happens.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **UNSHRUNK CLOTH**
> - **ῥάκος ἀγνάφου** (*rhakos agnaphou*) = "a patch of unshrunk/uncarded cloth," which shrinks and tears the old garment. The image (with v.22) contrasts the incompatibility of the new with the old.

---

### **Verse 22**

And no one puts new wine into old wineskins; otherwise the wine will burst the wineskins, and the wine is destroyed, and the wineskins; but new wine into fresh wineskins."

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **NEW WINE / FRESH WINESKINS**
> - **οἶνον νέον εἰς ἀσκοὺς καινούς** (*oinon neon eis askous kainous*) = "new wine into fresh wineskins." Fermenting new wine expands and bursts brittle old skins. What the "new" is — Yeshua's message, the kingdom's arrival — is left for the reader; the parable states the principle, not the application.

---

### **Verse 23**

And it happened *that* he was passing through the grainfields on the sabbath, and his disciples began to make *their* way, plucking the heads of grain.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **PLUCKING GRAIN ON THE SABBATH**
> - **τίλλοντες τοὺς στάχυας** (*tillontes tous stachyas*) = "plucking the heads of grain." Gleaning by hand was permitted (Deut 23:25); the dispute is whether plucking counts as reaping (forbidden sabbath work). The phrase "to make *their* way" (**ὁδὸν ποιεῖν**) is unusual Greek.

---

### **Verse 24**

And the Perushim (Pharisees) were saying to him, "See, why do they do on the sabbath what is not lawful?"

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **THE SABBATH CHARGE**
> - The Perushim (Pharisees) treat plucking as prohibited labor. The question sets up the David precedent and the two sabbath sayings (vv.25–28). (Pharisees' question; not divine.)

---

### **Verse 25**

And he says to them, @@"Did you never read what David did when he had need and was hungry, he and those with him?@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **THE DAVID PRECEDENT**
> - **οὐδέποτε ἀνέγνωτε** (*oudepote anegnōte*) = "did you never read?" — the argument-from-scripture form. The episode is **1 Samuel 21:1–6**: David, in need, eats the consecrated bread. Marked as divine speech (Rule 30 — Yeshua speaking, vv.25–28).

---

### **Verse 26**

How he entered into the house of God in *the time of* Evyatar (Abiathar) the high priest and ate the loaves of presentation, which it is not lawful to eat except for the priests, and gave also to those being with him?"

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "EVYATAR (ABIATHAR)" — A KNOWN CRUX**
> - **ἐπὶ Ἀβιαθὰρ ἀρχιερέως** (*epi Abiathar archiereōs*) = "in *the time of* Abiathar the high priest." In **1 Samuel 21** the priest is **Ahimelech** (Abiathar's father); Abiathar becomes prominent later. This is a long-recognized discrepancy; some manuscripts (D, W, and others) omit the phrase, easing the difficulty. The TT renders the NA28 text and flags the crux rather than harmonizing. **οἱ ἄρτοι τῆς προθέσεως** (*hoi artoi tēs protheseōs*) = "the loaves of presentation" (the showbread; Lev 24:5–9). Confidence: UNCERTAIN [TEXTUAL].

---

### **Verse 27**

And he was saying to them, @@"The sabbath came into being for the human, and not the human for the sabbath;@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "THE SABBATH FOR THE HUMAN"**
> - **τὸ σάββατον διὰ τὸν ἄνθρωπον ἐγένετο** (*to sabbaton dia ton anthrōpon egeneto*) = "the sabbath came into being for the human." *Anthrōpos* is generic (humanity), not male-specific — the TT renders "the human." The saying grounds the sabbath in human benefit (cf. its creation gift, Gen 2:2–3; Exod 20:8–11). Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL]. Marked as divine speech (Rule 30).

---

### **Verse 28**

so that the Son of Man is lord even of the sabbath."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "SON OF MAN … LORD OF THE SABBATH"**
> - **κύριός … καὶ τοῦ σαββάτου** (*kyrios … kai tou sabbatou*) = "lord even of the sabbath." Here *kyrios* is **not** the divine name (not an OT quotation) — it means "lord/master." Reading with v.27, "Son of Man" may carry its generic sense ("humanity") or its titular sense (the Daniel-7 figure); the inference from v.27 to v.28 works on either, and the TT preserves the openness. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL]. Marked as divine speech (Rule 30).

## CROSS-CHAPTER TRACKING (HB -> Mark)

**1 Samuel 21:1–6 -> Mark 2:25–26 — David and the consecrated bread:**

| Element | 1 Samuel 21 | Mark 2:25–26 |
|---------|-------------|--------------|
| Who eats | David and his men | (cited as precedent) |
| The bread | the bread of the Presence (showbread) | "the loaves of presentation" |
| The priest | **Ahimelech** | "**Abiathar** the high priest" (a known discrepancy) |
| The point | need overrides ritual restriction | the sabbath serves human need |

**Daniel 7:13 -> Mark 2:10, 2:28 — "Son of Man":**

| Element | Daniel 7:13 | Mark 2:10, 28 |
|---------|-------------|----------------|
| Phrase | "one like a son of man" | "the Son of Man" |
| Setting | coming with the clouds, given dominion | authority to forgive; lord of the sabbath |
| Sense | exalted heavenly figure | generic + titular held together |

**Genesis 2:2–3 / Exodus 20:8–11 -> Mark 2:27 — the sabbath:**

The sabbath as a creation gift "for the human" coheres with its institution at creation (Gen 2:2–3) and the Decalogue rationale (Exod 20:8–11) — rest grounded in human benefit, not human subordination to the day.
