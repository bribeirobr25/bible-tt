## READING GUIDE

**Main Text:** Primary translation — readable but faithful to Greek structure. **Notes:** Essential Greek features immediately below each verse.

**How the text is marked:**
- *Words in italics* — added for English grammar (not in the Greek text)
- {a:wind/spirit} — a word the Greek leaves open to two meanings, both kept
- @@"…"@@ — direct speech of God or Yeshua (Jesus)

Notes are tagged by type — **Critical · Lexical · Grammatical · Theological** — each shown with its colour (see the legend at the top of the Notes view).

**This is the opening of the earliest Gospel.** Mark begins with no genealogy and no birth narrative — it opens at full speed with a composite prophetic citation, the appearance of Yochanan the Immerser, the immersion of Yeshua, and a rapid sequence of the first acts of his public work. The narrative's signature word is *euthys* ("immediately"), which drives the pace; the TT preserves it at every occurrence. Mark uses "kingdom of God" (not Matthew's "kingdom of the skies"). Proper names follow TT transliteration: Yeshua (not "Jesus" alone), Yochanan (not John), Yarden (not Jordan).

## CHAPTER OVERVIEW

**What happens:** The Gospel opens by announcing the "beginning of the good news of Yeshua the anointed one." A composite citation (Malachi/Exodus + Isaiah) frames Yochanan the Immerser as the prophesied messenger preparing the way. John appears in the wilderness, clothed like Eliyahu, proclaiming an immersion of change-of-mind toward release of sins; crowds from Judea and Jerusalem come to be immersed in the Jordan. John announces a stronger one coming who will immerse "in holy wind/spirit." Yeshua comes from Nazareth and is immersed; the skies are torn open, the wind/spirit descends like a dove, and a voice declares, "You are my son, the beloved." The wind/spirit immediately drives him into the wilderness for forty days, tested by the Satan, among wild animals, served by messengers. After John is handed over, Yeshua comes proclaiming, "The time has been fulfilled, and the kingdom of God has drawn near." He calls Shimon, Andreas, Ya'aqov, and Yochanan from their fishing. In Kfar Nachum he teaches with authority in the synagogue and silences an unclean spirit; he heals Shimon's mother-in-law and many at the door by evening; he withdraws to pray, then travels proclaiming through the Galilee; finally he cleanses a leper, who spreads the report so widely that Yeshua can no longer enter a town openly.

**Key themes:** The abrupt, fast-paced opening unique to Mark; "good news" (*euangelion*) as the book's keynote; the composite prophetic citation and the wilderness preparation; the threefold theophany at the immersion (skies torn, wind/spirit descends, voice speaks); the divine voice in the *second person* ("You are my son" — contrast Matthew's "This is"); "kingdom of God" rather than "kingdom of the skies"; authority (*exousia*) — over teaching, over unclean spirits, over disease; the messianic silencing motif (Yeshua suppresses the demons' testimony); *euthys* ("immediately") as the engine of the narrative.

**Watch for:** "Son of God" in v.1 is text-critically disputed (bracketed in NA28). The v.2 citation is attributed to "Isaiah" although it opens with material from Malachi 3:1 / Exodus 23:20 — a known feature that generated the variant "in the prophets." The skies are *torn* (*schizomenous*, v.10) — a more violent verb than Matthew's "opened"; the same root returns at the temple curtain (15:38). The voice addresses Yeshua directly ("You are…"), not the crowd. In v.41 the manuscripts split between Yeshua being "moved with compassion" and "moved with anger." Mark's "kingdom of God" should not be harmonized to Matthew's "kingdom of the skies."

**Connection:** The wilderness preparation and the prophetic citation root the Gospel in the HB prophetic tradition (Malachi, Isaiah, Exodus). John's clothing echoes Eliyahu (2 Kings 1:8). The wind/spirit descending recalls Genesis 1:2 (*ruach elohim*). The voice merges Psalm 2:7 (royal sonship) and Isaiah 42:1 (the servant). The forty days in the wilderness echo Israel's forty years and Moshe's and Eliyahu's forty days.

## VERSE-BY-VERSE STUDY

### **Verse 1**

Beginning of the good news of Yeshua (Jesus) the anointed one, son of God.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "SON OF GOD" — TEXTUAL VARIANT**
> - **υἱοῦ θεοῦ** (*huiou theou*) = "of *the* son of God." This phrase is **bracketed in NA28**: it is absent from some major witnesses (א*, Θ, 28) and present in others (א¹, B, D, L, W). The decision is genuinely contested — the words may be original or an early scribal expansion of an opening title. The TT retains the NA28 reading and flags the dispute rather than silently choosing. Confidence: UNCERTAIN [TEXTUAL].
>
> 🟢 **"BEGINNING" — *ARCHĒ***
> - **Ἀρχή** (*Archē*) = "beginning / origin." The same word opens the LXX of Genesis 1:1 (*en archē*) and John 1:1. There is no article and no verb — the line functions as a title/superscription for the whole work (or at least its opening). Whether "beginning" governs only vv.1–13 or the entire Gospel is left open.
>
> 🟢 **"GOOD NEWS" — *EUANGELION***
> - **εὐαγγέλιον** (*euangelion*) = "good news / good announcement." Per locked glossary: "good news," not the later-loaded "gospel." In the first century the word also had imperial associations (announcements of an emperor's accession or victory).
>
> 🟢 **"THE ANOINTED ONE" — *CHRISTOS***
> - **Χριστοῦ** (*Christou*) = "anointed one" (translating Hebrew *mashiach*). Per locked glossary the TT renders the meaning, not the transliteration "Christ." Here it functions almost as a second name for Yeshua but retains its titular force.

---

### **Verse 2**

As it has been written in Yesha'yahu (Isaiah) the prophet, "See, I send my messenger before your face, who will prepare your way;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — COMPOSITE CITATION ATTRIBUTED TO ISAIAH**
> - The citation that follows (vv.2–3) is **composite**: v.2 quotes **Malachi 3:1** (with wording from **Exodus 23:20**), and v.3 quotes **Isaiah 40:3**. Mark attributes the whole to "Isaiah the prophet." This generated a variant: the Byzantine text reads "in the prophets" (ἐν τοῖς προφήταις) to remove the apparent inaccuracy. NA28 reads "in Isaiah" (the harder, better-attested reading). The TT preserves "Isaiah" and notes the composite source. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL].
>
> 🔵 **"MY MESSENGER" — *TON ANGELON MOU***
> - **τὸν ἄγγελόν μου** (*ton angelon mou*) = "my messenger." In Malachi the speaker is YHWH and the messenger prepares YHWH's own way; Mark applies the text so that the "way" prepared is Yeshua's. The pronoun shifts ("my… your") fuse the speakers without the narrator commenting.

---

### **Verse 3**

a voice of one calling in the wilderness: 'Prepare the way of the Lord, make his paths straight,'"

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — ISAIAH 40:3 — KYRIOS / YHWH**
> - **κυρίου** (*kyriou*) renders YHWH in the Isaiah 40:3 source. Per GS Option C: "the Lord" in the main text; the underlying referent is YHWH (noted here). As in Matthew 3:3, the LXX punctuation places "in the wilderness" with "calling" (the preacher is in the wilderness) rather than with "prepare" (the MT: the road is built in the wilderness). The TT follows the Greek. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL].
>
> *The implication — preparing "the way of the Lord (YHWH)" applied to Yeshua's coming — is left for the reader to weigh; the narrator does not state the identification.*

---

### **Verse 4**

Yochanan (John) came, the one immersing in the wilderness and proclaiming an immersion of change of mind toward release of sins.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **YOCHANAN — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Ἰωάννης** (*Iōannēs*) = Yochanan (Hebrew **יוֹחָנָן**, "YHWH is gracious"). Familiar: John. The TT uses the Hebrew form to keep the theophoric element visible.
>
> 🟢 **"IMMERSION OF CHANGE OF MIND" — *BAPTISMA METANOIAS***
> - **βάπτισμα μετανοίας** (*baptisma metanoias*) = "immersion of change of mind." Per locked glossary: *baptisma* = immersion (not "baptism"); *metanoia* = change of mind (not "repentance"). The genitive defines the character of the immersion.
>
> 🟡 **"RELEASE OF SINS" — *APHESIN HAMARTIŌN***
> - **εἰς ἄφεσιν ἁμαρτιῶν** (*eis aphesin hamartiōn*) = "toward release of sins." *Aphesis* = release / sending-away / remission (the concrete sense underlies the conventional "forgiveness"). The TT keeps the concrete "release" and notes the conventional rendering. Confidence: PROBABLE [TEXTUAL]. *(Flagged for Hellenist confirmation.)*

---

### **Verse 5**

And all the Yehudah (Judean) region and all the people of Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) were going out to him, and they were being immersed by him in the Yarden (Jordan) river, confessing their sins.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **HYPERBOLE / COLLECTIVE SUBJECT**
> - "All the Judean region and all the people of Jerusalem" — emphatic scope; the imperfect verbs ("were going out," "were being immersed") describe sustained, repeated activity rather than a single event.

---

### **Verse 6**

And Yochanan (John) was clothed in camel's hair and a leather belt around his waist, and eating locusts and wild honey.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **ELIYAHU ECHO — CLOTHING**
> - **2 Kings 1:8** describes Eliyahu as "a hairy man with a leather belt around his waist." John's clothing marks him as the Eliyahu figure (cf. Malachi 3:23 [4:5]). Locusts (Lev 11:22, permitted food) and wild honey mark a wilderness diet outside the settled economy.

---

### **Verse 7**

And he was proclaiming, saying, "There comes the one stronger than I after me, of whom I am not worthy to stoop down *and* loosen the strap of his sandals.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"STOOP DOWN" — *KYPSAS***
> - **κύψας** (*kypsas*) = "having stooped down" — a detail unique to Mark (Matthew omits the stooping). Loosening/carrying a master's sandals was a slave's task; John ranks himself below even that.

---

### **Verse 8**

I immersed you in water, but he will immerse you in *the* holy {a:wind/spirit}."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — TWO IMMERSIONS CONTRASTED**
> - **ἐγὼ … ὕδατι, αὐτὸς δὲ … πνεύματι ἁγίῳ** = "I … in water, but he … in holy wind/spirit." Mark's form is shorter than Matthew/Luke (no "and fire"). The contrast is between John's water-immersion and the coming one's wind/spirit-immersion. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL].
>
> 🟢 **"HOLY WIND/SPIRIT" — *PNEUMATI HAGIŌ***
> - **πνεύματι ἁγίῳ** (*pneumati hagiō*) = "holy wind/spirit." Per locked glossary *pneuma* = wind/spirit (slash, Rule 2), aligning with HB *ruach*. Anarthrous in Greek; the TT adds *the* in italics for English readability.

---

### **Verse 9**

And it happened in those days *that* Yeshua (Jesus) came from Natzeret (Nazareth) of the Galil (Galilee) and was immersed in the Yarden (Jordan) by Yochanan (John).

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **NATZERET, GALIL — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Ναζαρέτ** (*Nazaret*) = Natzeret (Nazareth); **Γαλιλαία** (*Galilaia*) = Galil (Galilee). Mark gives no birth narrative — Yeshua simply arrives from Nazareth, an obscure Galilean village.
>
> 🔵 **"WAS IMMERSED BY YOCHANAN"**
> - **ἐβαπτίσθη … ὑπὸ Ἰωάννου** (*ebaptisthē … hypo Iōannou*) = passive, "was immersed by John." Mark states the fact plainly, without Matthew's dialogue about propriety (Matt 3:14–15) — the question of why Yeshua is immersed is not raised here.

---

### **Verse 10**

And immediately, coming up out of the water, he saw the skies being torn open and the {a:wind/spirit} descending upon him like a dove.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — SKIES *TORN* OPEN — *SCHIZOMENOUS***
> - **σχιζομένους τοὺς οὐρανούς** (*schizomenous tous ouranous*) = "the skies being torn/split." Mark's verb (*schizō*) is more violent than Matthew's "opened" (*ēneōchthēsan*, Matt 3:16). The same root returns at the death scene — the temple curtain is "torn" (*eschisthē*, 15:38) — framing the Gospel between two tearings. The TT keeps "torn." Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL].
>
> 🟢 **"WIND/SPIRIT … LIKE A DOVE"**
> - **τὸ πνεῦμα ὡς περιστερὰν** (*to pneuma hōs peristeran*) = "the wind/spirit … like a dove." The simile attaches to the manner of descending; the text does not say a dove was seen. Slash per locked glossary; echoes *ruach elohim* of Genesis 1:2.

---

### **Verse 11**

And a voice came out of the skies: @@"You are my son, the beloved; in you I was well pleased."@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — DIVINE VOICE, SECOND PERSON**
> - **σὺ εἶ ὁ υἱός μου** (*sy ei ho huios mou*) = "**You** are my son" — addressed directly to Yeshua (contrast Matthew 3:17's third-person "**This** is my son," addressed to bystanders). The TT preserves Mark's second-person address; no harmonizing. Marked as divine speech (Rule 30). Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL].
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — COMPOSITE OT ALLUSION**
> - The declaration merges **Psalm 2:7** ("You are my son" — royal/Davidic enthronement) and **Isaiah 42:1** ("my chosen, in whom my being delights" — the first Servant Song); **ἀγαπητός** (*agapētos*, "beloved") may also echo **Genesis 22:2** (LXX, "your son, the beloved" — Yitschaq at the binding). Royal and servant identities stand together; the TT does not adjudicate. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL].

---

### **Verse 12**

And immediately the {a:wind/spirit} drives him out into the wilderness.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "DRIVES OUT" — *EKBALLEI***
> - **ἐκβάλλει** (*ekballei*) = "drives/throws out" — a forceful verb (the same used for casting out demons, v.34,39). Matthew/Luke soften it ("was led up / was led"). Mark's wind/spirit *expels* Yeshua into the wilderness. Historical present ("drives") preserved. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL].

---

### **Verse 13**

And he was in the wilderness forty days, being tested by the Satan, and he was with the wild animals, and the messengers were serving him.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"THE SATAN" — *TOU SATANA***
> - **τοῦ σατανᾶ** (*tou satana*) = "the Satan" (transliteration of Hebrew **הַשָּׂטָן**, *ha-satan*, "the adversary/accuser"). The TT keeps the text's term; *satan* = adversary. Mark gives no dialogue of the testing (contrast Matt 4 / Luke 4) — only the bare fact.
>
> 🔵 **"WITH THE WILD ANIMALS" — *META TŌN THĒRIŌN***
> - **μετὰ τῶν θηρίων** (*meta tōn thēriōn*) = "with the wild animals" — a detail unique to Mark. It may evoke Eden/peace-with-creation imagery or wilderness danger; the text does not specify. "The messengers were serving him" (**οἱ ἄγγελοι διηκόνουν**) — *angeloi* = messengers; the imperfect suggests ongoing service.

### **Verse 14**

And after Yochanan (John) was handed over, Yeshua (Jesus) came into the Galil (Galilee), proclaiming the good news of God

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"WAS HANDED OVER" — *PARADOTHĒNAI***
> - **παραδοθῆναι** (*paradothēnai*) = "to be handed over / delivered up." The same verb later describes Yeshua's own arrest — a verbal foreshadowing. John's removal marks the start of Yeshua's public proclamation.

### **Verse 15**

and saying, @@"The time has been fulfilled, and the kingdom of God has drawn near; change *your* minds and trust in the good news."@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "KINGDOM OF GOD" (NOT "OF THE SKIES")**
> - **ἡ βασιλεία τοῦ θεοῦ** (*hē basileia tou theou*) = "the kingdom of God." Mark (and Luke) use this phrase; Matthew's distinctive "kingdom of the skies" does not appear in Mark. The TT renders each Gospel's own wording — no harmonizing. **ἤγγικεν** (*ēngiken*, perfect) = "has drawn near": arrived-and-near; whether "is arriving" or "has arrived" is left open. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL].
>
> 🟢 **"TRUST IN THE GOOD NEWS" — *PISTEUETE EN***
> - **πιστεύετε ἐν τῷ εὐαγγελίῳ** (*pisteuete en tō euangeliō*) = "trust in the good news." *Pisteuō* = trust/believe; the construction with *en* ("in") is unusual for this verb and may reflect the keynote summary character of the saying.

### **Verse 16**

And passing alongside the sea of the Galilee, he saw Shimon (Simon) and Andreas (Andrew) the brother of Simon casting *a net* in the sea, for they were fishers.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **SHIMON, ANDREAS — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Σίμων** (*Simōn*) = Shimon (Simon); **Ἀνδρέας** (*Andreas*) = Andrew (a Greek name). **ἀμφιβάλλοντας** (*amphiballontas*) = "casting *a net*" (the word for the net is implied; added in italics).

### **Verse 17**

And Yeshua (Jesus) said to them, @@"Come after me, and I will make you become fishers of people."@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"FISHERS OF PEOPLE" — *HALIEIS ANTHRŌPŌN***
> - **ἁλιεῖς ἀνθρώπων** (*halieis anthrōpōn*) = "fishers of humans/people." *Anthrōpōn* is generic (humanity), not male-specific; the TT renders "people." The call plays on their trade. Marked as divine speech (Rule 30 — Yeshua speaking).

### **Verse 18**

And immediately, leaving the nets, they followed him.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"IMMEDIATELY" — *EUTHYS***
> - **εὐθύς** (*euthys*) = "immediately" — Mark's signature adverb (10+ times in this chapter). The TT preserves it at every occurrence; the rapid response underscores the call's authority.

### **Verse 19**

And going on a little, he saw Ya'aqov (James) the *son* of Zavdai (Zebedee) and Yochanan (John) his brother, also *themselves* in the boat mending the nets,

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **YA'AQOV, ZAVDAI — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Ἰάκωβος** (*Iakōbos*) = Ya'aqov — the same name as the patriarch Jacob; familiar English "James" (via Late Latin *Iacomus*). **Ζεβεδαῖος** (*Zebedaios*) = Zavdai (Zebedee). "The *son* of" is implied by the genitive and added in italics.

### **Verse 20**

and immediately he called them. And leaving their father Zavdai (Zebedee) in the boat with the hired men, they went away after him.

> **Notes**

> 🔵 **"WITH THE HIRED MEN" — *META TŌN MISTHŌTŌN***
> - **μετὰ τῶν μισθωτῶν** (*meta tōn misthōtōn*) = "with the hired men" — a detail suggesting Zavdai's fishing business was substantial enough to employ laborers; the sons leave an established livelihood.

### **Verse 21**

And they go into Kfar Nachum (Capernaum); and immediately on the sabbath, entering into the synagogue, he was teaching.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **KFAR NACHUM — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Καφαρναούμ** (*Kapharnaoum*) = Kfar Nachum (Hebrew "village of Nachum/comfort"); familiar Capernaum. A fishing town on the north shore of the sea of Galilee that becomes Yeshua's base of operations in Mark.

### **Verse 22**

And they were astounded at his teaching, for he was teaching them as one having authority and not as the scribes.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "AUTHORITY" — *EXOUSIA***
> - **ἐξουσία** (*exousia*) = "authority / power-to-act." Yeshua teaches as one possessing inherent authority, contrasted with the scribes (**γραμματεῖς**), who taught by citing tradition. *Exousia* is a thread through the chapter — over teaching (v.22), over unclean spirits (v.27), implicitly over disease. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL].

### **Verse 23**

And immediately there was in their synagogue a man in an unclean spirit, and he cried out,

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"UNCLEAN SPIRIT" — *PNEUMA AKATHARTON* (NO SLASH)**
> - **πνεῦμα ἀκάθαρτον** (*pneuma akatharton*) = "unclean spirit." Here *pneuma* is **not** rendered with the wind/spirit slash: "unclean wind" is not a live sense in the possession context. The slash is reserved for the divine/cosmic *pneuma* (vv.8,10,12). "In an unclean spirit" (**ἐν πνεύματι**) describes the man as within its power.

---

### **Verse 24**

saying, "What to us and to you, Yeshua (Jesus) Natzeri (of Nazareth)? Did you come to destroy us? I know who you are — the holy one of God."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE SPIRIT'S TESTIMONY (NOT DIVINE SPEECH)**
> - The unclean spirit names Yeshua "**the holy one of God**" (**ὁ ἅγιος τοῦ θεοῦ**). This is a non-divine speaker testifying *about* Yeshua; it is **not** marked as divine speech (Rule 30 — the demon is not God/Yeshua/the Spirit). "What to us and to you" (**τί ἡμῖν καὶ σοί**) is a Semitic idiom of dissociation ("what do we have in common?"). The plural "us" may speak for the class of unclean spirits.

---

### **Verse 25**

And Yeshua (Jesus) rebuked him, saying, @@"Be muzzled, and come out of him!"@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"BE MUZZLED" — *PHIMŌTHĒTI***
> - **φιμώθητι** (*phimōthēti*) = "be muzzled / be silenced" (literally as one muzzles an animal). The command both silences the testimony (the messianic-silence motif) and expels the spirit. Marked as divine speech (Rule 30 — Yeshua speaking).

---

### **Verse 26**

And the unclean spirit, convulsing him and crying out with a great voice, came out of him.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"CONVULSING" — *SPARAXAN***
> - **σπαράξαν** (*sparaxan*) = "convulsing / shaking violently." Mark records the physical violence of the exit — a vivid, concrete detail characteristic of his narration.

---

### **Verse 27**

And all were amazed, so that they questioned among themselves, saying, "What is this? A new teaching with authority! And he commands the unclean spirits, and they obey him."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"NEW TEACHING WITH AUTHORITY"**
> - **διδαχὴ καινὴ κατ᾽ ἐξουσίαν** (*didachē kainē kat' exousian*) = "a new teaching according to authority." The crowd links the exorcism back to the teaching of v.22 — the same *exousia* operates in word and act. (Crowd speech; not divine.)

---

### **Verse 28**

And immediately his report went out everywhere into the whole surrounding region of the Galilee.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"REPORT" — *AKOĒ***
> - **ἀκοή** (*akoē*) = "report / hearing / what is heard." The spreading fame motif begins here and culminates in v.45, where it constrains Yeshua's movements.

---

### **Verse 29**

And immediately, coming out of the synagogue, they came into the house of Shimon (Simon) and Andreas (Andrew) with Ya'aqov (James) and Yochanan (John).

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **THE FOUR + THE HOUSE**
> - The first four called (vv.16–20) are now together; Shimon's house in Kfar Nachum becomes a recurring setting. The narrative moves from public synagogue to private house without pause (*euthys*).

---

### **Verse 30**

Now the mother-in-law of Shimon (Simon) was lying down, fevered, and immediately they speak to him about her.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"THEY SPEAK" — HISTORICAL PRESENT**
> - **λέγουσιν** (*legousin*) = present tense narrating past action ("they speak"). Mark's frequent historical presents create immediacy; the TT preserves the present where Mark uses it. Shimon's having a mother-in-law indicates he is married.

---

### **Verse 31**

And approaching, he raised her up, grasping the hand; and the fever left her, and she was serving them.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"RAISED HER UP" — *ĒGEIREN***
> - **ἤγειρεν** (*ēgeiren*) = "raised up" — the same verb used for resurrection elsewhere. The healing is by touch (grasping the hand). "She was serving them" (**διηκόνει**, imperfect) — the same verb (*diakoneō*) used of the messengers in v.13.

---

### **Verse 32**

Now evening having come, when the sun set, they were bringing to him all those having *it* badly and the demon-possessed;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"WHEN THE SUN SET" — SABBATH'S END**
> - The double time-marker ("evening… when the sun set") notes that the sabbath (v.21) had ended — carrying the sick was now permissible. "Having *it* badly" (**κακῶς ἔχοντας**) = idiom for "being ill"; *it* is added in italics.

---

### **Verse 33**

and the whole city was gathered together at the door.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **HYPERBOLE — "THE WHOLE CITY"**
> - **ὅλη ἡ πόλις** (*holē hē polis*) = "the whole city" — emphatic. The crowd at the door of Shimon's house frames the scope of the demand on Yeshua.

---

### **Verse 34**

And he healed many having *it* badly with various diseases, and he cast out many demons, and he was not permitting the demons to speak, because they knew him.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — MESSIANIC SILENCE**
> - **οὐκ ἤφιεν λαλεῖν τὰ δαιμόνια** (*ouk ēphien lalein ta daimonia*) = "he was not permitting the demons to speak." The "messianic secret" motif (a major feature of Mark): Yeshua suppresses supernatural testimony to his identity. "Many" (not "all") healed reflects Mark's precise narration. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL].

---

### **Verse 35**

And very early, *while* still night, rising up, he went out and went away into a deserted place, and there he was praying.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"DESERTED PLACE" — *ERĒMOS TOPOS***
> - **ἔρημον τόπον** (*erēmon topon*) = "deserted/wilderness place." The same word-field as the wilderness of vv.4,12–13. Yeshua withdraws to pray (**προσηύχετο**, imperfect — sustained prayer) before the crowds gather.

---

### **Verse 36**

And Shimon (Simon) and those with him hunted him down,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"HUNTED HIM DOWN" — *KATEDIŌXEN***
> - **κατεδίωξεν** (*katediōxen*) = "hunted down / pursued closely" — a strong verb, almost adversarial. Shimon and the others pursue Yeshua to bring him back to the demand of the crowd.

---

### **Verse 37**

and they found him and say to him, "Everyone is seeking you."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **CROWD-PRESSURE (NOT DIVINE SPEECH)**
> - "Everyone is seeking you" — the disciples voice the crowd's demand. Yeshua's reply (v.38) redirects away from it. (Disciples' speech; not marked.)

---

### **Verse 38**

And he says to them, @@"Let us go elsewhere, into the neighboring towns, so that I may proclaim there also; for this I came out."@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"FOR THIS I CAME OUT" — *EIS TOUTO EXĒLTHON***
> - **εἰς τοῦτο γὰρ ἐξῆλθον** (*eis touto gar exēlthon*) = "for this I came out." "Came out" may mean "left Capernaum *this morning*" or carry a larger sense of mission/origin; the TT preserves the ambiguity. Marked as divine speech (Rule 30 — Yeshua speaking).

---

### **Verse 39**

And he came proclaiming into their synagogues in the whole Galilee and casting out the demons.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **SUMMARY STATEMENT**
> - A compressed summary of itinerant ministry — proclamation + exorcism — extending the Capernaum pattern across the Galilee. *Their* synagogues = the Galilean communities'.

---

### **Verse 40**

And there comes to him a leper, calling upon him *and kneeling* and saying to him, "If you are willing, you are able to cleanse me."

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"LEPER" — *LEPROS***
> - **λεπρός** (*lepros*) = "leper / scaly-skinned one." Greek *lepra* (= HB **צָרַעַת**, *tsara'at*) is a **broad category of skin/surface affliction**, not modern Hansen's disease. Such a person was ritually unclean and socially excluded (Lev 13–14). "*And kneeling*" (**γονυπετῶν**) is textually uncertain (bracketed) — added in italics. Confidence: PROBABLE [TEXTUAL].

---

### **Verse 41**

And moved with compassion, stretching out his hand, he touched *him* and says to him, @@"I am willing; be cleansed."@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "COMPASSION" vs "ANGER" — TEXTUAL VARIANT**
> - **σπλαγχνισθείς** (*splanchnistheis*) = "moved with compassion / from the inward parts" (NA28). A significant minority reading — **ὀργισθείς** (*orgistheis*, "moved with anger," Codex Bezae D + Old Latin) — is the *lectio difficilior* (the harder reading scribes would more likely soften than create), so some editors prefer it. The TT follows NA28 ("compassion") and flags the dispute. Confidence: UNCERTAIN [TEXTUAL].
>
> 🟢 **"HE TOUCHED" — *HĒPSATO***
> - **ἥψατο** (*hēpsato*) = "he touched." Touching a leper would ordinarily transmit ritual uncleanness; here the flow reverses — Yeshua's touch cleanses rather than defiles. Marked as divine speech (Rule 30 — Yeshua speaking).

---

### **Verse 42**

And immediately the leprosy left him, and he was cleansed.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"IMMEDIATELY … CLEANSED"**
> - **εὐθύς** again (the signature adverb) + **ἐκαθαρίσθη** (*ekatharisthē*, "was cleansed"). The healing is instant and complete; "cleansed" (not merely "cured") frames it in the ritual-purity terms of Leviticus.

---

### **Verse 43**

And sternly warning him, immediately he sent him out

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"STERNLY WARNING" — *EMBRIMĒSAMENOS***
> - **ἐμβριμησάμενος** (*embrimēsamenos*) = "sternly warning / snorting with strong feeling" — a forceful verb. With **ἐξέβαλεν** (*exebalen*, "sent/drove out" — the exorcism verb again), Mark's wording is unusually intense for a healing aftermath; the text does not explain the severity.

---

### **Verse 44**

and says to him, @@"See *that* you say nothing to anyone, but go, show yourself to the priest and offer for your cleansing what Moshe (Moses) commanded, for a testimony to them."@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **SILENCE COMMAND + TORAH OBSERVANCE**
> - The silence command (the secrecy motif again) is paired with sending the man to the priest per **Leviticus 14** (the cleansing ritual). **Μωϋσῆς** (*Mōysēs*) = Moshe (Moses). "For a testimony to them" (**εἰς μαρτύριον αὐτοῖς**) is ambiguous — evidence to the priests, or witness against them; the TT preserves the openness. Marked as divine speech (Rule 30 — Yeshua speaking).

---

### **Verse 45**

But he, going out, began to proclaim *it* much and to spread the word, so that *Yeshua* was no longer able to enter openly into a city, but was outside in deserted places; and they were coming to him from everywhere.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **THE SILENCE BROKEN — REVERSAL**
> - The cleansed man "proclaims" (**κηρύσσειν** — the same verb used of John and Yeshua) and spreads the word, ignoring the command. The result reverses positions: Yeshua, who moved freely, is now driven to the "deserted places" (the leper's former domain), while crowds come from everywhere. *It* and *Yeshua* are added in italics for English clarity.

## CROSS-CHAPTER TRACKING (HB -> Mark)

**Malachi 3:1 + Exodus 23:20 + Isaiah 40:3 -> Mark 1:2–3 — the composite citation:**

| Element | Source | Mark 1:2–3 |
|---------|--------|------------|
| "I send my messenger before your face" | Malachi 3:1 / Exodus 23:20 | v.2 |
| "prepare the way… a voice calling in the wilderness" | Isaiah 40:3 | v.3 |
| Attribution | (composite) | "in Isaiah the prophet" |
| Divine name | YHWH (Isa 40:3) | *kyrios* — "the Lord" (Option C) |

Mark fuses three texts and attributes the whole to Isaiah (the most prominent source). The Byzantine "in the prophets" is a later harmonizing correction.

**Psalm 2:7 + Isaiah 42:1 -> Mark 1:11 — "You are my son, the beloved":**

| Element | Psalm 2:7 | Isaiah 42:1 | Mark 1:11 |
|---------|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| Address | "You are my son" | "my chosen… my being delights" | "You are my son, the beloved" |
| Relationship | Royal sonship (Davidic king) | Servant calling | Composite: son + beloved + delight |
| Person | — | — | **Second person** ("You"), spoken to Yeshua |

Mark's voice addresses Yeshua directly ("You are"), unlike Matthew's third-person "This is." **ἀγαπητός** ("beloved") may also echo Genesis 22:2 (LXX, "your son, the beloved" — Yitschaq at the binding).

**Genesis 1:2 -> Mark 1:10 — wind/spirit at a threshold:**

| Element | Gen 1:2 | Mark 1:10 |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Term | רוּחַ אֱלֹהִים (*ruach elohim*) | τὸ πνεῦμα (*to pneuma*) |
| Action | hovering over the waters | descending (like a dove) as Yeshua comes up from the water |
| Setting | before creation | before the public mission |

The wind/spirit appears at a threshold of new beginning, over water, in both texts.
