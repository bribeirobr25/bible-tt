## Yeshua (Jesus)
**See:** matthew/PEOPLE.md
**In Mark:** The central figure from the first line — "the good news of Yeshua the anointed one, son of God" (1:1). Immersed by Yochanan, with the wind/spirit descending and the voice "You are my son, the beloved" (1:9–11); driven into the wilderness, tested by the Satan (1:12–13). Proclaims "the kingdom of God has drawn near" (1:15), calls the first disciples (1:16–20), teaches and exorcises with authority (1:21–28), heals and withdraws to pray (1:35). Claims authority to forgive sins (2:1–12), eats with sinners (2:13–17), and declares the Son of Man "lord even of the sabbath" (2:28). Heals on the sabbath provoking the first death-plot (3:1–6), appoints the Twelve (3:13–19), answers the Beelzevul charge (3:22–30), and redefines family around the will of God (3:31–35). The canonical bio fields (birth, parents, profession, death by crucifixion) live in matthew/PEOPLE.md; this entry adds the Mark-specific narrative role.

## Yochanan the Immerser (John the Baptist)
**See:** matthew/PEOPLE.md
**In Mark:** The wilderness herald who opens the Gospel — the prophesied "messenger" preparing the way (1:2–3), immersing in the Yarden (Jordan) and proclaiming "an immersion of change of mind toward release of sins" (1:4–8). Clothed like Eliyahu (1:6), he announces a stronger one coming who will immerse "in holy wind/spirit." He immerses Yeshua (1:9); his being "handed over" marks the start of Yeshua's Galilean proclamation (1:14). His disciples' fasting prompts a controversy (2:18). The canonical bio fields (lineage, lifestyle, death under Antipas) live in matthew/PEOPLE.md; this entry adds the Mark-specific role.

## Miryam (Mary)
**See:** matthew/PEOPLE.md
**In Mark:** Appears in Mark 1–3 only as "his mother," unnamed, in the family scene of 3:31–35 — she and Yeshua's brothers stand "outside" and send for him, and Yeshua redefines kinship around "whoever does the will of God." Mark names her "Miryam (Mary)" only later (6:3). The canonical bio fields live in matthew/PEOPLE.md; this entry adds the Mark-specific role.

## Shimon Kefa (Simon Peter)
**Meaning:** Shimon (Hebrew שִׁמְעוֹן, "he has heard"); Kefa (Aramaic כֵּיפָא, "rock"), Greek Petros
**Origin:** Galilean fisherman of Kfar Nachum (Capernaum)
**Familiar name:** Simon Peter
**Profession:** Fisherman (1:16); later leader among the Twelve
**Hometown:** Kfar Nachum (Capernaum); family origin Bethsaida (cf. John 1:44)
**First mention:** Mark 1:16
**Mentioned in:** Mark 1:16–18, 29–31, 36; 3:16; and throughout the Gospel
**Key events in Mark 1–3:** Called from his nets with his brother Andreas (1:16–18); his house in Kfar Nachum becomes Yeshua's base, and his fevered mother-in-law is healed (1:29–31); he leads those who seek out the praying Yeshua (1:36); named first among the Twelve and renamed Kefa/Peter (3:16).
**Spouse(s):** married (a mother-in-law is named, 1:30; cf. 1 Cor 9:5)
**Archaeological evidence:** A first-century house venerated as Peter's at Capernaum (beneath a later octagonal church) is a traditional identification, not proven.
**Extra-biblical mentions:** Paul's letters (1 Cor 1:12; 9:5; 15:5; Gal 1:18; 2:7–14); later tradition of martyrdom at Rome.
**Historicity status:** PROBABLE
**Books appearing in:** Mark; Matthew; Luke; John; Acts; 1–2 Peter; 1 Corinthians; Galatians
**Character arc:** In Mark, the first called and first named of the Twelve; his home and family anchor the early Galilean ministry. His fuller arc (confession, denial, restoration) develops later in the Gospel.

**Note:** The renaming "Kefa/Peter" ("rock") is recorded at 3:16 without the explanation Matthew gives (Matt 16:18). The TT keeps the Aramaic *Kefa* alongside the familiar "Peter."

## Andreas (Andrew)

**Familiar name:** Andrew
**Profession:** Fisherman (1:16)
**Hometown:** Kfar Nachum (Capernaum); family origin Bethsaida (cf. John 1:44)
**First mention:** Mark 1:16
**Mentioned in:** Mark 1:16–18, 29; 3:18
**Key events in Mark 1–3:** Called with his brother Shimon (Simon) from casting nets (1:16–18); present at the house in Kfar Nachum (1:29); named among the Twelve (3:18).
**Historicity status:** PROBABLE
**Books appearing in:** Mark; Matthew; Luke; John; Acts
**Character arc:** One of the first four called; in Mark he appears only in the early Galilean scenes and the list of the Twelve.

## Ya'aqov son of Zavdai (James son of Zebedee)
**Meaning:** Ya'aqov (Hebrew יַעֲקֹב, the patriarch's name; Greek Ἰάκωβος), familiar "James"
**Origin:** Galilean fisherman, son of Zavdai (Zebedee), brother of Yochanan (John)
**Familiar name:** James
**Profession:** Fisherman (1:19–20)
**First mention:** Mark 1:19
**Mentioned in:** Mark 1:19–20, 29; 3:17
**Key events in Mark 1–3:** Called with his brother from the boat, mending nets, leaving their father Zavdai (Zebedee) with the hired men (1:19–20); present at Shimon (Simon)'s house (1:29); named among the Twelve and, with his brother, surnamed Boanerges, "sons of thunder" (3:17).
**Historicity status:** PROBABLE
**Books appearing in:** Mark; Matthew; Luke; Acts
**Character arc:** With Shimon Kefa (Simon Peter) and his brother, part of the inner circle later in the Gospel; the first apostle martyred (Acts 12:2). Distinct from Ya'aqov son of Chalfai and from Ya'aqov the brother of Yeshua.

## Levi
**Meaning:** Hebrew לֵוִי (*Levi*), the tribal name; here a personal name
**Origin:** Toll/tax collector at Kfar Nachum (Capernaum), son of Chalfai (Alphaeus)
**Familiar name:** Levi
**Profession:** Tax/toll collector (*telōnēs*) (2:14)
**First mention:** Mark 2:14
**Mentioned in:** Mark 2:14–15
**Key events in Mark 1–3:** Called from the tax booth with "Follow me," and rises to follow (2:14); the meal at his (or Yeshua's) house with tax collectors and sinners follows (2:15–17).
**Historicity status:** PROBABLE
**Books appearing in:** Mark; Luke (Levi); Matthew (where the called tax collector is named Matthew, 9:9)
**Character arc:** The called outsider — a participant in the imperial-Herodian toll system summoned directly into discipleship.

**Note:** Mark's "Levi the son of Chalfai (Alphaeus)" (2:14) is traditionally identified with the apostle "Mattai (Matthew)," whom Matthew 9:9 names as the called tax collector; the identification is traditional, not stated by Mark. A "Ya'aqov son of Chalfai" also appears among the Twelve (3:18) — whether a brother is unstated.

## Yehudah Ish-Keriot (Judas Iscariot)
**Meaning:** Yehudah (Hebrew יְהוּדָה, "praise"); "Ish-Keriot" likely *ish Keriot*, "man of Keriot" (a Judean town)
**Familiar name:** Judas Iscariot
**First mention:** Mark 3:19
**Mentioned in:** Mark 3:19
**Key events in Mark 1–3:** Named last among the Twelve, "who also handed him over" (3:19).
**Historicity status:** PROBABLE
**Books appearing in:** Mark; Matthew; Luke; John; Acts
**Character arc:** Introduced already with the note of betrayal; his act ("handed over," the same verb used of Yochanan and of Yeshua's arrest) is foreshadowed at first mention.
**Note:** "Iscariot" most likely identifies origin (Keriot in Judah), making him possibly the only non-Galilean among the Twelve; other derivations (from *sicarius*, "dagger-man") are proposed but less likely.
