**Main Text:** Primary translation — readable but faithful to Greek structure. **Notes:** Essential Greek features immediately below each verse.

**How the text is marked:**
- *Words in italics* — added for English grammar (not in the Greek text)
- {a:wind/spirit} — a word the Greek leaves open to two meanings, both kept
- @@"…"@@ — direct speech of God or Yeshua (Jesus)

Notes are tagged by type — **Critical · Lexical · Grammatical · Theological** — each shown with its colour (see the legend at the top of the Notes view).

**This chapter turns conflict into formation.** The sabbath healing of the withered hand triggers the first death-plot (3:6). Yeshua withdraws to the sea amid huge crowds, silences the unclean spirits who name him, appoints the Twelve, and answers the charge that he casts out demons by Beelzevul — including the saying about an unforgivable blasphemy "against the holy wind/spirit." He redefines family around doing the will of God. Mark uses "kingdom" and "house" imagery; the divine speech of Yeshua is marked throughout.

## CHAPTER OVERVIEW

**What happens:** In the synagogue Yeshua heals a man with a withered hand on the sabbath, first asking, "Is it lawful on the sabbath to do good or to do harm, to save a life or to kill?" The Perushim (Pharisees) join with the Herodians to plot his destruction. He withdraws to the sea, where great crowds come from all directions; he heals many, and the unclean spirits cry, "You are the Son of God," but he orders them to be silent. On a mountain he appoints twelve to be with him and to be sent out — Shimon (renamed Kefa), the sons of Zavdai (named "sons of thunder"), and the rest, ending with Yehudah Ish-Keriot. When his own family thinks he is beside himself, and scribes from Jerusalem say he casts out demons by Beelzevul, he answers in parables: "How can the Satan cast out the Satan?" A divided kingdom or house cannot stand; the strong one must first be bound. He warns that blasphemy against the holy wind/spirit has no forgiveness. Told his mother and brothers are outside, he says, "Whoever does the will of God, this one is my brother and sister and mother."

**Key themes:** Sabbath as the arena of "doing good"; the first plot to kill (3:6); the messianic secret extended to the demons' confession; the constitution of the Twelve as a new core; the Beelzevul controversy and "a kingdom/house divided"; binding the strong one; the warning about blasphemy against the holy wind/spirit; family redefined around the will of God.

**Watch for:** The unclean spirits' "You are the Son of God" (3:11) is **not** marked as divine speech — they are not God or Yeshua. The list of the Twelve carries TT transliterations (Kefa, Bar-Talmai, Mattai, Toma, etc.); "Boanerges" is glossed in-text as "sons of thunder," as Mark himself glosses it. "Blasphemy against the holy wind/spirit" (3:29) keeps the *pneuma* slash; the "eternal sin" is defined by the context (v.30: they called the holy spirit's work an unclean spirit).

**Connection:** "Doing good" and "saving a life" on the sabbath engage the HB sabbath laws (Exod 20; Deut 5) and the principle that the sabbath preserves life. Appointing twelve evokes the twelve tribes of Israel. The "strong one" bound recalls Isaiah 49:24–25. Redefining family around God's will reframes HB kinship and covenant.

## VERSE-BY-VERSE STUDY

### **Verse 1**

And he entered again into the synagogue, and there was a man there having the withered hand.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"WITHERED HAND" — *EXĒRAMMENĒN***
> - **ἐξηραμμένην … τὴν χεῖρα** (*exērammenēn … tēn cheira*) = "the withered/dried-up hand." The perfect participle implies a settled, chronic condition. The definite article ("the hand") treats the man's affliction as known.

---

### **Verse 2**

And they were watching him closely, *to see* whether he will heal him on the sabbath, so that they might accuse him.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"WATCHING CLOSELY" — *PARETĒROUN***
> - **παρετήρουν** (*paretēroun*) = "were watching closely / lying in wait." The imperfect signals sustained, hostile surveillance; the goal is an accusation (**κατηγορήσωσιν**), not understanding.

---

### **Verse 3**

And he says to the man having the withered hand, @@"Rise into the middle."@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"INTO THE MIDDLE" — PUBLIC ACT**
> - **ἔγειρε εἰς τὸ μέσον** (*egeire eis to meson*) = "rise into the middle." Yeshua makes the healing deliberately public, confronting the surveillance directly. Marked as divine speech (Rule 30 — Yeshua speaking).

---

### **Verse 4**

And he says to them, @@"Is it lawful on the sabbath to do good or to do harm, to save a life or to kill?"@@ But they were silent.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "TO DO GOOD … OR TO KILL?"**
> - **ἔξεστιν τοῖς σάββασιν ἀγαθὸν ποιῆσαι ἢ κακοποιῆσαι, ψυχὴν σῶσαι ἢ ἀποκτεῖναι** = "Is it lawful on the sabbath to do good or to do harm, to save a life or to kill?" The question reframes the sabbath around preserving life (a recognized principle: saving life overrides the sabbath). The stark pairing exposes the watchers, who intend harm (v.6) on the very day they would forbid healing. "They were silent" (**ἐσιώπων**). Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL]. Marked as divine speech (Rule 30).

---

### **Verse 5**

And looking around at them with anger, grieved at the hardness of their heart, he says to the man, @@"Stretch out the hand."@@ And he stretched *it* out, and his hand was restored.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — YESHUA'S ANGER AND GRIEF**
> - **μετ᾽ ὀργῆς, συλλυπούμενος ἐπὶ τῇ πωρώσει τῆς καρδίας αὐτῶν** = "with anger, grieved at the hardness of their heart." Mark alone records Yeshua's anger and grief (Matthew and Luke omit it). **πώρωσις** (*pōrōsis*) = "hardening/callousness." The healing is by word alone ("stretch out the hand"); no work is performed by Yeshua's hands. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL]. Marked as divine speech (Rule 30).

---

### **Verse 6**

And the Perushim (Pharisees), going out, immediately were giving counsel with the Herodians against him, *as to* how they might destroy him.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE FIRST DEATH-PLOT**
> - **συμβούλιον ἐδίδουν … ὅπως αὐτὸν ἀπολέσωσιν** = "were giving counsel … how they might destroy him." Already in chapter 3 the opposition turns lethal. The **Ἡρῳδιανοί** (*Hērōdianoi*, "Herodians") — supporters of the Herodian dynasty — are otherwise opposed to the Perushim (Pharisees); their alliance shows the breadth of the hostility. The death-plot frames the whole Gospel. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL].

---

### **Verse 7**

And Yeshua (Jesus) withdrew with his disciples to the sea, and a great multitude from the Galil (Galilee) followed; and from Yehudah (Judea)

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **WITHDRAWAL + GROWING CROWD**
> - **ἀνεχώρησεν** (*anechōrēsen*) = "withdrew" — a strategic retreat from the plot, yet the crowds only grow. The geographic catalogue (vv.7–8) emphasizes the reach of his fame.

---

### **Verse 8**

and from Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) and from Idumea and beyond the Yarden (Jordan) and around Tsor (Tyre) and Tsidon (Sidon), a great multitude, hearing what he was doing, came to him.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **TSOR, TSIDON — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Τύρος** (*Tyros*) = Tsor (Hebrew **צֹר**); **Σιδών** (*Sidōn*) = Tsidon (Hebrew **צִידוֹן**) — Phoenician coastal cities. **Ἰδουμαία** (*Idoumaia*) = Idumea (Edom). The list spans Jewish and Gentile-adjacent regions, north and south.

---

### **Verse 9**

And he told his disciples that a little boat should stay ready for him because of the crowd, so that they might not press upon him;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **THE BOAT AS REFUGE**
> - **πλοιάριον** (*ploiarion*) = "little boat." The crowd-pressure motif (1:33,45; 2:2) intensifies; the boat keeps an escape from being crushed (**θλίβωσιν**, "press upon").

---

### **Verse 10**

for he healed many, so that they fell upon him in order to touch him — as many as had afflictions.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"AFFLICTIONS" — *MASTIGAS***
> - **μάστιγας** (*mastigas*) = literally "whips/scourges" — used figuratively for diseases ("afflictions/scourges"). They "fell upon him" (**ἐπιπίπτειν**) to touch him, expecting healing by contact.

---

### **Verse 11**

And the unclean spirits, whenever they beheld him, were falling before him and crying out, saying, "You are the Son of God."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE SPIRITS' CONFESSION (NOT DIVINE SPEECH)**
> - **σὺ εἶ ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ θεοῦ** (*sy ei ho huios tou theou*) = "You are the Son of God." Spoken by the **unclean spirits** — a non-divine speaker — so it is **not** marked as divine speech (Rule 30), exactly as at 1:24. The demons know his identity; the messianic-secret motif requires silencing it (v.12). The TT records their words in ordinary quotation.

---

### **Verse 12**

And he was sternly warning them many *times* that they should not make him known.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **MESSIANIC SILENCE EXTENDED**
> - **πολλὰ ἐπετίμα** (*polla epetima*) = "was sternly warning many *times*." The silencing now covers the demons' confession of sonship (cf. 1:34). The secret extends from miracles to identity.

---

### **Verse 13**

And he goes up into the mountain and calls to him those whom he himself wanted, and they went off to him.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **THE MOUNTAIN; "WHOM HE WANTED"**
> - **τὸ ὄρος** (*to oros*) = "the mountain" — a place of revelation/appointment in the HB pattern (Sinai). **οὓς ἤθελεν αὐτός** = "whom he himself wanted" — the initiative is entirely his.

---

### **Verse 14**

And he made twelve, [whom he also named emissaries,] that they might be with him and that he might send them to proclaim

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "MADE TWELVE" + TEXTUAL VARIANT**
> - **ἐποίησεν δώδεκα** (*epoiēsen dōdeka*) = "he made twelve." The number evokes the twelve tribes of Israel — a symbolic reconstitution. The clause "[whom he also named **emissaries**]" (**ἀποστόλους**, *apostolous*, "sent-ones") is **textually disputed** (present in א, B, C* a.o.; absent from others — possibly imported from Luke 6:13); NA28 brackets it. The two purposes — "be with him" and "be sent" — define the role. Confidence: UNCERTAIN [TEXTUAL] (for the bracketed clause).

---

### **Verse 15**

and to have authority to cast out the demons.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **DELEGATED AUTHORITY — *EXOUSIA***
> - **ἐξουσίαν** (*exousia*) = "authority" — the same word as 1:22,27; 2:10. The Twelve are given a share in the authority Yeshua exercises (proclamation + exorcism).

---

### **Verse 16**

[And he made the twelve,] and he put the name Kefa (Peter) on Shimon (Simon),

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **KEFA — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Κηφᾶς / Πέτρος** → Kefa (Peter): Aramaic **כֵּיפָא** (*Kefa*, "rock"), rendered into Greek as *Petros*. Mark says he "put the name" (**ἐπέθηκεν ὄνομα**) on Shimon — a renaming with significance. The opening "[And he made the twelve]" repeats v.14 and is textually uncertain.

---

### **Verse 17**

and Ya'aqov (James) the *son* of Zavdai (Zebedee) and Yochanan (John) the brother of James — and he put on them the name Boanerges, which is "sons of thunder" —

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **BOANERGES — MARK'S OWN GLOSS**
> - **Βοανηργές, ὅ ἐστιν Υἱοὶ Βροντῆς** (*Boanērges, ho estin Huioi Brontēs*) = "Boanerges, which is 'sons of thunder.'" Mark transliterates a Semitic nickname and **glosses it himself** — a recurring Markan habit (cf. 5:41; 7:34; 15:34). The TT keeps the transliteration and Mark's own translation.

---

### **Verse 18**

and Andreas (Andrew) and Philippos (Philip) and Bar-Talmai (Bartholomew) and Mattai (Matthew) and Toma (Thomas) and Ya'aqov the *son* of Chalfai (Alphaeus) and Taddai (Thaddaeus) and Shimon the Kena'ani (Cananaean)

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **THE TWELVE — TT TRANSLITERATIONS**
> - **Βαρθολομαῖος** = Bar-Talmai ("son of Talmai"); **Μαθθαῖος** = Mattai (Matthew); **Θωμᾶς** = Toma (Aramaic "twin"); **Θαδδαῖος** = Taddai (Thaddaeus). **Σίμων ὁ Καναναῖος** = Shimon the Kena'ani — from Aramaic *qan'ana* ("zealous"), i.e. a Zealot (so Luke 6:15), not "from Cana." The TT keeps the transliteration and notes the sense.

---

### **Verse 19**

and Yehudah Ish-Keriot (Judas Iscariot), who also handed him over.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **YEHUDAH ISH-KERIOT — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Ἰούδας Ἰσκαριώθ** = Yehudah Ish-Keriot. "Iscariot" likely = *ish Keriot* ("man of Keriot," a Judean town), distinguishing him from other bearers of the common name Yehudah. **ὃς καὶ παρέδωκεν αὐτόν** = "who also handed him over" — the same verb (*paradidōmi*) used of John (1:14) and later of Yeshua's arrest.

---

### **Verse 20**

And he comes into a house; and the crowd comes together again, so that they were not even able to eat bread.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **THE PRESSING CROWD (FRAME)**
> - This opens a "sandwich" (intercalation): the family's approach (vv.20–21) frames the Beelzevul (Beelzebul) dispute (vv.22–30) and resumes at vv.31–35 — a characteristic Markan structure.

---

### **Verse 21**

And hearing *it*, those *who were* his own went out to seize him, for they were saying that he is beside himself.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **"HIS OWN … HE IS BESIDE HIMSELF"**
> - **οἱ παρ᾽ αὐτοῦ** (*hoi par' autou*) = "those *who were* his own" — most naturally his family (resumed in v.31). **ἐξέστη** (*exestē*) = "he is beside himself / out of his mind." Mark records the family's alarm bluntly; the TT does not soften it. (Family's speech; not divine.)

---

### **Verse 22**

And the scribes who had come down from Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) were saying that he has Beelzevul (Beelzebul), and that by the ruler of the demons he casts out the demons.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **BEELZEVUL — TT TRANSLITERATION**
> - **Βεελζεβούλ** (*Beelzeboul*) = Beelzevul — a name for the ruler of the demons, likely from a Semitic title ("lord of the [exalted] dwelling," or polemically "lord of dung/flies"; cf. Baal-zebub, 2 Kings 1:2). The scribes' charge: his exorcisms are demonic, not divine. (Scribes' accusation; not divine.)

---

### **Verse 23**

And calling them to him, he was speaking to them in parables: @@"How can the Satan cast out the Satan?@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"THE SATAN" — *SATANAS***
> - **σατανᾶς** (*satanas*) = "the Satan" (Hebrew *ha-satan*, "the adversary"), as at 1:13. The opening question exposes the incoherence of the charge: division within the demonic realm would be self-defeating. Marked as divine speech (Rule 30 — Yeshua speaking, vv.23–29).

---

### **Verse 24**

And if a kingdom is divided against itself, that kingdom cannot stand;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **A KINGDOM DIVIDED**
> - **βασιλεία … μερισθῇ** (*basileia … meristhē*) = "a kingdom divided." The first of paired analogies (kingdom, house) establishing that internal division means collapse — refuting the Beelzevul charge.

---

### **Verse 25**

and if a house is divided against itself, that house will not be able to stand.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **A HOUSE DIVIDED**
> - **οἰκία** (*oikia*) = "house/household." The parallel to v.24; "house" resonates with the family frame (vv.20–21,31–35) — a deliberate echo within the intercalation.

---

### **Verse 26**

And if the Satan rose up against himself and is divided, he cannot stand but has an end.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"HAS AN END" — *TELOS***
> - **τέλος ἔχει** (*telos echei*) = "has an end." The logic completed: a self-divided Satan would be finished, not effective — so the exorcisms cannot be Satan's work.

---

### **Verse 27**

But no one is able, entering into the house of the strong *one*, to plunder his goods, unless he first binds the strong *one*, and then he will plunder his house.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **BINDING THE STRONG ONE**
> - **τὸν ἰσχυρόν … δήσῃ** (*ton ischyron … dēsē*) = "binds the strong *one*." The "strong one" (Satan) must be bound before his "house" can be plundered — i.e. the exorcisms presuppose Satan already being overpowered. Echoes Isaiah 49:24–25 (taking spoil from the mighty).

---

### **Verse 28**

Truly I say to you that all *things* will be forgiven the sons of men — the sins and the blasphemies, whatever they may blaspheme;

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"AMEN I SAY TO YOU" — *AMĒN***
> - **ἀμὴν λέγω ὑμῖν** (*amēn legō hymin*) = "truly/amen I say to you" — a solemn introductory formula. **οἱ υἱοὶ τῶν ἀνθρώπων** = "the sons of men" (humanity). The breadth of forgiveness is stated before the one exception (v.29).

---

### **Verse 29**

but whoever blasphemes against the holy {a:wind/spirit} has no forgiveness *for* the age, but is guilty of an eternal sin" —

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — BLASPHEMY AGAINST THE HOLY WIND/SPIRIT**
> - **εἰς τὸ πνεῦμα τὸ ἅγιον** (*eis to pneuma to hagion*) = "against the holy wind/spirit" — the *pneuma* slash is kept (the divine spirit, as in 1:8). **οὐκ ἔχει ἄφεσιν εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα** = "has no forgiveness *for* the age" (*aphesis* again; *eis ton aiōna* = "to the age / forever"). **ἁμαρτήματος αἰωνίου** = "an eternal sin." The context (v.30) defines the blasphemy: calling the holy spirit's work the work of an unclean spirit. The TT renders the saying without softening its severity. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL]. Marked as divine speech (Rule 30).

---

### **Verse 30**

because they were saying, "He has an unclean spirit."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE NARRATOR DEFINES THE BLASPHEMY**
> - **ὅτι ἔλεγον· πνεῦμα ἀκάθαρτον ἔχει** = "because they were saying, 'He has an unclean spirit.'" The narrator pins the "eternal sin" to a specific act: attributing the holy spirit's work to an unclean spirit. *Pneuma akatharton* ("unclean spirit") takes **no** slash — "wind" is not in view. (Scribes' words, reported by the narrator; not divine.)

---

### **Verse 31**

And his mother and his brothers come, and standing outside, they sent to him, calling him.

> **Notes**
>
> 🟡 **THE FAMILY RESUMES (FRAME CLOSES)**
> - The mother and brothers (the family of vv.20–21) arrive and stay **outside** — a spatial contrast with those "around him" (v.34). Mark names neither the mother (Miryam) nor the brothers here; the focus is relational position.

---

### **Verse 32**

And a crowd was sitting around him, and they say to him, "See, your mother and your brothers *and your sisters* outside are seeking you."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"AND YOUR SISTERS" — TEXTUAL NOTE**
> - **καὶ αἱ ἀδελφαί σου** ("and your sisters") is present in some witnesses (A, D a.o.) and absent from others (א, B, C, K, L); the TT includes it in italics as text-uncertain. The crowd relays the family's request. (Crowd's speech; not divine.)

---

### **Verse 33**

And answering them he says, @@"Who is my mother and my brothers?"@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **THE REDEFINING QUESTION**
> - **τίς ἐστιν ἡ μήτηρ μου καὶ οἱ ἀδελφοί μου;** = "Who is my mother and my brothers?" Not a denial of family but a redefinition, answered in vv.34–35. Marked as divine speech (Rule 30 — Yeshua speaking).

---

### **Verse 34**

And looking around at those sitting around him in a circle, he says, @@"See, my mother and my brothers.@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🟢 **"LOOKING AROUND" — THE NEW CIRCLE**

### **Verse 35**

For whoever does the will of God, this one is my brother and sister and mother."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — FAMILY REDEFINED BY THE WILL OF GOD**
> - **ὃς … ἂν ποιήσῃ τὸ θέλημα τοῦ θεοῦ** = "whoever does the will of God." Kinship is reconstituted around obedience to God rather than blood — "brother and sister and mother" (no "father," perhaps reserving that for God). The TT renders the reconstitution without drawing the inference for the reader. Confidence: VERIFIED [TEXTUAL]. Marked as divine speech (Rule 30).

## CROSS-CHAPTER TRACKING (HB -> Mark)

**Exodus 20:8–11 / Deuteronomy 5:12–15 -> Mark 3:4 — sabbath and life:**

| Element | HB sabbath law | Mark 3:4 |
|---------|----------------|----------|
| Command | rest on the sabbath | (presupposed) |
| Principle drawn | rest, release, life | "to do good … to save a life" overrides |
| Question posed | — | good vs. harm, save vs. kill |

Yeshua frames the sabbath around preserving life — coherent with the rationale of rest-as-gift (Deut 5:14–15) rather than rest-as-restriction.

**The Twelve Tribes -> Mark 3:14–19 — "he made twelve":**

| Element | Twelve Tribes of Israel | Mark 3:14 |
|---------|--------------------------|-----------|
| Number | twelve sons/tribes | twelve appointed |
| Function | constitute Israel | constitute a new core around Yeshua |
| Symbolism | covenant people | reconstituted people (implied, not stated) |

**Isaiah 49:24–25 -> Mark 3:27 — plundering the strong:**

| Element | Isaiah 49:24–25 | Mark 3:27 |
|---------|-----------------|-----------|
| Image | taking prey/spoil from the mighty | plundering the strong one's house |
| Condition | YHWH contends and rescues | the strong one must first be bound |

The exorcisms presuppose the "strong one" (Satan) already overpowered — the spoil can be taken only after the binding.
