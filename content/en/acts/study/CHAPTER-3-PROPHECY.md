## Note on Acts' mode of allusion

Acts 3 is the **second apostolic sermon**, and like the Pentecost speech (Acts 2) it is **saturated with the Hebrew Bible** — but its prophetic mode differs from Acts 2. Where Acts 2 quotes Joel and the Psalms with explicit citation formulae, Acts 3 carries **one formal, named citation** (Mosheh / Deuteronomy 18, "Moses indeed said…," 3:22–23), one **divine-marked covenant quotation** (the LORD's word to Avraham, Gen 22:18, 3:25 — the chapter's one `@@…@@` saying, Rule 30 / A-004), and an outer ring of **allusions** (the bush-name of Ex 3, the Servant Songs of Isaiah, the lame-leaping of Isa 35, Samuel heading the prophets, the *apokatastasis* hope). **Speech-marking (Rule 30 / A-004):** Kefa's (Peter's) sermon is **human** speech and is not marked; the Deuteronomy 18 citation is **Moses's reported speech *about* the LORD** (κύριος = YHWH) and is therefore **not** divine-marked; only the LORD's first-person covenant word to Avraham (Gen 22:18) is marked `@@…@@`. Where κύριος in a citation stands for YHWH, the entries note it per **Option C** (the main text renders "the Lord" + Tier-2; the source-referent is YHWH).

## Deuteronomy 18:15–19 (with Leviticus 23:29) — the prophet like Moses (Acts 3:22–23)

**Verse:** Acts 3:22–23
**Text says:** "Mosheh (Moses) indeed said that 'A prophet for you *the* Lord God will raise up from your brothers, like me; him you shall hear in all *things*, whatever he may speak to you. And it will be *that* every soul, whoever does not hear that prophet, will be utterly destroyed from the people.'"
**Context:** The pivot of Kefa's (Peter's) sermon: having named the suffering-and-raised Messiah (3:13–18) and called for change of mind (3:19–21), he grounds the call in scripture — Moses himself promised a prophet like himself, and the prophetic line from Samuel announced "these days" (3:24). The Moses words are **Moses's reported speech *about* the LORD**, so they are not divine-marked (Rule 30 / A-004).
**Subject:** The prophet-like-Moses, heeded on pain of being cut off. Acts quotes Deuteronomy 18:15–19 (LXX) — the LORD's promise through Moses to "raise up (*anastēsei*) a prophet… like you (me), from among their brothers," whom Israel must heed. Acts **conflates** the closing warning of Deut 18:19 ("I will require it of him") with the "cut off / utterly destroyed from the people" language of **Leviticus 23:29** (*exolethreuthēsetai ek tou laou*) — a sharpening drawn from the Day-of-Atonement penalty. In Deut 18 "the LORD your God" is YHWH (κύριος ὁ θεός = YHWH, Option C); the early proclamation identifies the prophet-like-Moses with Yeshua.
**Fulfillment status:** CLAIMED
**Fulfillment notes:** This **is the chapter's one formal, named citation** — introduced "Mosheh indeed said that…" — but it is *Moses's* speech *about* YHWH, not YHWH's quoted direct speech, so it is **not** marked `@@…@@` (Rule 30). Two features carry the TT's restraint: (1) the **conflation** — the threat-clause is not Deut 18:19 verbatim but a blend with Lev 23:29 ("utterly destroyed from the people"), an interpretive sharpening of "I will require it" into a karet ("cut off") warning; the TT renders what Acts wrote and flags the blend (3:23 note). (2) The **identification** of the prophet-like-Moses with Yeshua is the *sermon's* explicit claim, not a feature of Deut 18 itself, where the promise concerns the prophetic office generally (and, in its first setting, a succession of prophets). The CLAIMED label reflects that Acts cites Deut 18 explicitly and applies it to Yeshua; the singular-vs.-collective reference of the Deuteronomy promise is the live interpretive question the TT does not resolve (Rule 13). **[textual — VERIFIED (citation); the Yeshua-identification — CLAIMED; the Lev 23:29 conflation — PROBABLE]** → See Cross-Chapter Tracking (Deut 18:15–19 → Acts 3:22–23) in `content/en/acts/CHAPTER-3.md`.
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: Deuteronomy 18:15–19 is read as the promise of true prophetic succession in Israel (over against the divination of the nations, 18:9–14) — a standing institution of prophets faithful to YHWH, not a single end-time figure; the "cut off" language belongs to Lev 23:29 and the karet penalties. The identification with Yeshua is not accepted; "a prophet like Moses" is read of Joshua and the prophetic line, or held as an unrealized hope. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: Deut 18:15–19 is read messianically — the singular "prophet like Moses" fulfilled in Yeshua, the new lawgiver and mediator (cf. Jn 1:21, 45; 6:14; the transfiguration "hear him," Lk 9:35). Acts 3:22–23 is treated as the apostolic interpretation that heeding this prophet is now decisive for belonging to the people. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: Deuteronomy 18:18 ("a prophet like you from among their brothers") is widely cited in Islamic tradition as a foretelling of Muhammad — "their brothers" read as the Ishmaelite line — making this verse a contested proof-text between the traditions. The Quran presents Musa (Moses) as a major prophet and announces a messenger to come (cf. Surah 7:157; 61:6). [DOCUMENTED]

## Genesis 22:18 (cf. 12:3) — "in your seed all the families of the earth will be blessed" (Acts 3:25)

**Verse:** Acts 3:25
**Text says:** "You are the sons of the prophets and of the covenant which God covenanted with your fathers, saying to Avraham (Abraham), @@'And in your seed all the families of the earth will be blessed.'@@"
**Context:** The climax of the sermon's appeal: the hearers are "sons of the prophets and of the covenant," heirs of the Abrahamic promise — and the risen servant has been sent "to you first" (3:26). The quoted line is **the LORD's direct covenant word to Avraham**, and is therefore marked `@@…@@` (Rule 30 / A-004) — the chapter's one divine-marked saying.
**Subject:** The covenant blessing of "all the families of the earth" through Avraham's seed. Acts quotes the LORD's oath to Avraham — closest to **Genesis 22:18** ("in your seed all the nations of the earth will be blessed," spoken after the binding of Yitschaq/Isaac), with the wording "all the **families** (*patriai*) of the earth" drawing on **Genesis 12:3** (the first call: "in you all the families of the earth will be blessed"; cf. 18:18; 26:4; 28:14). The speaker is YHWH (the covenant-maker); the divine first person ("in *your* seed") makes it direct divine speech. Acts reads the "seed" toward Yeshua and the "all the families" toward the nations, while addressing Israel "first."
**Fulfillment status:** CLAIMED
**Fulfillment notes:** This **is a citation of divine speech** — the LORD's covenant word quoted in the first person — and so it is marked `@@…@@` (the only such marking in the chapter; A-004). The wording **blends two Genesis passages**: the "in your seed" of 22:18 (the post-Akedah oath) with the "all the families (*patriai*) of the earth" of 12:3 (the opening call); the TT renders the conflated form Acts gives and notes the blend (3:25 note). The CLAIMED label reflects that Acts deliberately roots the gospel-to-the-nations in the Abrahamic covenant; the "all the families of the earth" horizon (held in view, though Israel is addressed "first," 3:26) is the same universal reach Luke's Gospel sounds (Lk 2:32; 3:6). The reference of "seed" — collective (Israel/Avraham's line) vs. singular (the Messiah) — is the interpretive question the TT does not resolve (Rule 13; cf. the same crux at Gal 3:16). **[textual — VERIFIED (citation); the Gen 12:3 conflation — PROBABLE; the singular-"seed" reading — CLAIMED]** → See Cross-Chapter Tracking (Gen 22:18 / 12:3 → Acts 3:25). For the same oath in the Lukan canticles, see `content/en/luke/study/CHAPTER-1-PROPHECY.md` (Gen 17:7, 22:16–18 → Luke 1:55, 72–73).
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: Genesis 22:18 and 12:3 are foundational covenant texts — the blessing of the nations through Avraham and his seed, grounding Israel's election and vocation. "Seed" is read collectively of Avraham's descendants; the blessing of the nations through Israel remains a live hope. The christological reading of "seed" as the Messiah is not accepted. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: The promise is read as fulfilled in Yeshua, the singular "seed" of Avraham through whom the nations are blessed (the Pauline reading, Gal 3:8, 16); Acts 3:25 is the inaugural offer of that blessing — to Israel "first" (3:26), then the nations (cf. 1:8; 13:46–47). [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: Ibrahim (Abraham) is central in Islam as the model monotheist and father of the line of prophets through both Ismail and Ishaq; the covenant and blessing are affirmed, but the Genesis-22 "in your seed all the families blessed" formula and its christological application are not part of the Quranic presentation. [DOCUMENTED]

## Isaiah 52:13–53:12 — the Servant (*pais*) glorified, the suffering Messiah foretold (Acts 3:13, 14, 18, 26)

**Verse:** Acts 3:13, 3:14, 3:18, 3:26
**Text says:** "The God of Avraham… glorified his **servant** Yeshua (Jesus), whom you handed over and denied…" (3:13); "But you denied the holy and righteous *one*…" (3:14); "but God, the *things* which he foretold through *the* mouth of all the prophets — that his Messiah would suffer — he thus fulfilled" (3:18); "To you first, God, having raised up his **servant**, sent him…" (3:26).
**Context:** The doctrinal core of the sermon (3:13–18): the contrast pattern (you denied / God glorified; you killed / God raised), the titles of the Messiah, and the claim that the Messiah's suffering was "foretold through the mouth of all the prophets." The language is not a quotation but a sustained **echo of the fourth Servant Song**.
**Subject:** The Servant of YHWH glorified after suffering. "His **servant** (*pais*) Yeshua… glorified" (3:13, 26) echoes the opening of the fourth Servant Song, **Isaiah 52:13** LXX ("behold, my servant (*ho pais mou*) shall understand and be exalted and **glorified** (*doxasthēsetai*) exceedingly"); "the holy and **righteous one**" (3:14) draws on Isa 53:11 ("the **righteous one**, my servant, shall justify the many"); and "that his Messiah would **suffer**" (3:18) reads the suffering of the Servant (Isa 53:3–10) as the burden of "all the prophets" (cf. Lk 24:26–27, 46). *Pais* (παῖς) carries both "servant" and "child"; the TT renders "servant" with the Isaianic Servant in view (3:13 note).
**Fulfillment status:** CLAIMED
**Fulfillment notes:** This is **allusion / echo**, not a formula citation — Acts does not say "as it is written" or quote Isaiah 53 verbatim; instead it weaves the Servant Song's diction (*pais*, "glorified," "righteous one," the suffering) into the sermon's own argument. The connection is deliberate and recognizable: the *pais*-"glorified" pairing matches Isa 52:13 LXX closely, and "his Messiah would suffer… foretold through all the prophets" (3:18) is the early proclamation's standing reading of Isaiah 53 as the suffering-Messiah text. The CLAIMED label reflects that Acts explicitly asserts the suffering-Messiah-as-prophesied while leaving the source unnamed and the Servant's identity (Israel, a remnant, an individual, the Messiah) the very crux the Jewish/Christian traditions divide on — which the TT does not resolve (Rule 13; *pais* and *archēgos* flagged in the main-text notes). **[textual — PROBABLE (allusion, not citation); the suffering-Messiah-as-foretold — CLAIMED; the Servant's identity — POSSIBLE]** → See Cross-Chapter Tracking (Isa 52:13–53:12 → Acts 3:13, 26). For the Servant behind the baptismal voice, see `content/en/luke/study/CHAPTER-3-PROPHECY.md` (Isa 42:1 → Luke 3:22).
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: Isaiah 52:13–53:12 is the fourth Servant Song; the Servant is widely read **collectively** as Israel (or a faithful remnant) suffering among the nations, and in some traditions of an individual righteous sufferer — but not as a crucified-and-raised Messiah. The "righteous one, my servant" (53:11) and the exaltation of 52:13 are read within Israel's vocation; the application to Yeshua is not accepted. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: Isaiah 52:13–53:12 is read as the foremost prophecy of the suffering-and-glorified Messiah, fulfilled in Yeshua — the *pais* "glorified" (Isa 52:13) after being "handed over" and killed, the "righteous one" who bears the sins of the many (53:11). Acts 3:13, 18, 26 is treated as the apostolic application of the Servant Song. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: Islam honors Isa (Jesus) as Messiah and prophet but rejects the crucifixion as a real death (Surah 4:157, "they did not kill him… but it was made to appear so"); a suffering-and-atoning Servant is therefore not part of the Islamic reading, and Isaiah 53 is not engaged as a passion prophecy. [DOCUMENTED]

## Exodus 3:6, 3:15 — "the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob" (Acts 3:13)

**Verse:** Acts 3:13
**Text says:** "The God of Avraham (Abraham) and *of* Yitschaq (Isaac) and *of* Ya'aqov (Jacob), the God of our fathers, glorified his servant Yeshua (Jesus)…"
**Context:** The opening of the sermon's theological claim (3:13): the One who glorified Yeshua is named by the ancestral self-identification — establishing continuity with Israel's God before the indictment and the call.
**Subject:** YHWH named by the patriarchal/bush self-identification. "The God of Avraham and of Yitschaq and of Ya'aqov, the God of our fathers" reproduces the divine self-naming at the burning bush — **Exodus 3:6** ("I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob") and **Exodus 3:15** ("YHWH, the God of your fathers… this is my name forever") — the formula by which YHWH identifies himself to Moses. Acts uses it to assert that the God who acted in Yeshua is none other than the covenant God of the patriarchs.
**Fulfillment status:** DEBATED
**Fulfillment notes:** This is **allusion by self-identification formula**, not a prophecy and not a citation — Acts does not quote Exodus, and the bush-formula makes no predictive claim; it is a **theological-identity** statement (the God who glorified Yeshua = the God of the fathers). It is included here as **fulfillment-adjacent**: it frames the whole sermon's continuity-with-Israel argument, and at the bush this same formula is the ground of the "I will require it" / promise-keeping covenant. The DEBATED label reflects that no fulfillment is asserted — the entry marks an allusive identity-claim, not an echo of a prediction (Rule 13). **[comparative parallel — PROBABLE; the formula carries no predictive content]** → See Cross-Chapter Tracking (Ex 3:6, 15 → Acts 3:13).
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: Exodus 3:6, 15 is the bush revelation — YHWH names himself the God of the patriarchs and gives the name "to be remembered in every generation"; the formula grounds Israel's whole covenant story. Its reuse to identify the God who "glorified Yeshua" is recognized as Acts' rhetorical appeal to shared ground; it does not entail Acts' specific claims about Yeshua. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: The naming is read as Acts' insistence on continuity — the God of the exodus and the patriarchs is the God who raised and glorified Yeshua; there is no new deity, only the covenant God acting climactically (cf. the same formula in Yeshua's resurrection argument, Lk 20:37–38). [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: The Quran affirms the God of Ibrahim, Ishaq, and Yaqub as the one God (Allah) worshipped by the prophets (Surah 2:133, 136); the bush revelation to Musa is narrated (Surah 20:9–24; 28:29–35). The patriarchal-God formula is shared ground; its application to Yeshua's glorification is not part of the Islamic reading. [DOCUMENTED]

## Isaiah 35:6 — "the lame will leap" (Acts 3:8)

**Verse:** Acts 3:8
**Text says:** "and leaping up, he stood and was walking, and entered with them into the temple, walking and leaping and praising God."
**Context:** The climax of the healing (3:8): the man "lame from his mother's womb" (3:2) leaps, walks, and enters the temple — the sign that occasions the sermon. The narration, not a quotation, carries the echo.
**Subject:** The leaping of the lame as a sign of the promised restoration. "Leaping" (*hallomenos*) echoes **Isaiah 35:6** LXX ("then the lame will **leap** like a deer, and the tongue of the mute will be clear") — part of the vision of the wilderness blooming and the redeemed returning to Zion, a sign of the age of restoration. The healed man's entry into the temple he had only sat outside (3:2) enacts that restoration imagery.
**Fulfillment status:** CLAIMED
**Fulfillment notes:** This is **allusion by enacted sign**, not a citation — Acts narrates the healing without quoting Isaiah, but the verb "leaping" (*hallomai*) deliberately recalls Isa 35:6, casting the cure as a token of the prophesied restoration (and tying it to the *apokatastasis* / "restoration of all things" of 3:21). The CLAIMED label reflects that the echo is intentional and recognizable as a restoration-sign; no formula and no explicit fulfillment-claim is made — the connection is enacted in the narrative, not asserted in the sermon. **[comparative parallel — POSSIBLE]** → See Cross-Chapter Tracking (Isa 35:6 → Acts 3:8).
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: Isaiah 35 is read as a vision of Israel's future restoration — the wilderness rejoicing, the blind, deaf, lame, and mute healed, the redeemed returning to Zion on the "Way of Holiness"; the lame leaping is an image of that age, not a prediction of a specific healing. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: The healing is read as a sign that the age of restoration foretold in Isa 35 has dawned in Yeshua (cf. Lk 7:22 / Isa 35:5–6, Jesus's answer to John); Acts 3:8 enacts it through the apostles' ministry "in the name." [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: The Quran affirms that Isa healed the blind and the leper by God's permission (Surah 3:49; 5:110) as signs of prophethood; the specific Isaiah-35 restoration framework is not part of the Quranic presentation. [DOCUMENTED]

## 1 Samuel 3 — Samuel heading the prophetic line (Acts 3:24)

**Verse:** Acts 3:24
**Text says:** "And indeed all the prophets, from Shmuel (Samuel) and those *who came* after, as many as spoke, also announced these days."
**Context:** Following the Deuteronomy 18 citation (3:22–23), Kefa widens the witness: not Moses only, but the whole prophetic line "from Samuel" forward "announced these days" — the present time as the prophets' horizon, reinforcing the suffering-Messiah claim of 3:18.
**Subject:** Samuel as the head of the line of prophets. Acts names **Shmuel (Samuel)** as the first of "the prophets… and those after" — reflecting the tradition (rooted in **1 Samuel 3**, where "the word of YHWH came to Samuel" and "all Israel knew that Samuel was confirmed as a prophet of YHWH," 3:20–21) that Samuel inaugurates the prophetic line proper after Moses. The reference is to the office and the corporate witness, not to a specific Samuel oracle.
**Fulfillment status:** DEBATED
**Fulfillment notes:** This is **allusion by named figure**, not a citation and not a quotation of any Samuel prophecy — Acts invokes Samuel as the **head of the prophetic line**, summarizing the whole prophetic corpus as having "announced these days." It is included here as **fulfillment-adjacent**: it generalizes the suffering-Messiah-as-foretold claim (3:18) across all the prophets, but cites none. The DEBATED label reflects that no specific prediction is in view — the entry marks a corporate, summary appeal to the prophetic witness, and the claim that the prophets collectively "announced these days" is the sermon's interpretive assertion (Rule 13). **[comparative parallel — POSSIBLE; corporate/summary appeal, no single oracle]** → See Cross-Chapter Tracking (1 Sam 3 → Acts 3:24).
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: 1 Samuel 3 narrates Samuel's call — "the word of YHWH came to Samuel," and he is "confirmed as a prophet of YHWH" (3:20); the tradition reckons Samuel among the first of the classical prophets after the period of the judges. The claim that "all the prophets from Samuel announced these days" (i.e. Yeshua's) is Acts' interpretation, not accepted in the Jewish reading. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: Samuel as the head of the prophetic line is read as Acts' way of placing the whole prophetic witness behind the proclamation — the prophets from Samuel onward all pointed toward "these days" of the Messiah's coming, suffering, and the call to repentance. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: Samuel is recognized in Islamic tradition (often as the unnamed prophet of Surah 2:246–248, who anoints Talut/Saul); the specific "head of the prophets who announced these days" framing is not part of the Quranic presentation. [DOCUMENTED]

## Malachi 3:23–24 (Eng. 4:5–6); cf. Acts 1:6 — the restoration / *apokatastasis* hope (Acts 3:19–21)

**Verse:** Acts 3:19–21
**Text says:** "Change *your* minds, therefore, and turn, for the wiping out of your sins, so that times of refreshing may come from *the* face of the Lord… whom heaven must receive until *the* times of restoration of all *things*, of which God spoke through *the* mouth of his holy prophets from of old."
**Context:** The call of the sermon (3:19–21): repentance and "turning," "times of refreshing," and the "restoration of all things" awaited until the Messiah's return. The restoration language gathers the prophetic hope of Israel's renewal; κύριος here ("the face of the Lord," 3:20) = YHWH (Option C).
**Subject:** The eschatological restoration and turning of hearts. "Restoration of all things" (*apokatastasis pantōn*, 3:21) and "change of mind and turn" (3:19) draw on the prophetic hope of Israel's renewal — paradigmatically **Malachi 3:23–24** (Eng. 4:5–6), the returning Eliyahu (Elijah) who will "**turn** the hearts of fathers to children" before "the great and terrible day of YHWH," and the broader return-and-renewal prophecies (cf. Mal 3:7, "**return** to me and I will return to you"). The same root underlies the disciples' question at **Acts 1:6** ("are you at this time **restoring** (*apokathistaneis*) the kingdom to Israel?"). In "the face of the Lord" κύριος = YHWH.
**Fulfillment status:** PARTIAL
**Fulfillment notes:** This is **allusion to the prophetic restoration-hope**, not a citation — Acts names no source ("through the mouth of his holy prophets from of old," 3:21, is a *general* appeal, like 3:18, 24) and quotes no verse; the "turn… restoration" diction gathers the renewal hope (Malachi's turning of hearts; the return-prophecies) rather than fulfilling a single prediction. The PARTIAL label reflects that the restoration hope is **broader than any one realization**: its scope is debated (cosmic renewal; Israel's national restoration; universal), and Acts holds it open — the *apokatastasis* is awaited "until" the Messiah's return (3:21), not declared complete. The TT keeps *apokatastasis* without resolving its scope (3:21 note; Rule 13). **[comparative parallel — POSSIBLE; the apokatastasis scope — Uncertain]** → See Cross-Chapter Tracking (Mal 4:5–6 / Acts 1:6 → Acts 3:19, 21). For the Elijah-restoration in the Lukan birth narrative, see `content/en/luke/study/CHAPTER-1-PROPHECY.md` (Mal 3:1, 3:23–24 → Luke 1:17, 76).
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: Malachi 3:23–24 is read as the promise of Eliyahu's return to reconcile the generations before the Day of YHWH — a live expectation; and the return-and-renewal prophecies (the ingathering, the restored kingdom) remain unfulfilled hopes in mainstream Jewish reading. The "restoration of all things" tied to Yeshua's return is Acts' claim, not accepted. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: Acts 3:19–21 is read as the offer of "times of refreshing" through repentance and the promise that the Messiah, received in heaven, will return at the "restoration of all things" foretold by the prophets — the consummation of the kingdom; *apokatastasis* is variously read (cosmic renewal; some traditions, universal restoration). [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: Islam affirms an eschatological renewal and a Day of Judgment, and traditions speak of Isa's return; the specific Malachi-Elijah / *apokatastasis* framework of Acts is not part of the Quranic presentation. [DOCUMENTED]
