## Note on Acts 1's mode of allusion

Acts 1 contains the chapter's **two explicit formula citations** — both from the Psalms, both introduced "For it is written in *the* book of Psalms" (1:20): Psalm 69:25 and Psalm 109:8, applied to Yehudah (Judas) and to the vacant apostolic office. These are the only verses Acts 1 quotes. The chapter's remaining prophetic density is **allusive and scenic**: the ascension-in-a-cloud (Dan 7:13), the Mount of Olives setting (Zech 14:4), the reconstitution of the Twelve (the twelve-tribe / restoration motif), and the casting of lots (Prov 16:33). Both Psalm citations follow the **LXX** wording Luke uses, and both carry **κύριος Option C** consequences where YHWH stands behind the Greek (the governing log entry is A-005; the main text renders κύριος as "the Lord" + Tier-2). The TT therefore reserves explicit-citation language for the two Psalms and labels the rest as allusion at lower confidence. **Rule 30:** the risen Yeshua's speech (1:4–5, 7–8) is divine-marked in the main text, but it contains no HB citation; the two men in white (1:10–11) are *messengers* (the *angelos*-class) and are not marked.

## Psalm 69:25 — "Let his homestead become desolate" (Acts 1:20a)

**Verse:** Acts 1:20a
**Text says:** "For it is written in *the* book of Psalms: 'Let his homestead become desolate, and let there be no *one* dwelling in it.'"
**Context:** Kefa's (Peter's) address to the hundred and twenty (1:15–22), explaining Yehudah's (Judas's) defection and the necessity of replacing him. The citation is introduced with the explicit formula "For it is written in *the* book of Psalms" — the chapter's one citation-formula, governing both Psalm quotations (1:20a–b). It follows the Judas parenthesis (vv.18–19) and the divine-necessity statement of v.16 ("it was necessary *for* the scripture to be fulfilled").
**Subject:** The desolated dwelling of the enemy. Psalm 69 is an individual lament-and-imprecation; v.25 (Hebrew/MT 69:26; LXX 68:26) calls down ruin on the psalmist's persecutors: "Let their encampment be desolate; let there be no dweller in their tents." Luke's Greek **shifts the plural to the singular** — "his homestead… no *one* dwelling in it" — to apply the curse to the single figure, Judas. The LXX of Ps 68:26 (*genēthētō hē epaulis autōn ērēmōmenē*) stands behind the wording, adjusted to the singular. Psalm 69 is one of the most-cited psalms in the NT (cf. Jn 2:17; 15:25; Rom 15:3), read as a passion-psalm.
**Fulfillment status:** CLAIMED
**Fulfillment notes:** This **is an explicit citation** ("For it is written in *the* book of Psalms") — a direct, formula-introduced quotation, not an echo. Two features carry the TT's restraint: (1) the **plural→singular shift** — the MT and LXX both read the plural (the psalmist's enemies collectively); Acts singularizes to fit Judas, an interpretive narrowing the TT notes rather than smooths. (2) Psalm 69:25 in its own setting is **imprecation, not prediction** — the psalmist curses present persecutors; Peter reads it prophetically of Judas's end (the homestead = the "Field of Blood" of vv.18–19). The CLAIMED label reflects that Acts asserts the fulfillment explicitly (the formula + *edei*, "it was necessary," of v.16); the restraint is that the source is a curse re-applied, not a foretelling. There is no YHWH in the quoted clause itself, so Option C does not bear on the rendered words here (the divine name issue attaches to the framing, not the citation). **[textual — VERIFIED (citation); the plural→singular application — POSSIBLE]** → See Cross-Chapter Tracking (Ps 69:25 → Acts 1:20a) in `content/en/acts/CHAPTER-1.md`.
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: Psalm 69 is an individual lament with imprecations against the psalmist's persecutors; v.26 (Heb.) curses their encampment with desolation, in the plural. It is read in its own setting as the prayer of a suffering righteous one against his enemies, not as a prophecy of any single later figure. The NT singular-application to Judas is not part of Jewish reading. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: Psalm 69 is read as a passion-psalm of the righteous sufferer (applied to Yeshua in the Gospels); v.25 is read by Acts as prophetic of the betrayer's desolate end, the curse falling on the one who turned aside. The plural→singular move is read as Spirit-guided application "through the mouth of David" (v.16). [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: The Quran does not engage Psalm 69 or the figure of Judas; the betrayal narrative itself is read differently (many exegetes hold that Isa was not crucified, Surah 4:157), so the Acts application has no Quranic counterpart. [DOCUMENTED]

## Psalm 109:8 — "Let another take his overseership" (Acts 1:20b)

**Verse:** Acts 1:20b
**Text says:** "…and: 'Let another take his overseership.'"
**Context:** The second half of Peter's Psalm citation (1:20), under the same "it is written in *the* book of Psalms" formula. This clause is the operative one — it grounds the apostolic election that follows (vv.21–26): because the office is to pass to "another," a replacement for Judas must be chosen.
**Subject:** The vacated office passing to another. Psalm 109 is an intense individual imprecatory psalm; v.8 (LXX 108:8) reads "Let his days be few; let another take his **ἐπισκοπή** (*episkopē*)." In the LXX *episkopē* renders Hebrew **פְּקֻדָּה** (*pequddah*) — "office, charge, oversight, visitation." Luke quotes the LXX clause **verbatim** (*tēn episkopēn autou labetō heteros*) and reads *episkopē* as the apostolic *office* to be filled — the TT renders "overseership" to keep the office sense rather than the later ecclesiastical "bishopric" (see the main-text Tier-2 note at 1:20). The word also links to *klēros* ("lot/portion," v.17) and the casting of lots (v.26).
**Fulfillment status:** CLAIMED
**Fulfillment notes:** This **is an explicit citation** — quoted under the same formula as Ps 69:25 and rendered close to the LXX verbatim. The restraint points: (1) Psalm 109, like Psalm 69, is an **imprecation** against an enemy in its own setting (the psalmist's accusers), not a prediction of an apostolic vacancy; Acts reads it prophetically and, crucially, **operationally** — it is the warrant for the election of Mattityahu (Matthias). (2) The Hebrew *pequddah* ("office/charge") is broader than the institutional "office"; the LXX *episkopē* and Luke's use narrow it toward the apostolic charge — the TT flags the semantic range (Rule 13) rather than importing the later "bishopric." As with Ps 69, no YHWH stands in the quoted clause, so Option C does not alter the rendered words; the divine-speaker framing ("the holy {a:wind/spirit}… through *the* mouth of David," v.16) is what casts the human-voice psalm as prophecy. The CLAIMED label reflects the explicit citation and its direct grounding of the apostolic election. **[textual — VERIFIED (citation); the *episkopē* = "apostolic office" reading — PROBABLE]** → See Cross-Chapter Tracking (Ps 109:8 → Acts 1:20b).
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: Psalm 109 is among the strongest imprecatory psalms — the prayer of one falsely accused, calling down judgment on his accuser, including v.8 "let another take his charge/office." It is read in its own setting against the psalmist's enemy, not as a prophecy fulfilled in a later vacancy. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: Acts reads Ps 109:8 as prophetic of the apostolic office Judas forfeited, the warrant for filling the Twelve; "let another take his overseership" supplies both the necessity and the language (the *episkopē*) of the election that follows. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: The Quran does not engage Psalm 109 or the apostolic-replacement narrative; the reconstitution of the Twelve has no Quranic parallel. [DOCUMENTED]

## Daniel 7:13 — the cloud of the ascension (Acts 1:9, 11)

**Verse:** Acts 1:9, 11
**Text says:** "And having said these *things*, *as* they were watching, he was lifted up, and a cloud took him from under their eyes" (1:9); "This Yeshua (Jesus)… will so come in the manner *in* which you saw him going into the sky" (1:11).
**Context:** The ascension (1:9) and the two men's interpretation of it (1:10–11). Yeshua is lifted up (aorist passive — God the agent) and a cloud hides him; the two messengers promise a return "in the manner" of the departure.
**Subject:** The cloud as the vehicle of heavenly glory and the son-of-man motion "with the clouds." The glory-cloud is the standard HB sign of the divine presence (Ex 40:34; 1 Kgs 8:10–11); Daniel 7:13 specifically describes "one like a son of man" coming **with the clouds of heaven** (LXX/Theodotion *meta tōn nephelōn tou ouranou*) to the Ancient of Days to receive dominion. The ascension reverses the Daniel direction (up, into glory), and v.11's "will so come" turns the cloud-motion toward the return (cf. Lk 21:27; Mk 13:26, where Dan 7:13 is quoted of the coming).
**Fulfillment status:** PARTIAL
**Fulfillment notes:** This is **allusion, not citation** — Acts 1 does not quote Daniel 7:13 or use a citation formula; the cloud is a *scenic* echo of the glory-cloud generally (Ex 40) and of the son-of-man-with-clouds specifically (Dan 7:13). The PARTIAL label reflects two things: (a) the allusion is real but **diffuse** — a cloud at an ascension naturally evokes the glory-cloud without pinning to one verse; (b) Daniel 7:13's "coming with the clouds" is, in Daniel, a coming *to* the Ancient of Days (heavenward), which fits the **ascension** better than the descent — while v.11's "will so come" applies the same cloud-imagery to the **return**, so the Daniel motif spans both directions. The Gospel parallels (Lk 21:27 par.) quote Dan 7:13 of the coming, strengthening the connection but not making Acts 1 a citation. **[comparative parallel — POSSIBLE]** → See Cross-Chapter Tracking (Ex 40:34; Dan 7:13 → Acts 1:9, 11).
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: Daniel 7:13 is read as a vision of "one like a son of man" brought before the Ancient of Days and given everlasting dominion; identifications of the figure vary (the people of the holy ones, a heavenly representative, a messianic figure). The glory-cloud (Ex 40) marks God's presence. Neither is read as a prophecy of Yeshua's ascension. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: The ascension cloud is read as the glory-cloud receiving the exalted Yeshua, and the "will so come" of v.11 as the promise of the Danielic son-of-man's return with the clouds — ascension and parousia framed by the same imagery. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: The Quran affirms that God raised Isa to himself (Surah 4:158, *rafaʿahu llāhu ilayhi*), an exaltation rather than a death; the specific cloud-and-Daniel framing is not part of the Quranic account. [DOCUMENTED]

## Zechariah 14:4 — the Mount of Olives (Acts 1:12)

**Verse:** Acts 1:12
**Text says:** "Then they returned to Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) from *the* mount called *of* Olives (Har haZeitim), which is near Jerusalem, a sabbath's journey away."
**Context:** The disciples' return from the ascension site. The narrator names the place — the Mount of Olives — and gives its sabbath-distance from the city. The setting (vv.9–12) places the ascension, and by v.11 the promised return, on this mountain.
**Subject:** The Mount of Olives as the place of YHWH's eschatological coming. Zechariah 14:4 prophesies that "the feet of YHWH will stand on that day on the **Mount of Olives**, which faces Jerusalem on the east," and the mountain will split as YHWH comes to fight for his people and reign as king over all the earth (14:9). The ascension and the promise of return (v.11) are staged on the very mountain Zechariah names as the locus of the divine advent.
**Fulfillment status:** PARTIAL
**Fulfillment notes:** This is **allusion by setting**, not a citation — Acts names the Mount of Olives as a geographic fact (with its sabbath-distance), and does not quote or invoke Zechariah; the resonance is left for the reader who knows Zech 14. The PARTIAL label reflects that the connection is **suggestive but unstated**: the same mountain that Zech 14:4 marks as where YHWH's feet will stand is where Yeshua ascends and from which he is promised to return "in the manner" he left (v.11). Whether Luke intends the Zechariah echo or simply records the location is not made explicit, and the TT does not assert authorial intent (Rule 13). **Option C note:** in Zech 14, the one whose feet stand on the mountain is **YHWH** (rendered "the Lord" / YHWH per Option C, JHWH in DE on propagation); applying the place to Yeshua's coming fuses the referents — the TT marks the fusion as the reader's inference, not the text's claim. **[comparative parallel — POSSIBLE; the YHWH/Yeshua referent-fusion — POSSIBLE]** → See Cross-Chapter Tracking (Zech 14:4 → Acts 1:12).
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: Zechariah 14 is read as the prophecy of the Day of YHWH, when YHWH himself will stand on the Mount of Olives, the mountain will split, and YHWH will reign as king over all the earth — a still-future, unrealized hope. The mountain's association with the eschatological advent is well established; it is not connected to Yeshua's ascension. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: The ascension and the angels' promise of return are read against Zech 14:4 — the Mount of Olives as both departure point and the expected place of the Lord's eschatological coming. The geography is read as theologically charged, not incidental. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: The Quran does not engage Zechariah 14 or the Mount of Olives tradition; the eschatology of Isa's return is developed in hadith rather than in connection with this text. [DOCUMENTED]

## The twelve tribes / restoration motif — the reconstitution of the Twelve (Acts 1:15–26)

**Verse:** Acts 1:15–26
**Text says:** "It was necessary *for* the scripture to be fulfilled… *that* one of these become a witness of his resurrection together with us" (1:16, 22); "and the lot fell on Mattityahu (Matthias), and he was numbered with the eleven apostles" (1:26).
**Context:** The whole election scene (vv.15–26): Peter establishes the necessity, sets the criteria of apostleship (companionship from John's immersion to the ascension + witness to the resurrection), two are proposed, and Matthias is chosen — restoring the apostolic number to twelve before Pentecost.
**Subject:** The Twelve as the restored tribal structure of Israel. The number twelve answers to the **twelve tribes** (Gen 49; Num 1; Num 26 — the tribal census and the division of the land); the deliberate refilling of the Twelve, rather than leaving the apostles at eleven, signals the **reconstitution of Israel** at the threshold of the Spirit's outpouring. The motif connects to Yeshua's own word that the Twelve will "sit on thrones judging the twelve tribes of Israel" (Lk 22:30), and to the disciples' restoration-question of 1:6 ("restoring the kingdom to Israel").
**Fulfillment status:** PARTIAL
**Fulfillment notes:** This is **typological / structural allusion**, not a citation — there is no HB verse quoted for the twelve-tribe motif (the only quoted scriptures here are the two Psalms); the number itself carries the meaning. The PARTIAL label reflects that the restoration-of-Israel hope is **broader than this single act**: the reconstituted Twelve is a *sign* of the tribal restoration, but the kingdom-restoration question of 1:6 is explicitly left open ("not yours to know times or seasons," v.7), so the motif is enacted in part and deferred in part. Peter's "it was necessary for the scripture to be fulfilled" (v.16) attaches to the Psalm citations (the *mechanism* of replacing Judas), not to a twelve-tribe prophecy as such; the tribal symbolism is the text's structural claim rather than a fulfillment-of-prediction. **[comparative parallel — POSSIBLE]** → See Cross-Chapter Tracking (Num 1; the twelve tribes → Acts 1:15–26).
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: The twelve tribes are the foundational structure of Israel (Gen 49; Num 1; 26); the restoration of all twelve tribes is a recurring prophetic hope (e.g. Ezek 37; Isa 49:6). A restored twelvefold leadership would resonate with that hope; the specific identification of the apostolic Twelve as that restoration is not part of Jewish reading. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: The refilling of the Twelve is read as the deliberate reconstitution of Israel's tribal number, the foundation of the renewed people of God on whom the Spirit will be poured (ch. 2); it answers the restoration-question of 1:6 in an inaugurated, not yet consummated, sense. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: The Quran mentions the twelve tribes (*al-asbāṭ*, e.g. Surah 7:160) and twelve chiefs of the Israelites (5:12), but does not connect them to an apostolic Twelve or to a restoration enacted in Acts. [DOCUMENTED]

## Proverbs 16:33 — the lot's decision is the LORD's (Acts 1:26)

**Verse:** Acts 1:26
**Text says:** "And they gave lots for them, and the lot fell on Mattityahu (Matthias), and he was numbered with the eleven apostles."
**Context:** The conclusion of the election (after the prayer of v.24, "You, Lord, knower of *the* hearts of all, show *which* one… you chose"). The community casts lots and the lot falls on Matthias — the last use of the lot in Acts before the Spirit guides directly (ch. 2 onward).
**Subject:** The cast lot whose outcome is from YHWH. Proverbs 16:33 states the principle: "The lot is cast into the lap, but its **every decision is from YHWH**" (LXX Prov 16:33). The HB uses the lot as a recognized mode of discerning the divine choice — the priestly lots and the two goats (Lev 16:8), the allotment of the land (Num 26:55; Josh 14–19), the detection of Achan (Josh 7:14–18), the choice of Saul (1 Sam 10:20–21). The prayer "you chose" (v.24) and the cast lot (v.26) together express that the choice is God's, not the assembly's.
**Fulfillment status:** PARTIAL
**Fulfillment notes:** This is **allusion to a principle and a practice**, not a citation — Acts neither quotes Prov 16:33 nor uses a formula; it *enacts* the lot-practice the proverb describes and the HB models. The PARTIAL label reflects that the connection is to a **general scriptural principle** (the lot's outcome is YHWH's) rather than to a prediction fulfilled: there is nothing predictive to fulfill — the proverb states how divine choice is read in a cast lot. The prayer's "you chose" (v.24) makes the theological claim Prov 16:33 underwrites: the lot does not leave the choice to chance but discloses God's. **Option C note:** in Prov 16:33 the decider is **YHWH** ("from YHWH" / "from the LORD," JHWH in DE on propagation); the prayer of v.24 addresses **κύριε** ("Lord"), whose referent is itself debated (God the Father or the risen Yeshua who "chose" the Twelve, 1:2) — the TT keeps "Lord" and notes the ambiguity (see the main-text Tier-2 at 1:24). **[comparative parallel — POSSIBLE; the *kyrios* referent of the prayer — POSSIBLE]** → See Cross-Chapter Tracking (Prov 16:33; Lev 16:8 → Acts 1:26).
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: Proverbs 16:33 teaches that the outcome of a cast lot is determined by YHWH; the lot is a sanctioned means of discerning the divine will across the HB (the priestly lots, the allotment of the land, the choice of Saul). The principle is foundational; its application to the apostolic replacement is a Christian, not a Jewish, reading. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: The casting of lots for Matthias is read as the community's submission of the choice to God in line with Prov 16:33 — the last pre-Pentecost mode of discernment, after which the Spirit guides directly; "the lot fell" expresses divine, not random, choice. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: The Quran refers to the casting of lots in the story of Zakariya's guardianship of Maryam (Surah 3:44, *yulqūna aqlāmahum*, "casting their pens/reeds"), affirming lot-casting as a means of decision, but does not engage Prov 16:33 or the Matthias election. [DOCUMENTED]
