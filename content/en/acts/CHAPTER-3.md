## READING GUIDE

**Main Text:** Primary translation — readable but faithful to Greek structure. **Notes:** Essential Greek features immediately below each verse.

**How the text is marked:**
- *Words in italics* — added for English grammar (not in the Greek text)
- {a:wind/spirit} — a word the Greek leaves open to two meanings, both kept
- @@"…"@@ — direct speech of God or the holy wind/spirit

Notes are tagged by type — **Critical · Lexical · Grammatical · Theological** — each shown with its colour (see the legend at the top of the Notes view).

**This chapter pairs a healing with the second apostolic sermon.** At the temple's Beautiful Gate, Kefa and Yochanan heal a man lame from birth "in the name of Yeshua the Nazarene" (vv.1-10). The astonished crowd gathers at Solomon's portico, and Kefa preaches: it is not their own power but the God of the fathers who has glorified his servant Yeshua — whom they denied and killed but God raised — and faith in his name has made the man whole (vv.11-16). He calls them to change their minds, points to the prophets (Moses and Samuel), and reaffirms the Abrahamic covenant (vv.17-26). **Note on speech-marking (Rule 30):** Kefa's sermon is human speech and is not marked; the LORD's covenant word to Avraham (v.25, Gen 22:18) is divine speech and **is** marked @@…@@. The Moses citation (vv.22-23) is *Moses's* reported speech *about* the LORD, so it is not marked. **Watch the term-cruxes:** *archēgos* ("Author/Pioneer/Originator of life," v.15) and *pais* ("servant/child," vv.13, 26) carry the Isaianic-Servant overtone — both are flagged.

## CHAPTER OVERVIEW

**What happens:** Peter and John go up to the temple at the ninth hour of prayer (v.1). A man lame from birth, carried daily to the gate called Beautiful to beg, asks them for alms; Peter, with John, fixes his gaze and says he has no silver or gold but gives what he has: "in the name of Jesus the Nazarene, walk" (vv.2-6). Taking him by the hand, Peter raises him; his feet and ankles are made firm, and he enters the temple walking, leaping, and praising God (vv.7-10). As the amazed crowd runs together at Solomon's portico, Peter answers: why marvel, as if by our own power or piety? The God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob has glorified his servant Jesus, whom they handed over and denied before Pilate, asking for a murderer instead; they killed the Author of life, whom God raised — of which the apostles are witnesses; and it is faith in his name that has made the man whole (vv.11-16). Peter grants they acted in ignorance, as did their rulers, but God thus fulfilled what he had foretold through all the prophets — that his Messiah would suffer (vv.17-18). So: change your minds and turn, for the wiping away of sins, that times of refreshing may come and God may send the Messiah appointed for them, Jesus, whom heaven must receive until the restoration of all things spoken by the prophets (vv.19-21). Moses foretold a prophet like himself, to be heeded on pain of being cut off; all the prophets from Samuel announced these days (vv.22-24). They are the sons of the prophets and of the covenant: "in your seed all the families of the earth will be blessed" — and God, raising up his servant, sent him first to them, to bless them by turning each from wickedness (vv.25-26).

**Key themes:** "The name" of Jesus as the power that heals and saves (vv.6, 16); the apostles as channels, not sources, of power (v.12); the God of the patriarchs glorifying his "servant" (*pais*) Jesus — the Isaianic Servant overtone; the contrast pattern (you denied / God glorified; you killed / God raised); the Author/Pioneer of life killed yet raised; faith in the name as the means of wholeness; the suffering Messiah as the fulfillment of "all the prophets"; the call to change of mind and "turning," with "times of refreshing" and the "restoration of all things"; the prophet-like-Moses (Deut 18) identified with Jesus; the Abrahamic covenant and the blessing of "all the families of the earth" — Israel addressed first, the nations in view.

**Watch for:** *Archēgos* (v.15) means "leader, founder, pioneer, originator, author" — the TT renders "Author *of life*" and flags the range. *Pais* (vv.13, 26) means both "servant" and "child" — here the Isaianic "Servant of the LORD" (Isa 52-53) is in view; the TT renders "servant" and notes the overtone. *Apokatastasis* (v.21), "restoration of all things," is theologically debated; the TT keeps it without resolving its scope. The Moses citation (vv.22-23, Deut 18:15-19) is Moses's speech *about* the LORD and is not divine-marked; the Abrahamic word (v.25, Gen 22:18) is the LORD's direct covenant speech and is marked. *Kyrios* = YHWH in vv.19 ("the face of the Lord") and 22 ("the Lord God").

**Connection:** The sermon is saturated with the Hebrew Bible. "The God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, the God of our fathers" is the self-identification of YHWH at the bush (Ex 3:6, 15). "His servant" echoes the Servant Songs of Isaiah (42; 49; 52:13-53:12). The prophet-like-Moses is Deuteronomy 18:15-19. The Abrahamic blessing of "all the families of the earth" is Genesis 22:18 (cf. 12:3; 18:18; 26:4). Samuel as the head of the prophetic line recalls 1 Samuel 3. The "restoration" (*apokatastasis*) draws on the prophetic hope of Israel's renewal (e.g. Mal 4:5-6; Acts 1:6).

## VERSE-BY-VERSE STUDY

### **Verse 1**

Now Kefa (Peter) and Yochanan (John) were going up to the temple at the hour of prayer, the ninth.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"AT THE HOUR OF PRAYER, THE NINTH"**
> - **ἐπὶ τὴν ὥραν τῆς προσευχῆς τὴν ἐνάτην** (*epi tēn hōran tēs proseuchēs tēn enatēn*) = "at the hour of prayer, the ninth" (≈ 3 p.m., the hour of the evening sacrifice). Kefa and Yochanan keep the temple's prayer-times — the community remains within Israel's worship (cf. 2:46). The pair recall Lk 22:8 and Acts 1:13; 4:13.

---

### **Verse 2**

And a certain man, lame from his mother's womb, was being carried, whom they used to set daily at the gate of the temple called Beautiful, to ask alms from those entering into the temple.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"LAME FROM HIS MOTHER'S WOMB" + "THE GATE CALLED BEAUTIFUL"**
> - **χωλὸς ἐκ κοιλίας μητρὸς αὐτοῦ** (*chōlos ek koilias mētros autou*) = "lame from his mother's womb" — lifelong, like the man of 4:22 (over forty years); the cure is therefore unmistakable. **τὴν ὡραίαν** ("the Beautiful") gate is otherwise unattested by that name; possibly the Nicanor Gate. **ἐλεημοσύνην** (*eleēmosynēn*) = "alms, mercy-gift."

---

### **Verse 3**

He, seeing Kefa (Peter) and Yochanan (John) about to enter into the temple, was asking to receive alms.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **THE REQUEST**
> - The man's expectation is alms; what he receives is healing (vv.6-8) — the narrative turns his small request into the occasion of a sign.

---

### **Verse 4**

And Kefa (Peter), gazing at him together with Yochanan (John), said, "Look at us."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"GAZING" — *ATENISAS***
> - **ἀτενίσας… εἶπεν· βλέψον εἰς ἡμᾶς** (*atenisas… eipen: blepson eis hēmas*) = "gazing… said, 'Look at us.'" *Atenizō* ("fix the eyes intently") is a Lukan favorite (2:14 wordfield; Acts 1:10). The mutual gaze prepares the encounter of faith (v.16).

---

### **Verse 5**

And he fixed *his attention* on them, expecting to receive something from them.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"EXPECTING TO RECEIVE"**
> - **προσδοκῶν τι… λαβεῖν** (*prosdokōn ti labein*) = "expecting to receive something" — i.e., money. The verb of expectation heightens the reversal of v.6.

---

### **Verse 6**

But Kefa (Peter) said, "Silver and gold *is* not *mine*, but what I have, this I give you: in the name of Yeshua *the* Messiah (Jesus) the Nazarene — *rise and* walk."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "IN THE NAME OF YESHUA" (the name-motif)**
> - **ἐν τῷ ὀνόματι Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ τοῦ Ναζωραίου** (*en tō onomati Iēsou Christou tou Nazōraiou*) = "in the name of Yeshua *the* Messiah the Nazarene." "The name" (*onoma*) is the chapter's keyword — the channel of power (vv.6, 16) and of salvation (cf. 2:21, 38; 4:12). **ἔγειρε καὶ** ("rise and") is bracketed in NA28 (a witness-divided reading); the TT marks "*rise and*" as the disputed words and keeps "walk." **[textual: "rise and" — variant.]**

---

### **Verse 7**

And taking him by the right hand, he raised *him* up; and immediately his feet and ankles were made firm,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"WERE MADE FIRM" — *ESTEREŌTHĒSAN***
> - **ἐστερεώθησαν** (*estereōthēsan*) = "were made firm/solid" (the bones/joints). The same root (*stereoō*) returns at v.16 ("his name made *him* firm") — the physical firmness and the firmness given by the name are linked verbally.

---

### **Verse 8**

and leaping up, he stood and was walking, and entered with them into the temple, walking and leaping and praising God.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"WALKING AND LEAPING" — ISAIAH 35:6**
> - **ἁλλόμενος** (*hallomenos*, "leaping") echoes Isaiah 35:6 LXX ("then the lame will leap like a deer") — a sign of the promised restoration. The healed man enters the temple he had only sat outside of (v.2).

---

### **Verse 9**

And all the people saw him walking and praising God,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **PUBLIC WITNESS**
> - "All the people" see — the healing is public and verifiable (as the lifelong lameness, v.2, made it undeniable), setting up the crowd of v.11.

---

### **Verse 10**

and they recognized him, that this was the *one* sitting for alms at the Beautiful Gate of the temple, and they were filled with amazement and ecstasy at what had happened to him.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"AMAZEMENT AND ECSTASY" — *THAMBOUS KAI EKSTASEŌS***
> - **θάμβους καὶ ἐκστάσεως** (*thambous kai ekstaseōs*) = "amazement and ecstasy/astonishment." Their recognition that it is the *same* lifelong beggar makes the sign incontestable — the crowd's wonder is the occasion of the sermon.

---

### **Verse 11**

And as he held on to Kefa (Peter) and Yochanan (John), all the people ran together to them at the portico called *Solomon's* (Shlomoh's), utterly astonished.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"SOLOMON'S PORTICO"**
> - **τῇ στοᾷ τῇ καλουμένῃ Σολομῶντος** (*tē stoa tē kaloumenē Solomōntos*) = "the portico called Shlomoh's (Solomon's)" — a colonnade on the temple's east side, a gathering place for the early community (5:12; cf. Jn 10:23). **ἔκθαμβοι** = "utterly astonished."

---

### **Verse 12**

And seeing *this*, Kefa (Peter) answered the people, "Men, Israelites, why do you marvel at this, or why do you gaze at us, as if by our own power or piety we have made him walk?

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "NOT BY OUR OWN POWER OR PIETY"**
> - **ὡς ἰδίᾳ δυνάμει ἢ εὐσεβείᾳ** (*hōs idia dynamei ē eusebeia*) = "as if by our own power or piety." Kefa deflects all credit from the apostles — the power is God's, through the name (v.16). This is the interpretive key to the sign: the apostles are witnesses, not wonder-workers. → Companion §A1.

---

### **Verse 13**

The God of Avraham (Abraham) and *of* Yitschaq (Isaac) and *of* Ya'aqov (Jacob), the God of our fathers, glorified his servant Yeshua (Jesus), whom you handed over and denied before *the* face of Pilate, *when* that one had decided to release *him*.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "HIS SERVANT" (*PAIS*) — THE ISAIANIC SERVANT**
> - **ὁ θεὸς Ἀβραὰμ καὶ… ἐδόξασεν τὸν παῖδα αὐτοῦ Ἰησοῦν** (*ho theos Abraam… edoxasen ton paida autou Iēsoun*) = "the God of Avraham… glorified his **servant** (*pais*) Yeshua." The opening names YHWH by the patriarchal self-identification (Ex 3:6, 15). **παῖς** (*pais*) = "servant" or "child"; here the **Servant of the LORD** of Isaiah 52:13 ("my servant… shall be glorified," LXX *doxasthēsetai*) is in view — the TT renders "servant" and notes the child/servant range. **[*pais*: servant (Isaianic) — PROBABLE; "child" possible.]** → Companion §A2 (the Servant).

---

### **Verse 14**

But you denied the holy and righteous *one*, and asked *for* a man, a murderer, to be granted to you,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"THE HOLY AND RIGHTEOUS ONE" vs "A MURDERER"**
> - **τὸν ἅγιον καὶ δίκαιον** (*ton hagion kai dikaion*) = "the holy and righteous *one*" — titles of the Messiah (cf. 7:52; Isa 53:11 "the righteous one, my servant"). The antithesis — they asked for **ἄνδρα φονέα** ("a man, a murderer," i.e. Barabbas, Lk 23:18-25) instead of the righteous one — sharpens the indictment.

---

### **Verse 15**

but the Author of life you killed, whom God raised from *the* dead — of which we are witnesses.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "THE AUTHOR OF LIFE" — *ARCHĒGOS***
> - **τὸν δὲ ἀρχηγὸν τῆς ζωῆς ἀπεκτείνατε** (*ton de archēgon tēs zōēs apekteinate*) = "but the **Author** of life you killed." *Archēgos* = "leader, founder, pioneer, originator, author" — one who originates and leads the way (cf. Heb 2:10; 12:2). The TT renders "Author *of life*" and flags the range; the paradox is sharp — they killed the very originator of life, "whom God raised." **[*archēgos*: Author/Pioneer/Originator — the full range is intended; Uncertain which single English word.]** → Companion §A2.

---

### **Verse 16**

And upon the faith of his name, this *one* whom you see and know — his name made *him* firm; and the faith *that is* through him gave him this wholeness before you all.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "HIS NAME MADE HIM FIRM" (a dense, doubled verse)**
> - **ἐπὶ τῇ πίστει τοῦ ὀνόματος αὐτοῦ… ἐστερέωσεν τὸ ὄνομα αὐτοῦ** = "upon the faith of his name… his name made *him* firm." The Greek is famously crowded (faith / the name / this man / the faith through him); the TT keeps the doubling rather than smoothing it. *Estereōsen* ("made firm") ties back to the ankles "made firm" in v.7. **ὁλοκληρίαν** (*holoklērian*) = "wholeness, complete soundness." The healing is "the name" + "faith through him." **[the syntax is genuinely tangled; rendered to preserve, not resolve.]**

---

### **Verse 17**

"And now, brothers, I know that you acted according to ignorance, just as also your rulers;

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"ACCORDING TO IGNORANCE" — *KATA AGNOIAN***
> - **κατὰ ἄγνοιαν ἐπράξατε** (*kata agnoian epraxate*) = "you acted according to ignorance." Kefa shifts from indictment (vv.13-15) to appeal; ignorance does not excuse but opens the door to "change of mind" (v.19; cf. Lk 23:34). "Your rulers" too — the leaders are included.

---

### **Verse 18**

but God, the *things* which he foretold through *the* mouth of all the prophets — that his Messiah would suffer — he thus fulfilled.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "THAT HIS MESSIAH WOULD SUFFER"**
> - **παθεῖν τὸν Χριστὸν αὐτοῦ** (*pathein ton Christon autou*) = "that his Messiah would suffer." A suffering Messiah — read as the burden of "all the prophets" (esp. Isaiah 53) — is the interpretive claim of the early proclamation (cf. Lk 24:26-27, 46). Human guilt (vv.13-15) and divine foretelling are held together. → Companion §B1.

---

### **Verse 19**

Change *your* minds, therefore, and turn, for the wiping out of your sins,

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "CHANGE YOUR MINDS… AND TURN"**
> - **μετανοήσατε… καὶ ἐπιστρέψατε** (*metanoēsate… kai epistrepsate*) = "Change *your* minds… and turn." **μετάνοια** = "change of mind" (locked GS term); paired with **ἐπιστρέφω** ("turn [to God]") — the prophetic call to *shuv* (return). **εἰς τὸ ἐξαλειφθῆναι** ("for the wiping out") — the goal/result; *exaleiphō* = "wipe away, erase" (a ledger metaphor). → Companion §B2.

---

### **Verse 20**

so that times of refreshing may come from *the* face of the Lord, and *that* he may send the *one* foreordained for you, *the* Messiah, Yeshua (Jesus),

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"TIMES OF REFRESHING" + κύριος = YHWH**
> - **καιροὶ ἀναψύξεως ἀπὸ προσώπου τοῦ κυρίου** (*kairoi anapsyxeōs apo prosōpou tou kyriou*) = "times of refreshing from *the* face of the Lord." *Kyrios* = **YHWH** (Option C). *Anapsyxis* ("refreshing, relief") is rare — the eschatological relief tied to repentance. The Messiah is "**foreordained**" (*prokecheirismenon*) for them.

---

### **Verse 21**

whom heaven must receive until *the* times of restoration of all *things*, of which God spoke through *the* mouth of his holy prophets from of old.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "RESTORATION OF ALL THINGS" — *APOKATASTASIS***
> - **ἄχρι χρόνων ἀποκαταστάσεως πάντων** (*achri chronōn apokatastaseōs pantōn*) = "until *the* times of **restoration of all *things***." *Apokatastasis* = "restoration, re-establishment" — the prophetic renewal of all things (cf. the disciples' "restore the kingdom," 1:6, same root). Its scope is debated (cosmic renewal; Israel's restoration; universal); the TT keeps it without resolving. **[*apokatastasis*: scope debated — rendered literally, Uncertain.]** → Companion §B3.

---

### **Verse 22**

Mosheh (Moses) indeed said that 'A prophet for you *the* Lord God will raise up from your brothers, like me; him you shall hear in all *things*, whatever he may speak to you.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE PROPHET LIKE MOSES (Deut 18:15-19)**
> - **προφήτην ὑμῖν ἀναστήσει κύριος ὁ θεὸς… ὡς ἐμέ** (*prophētēn hymin anastēsei kyrios ho theos… hōs eme*) = "A prophet for you *the* Lord God will raise up… like me." This is **Moses's** reported speech *about* the LORD (κύριος = YHWH, Option C), so it is **not** divine-marked. The prophet-like-Moses (Deut 18:15-19) is identified with Yeshua; "you shall hear him" makes heeding him decisive (v.23). → Companion §B4.

---

### **Verse 23**

And it will be *that* every soul, whoever does not hear that prophet, will be utterly destroyed from the people.'

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"WILL BE UTTERLY DESTROYED FROM THE PEOPLE"**
> - **ἐξολεθρευθήσεται ἐκ τοῦ λαοῦ** (*exolethreuthēsetai ek tou laou*) = "will be utterly destroyed from the people" — Acts sharpens Deut 18:19 (with language from Lev 23:29) into a "cut off from the people" warning. Heeding the prophet-like-Moses is the dividing line.

---

### **Verse 24**

And indeed all the prophets, from Shmuel (Samuel) and those *who came* after, as many as spoke, also announced these days.

> **Notes**
>
> 🔵 **"FROM SAMUEL" — THE PROPHETIC LINE**
> - **ἀπὸ Σαμουὴλ καὶ τῶν καθεξῆς** (*apo Samouēl kai tōn kathexēs*) = "from Shmuel (Samuel) and those after." Samuel heads the line of the "former prophets"; the whole prophetic witness "announced these days" — the present time as the prophets' horizon.

---

### **Verse 25**

You are the sons of the prophets and of the covenant which God covenanted with your fathers, saying to Avraham (Abraham), @@'And in your seed all the families of the earth will be blessed.'@@

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — THE ABRAHAMIC COVENANT (Gen 22:18); DIVINE SPEECH**
> - **ἐν τῷ σπέρματί σου ἐνευλογηθήσονται πᾶσαι αἱ πατριαὶ τῆς γῆς** (*en tō spermati sou eneulogēthēsontai pasai hai patriai tēs gēs*) = "And in your seed all the families of the earth will be blessed" (Gen 22:18; cf. 12:3). This is **the LORD's direct covenant word to Avraham** → marked @@…@@ (Rule 30 / A-004). "Sons of the prophets and of the covenant" — the hearers are heirs of the promise; "all the families of the earth" holds the nations in view, though Israel is addressed "first" (v.26). → Companion §B5 (the Abrahamic blessing).

---

### **Verse 26**

To you first, God, having raised up his servant, sent him, blessing you in *the* turning *of* each *of you* from your wickednesses."

> **Notes**
>
> 🔴 **CRITICAL — "TO YOU FIRST" + "HIS SERVANT" (*PAIS*)**
> - **ὑμῖν πρῶτον ἀναστήσας ὁ θεὸς τὸν παῖδα αὐτοῦ ἀπέστειλεν αὐτὸν** = "To you **first**, God, having raised up his **servant** (*pais*), sent him." "First" implies a "then" (the nations), consistent with Gen 22:18 (v.25) and the program of 1:8. *Pais* again = the Servant (cf. v.13). The blessing is moral-transformative: "in turning each *of you* from your wickednesses." **ἀναστήσας** may mean "raised up [in history]" or "raised [from the dead]" — the TT keeps "having raised up." **[*anastēsas*: raised-up/resurrected — Possible both.]**

## Glossary — Acts 3

| Term | Greek | Meaning in context |
|------|-------|--------------------|
| **the name** | ὄνομα (*onoma*) | "The name" of Yeshua — the channel of healing (vv.6, 16) and salvation; the chapter's keyword. |
| **made firm** | στερεόω (*stereoō*) | The ankles "made firm" (v.7); "his name made *him* firm" (v.16) — the same root links sign and source. |
| **servant** | παῖς (*pais*) | "Servant" (or "child") — the Isaianic Servant of the LORD (vv.13, 26; Isa 52-53). |
| **Author** | ἀρχηγός (*archēgos*) | Leader / founder / pioneer / originator / author — "the Author of life" (v.15). |
| **wholeness** | ὁλοκληρία (*holoklēria*) | Complete soundness, full health (v.16). |
| **change of mind** | μετάνοια / μετανοέω (*metanoia*) | The locked rendering (v.19); paired with "turn" (*epistrephō*). |
| **times of refreshing** | καιροὶ ἀναψύξεως (*kairoi anapsyxeōs*) | Eschatological relief from "the face of the Lord" (v.20). |
| **restoration of all things** | ἀποκατάστασις πάντων (*apokatastasis pantōn*) | Prophetic renewal of all things (v.21); scope debated. |
| **the Lord** | κύριος (*kyrios*) | Option C: YHWH in vv.19-20 ("the face of the Lord") and v.22 ("the Lord God"). |
| **utterly destroyed** | ἐξολεθρεύω (*exolethreuō*) | "Cut off" from the people (v.23; Deut 18:19 + Lev 23:29). |

## Cross-Chapter Tracking (HB -> Acts)

| HB / earlier source | Theme | In Acts 3 |
|---------------------|-------|-----------|
| Ex 3:6, 15 | "The God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob" | YHWH named by the patriarchal self-identification (v.13). |
| Isa 52:13-53:12 | The Servant of the LORD glorified after suffering | "His **servant** (*pais*) Yeshua… glorified" (vv.13, 26); the suffering Messiah (v.18). |
| Isa 35:6 | "Then the lame will leap like a deer" | The healed man "leaping" into the temple (vv.8). |
| Deut 18:15-19 | The prophet like Moses | Cited and identified with Yeshua (vv.22-23). |
| Gen 22:18 (cf. 12:3) | "In your seed all the families of the earth will be blessed" | The LORD's covenant word, marked as divine speech (v.25). |
| 1 Sam 3 | Samuel, head of the prophetic line | "All the prophets from Shmuel" announced these days (v.24). |
| Mal 4:5-6; Acts 1:6 | Restoration / turning of hearts | "Times of restoration of all things" (v.21); "change of mind and turn" (v.19). |
