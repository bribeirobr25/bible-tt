## The Son of Man "lifted up" — Numbers 21 parallel (John 3:14–15)

**Verse:** John 3:14–15
**Text says:** "And as Mosheh (Moses) lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up, so that everyone trusting in him may have life of the age."
**Context:** Yeshua (Jesus) speaks to Nikodemos (Nicodemus) at night. Verses 14–15 form the pivot of the discourse, moving from rebirth language (vv.3–8) to the salvific role of the Son of Man.
**Subject:** The Son of Man — the typological parallel maps the Numbers 21 bronze serpent (Moses lifted up; those who looked, lived) onto Yeshua's death (lifted up on the cross; those who trust, live). The double meaning of *hypsōthēnai* ("lifted up" / "exalted") collapses crucifixion and glorification into a single event.
**Fulfillment status:** CLAIMED
**Fulfillment notes:** The Numbers 21:8–9 parallel is cited explicitly by Yeshua (v.14) — not introduced by a fulfillment formula (cf. Matthew's ἵνα πληρωθῇ). The Greek verb ὑψόω (*hypsoō*) carries both physical ("lift up") and theological ("exalt") senses; in the Fourth Gospel this double meaning operates consistently at 3:14, 8:28, and 12:32–34. The bronze-serpent type was later destroyed by King Chizkiyahu (2 Kings 18:4) when the Yisraelites turned it into an idol — the healing object became a stumbling block. Whether John engages this ironic afterlife is unresolved. → See John 3 companion §A6 (*Hypsōthēnai* double meaning) and §B2 (full bronze-serpent background) for the lexical and historical-critical treatment.
**Readings:**
- **Jewish**: Numbers 21 is read as a historical narrative of wilderness rebellion and YHWH's provision of a healing sign, not as a future prophecy of a messianic figure. The bronze serpent's destruction in 2 Kings 18 closes the type within the Hebrew Bible's own canon. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Christian**: John 3:14 is the foundational text for typological reading of the cross. Patristic interpreters (Justin Martyr, *Dialogue* 91; Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 4.2.7) read the bronze serpent as a deliberate type of Christ's crucifixion. The Johannine triple use of *hypsoō* (3:14, 8:28, 12:32) is read as evidence that John deliberately collapses crucifixion and exaltation into one. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Islamic**: The Quran does not engage Numbers 21 or John 3:14 directly. Islamic tradition denies the crucifixion as actual death (Quran 4:157); the typological reading therefore has no Islamic uptake. [DOCUMENTED]
