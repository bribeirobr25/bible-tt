## Yochanan the Immerser (John the Baptist)
**See:** matthew/PEOPLE.md
**In John:** Introduced in the prologue as the witness (1:6–8, 15) — distinguished from the Logos. Receives a delegation from the Yehudim in Yerushalayim asking who he is (1:19–28); denies being the anointed one, Eliyahu (Elijah), or the prophet; identifies himself by quoting Isaiah 40:3. Testifies at the Yarden (Jordan): "Behold the lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world" (1:29), and witnesses the descent of the spirit "like a dove" (1:32–34). Reappears at 3:22–36 alongside Yeshua's own immersion ministry, framed as "the friend of the bridegroom" — declines centrality with the formula "he must increase, but I must decrease" (3:30). The canonical bio fields (birth, lineage, lifestyle, death by Antipas) live in matthew/PEOPLE.md per Phase 6.6G; this entry adds the John-specific witness role.

## Yeshua (Jesus)
**See:** matthew/PEOPLE.md
**In John:** Introduced cosmologically as the Logos (the word) — preexistent, with God, God himself (1:1–18). Named at 1:17. Called the lamb of God by Yochanan (1:29, 36); recognized as the anointed one by Andreas (1:41), the son of God and king of Yisra'el by Nathanael (1:49). Performs his first sign at Qanah (Cana) — water-to-wine at the wedding (2:1–11). Cleanses the temple at Pesach (Passover) (2:13–22), with a saying about the temple-of-his-body (2:19–22) that the followers understand only retrospectively. Receives Nikodemos by night for the rebirth/anōthen dialogue (3:1–21) — the chapter containing the lifted-Son-of-Man / Numbers 21 typology (3:14–15) and the formula "for God so loved the world" (3:16). The canonical bio fields (birth, parents, lifespan, profession, death by crucifixion) live in matthew/PEOPLE.md per Phase 6.6G; this entry adds the John-specific narrative role.

## Andreas (Andrew)
**Meaning:** "manly" / "courageous" (Greek Ἀνδρέας from ἀνήρ, "man" — a Greek name, unusual among Galilean fishermen and suggesting the Hellenistic cultural reach of the Bet-Tsaidah (Bethsaida) community)
**Origin:** BORN
**Birth year:** c. 5–10 BCE (POSSIBLE INFERENCE — POSSIBLE; placed roughly before Yeshua's ministry to allow for adult fishing-trade status by c. 27 CE)
**Death year:** traditionally c. 60–70 CE (LATER RECEPTION — POSSIBLE; martyrdom by crucifixion at Patras in Achaea per Acts of Andrew and patristic tradition — not in canonical NT)
**Lifespan:** ~70 years per LATER RECEPTION tradition (POSSIBLE)
**Father:** Yonah (John, Greek Ἰωάννης) — Jn 1:42; cf. Mt 16:17 "Simon Bar-Yonah"
**Mother:** not stated in the NT
**Spouse(s):** not stated in the NT
**Children:** not stated in the NT
**Location(s):** Bet-Tsaidah (Bethsaida) — Andreas's hometown (Jn 1:44); later associated with Kapharnachum (Capernaum) (Mt 4:13, Mk 1:21)
**First mention:** Jn 1:40
**Mentioned in:** Jn 1:40, 41, 44; 6:8; 12:22
**Key events in John 1–3:** Disciple of Yochanan (1:35–40); upon Yochanan's testimony, follows Yeshua at the Yarden — one of the first two called (1:37); spends the rest of the day with Yeshua at his lodging (1:38–39); brings his brother Shimon Kefa (Simon Peter) to Yeshua with the declaration "We have found the anointed one" (1:41–42) — the first explicit messianic confession in the Fourth Gospel.

**Familiar name:** Andrew
**Profession:** fisherman (Mk 1:16 — Synoptic vocation; consistent with Bet-Tsaidah's fishing economy on the Kinneret)
**Social class:** Galilean artisan / fishing-trade class — taxed under Herod Antipas
**Hometown:** Bet-Tsaidah (Bethsaida) on the north shore of the Kinneret (Sea of Galilee) (Jn 1:44)
**Places lived:** Bet-Tsaidah (origin); Kapharnachum (later Synoptic ministry); traditional missionary destinations include Scythia, Greece (LATER RECEPTION)
**Cause of death:** martyrdom per LATER RECEPTION tradition (Acts of Andrew, 2nd–3rd c. CE); not in canonical NT
**Siblings:** Shimon Kefa (Simon Peter) — Jn 1:40; Mt 4:18
**Archaeological evidence:** No direct archaeological evidence for Andreas as an individual. Bet-Tsaidah (Bethsaida) has been excavated (identified with et-Tell, with a competing identification at el-Araj on the lakeshore); both sites attest 1st-century fishing-village occupation consistent with the Gospel's setting.
**Extra-biblical mentions:** Eusebius, *Ecclesiastical History* 3.1 (apostolic-mission tradition placing Andreas in Scythia); Acts of Andrew (apocryphal, 2nd–3rd c. CE — martyrdom narrative). Not mentioned in Josephus.
**Historicity status:** PROBABLE — named in all four Gospels + Acts as one of the Twelve; multiple-attestation by Synoptics + John supports historicity even though direct archaeological evidence is absent.
**Books appearing in:** John; Matthew (4:18; 10:2); Mark (1:16, 29; 3:18; 13:3); Luke (6:14); Acts (1:13)
**Character arc:** A bridge-figure in John — disciple-of-Yochanan turned disciple-of-Yeshua, then bringer-of-his-brother. The Gospel's first explicit "we have found the anointed one" comes from his lips. Reappears briefly at 6:8 (the five-loaves boy) and 12:22 (bringing Greeks to Yeshua) — always in a connecting role. Acts 1:13 lists him among the Twelve in the upper room after the resurrection.

**Note:** The Greek name *Andreas* (from *anēr*, "man") is unusual among 1st-century Galilean Jews and suggests the Hellenistic cultural reach of Bet-Tsaidah; his brother's Aramaic name Shimon (Simon) shows the bilingual / bicultural milieu typical of the region. Both brothers are named in Acts as members of the post-resurrection community.

## Shimon Kefa (Simon Peter)
**Meaning:** "Shimon" — Hebrew שִׁמְעוֹן (*Shim'on*, "he has heard" — wordplay on hearing/listening); "Kefa" — Aramaic כֵּיפָא (*Kepha*, "rock"), rendered into Greek as Πέτρος (*Petros*); the name *Kefa* is given by Yeshua at 1:42 — the only place in John where the renaming is narrated, paralleled in Mt 16:18 (with different timing and framing).
**Origin:** BORN
**Birth year:** c. 1–10 BCE (POSSIBLE INFERENCE — POSSIBLE; adult fishing-trade status by c. 27 CE places birth in the late BCE / early CE window)
**Death year:** traditionally c. 64–68 CE (LATER RECEPTION — PROBABLE; martyrdom under Nero in Rome per 1 Clement 5:4 and later patristic tradition — Acts narrates his ministry through Acts 12 but does not describe his death)
**Lifespan:** ~65–70 years per LATER RECEPTION tradition (POSSIBLE)
**Father:** Yonah (John, Greek Ἰωάννης) — Jn 1:42; cf. Mt 16:17
**Mother:** not stated in the NT
**Spouse(s):** married — referenced indirectly via the healing of his mother-in-law (Mt 8:14, Mk 1:30, Lk 4:38); 1 Cor 9:5 mentions Kefa traveling with a wife
**Children:** not stated in the NT
**Location(s):** Bet-Tsaidah (Bethsaida) — origin shared with brother Andreas (Jn 1:44); later resident at Kapharnachum (Capernaum) per the Synoptics (Mt 8:14, Mk 1:21, 29)
**First mention:** Jn 1:40 (introduced as Andreas's brother); 1:42 (named and renamed)
**Mentioned in:** Jn 1:40, 41, 42, 44; 6:8, 68; 13:6–9, 24, 36–38; 18:10–11, 15–18, 25–27; 20:2–10; 21:2–22
**Key events in John 1–3:** Brought to Yeshua by his brother Andreas (1:41–42) — the second of the first-called pair. Renamed by Yeshua: "You are Shimon (Simon), the son of Yonah (John); you shall be called Kefa (Peter)" (1:42) — Yeshua looks at him and gives the name immediately, before any narrated action or confession; this differs from the Synoptic timing (Mt 16:18, after Kefa's confession at Caesarea Philippi).

**Familiar name:** Simon Peter
**Profession:** fisherman (Mk 1:16 — Synoptic vocation; shared with brother Andreas; the family fishing operation is implied by Mt 4:18, Mk 1:16 "casting a net into the sea")
**Social class:** Galilean artisan / fishing-trade class
**Hometown:** Bet-Tsaidah (Bethsaida) (Jn 1:44)
**Places lived:** Bet-Tsaidah; Kapharnachum (residence per Synoptics); later Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) per Acts; Rome per LATER RECEPTION
**Cause of death:** martyrdom under Nero per LATER RECEPTION tradition (1 Clement 5:4; Tacitus *Annals* 15.44 implies broader anti-Christian persecution under Nero c. 64 CE); not in canonical NT
**Siblings:** Andreas (Andrew) — Jn 1:40
**Archaeological evidence:** A 1st-century house at Kapharnachum, identified by some archaeologists as Kefa's residence (the so-called "House of Peter"), has been excavated near the Kapharnachum synagogue and shows continuous Christian veneration from the 1st century forward. Identification is widely accepted though not certain.
**Extra-biblical mentions:** 1 Clement 5:4 (c. 96 CE — martyrdom under Nero); Eusebius *Ecclesiastical History* 2.25, 3.1 (apostolic tradition); Tacitus *Annals* 15.44 implies the broader Neronian persecution context. Not mentioned by name in Josephus.
**Historicity status:** PROBABLE — named in all four Gospels + Acts + Pauline letters (1 Cor 1:12, 9:5, 15:5; Gal 1:18, 2:7–14); multiple-attestation across Synoptics + John + Acts + Paul supports historicity.
**Books appearing in:** John; Matthew (4:18; 10:2; 14:28–31; 16:13–23; 17:1–8; 18:21–22; 26:33–35, 69–75); Mark (1:16–18; 8:29; 14:29–31, 66–72); Luke (5:1–11; 22:31–34, 54–62; 24:12); Acts (1–15; especially 2, 10, 15); 1 Cor; Gal; 1 Pet; 2 Pet; Jude (cf. brother-of-Yaakov-of-Galilee tradition)
**Character arc:** In John 1, Kefa enters as Andreas's brother and is immediately renamed by Yeshua — no preliminary action required. The narrative trajectory begins with this re-identification (rock-name given before any rock-like behavior is demonstrated). The renaming sets the stage for John's chapter 21 restoration scene, where the same Shimon Bar-Yonah is asked three times "do you love me?" — answering for the three denials at the trial.

**Note:** The early renaming in John 1:42 contrasts with the Synoptic Caesarea Philippi scene (Mt 16:18, c. midway through Yeshua's ministry, after Kefa's confession). John's narrative compression treats the name as proleptic — given before it is earned. The TT renders both *Shimon* (Hebrew) and *Kefa* (Aramaic) per the locked transliteration policy.

## Philippos (Philip)
**Meaning:** "lover of horses" (Greek Φίλιππος from *philos* + *hippos*) — a Greek name, again signaling the Hellenistic cultural reach of Bet-Tsaidah (the same town as Andreas + Kefa); the same name as Philip the Tetrarch (Herod's son), illustrating the name's currency in Roman-period Palestine
**Origin:** BORN
**Birth year:** c. 5–10 BCE (POSSIBLE INFERENCE — POSSIBLE; same reasoning as Andreas — adult disciple-status by c. 27 CE)
**Death year:** traditionally late 1st century CE (LATER RECEPTION — POSSIBLE; tradition places him in Hierapolis in Phrygia per Polycrates of Ephesus cited by Eusebius — but Polycrates may have conflated Philip the apostle with Philip the evangelist of Acts 6, 8, 21)
**Lifespan:** unknown; ~70 years per LATER RECEPTION (POSSIBLE)
**Father:** not stated in the NT
**Mother:** not stated in the NT
**Spouse(s):** not stated for Philip the apostle; Polycrates mentions daughters but the identification is contested
**Children:** uncertain — see spouse note above
**Location(s):** Bet-Tsaidah (Bethsaida) — Philippos's hometown (Jn 1:44, explicit identification)
**First mention:** Jn 1:43
**Mentioned in:** Jn 1:43, 44, 45, 46, 48; 6:5, 7; 12:21, 22; 14:8, 9
**Key events in John 1–3:** Called directly by Yeshua: "Follow me" (1:43) — the only one of the first-called whom Yeshua actively summons (Andreas and Kefa came via Yochanan + Andreas; Nathanael comes via Philippos). Identified as from Bet-Tsaidah, "from the city of Andreas and Kefa" (1:44). Finds Nathanael and tells him: "We have found him of whom Mosheh (Moses) in the Torah and the prophets wrote — Yeshua of Natseret (Nazareth), the son of Yosef (Joseph)" (1:45). Responds to Nathanael's skepticism — "Can anything good come from Natseret?" (1:46) — with the simple invitation "Come and see" (1:46). Brings Nathanael to Yeshua.

**Familiar name:** Philip
**Profession:** not stated in the NT for Philip the apostle (distinct from Philip the evangelist of Acts 6 onwards, who is a deacon and proclaimer)
**Social class:** Galilean — implied by his Bet-Tsaidah origin and association with the fishermen Andreas + Kefa
**Hometown:** Bet-Tsaidah (Bethsaida) (Jn 1:44)
**Places lived:** Bet-Tsaidah; later traditions place him in Hierapolis (Phrygia, modern-day Turkey)
**Cause of death:** martyrdom per LATER RECEPTION tradition (Hierapolis); details vary
**Siblings:** not stated in the NT
**Archaeological evidence:** A tomb at Hierapolis (Pamukkale, Turkey) was identified in 2011 by Italian excavators as the traditional tomb of Philip the apostle. Identification is plausible but not certain — the same site may have been venerated for Philip the evangelist of Acts 6.
**Extra-biblical mentions:** Polycrates of Ephesus (late 2nd c. CE) cited by Eusebius *Ecclesiastical History* 3.31, 5.24 (Hierapolis tradition + the daughters); Papias of Hierapolis (early 2nd c. CE) mentions Philip's daughters as prophetic figures. Identifications with Philip the evangelist (Acts 6, 8, 21) are sometimes conflated in patristic sources.
**Historicity status:** PROBABLE — named in all four Gospels (Synoptic apostle lists + John's expanded narrative) + Acts 1:13; multiple-attestation supports historicity.
**Books appearing in:** John; Matthew (10:3); Mark (3:18); Luke (6:14); Acts (1:13)
**Character arc:** In John 1, Philippos is the active recruiter — the only first-called whom Yeshua summons directly, and immediately he goes to find Nathanael. His response to skepticism ("Come and see") is one of the Gospel's most quoted phrases. Reappears at 6:5–7 (the five-loaves dialogue, where his arithmetic answer signals incomprehension), 12:21–22 (the Greeks at the feast), and 14:8 ("show us the father, and that is enough"). A practical, sometimes-incomprehending recruiter-figure whose role is to bring others.

**Note:** Distinguish carefully from Philip the evangelist (Acts 6:5, 8:5–40, 21:8), a different figure who is one of the seven deacons and the father of the four prophetic daughters. Patristic traditions sometimes conflate the two.

## Nathanael
**Meaning:** "God has given" (Hebrew נְתַנְאֵל *Netan'el* — from *natan*, "to give" + *El*, "God"); the name appears 6× in OT figures (Num 1:8, etc.) and is consistent with 1st-century Palestinian Jewish naming
**Origin:** BORN
**Birth year:** c. 5–10 BCE (POSSIBLE INFERENCE — POSSIBLE; adult disciple-status by c. 27 CE)
**Death year:** not stated; traditions vary
**Lifespan:** unknown
**Father:** not stated in the NT (Tholmai, per the Bar-Tholmai/Bartholomew identification — see Note)
**Mother:** not stated in the NT
**Spouse(s):** not stated in the NT
**Children:** not stated in the NT
**Location(s):** Qanah (Cana) of Galilee per Jn 21:2 (the only place where Nathanael's hometown is specified)
**First mention:** Jn 1:45
**Mentioned in:** Jn 1:45–51; 21:2
**Key events in John 1–3:** Brought to Yeshua by Philippos with the introduction "we have found him of whom Mosheh and the prophets wrote — Yeshua of Natseret" (1:45). Responds skeptically: "Can anything good come from Natseret?" (1:46) — a remark whose force depends on Natseret's negligible reputation in the period. Accepts Philippos's invitation "Come and see" (1:46). Approached by Yeshua: "Behold, an Yisra'eli (Israelite) indeed, in whom is no deceit" (1:47). Asks how Yeshua knows him; Yeshua answers: "Before Philippos called you, when you were under the fig tree, I saw you" (1:48). Responds with the first full christological confession in John: "Rabbi, you are the son of God, you are the king of Yisra'el" (1:49). Yeshua promises: "You shall see greater things... you shall see the sky opened and the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of Man" (1:50–51) — the Ya'aqov (Jacob)'s-ladder echo (cf. Gen 28:12).

**Familiar name:** Nathanael
**Profession:** not stated in the NT
**Social class:** Galilean — implied by his Qanah origin
**Hometown:** Qanah (Cana) of Galilee — Jn 21:2 (the only specification; not stated in John 1, where Philippos identifies him only by his presence under the fig tree)
**Places lived:** Qanah; later traditions vary
**Cause of death:** not stated in the NT; traditions associate Bartholomew (a possible alternate identification) with martyrdom in Armenia or India
**Siblings:** not stated in the NT
**Archaeological evidence:** No direct archaeological evidence for Nathanael. Khirbet Qana (the most plausible site for biblical Qanah) has been excavated; pre-Byzantine 1st-century occupation is well-attested.
**Extra-biblical mentions:** None in canonical extra-biblical material under the name "Nathanael." If identified with Bar-Tholmai (Bartholomew, per the Synoptic apostle lists), later traditions (Eusebius *Eccl. Hist.* 5.10) place him in mission to India.
**Historicity status:** POSSIBLE — Nathanael appears only in John (1, 21); the Synoptic apostle lists name "Bar-Tholmai" (Bartholomew) but never "Nathanael." A common harmonization identifies the two (Nathanael bar Tholmai), but this is uncertain and the multiple-attestation argument is weaker than for Andreas / Kefa / Philippos.
**Books appearing in:** John (only — 1, 21); possibly Matthew (10:3 Bartholomew); possibly Mark (3:18); possibly Luke (6:14); possibly Acts (1:13)
**Character arc:** In John 1, Nathanael moves from skepticism ("can anything good come from Natseret?") to the highest confession in the chapter ("son of God, king of Yisra'el") within four verses. The fig-tree encounter (whose meaning is left unexplained) is the catalyst. Yeshua's response — the Ya'aqov's-ladder vision — connects Nathanael to the patriarchal foundation of Yisra'el (a true Yisra'eli in whom is no deceit, in contrast to the original Ya'aqov-of-the-deceptions). Reappears briefly at 21:2 in the post-resurrection fishing scene at the Kinneret.

**Note:** The identification with Bar-Tholmai (Bartholomew) of the Synoptic apostle lists is a longstanding harmonization but not stated by the text. Nathanael may be a distinct figure, or may simply be referred to by his personal name in John (where the Bartholomew patronymic is unused). The TT does not resolve the question. The fig-tree image (1:48) was a conventional symbol for Torah meditation in rabbinic literature; whether Yeshua's "I saw you under the fig tree" implies prophetic clairvoyance or pre-arranged encounter is open.

## Nikodemos (Nicodemus)
**Meaning:** "victory of the people" (Greek Νικόδημος from *nikē* + *dēmos*) — a Greek name common in 1st-century Palestinian Jewish circles (Greek names alongside Hebrew/Aramaic names were normal)
**Origin:** BORN
**Birth year:** not stated in the NT
**Death year:** not stated in the NT
**Lifespan:** not stated
**Father:** not stated in the NT (rabbinic Nakdimon ben Gorion, b. Gittin 56a, b. Ta'anit 19b–20a, has a father by name — but the identification of the rabbinic figure with the Johannine Nikodemos is speculative)
**Mother:** not stated in the NT
**Spouse(s):** not stated in the NT
**Children:** not stated in the NT
**Location(s):** Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) — implied by his Pharisaic + ruler-of-the-Yehudim status; he comes to Yeshua at night during the Pesach (Passover) visit (3:2)
**First mention:** Jn 3:1
**Mentioned in:** Jn 3:1–21; 7:50–52; 19:39
**Key events in John 1–3:** Identified as a Pharisee and "a ruler of the Yehudim" (3:1) — likely a member of the Sanhedrin (the council of seventy-one elders that served as the supreme judicial body in Yehudah under Roman oversight). Comes to Yeshua by night (3:2) — the "night" is a recurrent Johannine motif (cf. 9:4, 11:10, 13:30). Opens with a deferential acknowledgement: "Rabbi, we know that you have come from God as a teacher; no one can do these signs you do unless God is with him" (3:2). Receives Yeshua's anōthen / pneuma / hypsōthēnai discourse (3:3–15). The narrator may continue speaking through 3:21 (the speech-boundary is ambiguous; see John 3 companion §A5). Reappears at 7:50–52 (defending due process before the Sanhedrin against the rush to condemn Yeshua) and 19:39 (bringing burial spices — 100 litras of myrrh and aloes — for Yeshua's body, with Yosef of Arimathea).

**Familiar name:** Nicodemus
**Profession:** Pharisee + ruler of the Yehudim — religious-legal authority within the Jerusalem leadership
**Social class:** Pharisaic elite (the Pharisees were a lay renewal movement; "ruler of the Yehudim" implies institutional power, likely Sanhedrin membership)
**Hometown:** Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) — implied
**Places lived:** Yerushalayim
**Cause of death:** not stated in the NT
**Siblings:** not stated in the NT
**Archaeological evidence:** No direct archaeological evidence for Nikodemos as an individual. An ossuary inscription bearing the name "Nakdimon" was found in Jerusalem (1st century CE); rabbinic sources mention a wealthy Jerusalem figure named Nakdimon ben Gorion (b. Gittin 56a) — identification with the Johannine Nikodemos is speculative but not impossible.
**Extra-biblical mentions:** Rabbinic literature mentions Nakdimon ben Gorion as a wealthy Jerusalem patron in the late Second Temple period (b. Gittin 56a; b. Ta'anit 19b–20a). The identification with the Johannine Nikodemos is contested and unverifiable.
**Historicity status:** POSSIBLE — Nikodemos appears only in John (3, 7, 19); the rabbinic Nakdimon ben Gorion is a separately-attested figure but the identification is uncertain.
**Books appearing in:** John (only)
**Character arc:** In John 3, Nikodemos is the curious-but-confused inquirer — his question "how can these things be?" (3:9) signals incomprehension, and Yeshua's reply ("you are the teacher of Yisra'el and you do not know these things?") underscores it. But Nikodemos returns: at 7:50–52 defending Yeshua's right to a hearing, at 19:39 publicly aligning himself with Yeshua's burial. The arc is from secret night-visitor to public burial-spice-bringer — slow but real movement.

**Note:** The text says "this one came to him by night" without explaining the nocturnal timing. Three readings are possible and not mutually exclusive: (1) he feared public association with Yeshua; (2) night was the customary time for Torah study (cf. Joshua 1:8); (3) "night" carries symbolic weight in John's light/darkness motif. The TT preserves the ambiguity. See John 3 companion §C1 for the full discussion. The fig-tree-meditation analogue (Nathanael under the fig tree, 1:48) and the night-meditation (Nikodemos by night, 3:2) bracket the John 1–3 narrative arc in interesting ways.

## Yehudim (the Judeans / the Jews)
**Meaning:** literally "Judeans" — the group ethnically and territorially associated with Yehudah (Judah); rendered "Yehudim" in the TT rather than "Jews" to preserve the term's three-sense ambiguity per the Ioudaioi Policy (RULES-GS.md §Ioudaioi Policy; Entry J-006)
**Origin:** APPEARS — a corporate group, not an individual; first appears in John at 1:19 as the delegating authority that sends priests and Levites to Yochanan
**First mention:** Jn 1:19
**Mentioned in:** Jn 1:19; 2:6, 13, 18, 20; 3:1, 25; 4:9, 22; 5:1, 10, 15, 16, 18; 6:4, 41, 52; 7:1, 11, 13, 15, 35; 8:22, 31, 48, 52, 57; 9:18, 22; 10:19, 24, 31, 33; 11:8, 19, 31, 33, 36, 45, 54, 55; 12:9, 11; 13:33; 18:12, 14, 20, 31, 33, 36, 38, 39; 19:3, 7, 12, 14, 19, 20, 21, 31, 38, 40; 20:19 — the term appears approximately 70 times across the Gospel, more than in any other Gospel
**Profession:** religious-political authority (institutional sense) / civic identity (geographic sense); the group functions as a corporate actor sending emissaries, convening hearings, and (in the polemical sense) opposing Yeshua
**Hometown:** Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) and Yehudah (Judah) more broadly — though the geographic sense extends to Judean Jews throughout the Diaspora
**Historicity status:** VERIFIED — the existence of the Yehudim as a corporate referent (under all three senses) is attested at the highest confidence by Josephus, Tacitus, Roman administrative records, rabbinic literature, archaeological + epigraphic evidence (synagogues, mikvaot, ossuaries, Qumran scrolls)
**Books appearing in:** Matthew; Mark; Luke; John; Acts; Pauline letters (Romans 9–11 especially); Hebrews; Revelation; the OT throughout
**Character arc:** Three operative senses across John (per Ioudaioi Policy / Entry J-006). **(1) Geographic / ethnic** — "the Judeans" / "the people of Judea." Primary in geographic contexts: "the Yehudim had agreed" (9:22); "for the purification of the Yehudim" (2:6); "the Pesach (Passover) of the Yehudim" (2:13, 6:4, 11:55). **(2) Institutional** — "the Judean authorities" / "the Jerusalem leadership." Primary in delegation / hearing / order-issuing contexts: the 1:19 delegation of priests and Levites; the 5:10 sabbath-rebuke; the 9:22 expulsion-policy. **(3) Polemical / adversarial** — "those who oppose Yeshua." Primary in conflict scenes (chapters 5–10 especially): "the Yehudim sought all the more to kill him" (5:18); "the Yehudim took up stones" (10:31); "many of the Yehudim no longer walked with him" (6:66 implied by 6:60–66 in some readings, though the Greek there reads *mathētai*). Sense must be determined from context.
**Archaeological evidence:** The Yehudim's historicity is independently attested at the highest level — Yerushalayim's Herodian Temple Mount expansion, dozens of excavated 1st-century synagogues (Magdala, Gamla, Capernaum, Masada, Herodium), the Qumran community, hundreds of ritual immersion pools (mikvaot), thousands of Jewish ossuaries with Hebrew/Aramaic inscriptions, the Pilate Stone (verifying the Pontius Pilate of John 18–19's Roman administrative context). The institutional + geographic + ethnic dimensions of the Yehudim are archaeologically unimpeachable. **Anti-misuse safeguard:** the term *hoi Ioudaioi* in John does NOT mean "the Jewish people as a whole" across time; reading without sense-distinction has historically contributed to anti-Jewish interpretation and to the Christian-tradition's appropriation of the term as a generalized villain — a misuse the Ioudaioi Policy + this entry explicitly resist.
**Extra-biblical mentions:** Josephus, *Antiquities of the Jews* (entire work — the foundational Greco-Roman-era source for the Yehudim as a corporate referent); Josephus, *Bellum Judaicum* (the Jewish War, 66–70 CE); Tacitus, *Histories* 5 (the most extensive non-Jewish Roman account of the Yehudim); Suetonius, *Claudius* 25.4 (the "Chrestos" expulsion); Pliny, *Letters* 10.96 (Christian-Jewish boundary); Roman administrative inscriptions (Pilate Stone; tax records); rabbinic literature post-70 CE (Mishnah, Tosefta, Talmuds — the rabbinic-period self-attestation).

## Eliyahu (Elijah)
**See:** kings/PEOPLE.md
**In John:** Referenced at 1:21 and 1:25 when the Yehudim delegation asks Yochanan "are you Eliyahu?" — to which Yochanan answers "I am not." The question presupposes the Mal'akhi 4:5 expectation that Eliyahu would return before the day of YHWH; Yochanan's denial (in contrast to the Synoptic Mt 11:14, 17:11–13 where Yeshua identifies Yochanan as Eliyahu) is a Johannine theological choice. ## Yeshayahu (Isaiah)
**See:** isaiah/PEOPLE.md
